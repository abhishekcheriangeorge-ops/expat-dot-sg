The single most common pass mix-up on r/askSingapore is still some version of “HR says there’s no EP quota, so it has to be S Pass.” It’s worth getting straight before you resign from a job overseas, and definitely before you accept a “temporary” S Pass that quietly sets both your family options and your employer’s levy economics.

## The comparison, in one table

| Axis | Employment Pass | S Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it’s aimed at | Professionals, managers, executives | Mid-skilled associate professionals and technicians |
| Employer quota (DRC) | None | Yes, a sector Dependency Ratio Ceiling |
| Monthly levy | None | Yes, and you should verify the live MOM tier |
| Stage 2 assessment | [COMPASS](/guides/compass-framework-explained), where you need 40 points or more unless exempt | No COMPASS |
| Qualifying salary, roughly | From S$5,600 outside financial services, S$6,200 inside it, and it climbs with age. More below | Lower floors, around S$3,300+ outside financial services after the Sep 2025 steps, also age-banded |
| Dependant’s Pass gate | Fixed monthly salary of at least S$6,000 for eligible spouse and children | The same S$6,000 fixed monthly gate |
| The red flag you’ll see on forums | An offer that sits below the updated age-band floor | The “no EP quota” story |

The row that matters most is the quota row. **EP has no foreign-worker quota at all**, so when HR says they’ve run out, they’re describing a different problem and using the wrong word for it.

## The EP salary floors that keep breaking offers (the 2025–2028 calendar)

Here’s the thing that trips up even careful candidates: MOM runs new applications and renewals on two different clocks, so a number that’s fine for a colleague who’s renewing may be nowhere near enough for you applying fresh. This is an orientation snapshot from the EP eligibility page, and you should re-check it rather than trust a table you found in September.

| Sector | Current new-application floor, youngest band | Next published step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything except financial services | S$5,600 a month, rising with age to S$10,700 at 45+ | S$6,000 for new applications from 1 Jan 2027, and for renewals of passes expiring from 1 Jan 2028 |
| Financial services | S$6,200 a month, rising to S$11,800 at 45+ | S$6,600 and S$12,700 on those same 2027 and 2028 clocks |

Two things generate most of the forum heat. The first is candidates who signed at S$5,000 after the new-application step on 1 Jan 2025. The floor had already moved, and the offer letter was already signed. The second is people who conflate the qualifying salary with the COMPASS C1 salary percentiles, which are a different test doing a different job.

The practical version: fixed monthly salary is what MOM looks at. Packaging part of your pay as allowances to make the headline number look bigger fails you at Stage 1, before anyone gets as far as counting COMPASS points. If you want the full mechanics, they’re in our [Employment Pass guide](/guides/employment-pass-singapore).

## When “S Pass first” is honest, and when it’s laziness

There are real reasons an employer files S Pass. The role may genuinely be mid-skilled with pay that sits in S Pass bands, and no amount of arguing changes that. The firm may be shaky on COMPASS for reasons that have nothing to do with you, such as diversity, local employment share, or credentials, even where the cash comfortably clears the EP floor. Or you may want to start work while your EP documents are still incomplete, which is defensible if, and only if, there is a written conversion plan rather than a verbal promise.

Then there are the reasons that don’t survive a follow-up question. “We have no EP quota” is the big one, and it isn’t a thing. “S Pass is easier for Indians” or for PRC nationals or whoever else is being described that week confuses two separate systems: nationality concentration shows up in COMPASS C3 and C5, and it isn’t an entitlement that S Pass grants. And “everyone starts on S Pass here” quietly skips the important part, which is that converting later means a **brand-new EP application**, not an upgrade that happens automatically at review time. If you’re planning on that route anyway, the in-country checklist is [S Pass to EP upgrade](/guides/s-pass-to-ep-upgrade-in-singapore).

## Family and household maths

Both passes can sponsor Dependant’s Passes, provided your fixed monthly salary clears S$6,000 and the relationship rules are met. Check the current position on [DP eligibility](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/dependants-pass/eligibility) rather than assuming.

Where the two passes diverge for a household is renewal confidence. S Pass levy and quota uncertainty sits with the employer, but it doesn’t stay there: it shapes how willing they are to renew you, and that risk lands on your lease and your school deposits, which are the two things you can’t unwind cheaply. If a trailing partner is part of the picture, we cover the pass side in [Dependant’s Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore) and the job-market side in [trailing partner careers](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore).

## Before you sign

1. Ask HR which pass they’ll file, and ask for the fixed monthly salary figure that will actually appear on the application. Not the total package, the figure on the form.
2. Ask them to run MOM’s Self-Assessment Tool and send you a screenshot of the outcome.
3. If they say EP is impossible, ask whether the blocker is salary, COMPASS, FCF advertising, or quota. Only one of those four words belongs to S Pass, and their answer tells you whether you’re dealing with a real constraint or an assumption nobody has checked.
4. Price the levy and the renewal risk into your household model before you agree to anything. [Salary package decoding](/guides/salary-package-decoding) walks through how.
