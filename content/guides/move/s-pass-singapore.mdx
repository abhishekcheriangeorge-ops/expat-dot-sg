The S Pass sits between the Work Permit and the Employment Pass. Mid-skilled roles, a regulated salary floor, an employer quota, and a monthly levy the company pays for the privilege of hiring you. Day to day it can feel a lot like an EP: same office, same commute, same lease. Where it differs is in the company's economics, and that's usually where the offer narrative starts bending.

One story comes up on r/askSingapore over and over. HR tells a candidate they "have no EP quota" and so it has to be an S Pass. **The Employment Pass has no foreign-worker quota.** None. The S Pass does. So when someone says "EP quota" to you, they almost certainly mean one of three other things: they're nervous about COMPASS, they don't want to pay at EP salary levels, or they've muddled it up with the S Pass Dependency Ratio Ceiling. It's a fair question to ask which one.

## S Pass vs EP, from where you're sitting

| Topic | S Pass | Employment Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it's for | Mid-skilled / associate professional & technician band | Professionals / managers / executives |
| Employer quota (DRC) | Yes, capped by sector | No |
| Monthly levy | Yes (harmonised rate, verify the live MOM table) | No |
| COMPASS | No | Yes (unless exempt) |
| Family (DP) | Possible from S$6,000 fixed monthly salary | Same S$6,000 DP threshold for eligible EP holders |
| What people flag on forums | "Company claims no EP quota" | Offer below updated qualifying salary |

## Salary and levy: the 2025 step

MOM's COS 2025 third-step increase applies to new S Pass applications from 1 September 2025, and to renewals for passes expiring from 1 September 2026. The orientation figures:

- All sectors except financial services: from about S$3,300 at the youngest band, rising with age (mid-40s band toward ~S$4,800)
- Financial services: a higher floor, from about S$3,800, also rising with age
- Levy: Tier 1 harmonised toward S$650/month from 1 Sep 2025, though you should verify the current MOM levy page

Further steps for later years are already published on the eligibility page, so this isn't the last move you'll see. Take the age banding seriously, because it catches experienced hires off guard. Someone in their 40s needs a higher fixed monthly salary than a 23-year-old with the identical job title, which means an offer that comfortably cleared the bar for whoever held the role before you may not clear it for you.

## Quota: why your employer cares more than you do

S Pass headcount is capped by the sector Dependency Ratio Ceiling, and services tends to be the tightest of the common office sectors. The quota is calculated off the company's local CPF-declared headcount, so if finance files CPF late, the apparent quota shrinks and workers get pushed onto higher levy tiers. All of that is an employer problem. It reaches you dressed up as "we can only do S Pass," or later as "renewal is uncertain."

Ask these in writing, before you resign from anything:

1. Is this role budgeted as S Pass or EP?
2. Does the company currently have S Pass quota headroom?
3. What happens at renewal if quota or salary bands move?

## Bringing the family

An S Pass holder on a fixed monthly salary of at least S$6,000 can generally sponsor a legally married spouse and unmarried children under 21 for Dependant's Passes. That's the same headline DP salary gate an EP holder faces, so "S Pass means no family" isn't true. What doesn't transfer automatically is everything else you've read on EP forums: the LTVP threads, the Letter of Consent stories, the trailing-partner advice. Check the live DP and LTVP pages against your own numbers before you move a household across the world on the strength of a comment.

## Before you accept

If your pay clears the EP qualifying salary and the role is genuinely professional-grade, push back on "no EP quota" and bring MOM's own EP key facts into the conversation. Keep certified diplomas and a tidy employment history to hand while you're at it. S Pass document scrutiny is real, and it's slower than people expect.

Be clear-eyed about the upgrade path too. **Moving from S Pass to EP later is a fresh application**, not a promotion that follows your pay rise: qualifying salary, Fair Consideration advertising unless you're exempt, COMPASS unless you're exempt. A raise that clears the EP floors helps enormously. It doesn't convert the pass by itself.
