**Short answer:** You don't need Singpass on day one to get online or to start banking. Land with a prepaid +65 SIM, finish pass issuance and biometrics with HR, register Singpass once your FIN is recognised, then open the payroll account and set up PayNow. Singpass is an accelerator, not the first lock in the chain.

Forum threads keep inventing a circular dependency out of this. The bank needs Singpass, Singpass needs a SIM, the SIM needs a bank. It isn't actually circular, and the knot comes undone the moment you separate the bridge tools you'll use for a fortnight from the permanent ones you'll keep for years.

## If you've only got X, do Y

| What you've got so far | What to do next | What not to wait for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passport and a flight | Submit the [SG Arrival Card](/guides/sg-arrival-card-expats) within 3 days before landing | Singpass |
| Passport, and you're standing in Changi | A prepaid or tourist +65 SIM, or an eSIM | A postpaid contract |
| IPA, no physical card yet | Biometrics and issuance with HR, keep the prepaid topped up, and ask HR which bank payroll actually uses | Perfect long-term housing |
| Notification Letter or issued pass, plus a FIN | Singpass registration, using the app and face verification where it's offered | Opening every government account first |
| Local number, pass or IPA, and address proof | The bank account. Singpass is optional on plenty of paths | Credit cards |
| A local salary account | PayNow registered on your FIN, then GIRO for the utilities | Multi-currency "optimisation" |

## The sequence that usually works

1. Before wheels-down, submit the free [SG Arrival Card](/guides/sg-arrival-card-expats). Pack the IPA or pass letter, your passport, and whatever medical documents HR flagged.
2. On day 0 or 1, buy a prepaid SIM on your passport. Save HR, your relocation contact and building security into the phone while you're thinking about it, and set up SimplyGo or contactless transit ([MRT payment options](/guides/mrt-bus-transport-literacy)).
3. During issuance week, turn up to EPSC and biometrics when they're scheduled. MOM's Notification Letter, which comes after issuance, is what lets you start work while the physical card is still being printed, so you aren't sitting idle waiting on plastic. The clocks are in [issuance, EPSC and the card](/guides/work-pass-issuance-epsc-notification), and check the live wording on [MOM's EP apply page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/apply-for-a-pass/).
4. Then Singpass, once the FIN and the Singapore mobile number are both ready. Use face verification in the app where it's offered. Never share an OTP or scan a QR code for a helpful stranger in an "HR WhatsApp" group. It's a well-worn local scam and we've written it up in [Singpass scams and phishing](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats).
5. Then the bank. Open the payroll account with your passport, IPA or pass, and address proof. Singpass and MyInfo make it quick, but non-Singpass upload paths do exist, and the [DBS foreigner document list](https://www.dbs.com.sg/personal/support/bank-account-opening-documents-required.html) is one published example of what they want.
6. Finally PayNow and utilities. Register PayNow on your FIN, then sort SP and fibre once the keys are real, which we cover in [utilities and telecom](/guides/utilities-telecom-setup).

While steps 3 to 5 are clearing, you're bridging on foreign debit and credit cards, an employer cheque, or a licensed remittance wallet. **Don't drain the foreign cards before payroll has actually landed once** — a stalled account opening and an empty card in the same week is a genuinely bad fortnight.

## Bridge stack versus permanent stack

| What you need | Bridge (days 0–14) | Permanent (once the FIN and pass are stable) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile | Prepaid or tourist SIM | Postpaid, or keep the prepaid and port it |
| Identity online | Passport and IPA PDFs | Singpass and MyInfo |
| Money in | Foreign cards, or a Wise-style wallet | Local salary account plus PayNow |
| Housing admin | Hotel or serviced-apartment letter | Stamped tenancy and an SP account |

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need Singpass before I can open a Singapore bank account?

No. Singpass speeds up the MyInfo autofill, which is genuinely pleasant, but banks commonly accept a passport plus IPA or issued pass plus proof of address without it. If a branch insists on a Singpass you don't have yet, ask for the non-Singpass checklist, or go to whichever bank your employer's payroll already uses. One officer's habit isn't always the bank's policy.

### Can I get a SIM with only IPA or passport?

For prepaid, yes. Postpaid staff will often want to see a physical pass, a FIN or Singpass before they'll put you on a contract. That refusal is routine rather than personal, so keep the prepaid number alive and port it later.

### What if payroll hits before my account opens?

Tell HR on day one, not on payday. The usual options are delaying the first credit, a temporary cheque, or opening at the partner bank with an introduction letter from HR. Escalating through payroll works far better than arguing your own case in a branch queue.

### Should I register PayNow to my mobile number or FIN?

Use your **FIN** once you have one. Prepaid numbers get recycled, and a FIN-linked PayNow survives you changing SIM. The detail is in [PayNow for foreigners](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore).

## What happens next

Once the bank and Singpass both work, you can move on to stamped housing, fibre, registering with a GP, and the trailing partner's work rights. Living mode starts when renewals and belonging matter to you more than issuance does, so switch over to the [Living hub](/living) when week one stops feeling like a crisis.
