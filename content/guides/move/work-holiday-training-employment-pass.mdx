Internship threads on r/askSingapore keep inventing a third option: "I'll just work on my visit pass." That is illegal. There are two published rails for short stints here, the Work Holiday Pass family and the Training Employment Pass (TEP), and they solve genuinely different problems. Neither one is a back door to PR.

## How the two rails compare

| | Work Holiday Programme (WHP) | Work & Holiday (AU / NZ) | Training Employment Pass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who applies | You do, yourself | You do, yourself | Your employer does |
| Typical age | 18–25 | 18–30, and you need to be a citizen of AU or NZ | No tourist-style age band; student or trainee criteria instead |
| Stay and work | Up to 6 months | Up to 12 months | Up to 3 months, and **not renewable** |
| University gate | Undergrad or graduate of a recognised university in a listed country | A degree, or at least 2 years of undergrad study | Student: the training has to be part of your course at an acceptable institution, or you're on at least S$3,000 fixed monthly. Trainee from an overseas office: at least S$3,000 |
| Levy / quota | None | None | None |
| Family passes | No | No | No |
| After it ends | You leave, or an employer sponsors an EP or S Pass if you qualify | Same, and AU/NZ holders also can't work for the same employer for more than 6 months on this pass | You leave, or move to a different pass type. TEP itself doesn't renew |

## Work Holiday: what the forums get wrong

Start with the part nobody warns you about. Plenty of Singapore hiring managers have never heard of the WHP, so you will end up explaining your own pass to the person deciding whether to hire you. Bring the MOM link and lead with the two facts that actually change their answer: no levy, and no quota. That's what makes you cheap and quick to take on.

Apply for the pass and chase roles in parallel. Employers frequently want proof you can work legally before they'll issue an offer, which pushes you to apply early, while the pass has finite validity, which punishes you for applying too early and letting it burn before your start date. There's no clean solution to that tension, only timing it as well as you can.

WHP doesn't extend as WHP. Converting means a real EP or S Pass story with salary, COMPASS and quota rules attached, so read [the EP guide](/guides/employment-pass-singapore), [the S Pass guide](/guides/s-pass-singapore) and [Fair Consideration](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep) before you assume your host will just roll you over. And if you're headed into a regulated profession, medicine or law for instance, you still need local registration. The pass doesn't stand in for it.

The listed WHP source countries and regions are Australia, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Switzerland, United Kingdom and United States, with the university recognition rules set out on MOM's page. Verify that list live before you plan around it.

## Training Employment Pass: the internship rail

TEP is built for a genuine training attachment, not for a full-time hire dressed up as training. Your employer files it, and the fees and steps sit on MOM's apply page, with application and issuance fees and most cases turning around in about three weeks. Verify those figures live rather than trusting a forum post from two intakes ago.

MOM publishes a sample list of institutions, and people badly misread it. It's indicative, not a guarantee that your school clears, and prestige arguments don't help. If your university isn't on it, have your employer or you contact MOM early rather than submitting and hoping.

If the TEP doesn't come through, don't start anyway on a Short-Term Visit Pass. **A visit pass is not permission to work**, whatever the employer tells you about sorting it out later. Your real options are WHP if you're eligible, a proper work pass, or doing the training somewhere else.

## Where the Student's Pass fits

Full-time study sits on ICA's Student's Pass instead, which we cover in [the Student's Pass guide](/guides/student-pass-singapore). Part-time work for Student's Pass holders runs on a separate MOM exemption track, so don't reason across from one to the other. Mixing "I'm here on holiday" with paid work and no WHP, TEP or EP behind it is how people end up with an immigration problem rather than a LinkedIn story.

## Before you email HR

1. Map your nationality, age and student status onto WHP, AU/NZ Work & Holiday or TEP first, so you're asking for something that exists
2. Never accept "just come on a tourist visa and we'll sort it out"
3. Budget for a short lease rather than a standard one, and see [serviced and short-stay options](/guides/short-term-vs-long-term-rental)
4. If the plan is converting to an EP afterwards, start the COMPASS and FCF conversation early, not in your final month
5. Tuition Grant bonds sit on a completely different rail from WHP, so don't mix them up ([the TG bond guide](/guides/moe-tuition-grant-bond))
