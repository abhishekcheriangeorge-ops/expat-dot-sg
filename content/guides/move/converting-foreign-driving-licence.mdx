**Short answer:** As a residing foreigner you can usually drive on a valid foreign licence for about **12 months**, and after that you need a Singapore one. Converting means passing the BTT and turning up in person to a Traffic Police appointment; there is no online form for it. The JB weekend licence shortcut doesn’t work. And if Grab and the MRT already cover your life, you can skip the whole exercise.

If you have a teenager heading toward provisional independence, the conversion paperwork is the easy part and the family logistics are the hard part; we cover those in [teen driving independence](/guides/teen-driving-independence-singapore).

Driving here is genuinely optional for a lot of central lifestyles, and expensive when it isn’t — COE, parking, ERP and insurance all stack. Conversion, though, runs on a hard clock, and it’s the clock rather than the cost that catches EP holders out.

## The 12-month resident rule

If you are a foreigner residing in Singapore, Traffic Police guidance allows you to drive on a valid foreign licence, subject to the IDP and English translation rules that apply to your country, for a limited period. The figure commonly discussed is up to 12 months. After that you generally need a Singapore licence to keep driving, and nothing extends the window quietly because you were busy settling in.

New citizens and PRs get separate, shorter windows and their own conversion obligations. Read the live SPF page for your exact status rather than a forum post about somebody else’s.

| Scenario | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Tourist or short visit | Foreign licence plus IDP and translation rules; the insurance has to name you |
| EP or DP resident under ~12 months | You may drive on the foreign licence, subject to SPF rules |
| Resident past the allowed period | Convert, or stop driving |
| “I’ll get a Malaysia licence this weekend and convert it” | Doesn’t work. TP looks for genuine residency where the licence was issued |

## The conversion path, if your licence is recognised

The shape of it, as TP materials and people who have been through it describe:

1. Pass the Basic Theory Test (BTT) in Singapore
2. Gather originals plus photocopies: passport, FIN and pass, the foreign licence, a photo, translations if you need them, and residency evidence where it’s asked for
3. Turn up to a Traffic Police counter appointment. Online conversion is not a thing, which surprises people who assume everything here is an app
4. Receive a Singapore QDL, subject to approval

The document checklist gets noticeably stricter if you obtained the foreign licence **after** you already held a Singapore pass or PR. Traffic Police may want proof that you genuinely resided in the issuing country, because a fly-in test weekend is precisely what that check exists to catch.

## If your country isn’t on the easy-conversion path

You enrol with a driving centre, BBDC and its peers being the usual names, and work through the theory and practical requirements like any other learner. Budget the time as well as the lesson costs, which often land in the low thousands of SGD if you are starting cold. EP holders are eligible. Foreigner licences can carry different validity patterns from SC and PR licences, so confirm yours at issuance instead of assuming it matches what a colleague has.

## Do you actually need to drive?

| Lifestyle | How necessary a car is |
| --- | --- |
| CBD, East Coast, or a well-served MRT corridor | Often optional; MRT and Grab genuinely cover it |
| Bukit Timah landed, school-run heavy | A car or reliable private hire stops being a luxury and becomes infrastructure |
| Shift work outside MRT hours | A car, or a Grab budget you actually keep to |

## A practical timeline

Months 1–3 are for deciding honestly whether you will be driving in months 6–12. If the answer is yes, book the BTT early, because appointment slots and document chasing will eat weekends you would rather spend elsewhere. Sort the insurance in parallel too: being “named” on a relative’s policy is not automatic, so check rather than assume you are covered.

Convert because you are going to drive. Not because the relocation checklist printed it in bold.
