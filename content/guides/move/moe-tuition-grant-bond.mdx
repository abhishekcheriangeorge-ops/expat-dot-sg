International students who take MOE's Tuition Grant (TG) are making a trade: lower fees now, against a 3-year full-time employment bond in a Singapore entity after graduation. It's a real subsidy and plenty of people are glad they took it. The trouble, judging by r/askSingapore, is that most of them modelled only the fee discount, and then met liquidated damages, sureties and EP sponsorship friction all in the same month.

## Who is actually bonded

Singapore Citizens are not under the same 3-year employment bond. Singapore PRs and international students who receive TG for eligible diploma or undergraduate courses at Institutes of Higher Learning generally are. What settles it in your case is the Tuition Grant Agreement that you and your sureties signed, so read that document. A forum summary is not your contract, and neither is this page.

## What counts as serving the bond

| Rule | MOE's published framing |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 3 years of cumulative full-time employment after graduation |
| Start | You begin serving immediately after graduation, and you update your employment status on the TG&S portal within 3 months |
| Employers | Employment with a Singapore entity. You can change companies as long as the cumulative time still adds up |
| Overseas postings | Narrow. Typically Singapore-entity payroll, a majority-owned subsidiary or a secondment path, and often with prior MOE approval |
| Part-time and gig work | Does not count toward the bond |

MOE's employment page also nudges international graduates to attach S Pass or EP eligibility letters from their institution when job-hunting. That one is genuinely worth doing, because it takes some of the COMPASS theatre out of a recruiter conversation before it starts.

## Liquidated damages: the buy-out, and the ways people fall into it

You become liable for liquidated damages if you withdraw from or are terminated from the course without continuing TG, if you change course without taking TG on the new course, if you buy out the bond, or if you fail to fulfil the bond requirements after graduation. That last route is how most people actually get there, and rarely on purpose.

The published calculation has three parts: the total TG you received, any GST subsidy, and 10% interest per year, compounded at the end of each academic year. The compounding is why the figure your senior quotes from their cohort is almost never the figure you'll be shown.

Sureties are jointly liable under the agreement language graduates quote on Reddit, which is the part worth saying out loud to whoever signed for you while it is still hypothetical. Always run MOE's live LD figures rather than inherited ones, because cohort fees differ.

## Job loss and resignation: the windows behind the panic

The agreement language commonly discussed on Reddit asks you to secure Singapore employment within about one year of graduating, and to find re-employment within about six months of leaving a job, unless MOE grants relief. Those clocks are agreement-specific, so confirm yours on TG&S or with MOE **before you resign into a gap**. Resigning first and reading the agreement afterwards is the expensive order to do it in.

Three things pair with this on the immigration side. A graduate [LTVP for seeking employment](https://www.ica.gov.sg/reside/LTVP/apply/graduate-from-an-institute-of-higher-learning-seeking-employment-in-singapore) can buy you time to stay while you look. If an EP is cancelled, MOM's STVP rules still apply, and we've set those out in [between jobs and STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore). And deferment, whether for further study or approved overseas work, is a formal TG&S path that often carries Banker's Guarantee requirements. It is not a quiet WhatsApp to HR.

## Decide this before you sign Year 1

1. Model full fees against TG plus 3 years locked to the Singapore labour market, using today's EP floors and FCF advertising norms rather than the ones your seniors graduated into. See [FCF and MyCareersFuture](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep).
2. Tell your sureties the LD maths in writing. It's an awkward conversation to have now and a considerably worse one to have later.
3. Start the career-centre and internship pipelines early. Waiting until final exams is how that first-year clock gets tight.
4. Keep your TG&S employment updates current. Silent gaps are what produce LD letters.

The Tuition Grant is a genuine subsidy. It is also a bet that the Singapore labour market will still want to sponsor you three years from now. Price that bet honestly.
