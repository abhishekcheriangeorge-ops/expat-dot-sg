**Short answer:** Check every active ingredient on HSA's tool before you fly, not the brand name on the box. Non-controlled medicines are generally fine up to a **3-month supply** in original packs with a prescription. Anything with a controlled active needs **HSA approval before you arrive**, and HSA wants that application at least 2 weeks ahead. What your home country calls "prescription only" has no bearing on what Singapore decides.

r/askSingapore fills up every few weeks with last-week panic about ADHD stimulants, benzodiazepines, codeine and sleeping tablets. The part people miss is that Singapore's rules run off ingredients rather than products. The brand name doesn't help you, and neither does the fact that you buy the thing over the counter at home.

## Do this before you book the flight

1. Write out every active ingredient, not just the brand names on the boxes.
2. Run each one through HSA's [Check requirements](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/travelling-with-medication-and-medical-devices/personal-medications/) tool.
3. Sort what comes back into three piles: allowed without approval subject to quantity limits, controlled and therefore needing prior approval, and prohibited outright.
4. Where approval is needed, apply in English through HSA's personal-medication form and keep the approval letter with your boarding pass.

## The 3-month rule, and the conditions attached to it

You can usually bring in up to a 3-month supply without prior approval, but only when all of the following are true at once. Fail one and the allowance simply doesn't apply to you.

| Condition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Lawfully prescribed to you, or to an immediate family member, in your country of residence | It's a personal-use allowance, so it doesn't stretch to carrying something in for a friend |
| Nothing on HSA's Appendix A controlled list | Controlled means approval, and a small quantity doesn't get you out of it |
| Nothing prohibited, such as Fourth Schedule Misuse of Drugs items | Some actives simply aren't importable at all |
| Nothing in chewing-gum form, and no CTGTP products | These product classes carry separate bans of their own |

Carry the original labelled containers along with a prescription or doctor's letter naming you and listing the drug, strength, dose and quantity. The "nobody checked my bag" stories on Reddit are true often enough to be tempting. They are still not a compliance plan.

## Controlled medicines, where this usually goes wrong

These are the ones the forums panic about, and they commonly need **prior HSA approval**. Check your exact salt and strength live rather than trusting any list, including this one:

- ADHD stimulants, methylphenidate and amphetamine-class products among them
- Benzodiazepines, and many Z-drug sleep aids
- Certain opioids and codeine-containing products
- Other psychotropics sitting on HSA's controlled schedules

HSA asks for applications at least 2 weeks before arrival. An incomplete form gets rejected, and the rejection restarts that clock rather than pausing it, which is how a comfortable margin quietly becomes a missed flight's worth of trouble. Approvals are also tied to the arrival date you gave, so changing your flight means applying again. Travel with the approval email, the prescription and the original packaging together, not scattered across three bags.

## Packing, and the Changi end of it

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Keep medicines in their original labelled bottles or blister packs | Tip controlled or prescription stock into an unlabelled pill organiser |
| Pack a printed ingredient list in your hand luggage | Assume that OTC at home means OTC here |
| Declare when you're unsure, and keep the HSA emails on your phone | Hide controlled tablets on the grounds that it's medical |

If you land without an approval you needed, confiscation is the good outcome. Singapore's drug trafficking penalties are severe, and medical intent doesn't rewrite the Misuse of Drugs Act.

## Once you've landed

Your 3-month window, or whatever quantity was approved, is runway for building a local supply. Spend it on that rather than on getting comfortable.

1. Register with a private GP or specialist on your insurance panel — [GP / hospital triage](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital) covers how to choose.
2. Ask early whether your exact molecule is stocked here, because some brands need a switch.
3. Confirm what your [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) schedule actually says about outpatient and chronic medication cover.
4. If this is mental-health medication, read [mental health resources](/guides/mental-health-resources) well before the supply you brought runs out.

HSA's personal-import rules are built for travel and settling in. They aren't a standing mail-order pharmacy from overseas, so get a Singapore prescription path working in month one.
