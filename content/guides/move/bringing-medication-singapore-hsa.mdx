**Short answer:** Check every **active ingredient** on HSA’s tool before you fly. Non-controlled personal meds: up to a **3-month supply** in original packs with a prescription. Controlled actives need **prior HSA approval** — apply **≥2 weeks** before arrival. Your home label of “prescription only” does **not** decide Singapore’s rule.

r/askSingapore fills up with last-week panic about ADHD stimulants, benzodiazepines, codeine, and sleeping tablets. Singapore’s rules are ingredient-based — brand names and overseas OTC status are irrelevant.

## Do this before you book the flight

1. List every active ingredient (not just brand names).  
2. Run each through HSA’s [Check requirements](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/personal-medication/check-requirements) tool.  
3. Separate the pile into: allowed without approval (quantity limits), **controlled → prior approval**, or **prohibited**.  
4. If approval is needed, apply in English via HSA’s personal-medication form and keep the approval letter with your boarding pass.

## The 3-month rule (non-controlled)

You may usually bring up to a **3-month supply** without prior approval when **all** of the following are true:

| Condition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Lawfully prescribed to you (or immediate family) in your country of residence | Personal use only — not “for a friend” |
| No controlled substances from HSA’s Appendix A list | Controlled = approval regardless of small quantity |
| No prohibited substances (e.g. Fourth Schedule Misuse of Drugs) | Some actives are simply not importable |
| Not chewing-gum form / not CTGTP products | Special product classes have separate bans |

Carry original labelled containers plus a prescription or doctor’s letter listing your name, drug, strength, dose, and quantity. Reddit “nobody checked my bag” stories are not a compliance plan.

## Controlled meds — the Reddit failure mode

Common forum panic drugs that often need **prior HSA approval** (verify live for your exact salt/strength):

- ADHD stimulants (e.g. methylphenidate / amphetamine-class products)  
- Benzodiazepines and many Z-drug sleep aids  
- Certain opioids / codeine-containing products  
- Other psychotropics listed in HSA’s controlled schedules  

HSA asks for applications **at least 2 weeks before arrival**; incomplete forms are rejected and restart the clock. Approvals are date-specific — change your flight and you must re-apply. Carry: approval email + prescription + original packaging.

## Packing and Customs

| Do | Don’t |
| --- | --- |
| Keep meds in original labelled bottles / blister packs | Dump everything into an unlabelled pill organiser for controlled / RX stock |
| Pack a printed ingredient list in your hand luggage | Assume “OTC at home” = OTC here |
| Declare when unsure; keep HSA emails on your phone | Hide controlled tablets because “it’s medical” |

If you arrive without required approval, expect confiscation at best. Singapore’s drug trafficking penalties are severe — medical intent does not rewrite the Misuse of Drugs Act.

## After you land — continuity of care

Use the 3-month window (or approved quantity) to:

1. Register with a private GP or specialist on your insurance panel — see [GP / hospital triage](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital).  
2. Ask whether your exact molecule is stocked locally; some brands need switches.  
3. Confirm outpatient / chronic-med cover on your [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) schedule.  
4. For mental-health continuity, read [mental health resources](/guides/mental-health-resources) before your home supply runs out.

HSA personal-import rules are for **travel and settling-in**, not a forever mail-order pharmacy from overseas. Plan a Singapore prescription path in month one.
