**Short answer:** A Dependant's Pass lets your spouse and unmarried kids under 21 live here on the back of your main pass. It is **not** a work pass. An ordinary job needs its own EP, S Pass, or Work Permit, and the Letter of Consent now really only covers eligible business owners. And if your main EP goes, the DPs go with it, so have an STVP bridge ready for the whole household.

A Dependant's Pass (DP) is how your spouse and unmarried children under 21 come with you, riding on your main pass — usually an Employment Pass or S Pass. Worth knowing up front: having the main pass isn't enough on its own. MOM layers its own family-pass salary rules on top.

## Who actually qualifies

You need to clear MOM's salary and pass-type bar for dependants. Those thresholds move, and they've moved more than once in the last few years, so check today's number before you commit to anything from overseas. Especially a school contract.

## Working on a DP: please read this bit

This is the one that catches families out, so let's be blunt. A DP is not a work pass.

Since 1 May 2021, a DP holder who wants a normal job needs their own EP, S Pass, or Work Permit. The advice you'll still find on forums — that your employer can just sort out a Letter of Consent — stopped being true years ago. People are still repeating it.

The Letter of Consent does still exist, but it's now basically for DP holders running a business: an ACRA-registered sole proprietor, partner, or director holding at least 30% of the shares or equivalent. Your first LOC is time-boxed, usually to a year or to DP expiry, whichever lands first.

Renewals are where solo consultancies fall over. To renew, you need to be employing at least one Singaporean or PR on the prevailing Local Qualifying Salary, with **three consecutive months of CPF contributions already paid** before you submit. If you're a one-person business, you've got nothing to show against that bar. That's the story behind most of the "my LOC renewal got rejected" posts.

If a renewal does fail and your spouse still has Singapore clients, there are three honest options: pause the local invoicing, hire someone to the published standard, or move onto an employer-sponsored or self-sponsored EP through a properly capitalised Pte Ltd ([incorporating as a foreigner](/guides/incorporate-company-foreigner-singapore)). Carrying on quietly under an expired LOC isn't one of them. If the work is genuinely for overseas clients only, different rules apply — see [side income and freelancing](/guides/side-income-freelance-work-pass).

## When the main EP gets cancelled

Your DPs are tied to your pass. Cancel or lapse the main EP and your dependants generally lose their DP too, which means they need a lawful way to stay while things get sorted. That bridge is usually an employer-requested Short-Term Visit Pass. It can run up to 90 days if HR asks for it, though what HR asks for by default is often a lot shorter. An STVP doesn't allow work, and people report it behaving as single-entry, so a quick trip out can end the bridge entirely.

So: ask HR **in writing, before cancel day**, for the longest STVP they'll request, and make sure the request covers everyone in the household rather than just you. It's an awkward conversation to have while you're also negotiating an exit, and it's much more awkward to have afterwards. The full sequence, IR21 and the graduate LTVP angle included, is in [between jobs on an EP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore). The principal-side rules live on [MOM's cancel an Employment Pass page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/cancel-a-pass).

## Schools and housing

School waitlists and condo viewings will run in parallel with your IPA timing. That's uncomfortable, and it's also completely normal. Use provisional holds where a school offers them, and read the cancellation terms properly before you hand over anything large — a delayed main pass is exactly the scenario those penalties were written for. More on that in [school waitlist realism](/guides/school-waitlist-realism).

## Get these ready early

- Marriage and birth certificates, with official translations if yours aren't in English
- Passports with plenty of validity left
- Your main pass IPA or issued-pass details

## A sensible order to do things in

1. Main pass clarity, with the salary fixed for MOM
2. DP applications
3. Housing deposit — take a flexible first lease if schools are still waitlisted
4. School enrolment fees

## Babies born in Singapore

A newborn doesn't inherit your pass. You register the birth through LifeSG, sort out a passport, and get the employer to lodge a DP inside MOM's published window, while an ICA Special Pass covers the baby's stay in the meantime. All the clocks are in [newborn pass and birth registration](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore).

## One budgeting note

Don't budget for the trailing partner earning anything in month one. Build the household budget as a single-income posting until a second pass genuinely exists, and treat anything earlier as a nice surprise.
