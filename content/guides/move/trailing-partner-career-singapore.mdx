Most trailing partners relocate without a job, and then discover that Singapore's work-pass system doesn't treat "spouse of an EP holder" as a ticket into the labour market. The Facebook expat groups and the r/askSingapore threads are blunt about it, and they're right to be: assume the non-sponsored spouse may be out of the market for months, especially in retail, hospitality, or anything an employer can fill locally without paperwork.

That isn't a reason to arrive defeated. It is a reason to know which rail you're on before you start applying.

## Sort out the legal status first, because the rules changed

### If you're on a Dependant's Pass behind an EP, EntrePass or PEP

From **1 May 2021**, MOM aligned DP work rules with the wider work-pass framework. A DP holder who wants to work for an employer generally needs their own Employment Pass, S Pass or Work Permit. There is no general Letter of Consent for employment any more, whatever a 2019 blog post still says.

The LOC path that survives is mainly for eligible business owners. You have to be the sole proprietor, a partner, or a company director of an ACRA-registered business holding at least 30% shareholding or the equivalent CLG membership. Renewals are the part nobody reads until it matters: they typically require hiring at least one Singaporean or PR at the prevailing Local Qualifying Salary, with **CPF contributions for three consecutive months**.

### If you're on an LTVP or LTVP+ through a citizen or PR spouse

That's a different rail. Spouses and children of citizens or PRs hold ICA-issued LTVP or LTVP+, and they sit under their own LOC and pre-approved LOC regime. Don't mix that advice with EP-sponsored DP advice, in either direction. Most of the confidently wrong answers in expat groups come from someone generalising from the pass they happen to hold.

## What the job market actually feels like

Hiring managers gravitate to candidates who already have independent pass eligibility, because your DP is residency and not a hiring shortcut. Even a keen employer still has to clear COMPASS, salary and quota constraints before they can sponsor an EP or S Pass for you, and that assessment happens before they can say yes, not after.

Sectors with deep local talent pools are the hardest to break into. General retail and a lot of junior admin roles fall into that bucket, and in those roles there's simply no shortage for an employer to point at. What moves the needle instead is networks, a genuinely scarce skill, and an employer who is flexible by temperament. A polished LinkedIn profile on its own doesn't.

One thing that catches people off guard: volunteer work and informal gigs can still raise compliance issues. "Unpaid" is not automatically safe.

## Remote work for an overseas employer

MOM's published FAQ for Dependant's Pass holders draws a line that HR teams still argue about. Providing services to a Singapore (ACRA) organisation or client for gain puts you squarely back into work-pass territory, so you need a pass or an eligible LOC. Providing services to an overseas-based organisation or client doesn't require a work pass for that overseas engagement.

Get the FAQ in writing for a nervous US or EU employer, because it settles the argument faster than you explaining it in a call. Then confirm the IRAS treatment separately: permission to work is not a tax holiday, and the two questions are decided by different agencies. The full decision tree is in [side income and freelancing on work passes](/guides/side-income-freelance-work-pass).

## Entrepreneurship isn't a loophole

Running a business on a DP LOC means meeting MOM's owner criteria **before** you operate, not after the first invoice clears. A first LOC typically runs for the shorter of one year from issue or your DP expiry, and straightforward cases are commonly processed in about four weeks. Budget for the setup costs, for the local hire you'll need at renewal, and for the fact that the LOC dies if the DP is cancelled.

If a renewal fails because you can't justify a Local Qualifying Salary hire, the one option that isn't available to you is carrying on invoicing Singapore clients. Pause, hire to the published standard, or migrate to an EP path ([Dependant's Pass deep dive](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore), [incorporate](/guides/incorporate-company-foreigner-singapore)).

## Negotiate before the main offer is signed

This is the point of maximum leverage, and it doesn't come round again. Things worth asking the sponsored partner's employer for:

1. Clarity on the DP versus LTVP strategy for the household.
2. Whether a ONE Pass path is realistic, if a spouse LOC is what's blocking you ([ONE Pass](/guides/one-pass-singapore)).
3. Career coaching, or recruitment introductions to people who are actually hiring in-country.
4. Temporary housing that lasts long enough for a real job search, rather than a two-week hotel.
5. Realistic dual-career timelines written into the household plan. Underemployment is a wellbeing risk, not a soft skill.

For how to price those asks against housing and school support, see [salary package decoding](/guides/salary-package-decoding).

## A realistic first 90 days

1. Confirm your pass type and work rights in writing with the immigration counsel or HR who filed the main pass. Not with a colleague's spouse over brunch.
2. If you're looking for employment, target employers who routinely sponsor EP or S Pass holders, and lead with the scarce skill rather than the job title.
3. If you're founding something, validate the ACRA structure and your LOC eligibility before you sign clients.
4. Rebuild the network through chambers, professional associations and our [belonging guides](/guides/find-my-people-singapore). Cold applications on their own underperform here.
5. Revisit the household budget monthly, and don't assume dual income in month two.

Underemployment is a first-class relocation risk, in the same tier as housing and schooling. Plan for it before wheels-up rather than in month three.
