The classic landing-week mistake is treating your IPA like a stamp in the passport and booking a JB food trip or a regional kick-off while MOM issuance is still open. It feels harmless, because the approval letter is sitting right there in your inbox. It's also how a perfectly clean file turns into a long conversation at the counter on the way back in.

## What each of these documents actually is

| Document | What it proves | What it means for travel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPA | In-principle approval for a work pass | It is **not a finished pass**, and it isn't a blanket guarantee you get back in |
| Notification Letter | Your employer has completed the key issuance steps | Worth carrying, but you still confirm the ICA and airline path for your status |
| The physical card, or SGWorkPass | The pass has actually been issued | The normal baseline for work travel for most EP and S Pass holders |

## Before you book anything

1. Ask HR which MOM issuance steps are still open: medical, biometrics, card collection.
2. Ask in writing whether you're cleared to leave and return on the status you hold today. A manager saying "should be fine" in a corridor is not the thing you'll be relying on later.
3. File the right [SG Arrival Card](/guides/sg-arrival-card-expats) path for the trip you're actually making, not the one you made last time.
4. Keep HR's emergency contact reachable across time zones, because if something does go sideways you'll be a long way from the only people who can chase MOM.

## The safer pattern

Finish [issuance, EPSC and the Notification Letter](/guides/work-pass-issuance-epsc-notification) first. Then travel. Mid-issuance tourism is how clean IPA files become messy entry interviews, and the Causeway lunch reflex is the most common version of it — a run across to JB doesn't feel like international travel until you're coming back in on a status that's halfway through changing. That one has its own guide: [the JB hop mid-issuance trap](/guides/jb-hop-mid-ep-issuance-reentry-trap-singapore).
