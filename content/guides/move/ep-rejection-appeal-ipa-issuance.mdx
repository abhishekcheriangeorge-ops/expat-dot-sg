r/askSingapore is full of two posts: "HR said MOM rejected my EP, what are my chances if we appeal?" and "offer retracted after a non-rectifiable rejection." Before you spend a weekend drafting something to send MOM yourself, understand the structural problem. **You are not a party of record.** The application was filed by your employer or their authorised agent, and only they can enquire or appeal. You can feed them evidence and you can make it easy for them to act, but you cannot open a case of your own, and nobody at MOM is going to become your personal caseworker.

## The rejection usually tells you why

MOM's appeal page points employers at the rejection advisory in the EP eService, or at the rejection letter if the filing was done manually. The instruction there is blunt: appeal only if you can address those issues with new information. Resubmitting the same facts with a warmer cover note gets you the same answer, a few weeks later. No new facts, no different outcome.

Most fixable rejections fall into a handful of buckets, and they line up neatly with the situations MOM says don't need extra supporting documents once you've actually corrected them.

| Pattern | What has to change before you go back |
| --- | --- |
| Salary / COMPASS miss | Raise the fixed monthly salary, re-run SAT, and look at the firm's diversity and support factors |
| Qualification mismatch | Make the degree campus and verification proof match what was keyed into the form |
| FCF advertising gap | Complete ≥14 days on MyCareersFuture, unless a published exemption applies |
| IRAS / employer tax hygiene | The employer clears whatever tax admin the advisory flagged |
| Job / title vs substance | Rewrite the role description so it matches the PME duties you'd actually do |

Then there are the non-rectifiable outcomes: fraud, a prior immigration ban, undisclosed history. That's the category behind the HR emails that say MOM "cannot disclose." It isn't HR being cagey. Treat it as a closed door for *this* application, and not as a prompt to try again under a slightly different spelling of your name.

## Before you resign on the strength of "we'll appeal"

The temptation, once HR sounds confident, is to hand in your notice so you're free for the new start date. Don't. Four things are worth doing first, and we've written the full candidate-side sequence up separately in the [EP rejection appeal playbook](/guides/ep-rejection-appeal-candidate-playbook-singapore).

1. Ask HR for a screenshot of the SAT run at the salary you were actually offered, and for the rejection advisory text itself. Not a summary of it, not "they said it was a COMPASS thing."
2. Confirm the advertising was run properly rather than assumed. Our [Fair Consideration Framework](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep) guide covers what "properly" means.
3. Re-read [COMPASS](/guides/compass-framework-explained) with the exact job code and the firm's real factors in front of you. LinkedIn optimism scores nothing.
4. Keep housing and school deposits refundable until the IPA is genuinely in hand. [Decoding your offer](/guides/decoding-job-offer-singapore) has the clauses to look for.

A company that won't show you the advisory is not a company that's ready to sponsor an appeal, because the appeal has to answer that document line by line. If they can't share it with you, ask yourself whether anyone there has actually read it.

## IPA is not the plastic card

When the approval lands, what arrives is an in-principle approval letter. The IPA works as a pre-approved single-entry visa and gives you 6 months to enter Singapore and get the pass issued. It also tells you whether a medical examination is required once you're here. Clinic forms, the persistent myth that the WP medical PDF applies to you, and STVP extensions while the lab is still working all live on [EP medical for issuance](/guides/ep-medical-examination-issuance).

The thing people get wrong is flying on "application submitted." MOM's apply page says it plainly: bring candidates over once the pass is approved, not before. Visit-pass extensions exist, but they are not designed to rescue someone who arrived early with a family and a lease, and they won't be stretched to do it.

### IPA reassessment and "postpone your arrival"

Every few weeks a thread appears describing the same email: the employer writes to say MOM has asked the candidate to postpone arrival because the work-pass application needs reassessment. That usually means MOM is looking at the file again in light of new information. It is not an automatic rejection. It is also not nothing, because the IPA can still be revoked at the end of it.

What you can usefully do is narrow. Ask HR for MOM's exact wording, forwarded or screenshotted, rather than their reading of it. Don't fly, and freeze whatever housing and school money you can still freeze. Chasing MOM isn't available to you, only the employer or agent can do that. And if the company has gone quiet on start dates at the same time as it's blaming a delay at MOM, those two facts may be related, so insist on written status either way.

Until the reassessment clears in writing, the IPA is frozen, not travel-ready.

## Issue the pass, then notification letter, then EPSC, then card

Missed biometrics slots and letter extensions have their own guide: [EPSC missed slot and Notification Letter extension](/guides/epsc-missed-slot-notification-letter-extension-singapore).

| Step | Clock (verify live) | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get pass issued in EP eService | Within 6 months of IPA; candidate must be in Singapore | Employer / EA |
| Fees at issuance | S$225 per pass, plus S$30 MJV if applicable | Employer pays |
| Notification letter | Valid 1 month; you can start work and travel while waiting for the card | Email to both sides |
| Fingerprints / photo at EPSC | Within 2 weeks if the letter requires it | Candidate appointment |
| Physical card delivery | Around 5 working days after registration / doc check | Authorised recipients |

The issuance upload usually wants your passport arrival stamp page, the medical form or medical declaration, and the IPA declaration signed by authorised HR. Your address has to meet housing requirements, which is where FWTES problems and unhelpful landlords bite. That's covered in [short-term vs long-term rental](/guides/short-term-vs-long-term-rental), and it is not a situation where MOM will understand. The candidate-facing clocks for the Notification Letter, what to bring to EPSC, and card delivery are in [work pass issuance and EPSC](/guides/work-pass-issuance-epsc-notification).

### When the lab is slow and your STVP is running out

If issuance is stuck waiting on medical results, the employer can request an STVP extension 7–14 days before expiry, supported by a doctor's memo stating when the results will be ready. Leave it later than that and you're into overstay-fine territory, which is an unpleasant way to start a posting. Note that this MOM path is a different thing from an ICA tourist visit-pass extension, and people mix the two up constantly. See [visit-pass extension](/guides/short-term-visit-pass-extension) and [between jobs STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

## What to push HR to do

Three things, and none of them are unreasonable asks.

- Run or re-run the [Self-Assessment Tool](https://www.mom.gov.sg/eservices/services/employment-s-pass-self-assessment-tool) before refiling, at the salary that's actually going on the form.
- File the appeal **inside the 3-month window**, with only the documents the advisory asks for. Extra material doesn't demonstrate keenness, it just slows the assessment down.
- Get written proof the appeal was really submitted. "HR said they uploaded it" is a feeling, not a status.

Losing the card *after* issuance is a completely different playbook: [replace work pass card](/guides/lost-replace-work-pass-card).

## If this employer is done

Sometimes the honest read is that this sponsor has run out of road. Your options from there are a [S Pass](/guides/s-pass-singapore) route, the founder path via [EntrePass](/guides/entrepass-singapore), or a clean new sponsor under ordinary [Employment Pass](/guides/employment-pass-singapore) rules. If the refusal is complicated, particularly anything touching disclosure or a prior ban, that's the point to pay for proper advice: [immigration counsel](/directory/legal/intact-immigration).
