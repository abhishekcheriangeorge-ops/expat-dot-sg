Relocation is a project with dependencies, and the order you do things in matters more than the speed. Treat this as sequencing rather than a replacement for your employer's onboarding list. Where the two disagree, HR wins.

## First 7 days

The week-one goal is narrower than it feels. Be legally here, be reachable, and be able to receive money. Nearly everything else can wait a fortnight without consequence.

- Finish the pass formalities, collect and activate your pass card, and install SGWorkPass the same day rather than "at the weekend"
- Get a local SIM sorted early, because a lot of what follows depends on receiving an SMS
- Open or activate banking as far as the paperwork allows. Credit cards will wait for payslips, which we get into in [credit cards for foreigners](/guides/credit-cards-foreigners-singapore)
- Check into temporary housing and photograph everything: chute seals, aircon unit counts, any mould. It takes twenty minutes now and settles arguments months later
- Save the HR contacts you'd need in a hurry and find your nearest clinic and dentist before you need one. If you flew in with controlled medication, keep the HSA approval letter with your passport
- Start Singpass setup if you're eligible. It needs a FIN and an issued pass, so an IPA on its own often won't get you through

## First 30 days

Month one is where the reversible decisions quietly become permanent ones, so this is the stretch to take slowly.

- Stamp the tenancy agreement and file the Stamp Certificate somewhere you'll find it again, which we walk through in [the renting process](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits)
- Move chronic medication onto a local GP or specialist before your imported supply runs out, not after ([HSA medication rules](/guides/bringing-medication-singapore-hsa))
- If you're still in a serviced apartment, start the long-term search in earnest. Negotiate the [diplomatic clause](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) into the LOI and get landlord FWTES consent at the same time. You want both, not whichever one comes up first
- Chase school applications and waitlist follow-ups, and queue up [translations and apostilles](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore) early if ICA or MOM are going to ask for them
- Register with a GP and read your insurance cards properly, dental carve-outs included
- Get utilities, telecom, PayNow and GIRO running
- Do a commute dry-run at real rush hour rather than on a quiet Sunday
- Tell MOM your new residential address and mobile within **5 days** of any move, using [the EP notify guide](/guides/ep-notify-mom-address-passport), and mirror that same address to your bank and HR so the three don't drift apart
- If you renew your passport, update EP eService before you rely on the automated immigration gates
- Trailing partner: get the work rights confirmed in writing. Remote work for an overseas employer and work for Singapore clients are not the same question ([side income rules](/guides/side-income-freelance-work-pass))
- Work out your equity tax points if RSUs vest in year one ([RSU and ESOP tax](/guides/rsu-esop-tax-singapore))

## First 90 days

By the third month you know what life here actually costs, and that's precisely why optimisation belongs in this quarter rather than the first one.

- Settle utilities, fibre, your OEM plan choice and the recurring payments
- Build some kind of community foothold: a club, a hash, an interest group, the neighbours
- Compare your budget against what you actually spent, unsubsidised healthcare and dental included
- Apply for a local credit card once salary is landing and you have payslips behind you ([credit cards for foreigners](/guides/credit-cards-foreigners-singapore))
- Only then go chasing Multiplier-style bonus interest ([high-interest salary accounts](/guides/high-interest-salary-accounts-foreigners))
- Look at pass expiry and the PR long game last, once you know what living here is genuinely like
