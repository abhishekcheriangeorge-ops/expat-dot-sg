**Short answer:** A licensed serviced apartment is usually the only lawful way to lodge for under three months without gambling on an illegal condo short-let. Use it to pin down the neighbourhood and the school, get an address that banks and MOM will actually accept, then cut over to a proper lease. **Don't sign 24 months on day three.**

What a serviced apartment really buys you is time. It's furnished, utilities are usually folded into the rate, and you can leave when you want to. You pay for all of that against what an annual condo lease would have cost. The better reason to book one has nothing to do with convenience: it's one of the few URA-legal ways to stay here for under three months.

## When it's worth the premium

A serviced stay earns its price when something important is still unsettled. Your IPA hasn't landed, or the role itself is still a bit soft. The school decision is open because you're on a waitlist or still choosing between campuses. You want to walk a neighbourhood at night, in the heat, before you commit two years to it. Your employer is covering a 2–6 week landing package anyway. Or your pets, your helper, or your furniture are still in transit and you couldn't move in properly anyway.

## What each path actually costs you

| Path | Cash pattern | What it saves you from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serviced 2–8 weeks | High nightly / monthly rate; low exit friction | Wrong neighbourhood, bad school-run, illegal short-let fines |
| Rushed 24-month condo | Lower monthly; high break cost | Diplomatic-clause fights, deposit drama |
| Hybrid | Serviced bridge → proper LOI/TA | Most families' sweet spot |

Treat the serviced stay as your search window rather than a holiday. Build an agent shortlist, listen to what a block sounds like after dark, time the school run at the hour you'll actually be doing it, read the condo's pet and renovation rules, and find out who holds the utilities accounts. None of that is possible from another country, and not much of it is possible jet-lagged on day three.

## Address, banks, and MOM

This is the part people underrate. A licensed serviced residence gives you a Singapore address that a telco, a bank and pass admin will generally accept, which is what unblocks a SIM and an account while you're still hunting. An Airbnb-style condo stay often fails those same checks, and it leaves you with nothing usable when the FWTES and landlord-consent questions come round. That bites later rather than immediately: you go to update MOM inside the **5-day** residential-address clock and find the bridge address was never one you could declare. The full playbook is in [notifying MOM of EP changes](/guides/ep-notify-mom-address-passport).

So hold on to the booking confirmation with the full unit address, because you'll be asked for it more than once. Have a plan for cutover day before it arrives, covering meter photos, the EP eService address update and fibre. And if the long-term unit is private residential, put the landlord's FWTES "allow pass holder declarations" into the LOI as a condition precedent, not a polite assumption you make at the viewing.

## Getting out of the bridge and into a lease

1. Narrow to 2–3 neighbourhoods that work for the school and the commute at the same time. [Choosing a neighbourhood](/guides/choosing-neighbourhood-expat) walks through the trade-offs.
2. Engage an agent, and make sure you understand the LOI / Option / TA deposit stack before any money moves.
3. Get the diplomatic clause and any pet clauses in writing. A yes at a viewing isn't something you can hold anyone to afterwards.
4. Book movers for one clean cutover. Overlapping rents for more than a week gets expensive quickly if cash is tight.
5. Update your MOM address within 5 days once the long-term unit is live, using the [EP notify guide](/guides/ep-notify-mom-address-passport).
