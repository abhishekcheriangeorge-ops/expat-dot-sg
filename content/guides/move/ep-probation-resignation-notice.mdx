Probation feels casual. Pass cancellation isn't. If you resign in the first six months, treat it with the same immigration seriousness as a job change two years in, because MOM doesn't grade your exit on how new you were.

## Three clocks people mix up

1. Your contract notice, or probation termination. This is the day you stop working for that employer, and it's the only one of the three your contract actually controls.
2. MOM pass cancellation. This is the day the EP is cancelled in MOM's systems. It's your employer's action, not yours, and it doesn't happen by itself because you've cleared your desk.
3. Household dependant status. DP and LOC rights fall with the principal pass, so this clock isn't really yours at all, and it moves when the second one does.

Nothing forces those three dates to line up, and the trouble starts when you assume they have.

## What to actually do

Read the probation and notice clauses before you accept the offer, not on the morning you decide to leave. If you're going to another role, hold out for the [new IPA before you resign](/guides/changing-jobs-employment-pass), which is the single decision that prevents most of the mess below.

Then ask HR, in writing, for the date they intend to cancel with MOM, and whether they'll be requesting any short-term visit arrangement to cover you afterwards. While you're at it, write down everyone else the cancellation touches: DP and LOC holders in the household, the diplomatic clause in your lease, school deposits you've already paid.

One last thing, and people get caught by it at the worst possible moment. **Don't surrender your pass card at the airport** until HR has given you a written cancellation plan.

The issuance side of all this, notifications and EPSC and what arrives when, lives in [work pass issuance](/guides/work-pass-issuance-epsc-notification). This page is about the exit you take while you're still employed.
