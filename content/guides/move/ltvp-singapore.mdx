**Short answer:** an LTVP is not a Dependant's Pass, and treating it as a cheaper substitute is how people get into trouble. Parents of EP and S Pass holders usually need MOM's parent LTVP, which turns on a published individual fixed salary of **S$12k** (check the live figure). Work rights don't come attached; PLOC and LOC only reach specific ICA LTVP categories. And don't assume any healthcare subsidy comes with it.

The Long-Term Visit Pass exists for the family relationships that don't fit neatly onto an Employment Pass holder's Dependant's Pass. Parents of high-earning EP and S Pass holders, foreign spouses of Singapore citizens, some parents and grandparents of children on a Student's Pass, and a handful of other listed categories. The reason "can I bring my mother on an LTVP?" and "is it really S$12k?" come up constantly on r/askSingapore is that the thresholds are genuinely easy to misread, and getting them wrong means an awkward phone call home.

## Which pass your relative actually needs

| Scenario | Typical pass logic |
| --- | --- |
| EP holder's legally married spouse / kids | Usually a Dependant's Pass, not LTVP |
| EP/S Pass holder's parents | MOM LTVP if fixed monthly salary ≥ S$12,000 (individual, not household) |
| EP/S Pass common-law spouse / step-child / unmarried handicapped child 21+ | MOM LTVP if main pass salary ≥ S$6,000 |
| Singapore citizen marrying a foreigner | ICA LTVP sponsored by the SC (see PMLA) |
| Unmarried partner of an EP holder | Usually no DP; a MOM common-law spouse LTVP is possible if salary ≥ S$6,000 and the documents hold up (below) |
| Parent accompanying a child on Student's Pass | ICA LTVP possible with strict sponsor rules (often only one parent or grandparent applicant) |

When a chat group tells you otherwise, the MOM and ICA categories win. They always win.

## Proving a common-law relationship to MOM

The recurring Reddit question here is some version of "we share a flat and a joint bank account, is that enough?" It isn't, and MOM's evidence list is narrower than people expect. For a common-law spouse LTVP, the [documents required](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/long-term-visit-pass/documents-required) page asks for any one of the following.

1. A copy of a common-law marriage certificate, or
2. An affidavit by both partners declaring a common-law relationship recognised under the laws of their home country or region, notarised in that home country or region, or
3. A letter from that country or region's embassy acknowledging the relationship under its law

Note what options two and three have in common: the recognition has to come from somewhere, and that somewhere is your home jurisdiction, not your landlord. Passport biodata pages go up alongside. Anything not in English needs the original and an English translation uploaded as a single file. Price embassy-recognised translators here in Singapore before you accept an overseas quote priced per character, because the character-based quotes can be startling. More on that in [notary and apostille](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore).

One thing the LTVP still doesn't give you is the right to work. Remote work for an overseas employer runs down a separate MOM FAQ path, which we cover in [side income rules](/guides/side-income-freelance-work-pass). Local Singapore clients still need a proper pass or an LOC, no matter where the money lands.

## EP and S Pass parents: the S$12k rule

The [MOM LTVP eligibility](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/long-term-visit-pass/eligibility) page is unusually direct about this one. You need to hold an Employment Pass or S Pass yourself, and earn a minimum fixed monthly salary of S$6,000 for most LTVP family categories. That figure is based on your salary alone, not your household's combined income. The sponsor has to be an established Singapore-registered company, which in practice is your employer. And for parents specifically, the bar rises: you qualify only if your fixed monthly salary is at least **S$12,000**.

That individual-salary point is where the forum stories go wrong. When you read "our combined income was under 10k and we got rejected", the writer has usually either confused ICA's SC/PR parent sponsorship with MOM's work-pass parent LTVP, or assumed a spouse's salary counts toward the S$12k floor. It doesn't. Two decent salaries under one roof do not add up to a parent LTVP.

Your employer or their EA submits for EP and S Pass dependants, and the published fees and timelines sit on [MOM's apply page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/long-term-visit-pass/apply-for-a-pass) (verify live, and expect application and issuance fees per pass). Sequence the actual arrival after IPA or approval. MOM won't extend a short visit pass to paper over a gap, so a parent who flies in hopefully and waits is a parent who will be flying out again.

## The healthcare bill nobody budgets for

LTVP parents are not on the citizen or PR subsidy tracks, and the forum ICU stories exist for a reason. An unsubsidised stay at a public hospital is billed as private, and an elderly parent is exactly the person likely to need one. Price private medical insurance early, expect pre-existing conditions to be excluded rather than loaded, and budget for helper care if that's where this is heading. We've laid the options out in [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat). And be honest with yourself about PR for elderly parents with no Singapore contribution history: it is generally not realistic, and planning around it is planning around a hope.

## The SC spouse path: PMLA, then LTVP, then maybe PLOC

If you're marrying a Singapore citizen, do the Pre-Marriage LTVP Assessment first. PMLA is free, and completing it before the marriage shortens LTVP processing for an SC's foreign spouse to roughly 6 weeks instead of up to 6 months. Six months is a long time to be in limbo as a newly married couple, and the assessment costs you nothing but an evening. The SC sponsors through Singpass on the ICA e-Service.

ICA's published fees (verify live) are S$45 to apply and S$60 on issuance, plus S$30 for a Multiple Journey Visa where that applies. **Renew at least 3 months before expiry** — whatever validity is left over does not roll forward into the new pass, so late renewal is just money and time you give away.

## Work rights: PLOC doesn't come automatically

An LTVP on its own is not permission to work. Eligible spouses, and unmarried children under 21, of Singapore citizens or PRs may be able to get a Pre-approved Letter of Consent from MOM, and it's often applied for together with the LTVP itself. PLOC is the good version: it lets an eligible holder work without having to find the job first. Its validity is tied to the LTVP underneath it, so the two rise and fall together.

Parents on an LTVP, whether MOM or ICA, generally can't assume PLOC at all. And trailing partners in EP families sit on a DP with a different and post-2021 story, which we've covered in [trailing partner career](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore). If you're already on PLOC and wondering whether a standalone EP is worth the trouble, that comparison is in [LTVP and PLOC to EP](/guides/ltvp-ploc-to-ep-conversion-singapore).

## What an LTVP doesn't solve

It doesn't quietly build toward PR. PR is discretionary, and it isn't a side effect of having been on a visit pass for years. School fees, healthcare subsidies and HDB eligibility all keep following the citizenship, PR and pass rules rather than how long anyone has physically been here. And if you're moving from an EP or S Pass onto an LTVP after marrying an SC or PR, sequence it with HR and ICA carefully, because the easy mistake is creating a stretch of days where you hold neither and your employment is technically uncovered.
