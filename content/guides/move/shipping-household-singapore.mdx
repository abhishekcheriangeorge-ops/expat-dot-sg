**Short answer:** Unaccompanied household freight gets charged GST unless Singapore Customs grants you relief, and relief is granted rather than assumed. You file the **Declaration of Facts before the container or courier lands**, for used goods you've owned at least 3 months and are importing within 6 months of first arrival. Alcohol, tobacco and vehicles never qualify. Hand-carry luggage runs on an entirely different traveller rail.

Most families end up splitting the move: air freight for the things they need in the first fortnight, sea freight for everything else. Singles tend to ship very little and buy here instead. The expensive surprise is almost never the crate itself. It's the GST bill on unaccompanied personal effects, arriving because nobody actually filed for relief.

## GST relief, and why it catches people out

Customs' starting position is that importing used household articles and personal effects attracts GST. Relief for relocating foreigners and for returning citizens and PRs does exist, but you have to meet the published conditions, and they are specific enough that a casual reading will let you down.

Use the table to orient yourself, then check the live pages before you commit to anything.

| Condition | Where people come unstuck |
| --- | --- |
| You are moving your residence into Singapore | A tourist trip or a short visit is not a relocation, whatever the forum thread says |
| You own the items | Borrowed things and company assets get messy fast |
| Used and in your possession ≥ 3 months | Buying brand-new-in-box right before departure fails the spirit of the rule and usually the letter of it too |
| Import within 6 months of first arrival | A container that sails late can miss the window entirely |
| Don't sell or give the goods away within 3 months after arrival | That garage sale you were planning can undo the relief you just claimed |
| For unaccompanied freight: submit the Declaration of Facts before the goods arrive | When the paperwork is late or missing, the courier holds the package and bills you GST |

Hand-carried used personal effects, in reasonable quantities for your own use, follow a different traveller-relief story set out on the [duty-free and GST relief arrivals page](https://www.customs.gov.sg/individuals/going-through-customs/arrivals/duty-free-concession-and-gst-relief/). It's unaccompanied sea and air freight where the mover and DHL horror stories come from, so don't read one set of rules and apply it to the other.

Some things never get relief, no matter how genuine your move is: intoxicating liquors, tobacco, motor vehicles, private aircraft, boats and yachts, and commercial goods. Controlled items are a separate problem again, because they need Competent Authority approval on top of anything relief-related.

## Timing it against your IPA and your keys

Don't book a container against an IPA date you're merely hoping for. Flexible storage at either end costs less than a shipment that lands before you have somewhere to put it. Bear in mind too that most condo managements want move-ins booked in advance, with the lift padded and often only on weekdays, so the day the container clears is not necessarily the day it reaches your flat. Line your Bill of Lading or Air Waybill dates up with the DOF, because Customs wants the paperwork **before** the goods land, not shortly afterwards.

## What actually moves the price

- Volume, and whether you're in a shared container or an exclusive one
- Packing labour at origin
- Destination handling, including disposal of all that packing waste
- Insurance valuation
- GST or duty, if relief fails or the items were never eligible

Get quotes off the same inventory list or you're comparing nothing. And ask directly who is filing the DOF and the TradeNet permit: you, the mover, or a customs broker. That question has a habit of revealing that everyone assumed someone else was doing it.

## What not to pack without thinking

Alcohol volumes, tobacco, drones, weapons analogues, certain foods and anything that counts as a new sealed gift all create pain at the border. Declare honestly. The "just don't list it" advice you'll read online is precisely how holds and penalties begin.
