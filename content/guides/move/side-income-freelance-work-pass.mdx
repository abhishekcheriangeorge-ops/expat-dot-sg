r/askSingapore asks the same compliance question every few weeks, just in different costumes. Content creation on an EP. Wedding photography on a visit pass. "Remote US job while I'm on LTVP." A bit of weekend tutoring. The answer almost never turns on how small the invoice is. It turns on two things: who the client is, and what your pass actually authorises.

## If you hold a work pass (EP, S Pass, Work Permit, most training passes)

MOM's general FAQ leaves no wiggle room:

> All work pass holders must only work for their designated employer. They must not take on additional jobs or engage in activities to earn additional income in Singapore.

Read that as written. It covers weekend Grab driving, a paid Instagram campaign for a Singapore brand, coaching locals for cash, and "just one wedding." Even unpaid work gets messy if what you're doing looks like labour for a Singapore entity, so ask before you assume goodwill counts as clearance.

Registering a Pte Ltd doesn't get you round this, and neither does sitting as a director of someone else's company. Secondary directorship has its own separate MOM approval, which is exactly the point: it's a distinct product with its own rules, not a side door into invoicing local clients. The company-formation side is in [incorporating as a foreigner](/guides/incorporate-company-foreigner-singapore).

## Dependant's Pass and stay passes: it depends where the client is

MOM's Dependant's Pass FAQ draws a genuinely bright line here, and the line runs through the client rather than through you. Where you're sitting when you do the work matters less than who is paying for it and where they're registered.

| What you're doing while physically in Singapore | Work pass needed? |
| --- | --- |
| Working for, or providing services to, a Singapore-based (ACRA-registered) organisation or client for gain | Yes: an EP, S Pass, DP-WP, or an eligible [business-owner LOC](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/loc-for-dependants-pass-business-owners/eligibility) |
| Working for, or providing services to, an overseas-based organisation or client | No work pass for that overseas engagement, per the MOM FAQ |

Employers sometimes panic when a new hire's spouse tells them "MOM said it's fine." What usually settles it is the FAQ link in writing. Then a separate conversation about tax, because work-pass permission and IRAS are two different questions and clearing one tells you nothing about the other. Tax residency and "income received in Singapore" have their own logic, which we walk through in [foreign income and tax](/guides/foreign-income-and-tax).

## The personalised passes aren't open freelance licences either

People reach for these once the employer-tied answer disappoints them. None of them is a general permission to sell your services to whoever turns up.

| Pass | What it actually does for side income |
| --- | --- |
| [PEP](/guides/personalised-employment-pass-pep) | Employer-agnostic, but it is for employment. Freelancers, sole proprietors and shareholder-directors are generally ineligible for the pass in the first place |
| [Tech.Pass](/guides/tech-pass-singapore) | Genuinely multi-activity, covering employee, founder, investor, consultant and mentor roles, but within the programme's own rules rather than as a blanket exemption |
| [ONE Pass](/guides/one-pass-singapore) | Top-talent flexibility with published family LOC paths. Check each activity against MOM's pages before you commit to it |
| [EntrePass](/guides/entrepass-singapore) | Tied to the venture you founded under EntrePass rules. Not a licence for random gig work |

## Working out where you sit

If you're on an employer-tied work pass, meaning EP, S Pass or Work Permit, start from the assumption that there is **no Singapore side income available to you at all** and make anyone who disagrees show you the rule.

If you're on a DP or LTVP and the money is coming from a Singapore client or an ACRA-registered entity, get the pass or the LOC sorted **before you invoice, not after**. Worth knowing in advance that LOC renewals need a local hire at the Local Qualifying Salary plus 3 months of CPF contributions, which is the bar a one-person operation simply cannot show against. That trap is covered properly on [Dependant's Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore).

If you're on a DP or LTVP and every client is clearly overseas, with no Singapore entity anywhere in the chain, MOM's FAQ says you don't need a work pass for that work. You do still need to sort out tax, banking, and how the contract is structured.

And if what you actually want is to sell services into the Singapore market properly, build the pass path that allows it. We've mapped those options in [trailing partner career](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore). Inventing a YouTube exception isn't one of them; the creator and weekend-gig versions of that mistake are collected in [EP side hustle and content-creator traps](/guides/ep-side-hustle-content-creator-traps-singapore).

## Three things the forums keep getting wrong

"Influencer income isn't really a job." If you're paid for activity in Singapore for a Singapore client, treat it as work, whatever the invoice calls the fee.

"LTVP means I can freelance locally." It doesn't, not on its own. Spouses of citizens and PRs may have a pre-approved LOC path; the common-law LTVP that comes through an EP-holding family usually doesn't. Confirm which LTVP product you actually hold before you invoice anyone.

"Student Pass part-time work is fine, so this must be fine too." Student work exemptions are narrow and written for a specific situation. They don't generalise to EP households.

When the amount at stake is material, pay for regulated advice. [Immigration counsel](/directory/legal/intact-immigration) for the pass structure, a tax adviser for the IRAS side. Not a Discord poll.
