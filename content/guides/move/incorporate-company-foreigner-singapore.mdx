Founders on r/askSingapore keep asking which visa lets them start a company, and the question itself contains the confusion. ACRA and MOM are doing two different jobs. Anyone can own shares in a Singapore Pte Ltd, including someone who has never set foot in the country. Actually running that business from here usually needs a work pass. And separately from both of those, every local company needs a director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore. We go deeper on that split in [EntrePass vs resident director](/guides/entrepass-resident-director-vs-pass-singapore).

## ACRA first: foreigners file through a CSP

ACRA's eligibility page is blunt about this. As a foreigner you have to engage a Corporate Service Provider to reserve the name and register the structure. Filing it yourself through Bizfile with Singpass is a convenience for citizens and PRs, not an expat path, so budget for the CSP from the start.

Every local company also has roles it must fill:

| Role | Hard rule (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| ≥1 director | Ordinarily resident in Singapore, so typically a citizen, a PR, or a qualifying pass holder (EP / PEP / ONE Pass, as listed on ACRA's page) |
| Company secretary | A natural person who meets local residency rules, and **cannot** be the same person as your sole director |
| FIN holders | Check with MOM / ICA before you accept officer roles |

Overseas founders usually solve the resident-director problem by taking a nominee from their CSP. Be clear about what you're buying, though. A nominee is a real director under the Companies Act, with the duties that come with the title, and they'll run due diligence on you as thoroughly as you should be running it on them.

## Incorporating isn't permission to work

Where you sit today decides what you can actually do with the company once it exists:

| Your status | Incorporate / own shares | Actively work the SG business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overseas foreigner | Yes, via a CSP plus a resident director | You need a work pass if you move here to run it |
| EP (sponsored by Employer A) | Possible, but officer roles need MOM clarity first | You cannot quietly operate a second Singapore business on the side; appointing EP holders across related companies needs an **LOC** |
| Dependant's Pass | Register, then apply for the LOC for DP business owners | The LOC is the work authorisation, and renewals can demand local hires |
| EntrePass track | Align with the innovator / funding gates | [EntrePass](/guides/entrepass-singapore) is the founder pass; a shelf company on its own isn't |
| Visit pass / tourist | Holding shares via a CSP isn't a work-from-Starbucks visa | Illegal employment risk |

### If you already hold an EP

MOM lets an eligible company appoint an EP holder from a related company to its board, but only with your primary employer's consent and a Letter of Consent. The relatedness has to be visible in ACRA shareholding records, so the two companies need to be related on paper and not merely in spirit. The LOC's validity tracks your EP, which means it falls when the EP does.

Unpaid charity board work is a different question with a different answer. A paid commercial directorship isn't "networking," however it's framed in the offer email. The consent pack is detailed in [EP secondary directorship LOC](/guides/ep-secondary-directorship-loc-singapore).

ACRA's own note to foreigners says the same thing from the other side: EP holders should generally seek MOM consent before accepting roles. Read it alongside our [side income and moonlighting](/guides/side-income-freelance-work-pass) guide, because registering a Pte Ltd doesn't make weekend consulting for Singapore clients legal.

### If you're on a Dependant's Pass

ACRA points DP holders at the business LOC, applied for after the entity is registered rather than before. Stories about LOC renewals failing for want of a local hire are common on the forums, so model the payroll before anyone treats this as a replacement for a trailing-spouse plan. More in [trailing partner career](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore) and the [Dependant's Pass guide](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore).

## The self-sponsored EP myth

"Incorporate, then hire myself on an EP" isn't a productised MOM pathway, whatever the packages advertise. COMPASS, salary substance, FCF advertising unless you're exempt, and firm-level factors all still apply to a company of one. Paying yourself a paper salary you never actually receive is exactly the kind of thing that surfaces when IRAS and a pass renewal land in the same year.

Where the profile fits, go the ordinary routes instead: [EntrePass](/guides/entrepass-singapore), an employer-sponsored [EP](/guides/employment-pass-singapore), a [PEP](/guides/personalised-employment-pass-pep), or the [ONE Pass](/guides/one-pass-singapore).

## Banking and compliance after ACRA

Corporate account KYC is heavier going than the account you opened as an employee, and our [bank account opening guide](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) covers the easy version. Expect questions about controllers, source of funds, and what the company actually does day to day. Annual returns, tax filings, and your nominee director's accountability also carry on regardless of whether you're living in Jakarta.

For complex structures or pass collisions, talk to [immigration counsel](/directory/legal/intact-immigration).
