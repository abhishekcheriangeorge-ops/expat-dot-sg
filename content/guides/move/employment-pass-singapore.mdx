**Short answer:** An Employment Pass is sponsored by your employer, not by you. You need a fixed monthly salary at or above MOM's age-banded floor, which is currently around S$5,600 at the youngest band outside financial services and S$6,200 inside it, rising with age. You also usually need COMPASS ≥40 unless an exemption applies. An IPA is not a finished pass. Check the live MOM eligibility table for the month you're actually applying in, because these numbers move.

Singapore's Employment Pass is the main work pass for foreign professionals, managers and executives. The Ministry of Manpower issues it, it's tied to one specific employer, and it sits at the centre of almost every corporate relocation here. Hold on to that employer tie as you read the rest of this, because nearly everything awkward about the EP flows from the same fact: the pass belongs to the job, not to you.

## Who the EP is for

The EP is aimed at mid-to-senior talent, and MOM assesses two things at once. There's you — your qualifications, your experience, your salary — and there's the employer's hiring context, which gets scored through the Complementary Assessment Framework, universally shortened to COMPASS.

In practice that means you need a job offer from a Singapore-registered company able to sponsor the pass, a fixed monthly salary at or above the prevailing EP minimum for your age band and sector, and qualifications or professional experience that genuinely match the role.

## Stage 1: the salary floor people keep misquoting

Stage 1 is an absolute floor. Miss it and no number of COMPASS points rescues you, which is why it's worth checking before you get emotionally invested in an offer. Here's the orientation from MOM's eligibility tables, which you should still re-check before you sign anything:

| Sector | Youngest-band floor, current new applications | Age 45+ band | Next published step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All except financial services | S$5,600/mo | S$10,700/mo | S$6,000 and S$11,500, for new applications from **1 Jan 2027** and renewals of passes expiring from 1 Jan 2028 |
| Financial services | S$6,200/mo | S$11,800/mo | S$6,600 and S$12,700 on the same clocks |

The floor rises progressively with age from 23, so the headline number in every press release is the number for the youngest applicants and nobody else. A signed S$5,000 offer after the 1 Jan 2025 step for new applications is a recurring r/askSingapore disaster, and it usually isn't the candidate's fault: HR's draft still "passes" because someone is reading an outdated Self-Assessment Tool screenshot.

The dates are the other trap. New applications and renewals move on different clocks, so don't assume your renewal still sits on the floor that applied when you first arrived.

Only fixed monthly salary counts toward the floor. Variable bonuses and a good many allowances will not patch a shortfall, however healthy the total package looks on the offer letter. If your number is close to the line, the side-by-side with S Pass quota and levy is in [EP vs S Pass comparison](/guides/ep-vs-s-pass-comparison).

## COMPASS in one paragraph

COMPASS scores you on individual factors, meaning your salary relative to local peers and your qualifications, and scores your employer on firm-related factors like diversity and support for local employment. Come in below the passing score and you're generally looking at a rejection unless a clear exemption applies. The practical implication is that COMPASS belongs in the design of the offer itself, which means raising it while the offer is still being drafted rather than after you've resigned from a job on the other side of the world.

Before most EP submissions the employer also has to run the Fair Consideration Framework clock, which typically means advertising the role on MyCareersFuture for ≥14 days unless an exemption applies (fixed monthly salary of ≥ S$22,500, a small firm, or ICT). How you should read those ads as a candidate is in [FCF and MyCareersFuture](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep).

## How the application actually runs

1. Your employer, or an employment agent they've appointed, submits through EP Online.
2. MOM may come back asking for clarification or extra documents. That's routine, not a warning sign.
3. If it succeeds, an in-principle approval letter, the IPA, is issued.
4. You complete the formalities in Singapore, card issuance included, after you arrive or once you're otherwise eligible.

Processing times vary widely. Build contingency into your resignation date and your shipping dates, and don't plan around a fixed calendar week just because a colleague's came through quickly.

Rejected, stuck on appeal, or lost in the IPA medical and card clocks? [EP rejection, appeal and IPA issuance](/guides/ep-rejection-appeal-ipa-issuance) walks through it. Two things worth knowing right now: only the employer can appeal, and you have a **3-month** window from the rejection.

## After you land

Once the pass is issued, carry the card, or the digital equivalent where that's accepted. Update your bank, telco and landlord records with your FIN. Register for Singpass as soon as you're eligible, because it unlocks most government and bank digital services. You need the FIN first for that, and people regularly fail registration by trying on the IPA alone.

Then there's the maintenance nobody briefs you on. Keep your residential address, mobile number and passport particulars current — the **5-day** address and mobile clock, plus the lag before automated gates catch up with a renewed passport, are covered in [notify MOM of EP changes](/guides/ep-notify-mom-address-passport). And note your expiry date early. Renewal is a living-here problem you work on over months, not a last-week scramble.

## Changing employers, or switching while an EP is still pending

The full playbook is [changing jobs on an Employment Pass](/guides/changing-jobs-employment-pass). The short version has three moving parts.

If you're already on an EP, the new employer applies for a new pass, you resign after IPA rather than before, and the old pass gets cancelled once the new one is issued. That's MOM's change-jobs FAQ position, and [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) covers what it looks like when the new role is a genuine change of direction.

If you're still pending or holding an IPA with Company A when Company B turns up, you generally cannot park two live EP outcomes indefinitely. Ask A to withdraw before B files, or you risk a messy dual filing that serves nobody.

And a job change triggers IR21 tax clearance with the employer you're leaving, even if you never leave Singapore. That means withheld final pay to budget around ([tax clearance](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving)).

## Leaving, or between jobs: cancel, then STVP

When employment ends it's the employer who cancels the EP, within a week after your last day, and they're allowed to cancel up to 14 days in advance. If you're staying in Singapore past that point, MOM's cancel page expects an employer-requested Short-Term Visit Pass to cover your stay, which can run **up to 90 days** from the application date.

Ask HR for it **in writing, before cancel day**. Left alone, default HR behaviour is often a much shorter window than the one that's available, and nobody volunteers the longer one. An STVP is not work authorisation either, so it buys you lawful presence and nothing more. Your dependants fall off the main pass as it goes, and that cascade is on [Dependant's Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore). The full operational sequence, IR21 and travel and the graduate LTVP route included, is in [between jobs on an EP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

## Where it usually goes wrong

Four patterns account for most of the trouble. Package structure is the big one: allowances that aren't fixed monthly salary may simply not count toward the EP floor, so a generous-looking total can still fail Stage 1. Age band against offer is close behind, because candidates in their mid-30s and mid-40s need noticeably higher floors than the youngest-band headline everyone quotes at each other. Job title against reality matters more than people expect, since MOM looks at substance and an inflated title with no matching duties invites scrutiny rather than deflecting it. And dependant timing catches families out, because family passes usually follow the main pass — sequence your housing and school deposits with that in mind.
