**Short answer:** Decide on a curriculum thesis first — IB, British, American, or a national system — then price five years of fees including bus and CCAs, and only then argue about geography. The waitlists at the famous names are real. Start from your constraints, not from campus tours.

Singapore's international school market is deep, well run, and emotionally overheated in roughly equal measure. Read this alongside [waitlist realism](/guides/school-waitlist-realism), because the landscape only makes sense once you know which seats actually exist.

## Start with a curriculum thesis

| Pathway | Continuity strength | Typical next-country fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IB (PYP/MYP/DP) | Strong for families who move often | The DP is recognised in a lot of places |
| British / IGCSE / A-Level | Well-worn UK and Commonwealth pipelines | Good bet if UK universities are likely |
| American / AP | Mature US college counselling ecosystems | Fits a US return, or US universities |
| National systems (French, German, Japanese, Swiss…) | Best language and cultural continuity | Ideal if you'll repatriate into that system |

Match the school to the country you're most likely to be in next, not to the sports hall that photographs well. If you've got teenagers, the choice between IB DP, A-Levels and AP deserves its own read, along with how much a mid-stream transfer hurts and why IGCSE rarely opens a local JC door. That's all in [secondary pathways](/guides/secondary-ib-alevel-pathways-singapore), and it's worth doing before you book tours.

## The American-pathway shortlist, minus the noise

The recurring "is Stamford American any good?" and "SAS vs OFS" threads tend to turn into proxy wars between parents defending their own fee decisions. A few distinctions are actually useful for planning.

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Curriculum continuity versus the facilities brochure | Boarding, a theatre and a competition pool tell you nothing about college counselling fit |
| Nationality mix and seat availability | Some campuses are easier to get into on certain passports, particularly in the crowded grades |
| Geography | [SAS](/schools/singapore-american-school) in Woodlands, [Stamford](/schools/stamford-american) at Lorong Chuan and Woodleigh, [OFS](/schools/overseas-family-school) in Pasir Ris, [Nexus](/schools/nexus-international) in Aljunied and [XCL](/schools/xcl-world-academy) in Yio Chu Kang each put you in a completely different rental belt |
| Learning support capacity | Treat SEN places as a separate waitlist, because that's what they are — see [special needs](/guides/special-needs-support) |

**Housing follows school, not the other way round.** Tour with commute maths in hand and year-group availability confirmed in writing before you sign a lease on the wrong side of the island. Peer rankings are entertainment. A dated email from admissions is a plan.

## Campus geography, and the lease it locks you into

| School cluster (examples) | Housing implication |
| --- | --- |
| Woodlands and north ([SAS](/schools/singapore-american-school)) | Live north or west, or accept long drives. Decide before you sign anything in River Valley |
| Woodleigh and central-north ([Stamford](/schools/stamford-american)) | Lorong Chuan, Serangoon and Novena. Not Woodlands, which people genuinely do confuse |
| Pasir Ris and east ([OFS](/schools/overseas-family-school)) | Living east helps enormously, though plenty of OFS families stay town-ish and pay the bus tax instead |
| Lakeside and west ([CIS](/schools/canadian-international-school)) | Pulls you towards Jurong, Clementi and west Bukit Timah |
| Bukit Batok and west ([Dulwich](/schools/dulwich-college-singapore)) | West-side housing, with a peak-hour reverse commute to the CBD that's its own problem |
| Portsdown and west-central ([Tanglin Trust](/schools/tanglin-trust-school)) | Holland V, one-north and Bukit Timah patterns |
| East ([UWCSEA East](/schools/uwcsea-east) and peers) | East Coast and Marine Parade gravity |
| Dover and west ([UWCSEA Dover](/schools/uwcsea-dover), [GESS](/schools/gess)) | West-central, Dairy Farm, Clementi-adjacent |

School first or house first is a false binary that families spend weeks arguing about. In practice, year-group availability decides both for you. More on the housing side in [choosing a neighbourhood](/guides/choosing-neighbourhood-expat).

### The school bus is a second rent line

The "SAS Woodlands vs OFS Pasir Ris vs Stamford" threads ([moving with young kids](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1s7j0lb/moving_to_singapore_with_2_young_kids_help_with/)) keep rediscovering the same arithmetic.

| Decision | What to verify in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Live near campus | Door-to-gate time at peak morning, and what's nearby for playgrounds and childcare |
| Live near the office and bus the children | Operator catchment, one-way minutes, what happens if siblings end up on split campuses, and the term fee |
| Live somewhere in between | Usually the worst of both: a long adult MRT ride and a long child bus ride |

Ask admissions for the current bus vendor's contact details and sample route times for the postal codes you're considering, and do it before you sign a tenancy rather than after. "Singapore is small" is perfectly true. So is a 70-minute each-way kindergarten bus, and both of those facts can apply to your family at once. Fold the bus into the five-year fee stack below, and into holiday logistics in [kids activities](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps).

## Mandarin and bilingual tracks

Families who want Mandarin exposure without committing to an MOE pathway usually end up conflating three quite different products.

| Path | What it usually is | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bilingual or immersion tracks inside an IS | For example, [CIS](/schools/canadian-international-school) bilingual options — confirm grade by grade | Tracks fill early, and intensity tails off in the upper years at some campuses |
| Optional Mandarin CCA or language slots | Common at British and American schools, [Dulwich](/schools/dulwich-college-singapore) among them | "Has Mandarin" is not immersion. Ask for weekly contact hours and you'll see the gap |
| Outside enrichment (Berries, online 1:1, a home tutor) | The bridge for non-Mandarin-speaking homes | Waitlists, and a hard sell on prepaid packages — see [Mandarin enrichment](/guides/mandarin-enrichment-expat-kids-singapore) and [kids activities](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps) |

If your next posting genuinely needs working Mandarin, shortlist on the track and the hours, then look at the sports hall. We go deeper on matching the goal to the product, prepaid package traps included, in [Mandarin enrichment for expat kids](/guides/mandarin-enrichment-expat-kids-singapore).

## Fee anatomy: ask for the whole stack

| Line item | Why families miss it |
| --- | --- |
| Tuition | The brochure number, and the only one anyone quotes |
| Enrolment / application | Non-refundable, which is the point |
| Capital or building levy | Lands years in, long after you'd stopped expecting it |
| Bus | A geography tax that can rival a month of helper cost |
| Lunch, uniforms, laptops, CCAs | The slow drip that never appears in the comparison spreadsheet |
| Sibling discounts | Real, but only if the policy still exists when your second child starts |

Compare five-year totals rather than September invoices. The gap between two schools can invert once the levies and buses are in.

## A shortlist method that survives contact with reality

Start from curriculum and language needs, then get year-group seat reality in writing and dated, because a verbal "we usually have space in Year 4" is worth nothing in March. Run the commute from two plausible housing belts, not one. Ask about pastoral care and learning support as they apply to *your* child rather than in general. Facilities and the cafeteria come last, which is the reverse of how most tours are choreographed.

If you're thinking about moving campus mid-year, the fee forfeiture stacks on top of pass and tenancy calendars in ways that catch people out — [mid-year school transfer knock-ons](/guides/mid-year-school-transfer-pass-knockons-singapore) has the sequencing.

## Corporate nomination and "guaranteed seats"

Some employers do buy preferential access. It isn't universal, it isn't free, and it isn't a seat until someone writes it down. **Ask HR for the written policy**, and treat forum folklore about firms buying spots as a planning input rather than as part of your offer.

## Local schools as a parallel track

Some foreign children can enter MOE pathways under specific rules. It's cheaper than international fees and culturally a different experience, with availability that's genuinely uncertain rather than diplomatically uncertain. The frame is in [local schools for expats](/guides/local-schools-for-expats).
