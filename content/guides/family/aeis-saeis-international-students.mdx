When P1 registration doesn’t come off, or your child is already past Primary 1 and you’ve arrived mid-stream, the Reddit chorus is “just do AEIS.” That’s half right. AEIS is a genuine MOE route into mainstream schools and plenty of expat families use it. But **passing the test does not get you a place at a brand-name school**, and MOE will not issue your child an entry permit simply so they can sit the paper. Both of those catch people out, and the second one catches them expensively.

## What AEIS is, and what S-AEIS is for

| | AEIS | S-AEIS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Admissions Exercise for International Students | Supplementary AEIS |
| Levels it usually covers | Primary 2–5, Secondary 1–3 | Primary 2–4, Secondary 1–2 |
| How the year runs | Apply around July, tests around Sep, start the following January | Apply around January, tests around Feb or Mar, start Apr or May the same year |
| What you’re guaranteed | Nothing. It’s performance plus available vacancies | Nothing, and there are usually fewer seats |

### Where the calendar stands right now (check this before you book flights)

| Cycle | Status at last review (2026-09-15) |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 AEIS | Applications closed. Tests scheduled in Singapore on 1–3 Sep 2026 per the MOE AEIS overview, with outcomes feeding Jan 2027 starts |
| 2027 S-AEIS | Applications expected in early January 2027, tests tentatively end Feb or early Mar 2027, starting Apr or May 2027 |

S-AEIS is the mid-year catch if you miss the main exercise, and it has fewer seats. That matters more than it sounds: “we’ll just sit S-AEIS” is not a backup plan you can safely cancel an international-school hold against. We’ve written the hold-versus-drop calendar up separately at [AEIS placement vs an international-school hold](/guides/aeis-placement-vs-international-hold-singapore).

One thing worth clearing up early, because it causes a lot of crossed wires: P1 for international students is its own registration path, not this one. That’s covered in [local schools for expats](/guides/local-schools-for-expats). AEIS is the mid-stream exam route, for children aged 7+ as of 1 January in the admission year ([MOE admissions hub](https://www.moe.gov.sg/international-students/admission)).

## The eligibility gates parents underestimate

Start with the age band. MOE’s live checker maps your child’s birth date to the paper they’re permitted to sit and the level they could be placed at. Sitting the wrong paper doesn’t get corrected on the day; it costs you the cycle.

For primary applicants, there’s the Cambridge English Qualifications gate, and this is the one that generates the most indignation. You have to hit the published minimum score before you submit the AEIS or S-AEIS application. Being a native English speaker is not a waiver. The recurring forum thread, some version of “my American kid obviously shouldn’t need B1,” runs straight into a published requirement that doesn’t care about the argument. Sort the certificate first, or you don’t have an application.

Then the one that sounds like paperwork and isn’t: you must already be able to enter Singapore. SEAB and MOE both say plainly that the entry proof they ask for **is not an immigration pass**, and that MOE won’t arrange entry just so a child can take the test. Families sometimes plan the whole thing backwards, assuming the exam unlocks the visa.

And if your child was previously an MOE student and there are fee arrears outstanding, those have to be cleared before any readmission assessment happens.

## The fees, which you don’t get back

Published for recent AEIS cycles, with GST extra at the prevailing rate, so verify on the live apply page:

| Exercise | Fee direction |
| --- | --- |
| AEIS Primary | S$340 |
| AEIS Secondary | S$630 |

You pay by card or PayNow in the Candidates Portal. If your child doesn’t pass, or passes and gets no placement, the fee is simply gone. Nobody is unkind about it, but nobody refunds it either.

## What the test is actually testing

| Track | What MOE says it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | Mathematics pitched at the preceding level, so aiming for P3 means knowing P2 content |
| Secondary | English and Mathematics, again pitched at the preceding level |

This isn’t an intelligence test, and it’s not really an ability test either. Children arriving from other curricula usually don’t fail because they’re behind; they fail because Singapore maths is taught through methods they’ve never seen, and because the English papers have an exam style you have to learn on its own terms. A bright child can miss on both counts.

So budget months of syllabus familiarisation rather than a weekend of past papers. Tutoring centres do have a role here, but think of them as translators between curricula. They don’t have seats to hand out, whatever the marketing implies.

## Placement realism, which is where the disappointment lives

Admission is subject to vacancies. MOE places children where seats actually exist, using your declared residential area where it can, and “where it can” is doing some heavy lifting in that sentence. The popular schools that local families ballot for are rarely what an AEIS outcome produces. Plan for a commute, some culture shock, and Mother Tongue lessons, rather than for the alumni network.

International-student school fees still apply once your child is placed, so check MOE’s fee checker. They sit far below the top international campuses, which is much of the appeal, but they are not citizen rates either ([fee context](/guides/local-schools-for-expats)).

Pass rules carry on applying too. If your child’s current pass doesn’t cover mainstream study, that’s a separate thing to fix, and [Student’s Pass](/guides/student-pass-singapore) covers it. And if there’s a SEN profile that needs a structured pathway, be honest with yourself: vacancy-assigned mainstream placement is a poor fit for that, and [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support) is a more useful starting point.

## How to decide, if you’re still relocating

| Your situation | Lean AEIS or S-AEIS | Lean international school |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long horizon, cost-sensitive, relaxed about school brand | Your main path, with a private backup | Backup only |
| Arriving mid-year with no flexibility for a January start | S-AEIS timing is worth considering | Often the cleaner answer |
| Short assignment, IB continuity, or SEN needs | Unlikely to work | Your main path, and see [waitlist realism](/guides/school-waitlist-realism) |
| Child already struggling in English | Delay a cycle, fix the CEQ and the language first | A bridge year ([Invictus](/schools/invictus-international) and similar) |

Whatever you choose, **keep the paid international-school hold until the AEIS outcome actually lands**. “We’ll cancel right after the test” is precisely how families end up holding neither seat.

One more thing if your child is at an international school or home-schooled: Direct School Admission offers to foreign students are typically conditional until the AEIS requirements are met. Being shortlisted on talent is not a way around the admissions exercise, however much it feels like one. Confirm the live MOE DSA and AEIS pages for your child’s particular pathway.
