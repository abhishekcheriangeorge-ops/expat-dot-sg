Dental is the quiet budget leak in expat Reddit threads: corporate inpatient plans often **carve out** teeth, EP holders have **no Medisave**, and CHAS-style subsidies are citizen/PR tools — not a foreigner discount code. Walk-in private clinics are straightforward; sticker shock happens on wisdom teeth, crowns, and implants.

## How to walk into a clinic

Bring:

- Passport or FIN card  
- Phone number + local address  
- Payment method  
- Insurance card / panel list if you have dental outpatient cover  

Private clinics will register you as a new patient and quote before major work. Public institutions are usable at **unsubsidised / private-patient** rates — do not expect citizen pricing because you pay cash.

## Fee realism (MOH benchmarks)

MOH publishes **CHAS dental fee benchmarks** for common private primary-care procedures (figures exclude GST; verify live). Useful anchors for negotiation, even if you are not CHAS-eligible:

| Procedure (benchmark band) | Approx. private primary-care range |
| --- | --- |
| Consultation | S$21–31 |
| Scaling | S$35–60 |
| Filling (simple → complex) | S$65–180 |
| Extraction (anterior → posterior) | S$70–164 |
| Root canal (anterior → molar) | S$400–1,400 |
| Permanent crown | S$750–1,400 |

Public hospitals publish **average private-patient** fees for specialist work. Examples from MOH’s 2024 averages (excl. GST; excl. consult/x-ray): impacted wisdom tooth surgery under local anaesthesia often clusters roughly **S$800–1,300** depending on institution; implant-with-crown averages commonly **S$4.5k–6.6k** per unit.

## Myths that waste money

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| “EP Medisave will cover my extraction” | EP holders generally **do not** contribute CPF / Medisave — see [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners) |
| “Polyclinic dental is subsidised for me” | Subsidies track citizenship/PR schemes; foreigners usually pay full rates |
| “Corporate health insurance includes dental” | Many group plans exclude or cap dental/optical — read the schedule |
| “Implants must be done in Singapore” | Elective big-ticket work is a common JB comparison shop — budget time, follow-ups, and complication risk |

## Practical year-one plan

1. Check employer outpatient / dental riders in week one — not after the toothache.  
2. Book a **scale and polish** early; humidity + coffee culture is not kind to delayed hygiene.  
3. Get a written quote before crowns / RCT / wisdom surgery; ask how it compares to MOH benchmarks.  
4. For implants or multi-unit work, compare Singapore public private-patient averages vs a reputable JB clinic **and** the cost of redo travel.  
5. Keep invoices for insurance claims and for any future PR / school admin packs that ask for medical history.

## Kids: braces and orthodontics

Teen orthodontics is a frequent relocation shock: international-school cohorts normalise braces, employer plans often **carve out** orthodontics entirely, and there is no foreigner CHAS orthodontic discount. Budget:

| Item | Planning note |
| --- | --- |
| Consult + records (x-rays / scans) | Cash unless outpatient dental rider explicitly covers diagnostics |
| Aligners / braces course | Multi-year SGD five-figure territory is common in private clinics — get a written staged quote |
| Retainers / repairs | Ask who pays if you leave Singapore mid-course |
| Mid-posting transfer | Confirm whether another country’s orthodontist will take over the same system |

Deep dive on braces / Invisalign Teen cash, NDCS vs private, and mid-posting transfer traps: [kids braces & orthodontics](/guides/kids-braces-orthodontics-singapore). Pair kids’ dental with [paediatrician pathways](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore) for infection / facial swelling triage, and do not assume school “dentist day” leaflets are a financing plan. Teen independence season (provisional licence, late enrichment) often collides with retainer / repair appointments — keep one shared household calendar with [teen driving independence](/guides/teen-driving-independence-singapore).

Pair adult care with [GP / hospital triage](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital) for facial swelling or trauma that belongs in urgent care, and [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) when you are shopping riders.
