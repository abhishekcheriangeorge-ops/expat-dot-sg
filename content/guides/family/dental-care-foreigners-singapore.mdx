Dental is the quiet budget leak, and it surfaces in expat Reddit threads more often than anything else people forgot to plan for. Three things stack up. Corporate inpatient plans very often carve teeth out entirely. EP holders have **no Medisave to fall back on**. And CHAS-style subsidies are citizen and PR tools, not a foreigner discount code you just haven't found yet. Walking into a private clinic is genuinely easy. The sticker shock arrives later, on wisdom teeth, crowns and implants.

## How to walk into a clinic

Take your passport or FIN card, a local phone number and address, a payment method, and your insurance card or panel list if you actually have dental outpatient cover. Private clinics will register you as a new patient and will quote before any major work.

Public institutions are usable too, but at unsubsidised, private-patient rates. Paying cash doesn't buy you citizen pricing, and it's worth going in knowing that rather than finding out at the counter.

## What treatment actually costs

MOH publishes CHAS dental fee benchmarks for common private primary-care procedures. The figures exclude GST and are worth verifying live, but they're useful anchors even if you're not CHAS-eligible, because a clinic quoting well outside the band ought to be able to explain why.

| Procedure (benchmark band) | Approx. private primary-care range |
| --- | --- |
| Consultation | S$21–31 |
| Scaling | S$35–60 |
| Filling (simple → complex) | S$65–180 |
| Extraction (anterior → posterior) | S$70–164 |
| Root canal (anterior → molar) | S$400–1,400 |
| Permanent crown | S$750–1,400 |

Public hospitals publish average private-patient fees for specialist work, and that's where the numbers get serious. From MOH's 2024 averages, excluding GST and excluding consult and x-ray, impacted wisdom tooth surgery under local anaesthesia tends to cluster somewhere around S$800–1,300 depending on the institution. Implant-with-crown averages commonly land at S$4.5k–6.6k per unit. That second figure is why implants become a planning exercise rather than an appointment.

## Myths that will cost you money

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| “EP Medisave will cover my extraction” | EP holders generally don’t contribute CPF or Medisave at all — see [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners) |
| “Polyclinic dental is subsidised for me” | Subsidies track citizenship and PR schemes; foreigners usually pay full rates |
| “Corporate health insurance includes dental” | Plenty of group plans exclude or cap dental and optical, so read the schedule before you book |
| “Implants must be done in Singapore” | Elective big-ticket work is a common JB comparison shop, but budget the time, the follow-ups and the complication risk |

## Your first year, in order

Check the employer outpatient and dental riders in week one, not in the week of the toothache. That's the difference between knowing your cap and discovering it. Book a scale and polish early too; humidity and Singapore's coffee culture are not kind to hygiene you keep postponing.

Before crowns, root canals or wisdom surgery, get a **written quote** and ask how it sits against the MOH benchmarks. For implants or multi-unit work, compare the Singapore public private-patient averages against a reputable JB clinic, and then add the cost of travelling back if something needs redoing. Keep every invoice, both for insurance claims and for any future PR or school admin pack that asks for medical history.

## Kids, braces and orthodontics

Teen orthodontics is one of the more reliable relocation shocks. International-school cohorts normalise braces, employer plans **often carve out orthodontics entirely**, and there is no foreigner CHAS orthodontic discount waiting at the end of it. So budget properly.

| Item | Planning note |
| --- | --- |
| Consult + records (x-rays / scans) | Cash, unless an outpatient dental rider explicitly covers diagnostics |
| Aligners / braces course | Multi-year SGD five-figure territory is common in private clinics, so get a written staged quote |
| Retainers / repairs | Ask who pays if you leave Singapore mid-course |
| Mid-posting transfer | Confirm whether an orthodontist in the next country will take over the same system |

For the detail on braces and Invisalign Teen cash, NDCS versus private, and the traps in a mid-posting transfer, we go deep in [kids braces and orthodontics](/guides/kids-braces-orthodontics-singapore). Pair your kids' dental with [paediatrician pathways](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore) so you know who triages an infection or facial swelling, and don't mistake the school's “dentist day” leaflet for a financing plan.

Teen independence season, with its provisional licence and late enrichment, tends to collide with retainer and repair appointments, so keep one shared household calendar and read [teen driving independence](/guides/teen-driving-independence-singapore) alongside this.

For adults, [GP and hospital triage](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital) covers the facial swelling or trauma that belongs in urgent care rather than a dental chair, and [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) is the one to read when you're shopping riders.

One last scheduling note. Concert and recital costume weeks are a poor time to discover an untreated toothache, so keep hygiene slots off the show blackouts ([concert and recital ops](/guides/international-schools-landscape)).
