Kids' passports expire on their own calendar, and that calendar has no idea your daughter has mocks in March. Parents on r/askSingapore keep discovering this mid-term, when it turns out the real critical path was never the holiday brochure — it was the airline's "6 months validity" rule and the queue for an embassy appointment.

Two things this guide isn't about. Leave and jet-lag logistics live in [home-leave jet lag](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore), and a newborn's first set of documents is a different process entirely: [newborn pass](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore). What follows is renewal timing, and how to travel in term time without wrecking something.

## What you're actually planning around

Start with the airline, because it bites first. Many carriers want at least 6 months of validity remaining on arrival, so a passport that's technically still valid can still get you turned away at check-in. Verify the rules for your actual route rather than assuming the general rule holds.

Then the embassy or consulate. Slots peak before every long break, and for some nationalities you're booking months ahead rather than weeks, which is the part people underestimate.

Your school adds its own blackouts on top. Mocks, exam weeks and compulsory camps rarely show up in the public calendar in enough detail to plan against, so ask the calendar office directly. And renewing a passport can force a pass update, so check how MOM and ICA want the two sequenced before you treat the new passport as the end of the job.

If your child is a dual national, you're standing in two queues at once. Start with the slower one.

## Travelling during term

Read the school's absence policy before you buy the tickets, not after. Some international school programmes are genuinely strict about term-time leave, and a refusal that arrives once the flights are paid for is an expensive lesson. While you're in the calendar, steer clear of boarding and exam windows ([IB/A-Level pathways](/guides/secondary-ib-alevel-pathways-singapore)).

Build a 48-hour buffer into the back end of the trip before anything high-stakes. Fever and jet lag both arrive on their own schedule, and putting a child into a test on day one back is a gamble you don't need to take.

If one parent is travelling with the kids, name the adult covering on-island before anyone leaves: the person who can collect a sick child or take them to a GP ([sick-child coverage](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital)). Holiday-camp logistics are a separate exercise again — see [kids activities](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps).

## Running this as a two-career household

Keep one shared expiry sheet covering every child and both adults. The nasty surprise usually lands around month 5, when someone works out that a passport clearing the calendar doesn't mean it clears the six-month rule.

Give one adult ownership of embassy appointment logistics. Two people saying "I'll look at it" reliably produces nobody looking at it.

Book the photo day after haircuts and braces adjustments, not before. A rejected photo doesn't just cost you the photo, it costs you the appointment slot, and the next one isn't next week. And scan the bio pages into a shared vault while you're at home on decent internet, because hotel Wi-Fi isn't a document management system.

## What this guide doesn't cover

It isn't legal advice on custody-related travel consent, and it isn't a substitute for your embassy's own appointment system. It also isn't immigration advice on pass replacements after a passport change: when the passport number changes, confirm what's actually needed with MOM or your employer's HR rather than guessing.
