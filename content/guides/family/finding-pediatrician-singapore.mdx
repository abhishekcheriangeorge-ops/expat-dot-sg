Pick a paediatric clinic near home, not near the CBD office you leave at 19:30. Forum threads from cash-pay foreigners converge on the same trade-off: **private continuity vs polyclinic price**, with the caveat that non-citizens usually **do not get citizen/PR subsidies**.

## Private vs polyclinic for foreign kids

| Path | Best for | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private paediatric clinic | Continuity, after-hours guidance, employer panel rates | Walk-in fees can surprise; confirm cash/card and insurance coding |
| Polyclinic | Vaccinations and routine reviews when queues are tolerable | Foreigner fees are unsubsidised — compare before assuming “cheap” |
| Children’s hospital ED (e.g. KKH / NUH pathways) | True emergencies | Not your well-child clinic |

Many families run a hybrid: polyclinic or panel GP for vaccines and mild URTIs; a named private paediatrician for complex issues and when you need someone who already knows your child. For NIR uploads, diphtheria/measles pass docs, and SC/PR vs foreigner fee bands, use the dedicated [childhood vaccinations for foreigners](/guides/childhood-vaccination-foreigners) guide before you book a polyclinic slot that rejects missing registry history.

### Foreigner fee reality (do not assume “poly = cheap”)

r/askSingapore cash-pay parents repeatedly discover that **non-citizen polyclinic fees sit near private GP rates** once citizen/PR subsidies disappear. Use poly for:

- Systematic vaccination calendars and nurse-led jabs when queues work  
- Milestone checks when you accept wait time  

Prefer private / panel paediatricians when:

- Employer insurance already prices the visit  
- You need continuity and after-hours guidance for a young infant  
- Time cost of poly queues exceeds the cash delta  

Publish fee pages from SingHealth / NUP polyclinics and your insurer panel list side by side before the first fever. “Locals said poly is free” is not a foreigner budget line.

## What to check before the first fever

- **Insurance panel status** — in-network rates matter more than brand names  
- Weekend / public-holiday hours and tele-advice practices  
- How vaccination records are stored for mobile kids (ask for a printable history)  
- Distance from home at 2 a.m., not from the office  
- Whether developmental concerns can be referred onward (see [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support))  

Bring previous immunisation records translated if needed. Establish the relationship during a **well visit** — not only in crisis.

## Dependant Pass and vaccines

If children are joining on Dependant Pass formalities, confirm any medical or vaccination requirements with MOM/ICA timing before you assume the school calendar is the only deadline. Keep a digital + paper copy of the immunisation booklet.

## After-hours plan

Write down tonight:

1. Clinic after-hours number (if any)  
2. Nearest 24-hour children’s / family clinic your insurer accepts (forum parents often name Thomson Medical’s 24h children’s clinic as a cash/card option — verify current hours and panel status yourself)  
3. Hospital ED you would actually drive to (**KKH** / **NUH** pathways for true paediatric emergencies)  
4. What “wait until morning” means for your child’s age  

### Cash-pay foreigner triage (fever night)

| Situation | Sensible first door | Usually wrong door |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild URTI, drinking well, daytime | Neighbourhood GP / panel paediatrician | Hospital ED |
| Young infant fever / red-flag symptoms | ED your insurer accepts — call ahead if stable | Waiting for a weekday polyclinic slot |
| Vaccine-only calendar | Polyclinic or panel well-child visit | Private paediatrician “because poly is free” (it is not, for foreigners) |
| Employer panel exists | Panel clinic first | Random CBD boutique clinic |

Cash-pay foreigners on r/askSingapore repeatedly learn that **polyclinic ≠ subsidised** for non-citizens — fees can sit near private GP rates ([cash-pay paediatrician](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1e73vx8/pediatrician_recommendation_for_nonlocal_pay_via/), [recommend paediatrician](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1g54za3/anyone_can_recommend_a_pediatrician_in_sgp/)). Ask HR for the **panel list + paediatric after-hours rule** in week one; print SingHealth / NUP foreigner fee pages before the first fever. CHAS cards are citizen/PR subsidy tools — do not plan an EP child’s care around a neighbour’s CHAS story.

### Allergy / EpiPen continuity (do this in week one)

If your child arrives with a food-allergy or anaphylaxis history, the paediatrician’s first job is not a well-child chat — it is unlocking a **Singapore prescription** and school action plan. Foreign auto-injector scripts are not refilled at the counter. Use the dedicated [kids allergy & EpiPen](/guides/kids-allergy-epipen-singapore) playbook (polyclinic referral vs private allergist, KKH pathways, preschool paperwork) before term starts.

### Myopia control (do not wait for the next home leave)

Singapore’s progression rates punish “we’ll fix glasses on home leave.” If classroom squinting, a rising Rx, or a school vision note shows up, ask the paediatrician for an **optometry / ophthalmology** path early — atropine, myopia-control lenses, or Ortho-K are clinical decisions, not optical-shop folklore. Use the [kids myopia control](/guides/kids-myopia-control-singapore) playbook for foreigner fee realism and outdoor-time habits.
