Pick a paediatric clinic near home, not near the CBD office you leave at 19:30. That sounds obvious right up until the first 2 a.m. fever, when the only distance that matters is the one between your bed and a waiting room. Forum threads from cash-pay foreigners land on the same trade-off — private continuity against polyclinic price — with one caveat that reshapes the whole sum: non-citizens usually don't get citizen or PR subsidies.

## Private vs polyclinic for foreign kids

| Path | Best for | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private paediatric clinic | Continuity, after-hours guidance, employer panel rates | Walk-in fees can surprise; confirm cash/card and insurance coding |
| Polyclinic | Vaccinations and routine reviews when queues are tolerable | Foreigner fees are unsubsidised — compare before assuming “cheap” |
| Children’s hospital ED (e.g. KKH / NUH pathways) | True emergencies | Not your well-child clinic |

Most families end up running a hybrid. Polyclinic or a panel GP handles vaccines and mild URTIs; a named private paediatrician handles the complicated things, and the nights when it matters that someone already knows your child. Before you book a polyclinic slot that gets turned away for missing registry history, read the dedicated [childhood vaccinations for foreigners](/guides/childhood-vaccination-foreigners) guide, which covers NIR uploads, diphtheria and measles pass documents, and the SC/PR versus foreigner fee bands.

### The foreigner fee reality

This is the part that catches people out. Cash-pay parents on r/askSingapore keep discovering that non-citizen polyclinic fees sit near private GP rates once the citizen and PR subsidies come off. "Locals said poly is free" is not a foreigner budget line.

Polyclinic still earns its place for systematic vaccination calendars and nurse-led jabs when the queue works for you, and for milestone checks when you can accept the wait. Go private or panel when your employer's insurance already prices the visit, when you need continuity and after-hours guidance for a young infant, or when the time cost of the queue is worth more to you than the cash difference. Put the published SingHealth and NUP polyclinic fee pages next to your insurer's panel list and compare them before the first fever, rather than during it.

## What to check before the first fever

- **Insurance panel status**, because in-network rates matter more than brand names
- Weekend and public-holiday hours, and whether the practice gives tele-advice
- How vaccination records are stored for a mobile child (ask for a printable history)
- Distance from home at 2 a.m., not distance from the office
- Whether developmental concerns can be referred onward, which is covered in [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support)

Bring previous immunisation records, translated if they need to be. And establish the relationship during a well visit rather than in a crisis. That first appointment goes better when nobody in the room is frightened.

## Dependant Pass and vaccines

If your children are joining on Dependant Pass formalities, confirm any medical or vaccination requirements against MOM and ICA timing instead of assuming the school calendar sets the only deadline. Keep the immunisation booklet in both digital and paper form; the copy you need is always the one that got left in the other country.

## Your after-hours plan

Write these four things down tonight, while everyone is well.

1. The clinic's after-hours number, if it has one
2. The nearest 24-hour children's or family clinic your insurer accepts (forum parents often name Thomson Medical's 24h children's clinic as a cash or card option — verify current hours and panel status yourself)
3. The hospital ED you would actually drive to, which means KKH or NUH pathways for true paediatric emergencies
4. What "wait until morning" means for a child of your child's age

### Cash-pay foreigner triage (fever night)

| Situation | Sensible first door | Usually wrong door |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild URTI, drinking well, daytime | Neighbourhood GP / panel paediatrician | Hospital ED |
| Young infant fever / red-flag symptoms | ED your insurer accepts — call ahead if stable | Waiting for a weekday polyclinic slot |
| Vaccine-only calendar | Polyclinic or panel well-child visit | Private paediatrician “because poly is free” (it is not, for foreigners) |
| Employer panel exists | Panel clinic first | Random CBD boutique clinic |

The lesson cash-pay foreigners keep relearning on r/askSingapore is that **a polyclinic is not subsidised** for non-citizens, and the fees can sit near private GP rates ([cash-pay paediatrician](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1e73vx8/pediatrician_recommendation_for_nonlocal_pay_via/), [recommend paediatrician](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1g54za3/anyone_can_recommend_a_pediatrician_in_sgp/)). Ask HR in week one for the panel list and the paediatric after-hours rule, and print the SingHealth and NUP foreigner fee pages before you need them. CHAS cards are a citizen and PR subsidy tool, so don't plan an EP child's care around a neighbour's CHAS story.

### Allergy and EpiPen continuity (do this in week one)

If your child arrives with a food-allergy or anaphylaxis history, the paediatrician's first job isn't a well-child chat. It's unlocking a **Singapore prescription** and a school action plan, because a foreign auto-injector script doesn't get refilled at the counter here. The [kids allergy & EpiPen](/guides/kids-allergy-epipen-singapore) playbook walks through the polyclinic referral versus private allergist choice, KKH pathways, and the preschool paperwork. Get it done before term starts.

### Myopia control (not a home-leave errand)

Singapore's progression rates punish the "we'll fix glasses on home leave" plan. If classroom squinting, a rising Rx, or a school vision note turns up, ask the paediatrician for an optometry or ophthalmology path early. Atropine, myopia-control lenses and Ortho-K are clinical decisions, not optical-shop folklore. The [kids myopia control](/guides/kids-myopia-control-singapore) playbook has the foreigner fee realism and the outdoor-time habits.
