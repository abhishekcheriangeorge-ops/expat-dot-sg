The IVF threads on r/askSingapore mix three different populations who are all talking past each other. There are Singapore Citizen couples on public co-funding, SC + foreigner couples arguing about the 35% tier, and EP + EP couples who are genuinely shocked that KKH or NUH still bills them like private patients. The clinical care here can be excellent. The subsidy story is gated on citizenship, and that is the part nobody mentions before your first consult.

One scheduling note before any of the money. Treatment calendars and home leave will collide, and retrieval weeks are the thing that cannot move. If you're a dual-career household, read [home-leave jet-lag ops](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore) before you stack flights on top of a cycle.

## The co-funding gate (read this first)

Government Assisted Conception Procedure (ACP) co-funding at public Assisted Reproduction centres needs **you or your spouse to be a Singapore Citizen** at the start of the ART cycle. That is the whole gate, and everything below hangs off it. MOH publishes the dollar caps; verify them live, because ours were last reviewed Mar 2026 on the scheme page.

| Procedure | SC + SC | SC + PR | SC + foreigner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh ART | 75%; up to S$7,700 | 55%; up to S$5,700 | 35%; up to S$3,600 |
| Frozen ART | 75%; up to S$2,200 | 55%; up to S$1,600 | 35%; up to S$1,000 |
| IUI | 75%; up to S$1,000 | 55%; up to S$700 | 35%; up to S$500 |

Where that leaves each household pattern:

| Couple pattern | Typical story |
| --- | --- |
| SC + SC / SC + PR / SC + foreigner (EP/LTVP/etc.) | Co-funding possible at public AR centres if age, clinical and cycle-count gates pass |
| PR + PR / EP + EP / two foreigners | **No MOH ART co-funding** — budget cash and check what insurance really does |

Then there's age. You want to start the ART cycle before age 40 for the main co-funded cycles. MOH does allow co-funding for up to 2 of the 6 co-funded ART cycles at age 40+, but only if you already attempted AR or IUI before 40, so the rule extends a door you were already standing in rather than opening a new one. Lifetime caps under the scheme are 3 fresh + 3 frozen co-funded ART cycles.

You'll also come across "write to your MP" anecdotes from SC + foreigner couples. Those threads are about advocacy and genuine edge cases. The published baseline is already the 35% SC–foreigner tier in the table above, so the starting point is not "foreigners get nothing".

## Public or private

The public AR centres named in MOH materials are KKH, NUH and SGH. The "six-month wait before stims" and "appointments a month out unless you're already stimulating" stories you'll read are about capacity, not about anyone being unhelpful to you. Fertility is time-sensitive in a way that queues are bad at respecting, which is why those threads read the way they do.

| Path | Upside | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public AR with co-funding you qualify for | Lowest cash for SC-linked couples | Queues, and less picking your own consultant |
| Public AR as a foreigner or otherwise ineligible | Same clinical brands | Often unsubsidised billing; forum reports of IVF billed as private even after a poly referral |
| Private fertility clinic | Scheduling and continuity | Cash, and MediSave only if you actually have MediSave |

Going private does not unlock MOH ART co-funding. Worth being blunt about, because private clinics sell on access and speed and the subsidy question tends not to come up in that conversation. Private centres may still accept MediSave for ACPs if the patient or spouse has a MediSave account and meets the withdrawal rules.

## PGT and the other cash add-ons

Pre-implantation genetic testing (PGT-M / PGT-SR) has its own MOH co-funding tables for eligible SC-linked couples, still citizen-gated, with separate biopsy and lab caps. The forum picture is consistent enough to believe: even subsidised couples often pay large cash sums for PGT kits and lab work. If you're an EP–EP couple, price PGT as a full cash line item rather than a MediSave footnote.

Ask the finance desk explicitly what is in the cycle quote and what sits outside it: ICSI, freezing storage, embryo biopsy, genetic lab fees. A quote for a cycle is not always a quote for everything that happens during the cycle, and that gap is where the unpleasant surprises live.

## Elective egg freezing

Singapore allows elective egg freezing at licensed AR providers, public AR centres included. But because elective freezing is not medically indicated, MOH's published position is that subsidies, ART co-funding and MediSave do not apply to elective retrieval or storage. Same clinical machinery as an IVF retrieval, entirely different financing story. Budget it as a private cash procedure and nothing about the bill will surprise you.

Medical fertility preservation, where the treatment itself risks infertility, runs on a different rail, with separate MediShield Life developments from mid-2026 for iatrogenic risk. Don't conflate chemotherapy preservation with elective social freezing. They involve the same lab and they are funded nothing alike.

## MediSave, only if you have it

MOH's published MediSave pattern for ACPs (ART/IUI) runs roughly like this: S$6,000 for the first cycle, S$5,000 for the second, then S$4,000 for the third and subsequent, against a lifetime limit around S$15,000 per patient.

Here is why that paragraph may not be for you. Employment Pass holders generally don't build MediSave the way PRs and citizens do, as we explain in [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners), so **an EP–EP budget cannot lean on "we'll MediSave it"**. One more thing that catches people: incomplete cycles abandoned for non-medical reasons fail MediSave rules on the MOH page, so walking away mid-cycle can cost you the withdrawal on top of everything else.

## Insurance, and sequencing into maternity

Most group hospital plans still exclude fertility treatment, and plenty exclude maternity as well unless you bought the right rider early. Audit the policy before you start stims, not after the first invoice arrives. Three questions get you most of the way:

1. Does outpatient and specialist cover pay for investigations only, or also for retrieval and transfer?
2. If you conceive, does the maternity waiting period still apply?
3. What are the neonatal and NICU limits if you deliver here as a foreigner? That last one is the big number, and we cover it in [maternity care](/guides/maternity-care-singapore).

## What the timelines actually look like

Pre-IVF investigations on their own can take weeks to months; MOH notes the clinical work-up can run up to around 3 months before a cycle starts. Public stims and monitoring are appointment-dense, which is why leave planning and travel matter more here than in almost any other medical pathway. Failed cycles are common, so if more than one attempt is your plan, price more than one attempt from the start rather than discovering the budget halfway through.

A successful pregnancy then hands you the next set of clocks: [birth registration and the newborn's pass](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore), and [Baby Bonus](/guides/baby-bonus-cda-mixed-status), which again only applies if the child is SC.

## Questions for the clinic finance desk

1. Are we co-funding eligible today, given the citizenship of each spouse at cycle start?
2. What are the fresh and frozen cycle cash quotes after any co-funding or MediSave?
3. What is included, and what are the add-ons (ICSI, PGT, freezing storage)?
4. If we upgrade to a named consultant, does co-funding change?
5. Who bills NICU if we deliver here later as foreigners?

Fertility care in Singapore is medically sophisticated and the clinics are good. For two work-pass holders it is usually a cash project rather than a Marriage & Parenthood subsidy story, and knowing which of those two you're in before you start makes every later decision cheaper.
