When both adults work, grandparents start to look like free help, and the arithmetic of a dual-career household makes that very tempting. Singapore immigration takes a different view: caregiving isn’t a free-form tourist activity you can stack onto a visit. The r/askSingapore threads about parents coming over “for six months to help with the baby” keep running into the same wall of Social Visit Pass limits and LTVP eligibility folklore, and into the quieter fact underneath all of it, which is that **grandma isn’t a Work Permit**.

Paid day care still belongs in [part-time babysitting](/guides/part-time-babysitter-nanny-singapore) and [preschool](/guides/preschool-childcare-singapore). This guide covers the long-stay grandparent layer for EP and DP households, which is where the immigration and the logistics tangle together.

## The pass products, and there isn’t a fourth

| Product | Typical use | Caregiving realism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social Visit Pass / visit entitlements | Short stays; tourism framing | Extensions are discretionary; don’t promise HR a six-month free nanny |
| Long-Term Visit Pass (LTVP) | Eligible parent relationships under published ICA categories | Not automatic because you “need childcare”; check current ICA parent/LTVP rules |
| Dependant’s Pass | Spouse / unmarried children of EP (usual MOM frame) | Grandparents are not a standard DP category |

The discretionary word in that first row is the one that catches people. An extension granted last year to a friend isn’t a precedent for yours, and it’s granted after arrival rather than before, which means you can be committed to a plan that hasn’t been approved yet.

## Running the household while they’re here

Jet lag plus newborn nights is a bad combination, so rotate the night shifts rather than handing both adults’ overtime to someone who landed yesterday. Have a named GP and an A&E plan in place before you need one, which we set out in [healthcare, GPs and hospitals](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital), and check that whatever travel insurance you buy genuinely covers older adults rather than quietly capping them.

Write the house rules down before the flight. Feeding, screens and discipline are where multi-generational goodwill goes to die, and the conversation is much easier to have in advance than at 7pm on a Tuesday with a tired toddler in the room. Book your bridge care, whether that’s a babysitter or a preschool place, **before** the visit ends rather than after, because the dual-career calendar collapses the week the help leaves and that’s the worst week to start a search.

One more thing that gets missed: the visiting parent can be very isolated. Give them one rail of their own, whether that’s a [nationality group](/guides/nationality-groups-singapore) or an [ANZA or AWA](/guides/anza-awa-associations) coffee, so the trip isn’t entirely unpaid labour in an unfamiliar country.

Treat grandparent cover as a time-boxed project inside your [dual-career household planning](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore). It isn’t a permanent headcount cut, however much it feels like one in month two.

## Caregiving and employment aren’t the same thing

Unpaid family caregiving isn’t a substitute for a domestic worker Work Permit, and paying grandma under the table for full-time childcare is a compliance smell rather than a clever arrangement. Use the legal care products instead. Condo access cards, school pickup authorisations and hospital next-of-kin forms all need named paperwork too, because a WhatsApp message from you doesn’t authorise anyone to collect a child or make a decision at a hospital counter. And if there’s SEN or medical complexity in the picture, that still needs professional rails, which is what [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support) is for.

## A soft-landing first week

1. Rest on day one, with no full daycare takeover
2. Walk the school or preschool pickup route once, with a parent
3. Save Singpass and ICA appointment screenshots and insurance PDFs into a shared folder
4. Set up one neighbour coffee or association meet-up early

## Where it usually goes wrong

Telling HR “my mum is moving here for a year” when what she holds is a tourist stamp. Cancelling the babysitter the week grandma lands, then discovering the slot is gone. Ignoring the grandparent’s own medical and mobility limits, which tend to be more relevant than anyone wants to say out loud. Leaving end-of-visit childcare as a problem for later. And treating an ICA refusal as a rude surprise, when the refusal came after the lease was already signed.

Grandparent cover is an immigration question and a care-logistics question at the same time. Design both before the airport run, not during it.
