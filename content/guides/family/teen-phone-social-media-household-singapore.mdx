International-school cohorts run on always-on group chats, and nobody asked you whether that was alright. On r/askSingapore you can watch dual-career households fighting the wrong war (“no phone, ever”) when what actually survives mocks, travel weeks and an 11pm class WhatsApp is a written household contract.

The mental-health rails live elsewhere: [teen counselling](/guides/teen-mental-health-school-counselling-singapore) and [mental health resources](/guides/mental-health-resources). If exam load is the pressure you're worried about, that's [IB vs A-Level](/guides/secondary-ib-alevel-pathways-singapore). This guide is the device and social operations side of running a household.

## What the contract should name

A contract only works if it names specifics. Principles get relitigated every single night; a clause with an actual time on it doesn't.

| Clause | Example household rule | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep window | Phones charge outside bedrooms after 22:30 on school nights | Humidity, FOMO and early buses all stack up |
| Exam blackout | No new game downloads in mock weeks | Panic installs are a tell |
| Group-chat hours | Class chat muted 23:00–06:30; urgent means call a parent | IS project chats ignore your timezone guilt |
| Location sharing | On for the first independent Grab and bus months | Safety without full surveillance theatre |
| Photo and story consent | No posting siblings or the home address | Condo and school ID leakage |
| Consequence ladder | Written down, boring, agreed in advance | Improvised bans teach nothing |

Family digital-safety orientation: [CSA](https://www.csa.gov.sg/). School well-being policies vary — ask the counsellor what the school’s device code actually enforces versus aspirational handbook language ([MOE](https://www.moe.gov.sg/) for system framing).

## Where dual-career households come unstuck

The failure modes are predictable, and most of them are about the adults rather than the teenager.

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| One parent bans, the other quietly undermines it on FaceTime | One joint contract, signed by both adults |
| Rules only appear after a crisis | Draft them in a calm week, revise quarterly |
| “No social media” while a CCA actually runs on Telegram | Allow listed apps, with time bounds |
| Monitoring apps installed without a conversation | Disclose the tools; secrecy destroys trust faster than TikTok |

Travel weeks need their own temporary amendment, the same way [bus and pickup ops](/guides/international-schools-landscape) do when one adult is off-island.

## When it stops being a house-rules problem

Some things aren't a screen-time negotiation, and treating them as one costs you time you don't have. If sleep has collapsed and grades are in freefall, if group-chat bullying or exclusion carries on after one adult has already intervened, or if you're seeing self-harm language or a sudden withdrawal, go to [teen counselling](/guides/teen-mental-health-school-counselling-singapore) **before** you negotiate another screen-time chart.

## A one-page starter contract

1. Devices sleep in the kitchen on school nights.
2. Class chats mute overnight, and teachers email for true emergencies.
3. New apps get a 24-hour parent look, which is a look and not veto theatre.
4. Public stories never show unit numbers, passes, or uniforms with names on them.
5. Review date: the first Sunday of each term.

Write it down. Put it on the fridge. It should be boring, and boring is the feature: a rule improvised mid-argument teaches nothing, while a rule that has been on the fridge since the start of term is simply how your house works.
