On r/askSingapore the hardest family threads are not “how do I file” — they are **cross-border kids**, **LTVP/DP spouses**, and “can they take the child out on holiday without me?” Singapore family procedure is structured; immigration and foreign recognition are the traps.

This is orientation, not legal advice. Cross-border custody moves on hours and passport control, not Facebook consensus. If the relationship involves **violence or coercion**, start with safety and PPO rails — [family violence for foreigners](/guides/family-violence-ppo-foreigners) — before sequencing divorce forms.

## Can you divorce in Singapore?

Family Courts civil divorce generally requires **all** of:

| Gate | Published rule |
| --- | --- |
| Status | You **or** your spouse is a Singapore citizen **or** has lived in Singapore for **3 continuous years** immediately before filing |
| Marriage length | Married at least **3 years**, unless the court grants permission for an earlier divorce |
| Marriage type | Married under **civil law** (Syariah marriages go to the Syariah Court) |

<OfficialSource>
Start with [Judiciary — understand the requirements for getting a divorce](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/family/understand-requirements-getting-divorce) and the [Divorce guide PDF](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/docs/default-source/family-docs/divorce_guide_english.pdf).
</OfficialSource>

Reddit’s “uncontested in four months” anecdotes assume you already agree **everything** (children, housing, maintenance). Contested ancillaries are a different project.

## Custody language that confuses foreigners

Singapore usually separates three ideas:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Custody** | Major decisions (education, religion, health) — often **joint** |
| **Care and control** | Who the child lives with day to day |
| **Access** | Contact for the parent without care and control |
| **Maintenance** | Financial support for the child (and sometimes a spouse) |

If you have children under 21, expect co-parenting programme requirements before filing — the Judiciary divorce guide flags Mandatory Co-Parenting Programme (CPP) for many cases.

## The Hague / ICAA clock

International child abduction is removing or retaining a child under **16** outside their habitual residence without a parent who has custody rights. Singapore implements the 1980 Hague Convention through the **International Child Abduction Act (ICAA)**.

| Situation | Practical move |
| --- | --- |
| Fear a parent will fly the child out | Speak to counsel **before** travel season; ask about ICA assistance to stop departure |
| Child already retained overseas | Check whether the other country is a Hague contracting state; speed matters for habitual residence |
| “We both have passports, so either of us can go” | Immigration stamps ≠ custody consent |

<OfficialSource>
[International child abduction overview](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/family/international-child-abduction) and [file an ICAA application](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/family/file-international-child-abduction-application). The Judiciary pages also cover requesting ICA assistance to prevent a child leaving Singapore.
</OfficialSource>

Non-Hague destinations (common in regional forums) are harder. Do not invent “embassy will force return” from a Reddit thread.

## Immigration knock-ons people miss

- A spouse on **Dependant’s Pass** or **LTVP** usually sits on the main pass / sponsorship story — separation can collapse stay rights even before a Final Judgment  
- Taking a Singapore-habitually-resident child “home for a visit” without consent can become an abduction case, not a holiday  
- Foreign divorce decrees still need Singapore recognition / enforcement work for local assets, schools, and banks  

Pair this with [LTVP](/guides/ltvp-singapore), [Dependant’s Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore), and [wills / CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore) so estate and pass paperwork do not contradict court orders.

## Practical sequence (before you escalate online)

1. Secure children’s passports / travel documents and know who holds them  
2. Book Singapore family counsel with **cross-border** experience — not only “uncontested package” shops  
3. Map pass expiry dates the same week you map school term dates  
4. If travel risk is real, ask counsel about court orders **and** ICA channels in the same conversation  
5. Keep ROM / marriage docs and apostilles organised — see [apostille & notary](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore)

Marriage abroad or at ROM does not auto-solve jurisdiction. If either of you may leave Singapore soon after separating, treat domicile and recognition as part of the first consult — the same warning at the end of [getting married as foreigners](/guides/getting-married-foreigners-singapore).
