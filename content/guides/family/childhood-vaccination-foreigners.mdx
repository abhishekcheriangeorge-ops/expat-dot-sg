Bringing a toddler on a Dependant’s Pass is not “just continue the home-country schedule at the nearest clinic.” Singapore expects **National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (NCIS)** alignment, **National Immunisation Registry (NIR)** records, and — for many foreign-born kids — **diphtheria and measles** documentation before long-term passes.

## Pass paperwork before the jab queue

From **1 Feb 2019**, MOH requires foreign-born children **12 and under** applying for MOM **Dependant’s Pass / LTVP** or ICA **Student’s Pass** to submit documented vaccination or immunity evidence for **diphtheria and measles** (verified via HPB/NIR processes) before the pass application proceeds. If doses are not yet due, follow-up after entry is still required.

Treat this as a relocation critical path — not a preschool afterthought.

## NIR first, then polyclinic

Polyclinics generally want overseas history **in NIR** before they continue the schedule. Parents upload records (English translation + original if needed), parent FIN/NRIC images, child’s pass/birth docs. Only nationally recommended vaccines are recorded; processing is often quoted around **10 working days** when documents are clear.

| Status | Fee reality (verify at clinic) |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore Citizen | Full subsidies for nationally recommended childhood vaccinations / screenings at polyclinics and eligible CHAS GPs |
| PR | Discounted / subsidised rates at polyclinics for NCIS items |
| EP/DP/other foreigner | **Unsubsidised** — polyclinic cash fees can approach private GP pricing; still useful for systematic recall |

Book the polyclinic appointment **after** NIR updates, not before.

## Private paediatrician vs polyclinic

Reddit’s cash-pay parents often keep a **private paediatric panel** for fever and continuity, then use polyclinic/NIR for the national schedule once records land. That hybrid is rational when:

- Employer insurance covers private acute visits but not vaccines  
- You need flexible evening slots  
- BCG or catch-up live vaccines need hospital pathways (e.g. some overseas-born SC cases at KKH)  

Foreigner babies still pay private rates for many hospital pathways — see [finding a paediatrician](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore) and [newborn admin](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore).

## Practical sequence

1. Scan home-country booklet; translate non-English pages  
2. Submit to NIR; wait for acceptance before mass polyclinic booking  
3. Align DP/Student’s Pass diphtheria/measles evidence with MOM/ICA checklists  
4. Ask HR whether vaccines are reimbursable (usually not)  
5. Keep one PDF folder: NIR confirmation + clinic receipts for school/preschool enrolment  

Vaccination is boring until a pass stalls. Front-load NIR and the two named diseases before you argue about brand preferences on HPV or travel extras.
