The budget question comes round on r/askSingapore every few weeks: can kids on a Dependant's Pass go to a government school? Yes, sometimes. That "sometimes" is doing a lot of work. Places aren't guaranteed, Singaporean and PR children are served first, MOE assigns you a school rather than letting you pick one, and international-student fees sit far above citizen rates. They're still usually cheaper than SAS, TTS or UWC-tier campuses, which is why families keep asking. Just treat local MOE as a pathway you attempt with a private backup in place, not as free public schooling.

## Why families look at it

Four things usually start the conversation. Cost is the obvious one: even at non-citizen rates, a government school costs a fraction of a premium international campus. Then there's the local language immersion and the PSLE / O-Level pathway, which matters more if you're thinking in decades than in three-year postings. Longer-stay or PR ambitions pull the same way. And if one child is already sitting in the MOE system, running two schools on two different calendars gets old quickly.

## P1 for international students (2026 cycle)

MOE runs a separate P1 registration path for international students, IS in their shorthand. It is not the citizen Phase 1–3 ballot that local parents spend years strategising over, so most of what you'll see argued about on local parenting forums doesn't apply to your family at all.

| Step | What MOE publishes (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| Indicate interest | An online form plus documents, in a short May window. The 2026 example: **19–25 May 2026**, 9am–4.30pm. Late submissions are not considered. |
| Outcome | An email by end October saying whether a place is offered |
| If offered | You register at the designated school named in that email |
| If not offered | Private or international school, or [AEIS](/guides/aeis-saeis-international-students) for Primary 2 the following cycle |

The age gate for this path is a child aged 6 to 6+ as of 1 January in the year of admission. Older children go through AEIS or S-AEIS instead, and MOE's [admissions hub](https://www.moe.gov.sg/international-students/admission) is where you check which one you're in.

## You get assigned a school, you don't choose one

Two long r/askSingapore threads, [local primary for foreign kids](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1houq07/local_primary_schools_for_foreign_kids/) and [DP plus government education](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1jfc9e5/dependent_pass_and_government_education/), land on the same answer and are blunt about it.

The "brand" primaries local families ballot for are effectively closed to foreigners once SC and PR demand has been absorbed. MOE places international students where vacancies exist. Your residential area is used where possible, which is not the same thing as a guarantee of a 1 km walk. Families in those threads describe cross-island commutes, taxi carpools, and moving house mid-posting because the assigned school sat outside anything resembling a bus catchment. Passing AEIS later doesn't buy you a choice of Raffles-adjacent branding either: same vacancy logic, same lack of say in the name on the uniform.

Three household assumptions are worth killing before they cost you money. The first is "we'll get the school next to our condo", so plan for an assignment somewhere else and a transport line in the budget. The second is that paying EP-level tax buys citizen fees, when fees follow the child's citizenship and residency, not the parent's tax status. The third is that a native English speaker skips the Cambridge-style gates, when AEIS at primary still has a CEQ score gate — the [AEIS guide](/guides/aeis-saeis-international-students) has the detail.

## Fees, and why "almost free" is the wrong frame

Use MOE's school fees checker for live monthly figures, because non-citizen fees rose across 2024–2026 under a published schedule and any number you hear secondhand is probably a year stale. Order-of-magnitude from that press release, school-fee component only, with the live checker and miscellaneous fees still to layer on top:

| Level | IS (Non-ASEAN) direction of travel | IS (ASEAN) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Climbing toward ~S$1,000+/month by 2026 | Lower than non-ASEAN but still material |
| Secondary | Materially higher than primary | Still a four-figure monthly band at the upper end |

For international students those fees are GST-inclusive in MOE's framing, while SC and PR GST is subsidised. Independent schools and SPED set their own fees and sit outside this picture entirely. One trap to flag before you build a spreadsheet around it: **the Child Development Account (Baby Bonus) cannot be used for mainstream GIRO school-fee payment**. If you had mentally earmarked the CDA as the school cheque, unearmark it now.

## Whether it actually fits your child

Class sizes run around 30 in early primary and get larger later, and the assessment intensity is genuine rather than folklore. Mother Tongue is the one that catches families out. Exemptions do exist for some approved foreign languages and some circumstances, but if your child has no Chinese, Malay or Tamil base, plan tutoring and treat it as part of the cost rather than a formality ([MTL hub](https://www.moe.gov.sg/primary/curriculum/mother-tongue-languages)). CCA load is heavier than what international schools file under "optional".

Two more checks before anyone resigns a job overseas. Ask whether the school you're assigned can actually staff the EAL or learning support your child needs, and if that's a live question, read [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support) first. And don't lock in housing around the school you're hoping for. Lock it around the school you were offered.

## How to decide

| Household | Lean local MOE | Lean international |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mid package, long horizon, flexible on school brand | Yes — try P1/AEIS with a private backup | Backup only |
| Short assignment, curriculum continuity, waitlist capital | Unlikely | Primary path |
| Special educational needs | Rarely, given capacity and foreigner SPED limits | Often clearer support pathways |
| Need a named campus before signing a lease | Unlikely until the outcome email lands | Primary path |

While you wait, bridge options include mid-fee internationals such as [Invictus](/schools/invictus-international), or a nationality-system school like [Lycée Français](/schools/lycee-francais) when curriculum continuity matters to you more than MOE branding.
