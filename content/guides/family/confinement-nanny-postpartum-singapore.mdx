The birth-cost threads on r/askSingapore ([give birth here vs home country](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1l1fe33/important_considerations_whether_to_have_my/)) keep circling the same soft cost: 28 days of confinement help can run to roughly what it would cost to fly grandparents in for the same stretch. For a foreigner household the harder part isn't the agency brochure. It's MOM's Malaysian-only Work Permit, the citizen versus non-citizen baby levy, and the fact that all of this admin lands on top of the [newborn pass clock](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore).

Confinement covers week one and not much further. The relay from there into infant care is its own problem for dual-career households, and we work through it in the [newborn to preschool handoff](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore).

## What a confinement nanny is, and isn't

| | Confinement nanny (pui yuet / yue sao) | MDW / long-term helper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical window | ~28 days postpartum in agency packages; the MOM Work Permit runs a maximum of 16 weeks from birth | Years, with the full MDW rules |
| Core job | Mother's recovery, newborn nights, meals, early breastfeeding support | Whole-household domestic work |
| Foreign hire path | Malaysian Work Permit under the confinement-nanny scheme | Separate MDW Work Permit and agency stack |
| After the window | It ends. This is not a backdoor helper | Continues for as long as you keep the MDW |

If you already have a helper, or are about to hire one, settle the roles before day zero: nanny for fourth-trimester intensity, helper for the ongoing household load. We cover the second half in [hiring a domestic helper](/guides/hiring-domestic-helper-mdw). What you cannot do is have one person legally cover both titles, and finding that out in week one is a bad time to find it out.

## The Work Permit, and the Malaysian-only gate

These are the scheme facts parents most often skip past:

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Nanny nationality | Malaysian only under this Work Permit |
| Nanny age | At least 23 and below 70 at application |
| Employer | 21+, and must have a newborn under 4 months |
| Duration | Up to 16 weeks from the child's date of birth |
| Renewable | No |
| Application fee | S$35 (verify on the live apply page) |
| Pass format | A Work Permit letter rather than a plastic card, given the short duration |

You can apply from 4 weeks before EDD up to 16 weeks after birth. Past that window MOM points you at an ordinary MDW Work Permit instead. Pay attention to how the duration rule is written, because it is the trap: the 16 weeks run from the child's date of birth, not from approval, so **applying late doesn't delay the clock, it shortens your permit**.

## The levy, or the citizenship tax on sleep

| Newborn status | Monthly levy (MOM) |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore citizen child | S$60 / month |
| Non-Singapore citizen child | **S$300 / month** |

If both parents are foreigners on EP or S Pass, your baby sits on the S$300 line. Citizen babies get the concession band, including in mixed-status households where the child is the citizen. If you already have a helper under your name, MOM combines the levies for GIRO, which is tidy and also makes it easy to stop noticing what the confinement band is costing you. Budget it as its own line.

Medical insurance for the nanny is required, on MDW-style minimums, so confirm the live MOM insurance notes before your policy start date rather than after. The salary you pay her is taxable too. IRAS rules apply even to a stint this short.

## What the packages actually cost

Agency 28-day stay-in packages commonly land in the mid four figures SGD. Then there is everything that sits outside that number:

- Ang bao and first-day gift culture, often hundreds of dollars; ask the agency what the norm is
- Extension days beyond day 28
- Groceries, confinement herbs and postnatal massage, usually sold as add-ons
- Transport for the nanny
- The levy and the insurance, if the agency hasn't baked them in

Treat "all-in" as a claim to test against that list rather than a feeling to trust. Ask in writing whether the MOM application, the levy and the insurance sit inside the package price. And don't let anyone fold this into the MDW security bond stories you'll read elsewhere. Different scheme, different rules.

## Choosing between the options

| Option | When it wins | When it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malaysian confinement nanny on a MOM Work Permit | You want structured postpartum help here and can carry the permit and levy on top of delivery bills | You leave the permit to the last week of pregnancy |
| A local SC or PR night nurse, no Work Permit needed | You can absorb higher day rates and want to skip the levy | Supply is thin, and English or method fit still varies |
| Family flying in | Culture match and trust, and often cheaper than a Singapore package | Visitor Pass length, housing space, and how much recovery load falls on your partner |
| Giving birth abroad and coming back | Lower clinical and confinement stack in some countries | Paperwork in two places, and the baby still needs a Singapore pass if you live here ([maternity care](/guides/maternity-care-singapore)) |
| Helper only from week one | You already have a trained MDW and clear night coverage | Newborn intensity plus household chores burns one person out |

The "just hire a nanny for 2.8–4k" version of this on the forums leaves out the foreign-baby levy, and it ignores that you'll be running the [birth registration and DP sprint](/guides/newborn-pass-birth-registration-singapore) in the same fortnight. Put the confinement booking on the same board as LifeSG registration and the embassy passport slot, because they are all competing for the same exhausted week.

## A sequence that holds up (third trimester to week 6)

1. Decide between delivering here and going home, with unsubsidised foreigner billing in the picture ([maternity care](/guides/maternity-care-singapore))
2. If you're staying, shortlist agencies and confirm Malaysian Work Permit eligibility at the same time, then apply as early as MOM allows
3. Parallel-path the helper or infant-care plan for month 2 and beyond ([preschool and childcare](/guides/preschool-childcare-singapore))
4. After the birth: LifeSG registration, then the passport, then the employer lodges the DP. Don't let confinement logistics eclipse the immigration clock
5. Book paediatric follow-ups on cash-pay terms if that's your situation ([finding a paediatrician](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore))

## What this guide doesn't cover

The full MDW hiring stack is a separate guide. Baby Bonus and CDA cash are gated on having an SC child, which is [mixed-status Baby Bonus](/guides/baby-bonus-cda-mixed-status). Clinical obstetric advice comes from your OB, not from us.

Postpartum help is a product you can buy, and buying it well changes how the first month feels more than almost anything else you'll spend money on. The immigration and levy rules around it are not optional extras on the brochure.
