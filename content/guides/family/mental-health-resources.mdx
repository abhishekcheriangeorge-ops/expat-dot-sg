Relocation stress, trailing-partner isolation, academic pressure on teens, and high-performance work cultures are common — and discussable. The forum pattern is also clear: many **employer outpatient plans exclude psychology / counselling**, so families discover the gap only after they need care.

## Crisis first

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call emergency services or go to a hospital emergency department. For suicidal crisis, Samaritans of Singapore operates **1767** (24/7). IMH provides 24-hour emergency psychiatric assessment.

For broader mental-health support (not only crisis), Singapore’s **national mindline 1771** offers 24/7 phone, WhatsApp (**6669 1771**), and webchat via mindline.sg. Youth aged **16–30** living in Singapore can also use [CHAT](https://www.imh.com.sg/CHAT/Get-Help/Pages/default.aspx) for confidential assessments.

Keep your embassy’s consular welfare contacts as a parallel line for nationals abroad.

## Insurance reality for foreigners

| Source | What to verify in writing | Common expat surprise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer group plan | Psychiatry, psychology, counselling line items; annual caps; session limits | “Outpatient covered” still excludes mental health |
| Dependent rider | Whether DP spouses/kids inherit the same outpatient mental-health benefits | Dependents often thinner than employee cover |
| International top-up | Pre-existing waiting periods; outpatient mental-health sub-limits | Expensive plans can still carve out history |
| Public subsidies | Citizen/PR pathways | EP/DP households usually pay private rates |

Community price talk for private sessions often clusters around **S$80–250+** depending on clinician seniority and modality. Treat that as budgeting context, not a quote — always confirm the clinic’s current fees.

Audit the benefits schedule the same week you land — see [health insurance for expats](/guides/health-insurance-expat). If the plan is thin, decide early whether an international top-up is worth it or whether you will self-fund a monthly session budget.

## Finding care without spinning

1. **Employer EAP** — often the fastest first call; check confidentiality and session counts  
2. **GP / family doctor** — useful triage and psychiatrist referral when medication may help  
3. **Private psychologists / counsellors** — match modality (CBT, trauma, couples) to the problem, not the brand  
4. **Home-country teletherapy** — sometimes usable if licensing and your insurer allow cross-border care  
5. **Community / nationality groups** — complements, not substitutes, for clinical care  

Directories and marketplace platforms (community-recommended options change) help filter by budget; still verify credentials and whether your insurer will reimburse.

## First 90 days checklist

- Know **1771**, **1767**, and the nearest hospital ED  
- Confirm EAP access and any mental-health outpatient wording  
- Identify two clinicians (or one clinic + EAP) you would actually call  
- Trailing partners: schedule support before isolation becomes the default — see [trailing partner career](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore)  

Waiting until someone “can’t cope” is a bad KPI. Early, boring support is cheaper than a crisis scramble mid-assignment.
