Leaving preschool isn't a matter of picking a brand. It's the year your family's whole operating system gets set: which system you're in (MOE primary or an international Year 1/2), how much language load your child carries, how far the commute runs, and who covers the 1–3pm cliff. The r/askSingapore P1 and "IS vs local primary" threads fill up every year with families who treated a waitlist as a plan and Mother Tongue as a footnote.

Seat scarcity is its own problem and we've covered it in [school waitlist realism](/guides/school-waitlist-realism). Fee bands and MTL rules on the local side sit in [local schools for expats](/guides/local-schools-for-expats). This guide is about the transition year itself.

## Two systems, two calendars

| Path | What you are actually buying | First-term shocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MOE primary (P1) | An assignment-heavy local system, on non-citizen fee bands | Interest and registration windows; Mother Tongue; CCAs come later |
| International primary (Y1/Y2) | Curriculum continuity for a child who has moved before | Application fees and waitlists; bus contracts; the enrichment upsell |

Don't let the two calendars blur together in your head. An IS deposit doesn't hold an MOE P1 seat, and a P1 balloting story doesn't unlock a Year 1 place at Tanglin or UWC. They are separate queues, with separate clocks and separate people to chase.

## The twelve months before Day 1

| Window | What to finish |
| --- | --- |
| T−12 to T−9 | Shortlist two systems, not six; map where you'd live against real bus and MRT routes |
| T−9 to T−6 | Get the IS applications in; diary the MOE international P1 interest windows |
| T−6 to T−3 | Settle the language strategy: MTL, Chinese enrichment, or English-only at an IS |
| T−3 to T−1 | Lock the after-school care rail; trial the bus; pack the medical, allergy and ADHD files |
| Week 0 | Name one parent as ops lead for forms, WhatsApp groups and early pickups |

Dual-career households come unstuck in the same place every time. Both adults quietly assume the other one owns the 1pm call, and nobody finds out otherwise until a Tuesday in week three. Have the argument early. [Dual-career spouse](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore) and [part-time babysitter and nanny](/guides/part-time-babysitter-nanny-singapore) go through what the options actually cost.

## Mother Tongue, without the panic

In local primary, MTL is a structural part of the week rather than a weekend hobby, and for a lot of EP and DP kids it is the hardest single thing about the first year. If you're going MOE primary with little or no Chinese at home, budget for tutoring at the start instead of after the first bad result — [Mandarin enrichment](/guides/mandarin-enrichment-expat-kids-singapore) covers what that looks like. Going international doesn't make language politics disappear either. It just moves them into optional Chinese tracks and playground code-switching.

## The after-school cliff

This is the real burnout trigger, and it arrives in fairly predictable shapes.

| Pattern | Why it breaks |
| --- | --- |
| "School ends at 1, we'll figure it out" | Both parents are still in meetings |
| Three enrichment trials stacked into week one | An exhausted kid, and a lot of Grab fares |
| Helper starting after term starts | Nobody covers the bridge |
| Ignoring heat and haze PE cancellations | Half-days land with no notice |

Keep enrichment light for the first six weeks. [Kids activities and holiday camps](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps) will still be there next term, and a child who is coping is worth more than a full calendar. Treat care continuity as infrastructure, not a treat.

## SEN and medical handoffs

If your child already has learning support, an allergy action plan, or ADHD medication, get **local letters in hand before Day 1**. Overseas PDFs on their own rarely unlock IS learning support or MOE school processes, so start that paperwork while you're still deciding on schools rather than after term begins. [Special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support) has the detail.

## Where it usually goes wrong

- Paying two IS application fees and calling that a P1 strategy
- Choosing a school 45 minutes from the office because friends go there
- Ignoring MTL until the first failed spelling test
- Starting the helper or babysitter search in week two of term
- Treating AEIS as a casual backup without reading the timelines in [AEIS/SAEIS](/guides/aeis-saeis-international-students)

Primary transition is a project, not a brand decision. Settle the system, the language, the commute and the care before anyone starts arguing about uniforms.
