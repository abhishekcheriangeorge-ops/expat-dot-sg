International-school fees buy you a curriculum. They don't automatically buy you an admissions operations team, and the gap between the two is what the r/askSingapore secondary threads keep circling. Parents want to know whether HCIS, SJII, Dulwich or UWC counselling will actually get UCAS and Common App filed on time, and what happens to all of that if the family gets posted somewhere else mid-DP.

Curriculum choice comes first, and that's in [IB versus A-Level pathways](/guides/secondary-ib-alevel-pathways-singapore). What campus counselling offices are really like is in [university counselling](/guides/university-counselling-international-schools-singapore). This guide is the layer underneath both: the operating system a mobile EP or DP household runs the whole application on.

## Know which rail you're on

The destinations run on genuinely separate rails, and each one wants different work from you. Don't try to make one set of materials serve all of them.

| Destination | Primary rail | Parent ops to own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK | UCAS | Predicted grades, reference, personal statement drafts, Oxbridge/medicine early deadlines |
| US / many liberal-arts | Common App (+ Coalition where used) | Essays, activities list, counsellor/teacher recs, testing plan |
| Australia / NZ | Often direct / agent portals | Prerequisite subjects; IB translation quirks |
| Singapore local unis | Separate from UCAS | Citizenship/PR pathways dominate. See [MOE post-secondary](https://www.moe.gov.sg/post-secondary/admissions) |

## A calendar that survives a posting

| Window | What must already be true |
| --- | --- |
| DP1 / Year 12 start | A named counsellor contact, and subject choices locked for the target countries |
| Testing year | SAT/ACT/IELTS dates booked around family travel, not parked "after mocks" indefinitely |
| Early autumn (UK) | Personal statement drafts **before** the Oxbridge and medicine cutoffs |
| Common App season | An activities CV that travels with the student; recommenders warned 6+ weeks out |
| Results / clearing | Someone in-country who can reply fast if offers land |

The way mobile families come unstuck is depressingly mundane. The only person who knows the portal password turns out to be a counsellor on leave during the exact month you're back home, and nothing moves until they're reachable again.

## When you move mid-posting

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Predicted grades reset | Ask both schools, **in writing**, who owns the UCAS reference |
| Teacher recommenders evaporate | Line up two adults who have taught the student recently, before you exit |
| CCA story breaks | Keep a dated activity log the parents control, not only the school portal |
| Curriculum switch (IB ↔ A-Level) | Re-validate university prerequisites before you resign a seat |

Treat counselling continuity the way you'd treat a SEN file transfer: same paper trail, same insistence on a written handover rather than a friendly verbal one. It's the discipline we ask for in [school waitlists](/guides/school-waitlist-realism), applied to a different office.

## What to ask the counselling office

1. What's the counsellor caseload, and who actually writes UK references as opposed to US recommendations?
2. Where did the last three years' leavers go, by country counts rather than the logo wall in reception?
3. Are predicted grades set by a committee or by the individual teacher?
4. How do you handle mid-year leavers for UCAS and Common App?
5. What testing support is there for SAT dates and fee codes, or is that ours to figure out?

Take that list with you on the tour: [UWCSEA Dover](/schools/uwcsea-dover), [Hwa Chong International](/schools/hwa-chong-international), [Dulwich](/schools/dulwich-college-singapore), [ACS International](/schools/acs-international), [Tanglin Trust](/schools/tanglin-trust-school).

## The household load

Application season is a second unpaid job, and someone in the house is going to end up doing it. In a dual-career home, say out loud which parent owns the portals and which owns testing logistics, so it's assigned rather than assumed. Then budget for EAP support and for sleep, because essay season and EP travel will collide ([parental burnout and EAP](/guides/parental-burnout-eap-singapore)). If a teenager is genuinely struggling, what helps is the clinical map in [mental health resources](/guides/mental-health-resources), not another essay tutor.

## What usually goes wrong

It's the same short list every year. Families start UCAS the week the school's internal deadline hits. They assume IB alone covers US testing expectations. A mid-contract move orphans the reference, and nobody notices until the reference is due. Schools get chosen on brochure logos instead of country-level outcome counts. And a parent rewrites the personal statement until it sounds like LinkedIn.

University applications are a project, and they respond well to being run like one. Tour the ops, not only the campus café.
