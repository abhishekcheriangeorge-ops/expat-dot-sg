One international-school fee is a lifestyle decision. Two or three is a household finance product, and it behaves like one. Parents on r/askSingapore keep arriving at the same discovery: "sibling discount" is rarely the clean brochure percentage you could underwrite a lease on, and "bursary" usually means something a lot narrower than the university-scholarship version of the word.

School choice itself still starts with our [landscape guide](/guides/international-schools-landscape) and [waitlist realism](/guides/school-waitlist-realism). This one is the layer underneath, about how fees stack and what assistance actually exists when you're an EP or DP household with more than one child in the system.

## What "sibling discount" usually means

| Claim you hear | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| "Second child is X% off" | Ask which fee lines it touches: tuition only, or the capital levy, bus, meals and CCA too |
| "Automatic if both are enrolled" | Confirm it needs the same campus and the same contract year, and ask whether a gap year resets it |
| "Third child is almost free" | Rare. Treat it as marketing until finance emails you a worked example |
| "Sibling priority means fee help" | **Priority on a waitlist is not cash relief**, and the two get conflated constantly |

Ask for a written fee schedule and sibling policy dated for the year you'll actually start. Last year's PDF circulating in a Facebook group is not a quote, and fee schedules move enough year to year that the difference is real money.

MOE’s public framing for [international schools](https://www.moe.gov.sg/international-students) is orientation, not a fee regulator for private IS tuition. Your contract is with the school (and sometimes an employer education benefit clause).
</OfficialSource>

## Corporate fee assistance is not a school bursary

These four things get discussed as if they're interchangeable, and they aren't. Each fails in its own particular way.

| Source | What it is | How it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employer education benefit | A cap per child or per household, written into the offer letter | The cap was drafted with one child in mind, and the second becomes your problem |
| School bursary or hardship fund | Means-tested or talent-linked, usually with limited seats | Expat families assume it's automatic. It generally isn't |
| Scholarship language | Merit, sport or arts awards | Easy to mistake for a fee waiver when you're new and reading fast |
| Relocation "school support" | A time-boxed subsidy, usually year one | Ends partway through the posting while fees keep rising |

Decode what your package actually promises before you shortlist three premium campuses, using [salary package decoding](/guides/salary-package-decoding). The household-level stacking belongs in [cost of living by household](/guides/cost-of-living-by-household).

## Planning that survives the sticker shock

Model the all-in figure for each child separately first: tuition, capital or building levy, bus, uniform, mandatory trips. Apply the sibling rules last, not first. Doing it the other way round produces a number that feels manageable and then isn't.

Then ask the finance office the only question that settles it: show me the sibling discount on a sample invoice for two children in the same year. A school that can do this in an afternoon is telling you something; a school that can't is telling you something too.

A few household-specific threads to pull on. If SEN or learning support is in the picture, fee assistance and support capacity are two separate conversations and should be held separately — see [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support). Preschool years burn real money before any "big school" sibling rule applies, which is covered in [preschool and childcare](/guides/preschool-childcare-singapore) and in the [handoff into school years](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore). And if you're a dual-career household, name out loud which parent's benefits letter is actually paying the fees, because assuming it's the other one is a common and expensive mistake: [dual-career couples](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore).

## Send these to admissions and HR in the same week

- Is sibling relief tuition-only?
- Does a mid-year join or a campus transfer keep the discount?
- What happens if one sibling leaves, boards, or takes a year out?
- Does employer reimbursement require original invoices in a named parent's name?
- Are bursary applications open to work-pass families, or are they weighted towards citizens and PRs?

If what comes back is a warm "we're a family-friendly school," you don't have a budget yet. You have a sentiment.
