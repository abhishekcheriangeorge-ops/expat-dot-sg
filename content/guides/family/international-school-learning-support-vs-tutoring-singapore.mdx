When a child's grades wobble, the reflex answer on r/askSingapore is "get a tutor." International-school learning support isn't that. It's scheduled pull-outs, strategies the classroom teacher actually uses, and sometimes an IEP-like plan. What it is not is unlimited 1:1 homework rescue. Dual-career households burn a lot of money buying learning support and tutoring at the same time without ever writing down what either one is meant to achieve.

School shortlists still start with the [landscape](/guides/international-schools-landscape). If you're heading down a deeper SEN pathway, that's [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support). This guide is narrower: it's about the cash decision between support and tutoring.

## Two products, two invoices

These get conflated constantly, mostly because they all turn up as an invoice with your child's name on it. They're buying you quite different things.

| Product | What you're buying | Where it stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-school learning support | Screening, strategies, small-group or pull-out sessions, teacher collaboration | Hours are capped and waitlists are real. It isn't a private tutor on the school's payroll |
| Private tutoring | 1:1 content rescue, exam technique, confidence | Quality varies enormously, and a good tutor can quietly mask a school support that never arrived |
| External ed psych / ST/OT | Diagnosis and therapy | Cash for most EP families, and the school may or may not act on the report you paid for |
| Enrichment centres | Subject drills, the maths chains and so on | Easy to mistake for learning support. It isn't |

## Which one to reach for first

The honest test is whether the problem lives in the classroom or in the content. If your child can't access the lesson itself, an evening tutor doesn't fix that, because the school day that produced the gap simply happens again tomorrow.

| Signal | School support first | Add private tutoring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher flags processing or attention in class | Yes, and ask for the observation notes in writing | A tutor alone rarely fixes classroom access |
| Content gaps after a curriculum switch | After the placement conversation | A short tutoring block with a date on it |
| Exam technique only, in an IB or IGCSE year | Ask whether the school runs revision clinics | Targeted subject tutor, with an end date |
| You're heading towards a formal diagnosis | School plus a qualified clinician | A tutor is not a diagnostician |

If medication continuity is part of the picture, that's its own set of logistics: [kids ADHD medication](/guides/kids-adhd-medication-singapore). And language enrichment is a separate invoice again, which is worth knowing before you commit the same evenings twice over ([Mandarin enrichment](/guides/mandarin-enrichment-expat-kids-singapore)).

## Questions that prevent invoice regret

Ask these before you sign anything, and ask them of the learning-support lead rather than the admissions officer who showed you round.

1. How many learning-support hours a week are already included in the fees?
2. What triggers an IEP-like plan, and who sits in that meeting?
3. Will the school read and act on an external ed-psych report?
4. What's the tutoring goal, in one sentence, and what's **the stop date**?
5. Who owns the progress review: the form tutor, the learning-support lead, or you?

Question four is the one people skip, and it's the one that costs the most.

## Keeping the bill honest

One tutor per subject, unless you've written down why this case is different. No open-ended "until grades improve" retainers, because nobody ever cancels those. Remember that CCA and enrichment load competes for the same sleep your child needs to make any of this stick ([kids activities](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps)). And in a dual-career household, name which adult owns the weekly progress call, or it quietly becomes nobody's ([dual-career spouse](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore)).

One last thing for dual-career parents. Once the learning-support referral is actually filed, the job changes from "buy another tutor" to managing a waitlist: one named person chasing it, an interim cap on what you're spending, and a review date you don't move ([LS waitlist ops](/guides/international-schools-landscape)).
