The r/askSingapore threads about finding a job on a Dependant's Pass all circle the same expensive mistake, and it's a household one rather than a legal one: the trailing partner's income goes into the relocation spreadsheet as month-two cash. Since the 2021 reset, dual-career here is a project you plan for. It isn't a perk that comes attached to the DP.

This guide takes the household view. For the pass mechanics and the LOC edge cases in depth, go to [trailing partner careers](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore).

## The four things people keep getting wrong

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "DP means she can work while we settle" | A Dependant's Pass gives stay rights, and that's all it gives. Local employment generally needs the spouse's own EP, S Pass, or Work Permit |
| "HR said LOC is fine" | General employment LOC for EP-sponsored DPs ended in the 2021 reset. What's left is mainly for eligible business owners |
| "Being married to an EP holder helps COMPASS" | It doesn't. Spouse status is no shortcut around salary, quota, or COMPASS for the hire |
| "Remote job for US HQ is always fine" | There's a published MOM FAQ path for overseas-employer remote work. Get it in writing for nervous HR, then check the IRAS side |

The thread running through all four: the pass that lets your family live here and the pass that lets a person work here are assessed separately, and being married to the sponsored partner doesn't bridge them.

## The legal routes, and what each one costs you

| Goal | Usual path | Household implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local employment at a Singapore entity | An employer sponsors an EP or S Pass, and the candidate has to qualify on their own merits | The DP is cancelled once the EP or S Pass issues, so the spouse becomes independently sponsored |
| Sector roles under Work Permit quotas | DP-WP (Dependant's Pass Work Permit) where eligible | Validity is tied to the DP, and levy and quota still apply. Confirm sector fit before you celebrate |
| Run an ACRA business on the DP | LOC for DP business owners, if the shareholding and structure rules are met | Setup cost, then local-hire tests at renewal. This isn't a casual side hustle |
| Unpaid charitable volunteering | Often no work pass at all, if it is genuinely unpaid and genuinely charitable | Still not a loophole for an "unpaid internship" at a for-profit |

Trailing husbands hit exactly the same legal stack. The forums exaggerate how scarce the *roles* are, not how scarce the *rules* are.

## Budget as though it's a single-income year

Price the first 6–12 months on one salary plus reserves. That means rent and utilities carried by one income, unsubsidised [preschool and childcare](/guides/preschool-childcare-singapore) if you had both planned to be working, and a line for a helper or backup care in case the search drags on. Career coaching or agency fees are worth negotiating into the main package before wheels-up, while you still have leverage.

Ask the sponsored partner's employer for dual-career support **in writing**. A posting that collapses halfway through costs them far more than a coaching retainer does, and that is the argument to make rather than a personal appeal. The levers are in [salary package decoding](/guides/salary-package-decoding).

## What the search actually looks like

Senior and scarce-skill roles clear EP sponsorship far more often than junior generalist ones. That's unwelcome news if the plan was a deliberate step down while the children settle, and it's worth knowing before you start applying rather than after.

Retail, hospitality and a lot of admin seats are local-talent deep, and DP status doesn't help there at all. "My overseas employer has a Singapore branch" is the line that misleads people most often; it usually still means that entity has to sponsor a pass.

For warm introductions, school Parents' Associations, chambers and nationality networks tend to outperform cold LinkedIn on its own.

## Belonging is not the soft part of this plan

Underemployment without a social structure is a wellbeing risk, not a lifestyle preference. The trailing-spouse advice that ages well is fairly consistent. Get onto recurring association calendars ([ANZA](/clubs/anza), [AWA](/clubs/awa), nationality groups) before writing any country-club cheques, which is what [our ANZA and AWA guide](/guides/anza-awa-associations) walks through. Volunteer with the school PA once the children are placed. Pick up hobby regulars from [find my people](/guides/find-my-people-singapore). And if your identity was career-primary, put mental-health check-ins in the calendar deliberately instead of waiting until you need them — [mental health resources](/guides/mental-health-resources) has the options.

## The first 90 days

1. Confirm with the counsel or HR who filed the main pass whether you're on a DP or an LTVP, and get the work rights in writing.
2. Freeze the dual-income assumption in the budget until an IPA or equivalent actually exists.
3. Target employers who file EP and S Pass applications routinely, and lead with scarce skills rather than "trailing spouse".
4. If you're founding something, validate ACRA and LOC eligibility before you sign Singapore clients.
5. Put belonging activities in the weekly calendar so the search doesn't quietly turn into isolation.

A dual-career posting survives when the household plans for the underemployed version first. Then the second pass, when it lands, is good news rather than a rescue.
