If your child needs learning or developmental support, treat school selection as a **clinical compatibility problem**, not a branding exercise. Forum consensus for foreign families is blunt: Singapore’s subsidised early-intervention stack is built for **citizens and PRs** — plan private capacity and school offers **before** you resign overseas.

## The EIPIC eligibility wall

ECDA’s Early Intervention Programme for Infants and Children (**EIPIC**) centre-based programmes are for children who are:

- From birth to six years old  
- **Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents**  
- Assessed by a paediatrician as needing medium-to-high early intervention  
- Not already in conflicting MSF/ECDA-funded EI programmes  

Foreign children who are not PRs are directed to **private** early-intervention providers. That is not a paperwork quirk — it is the budget model.

## Cost reality for EP / DP households

| Cost centre | What forums and providers repeatedly flag | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private OT / speech / behavioural therapy | Often ~**S$150–250+/hour** community ballpark | Budget weekly sessions, not one assessment |
| Developmental paediatric reviews | Private specialist fees stack quickly | Start assessments early; keep portable reports |
| International school learning support | Fees + possible pathway surcharges | Confirm in writing before deposit |
| Local SPED (foreigner places) | Limited slots; high fees; long queues | Do not assume Pathlight-style options |

Employer medical plans frequently **exclude** ongoing therapy for dependents. Audit the schedule — see [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) — and assume self-pay unless HR proves otherwise.

## International schools: ask hard questions

Dover Court and Singapore American School appear often in SEN threads because of inclusion reputation — and because **support places are capacity-constrained**. Waitlists measured in many months are reported; treat “we’ll figure it out after we land” as a relocation risk.

Questions for every admissions team:

- Learning-support staffing ratios and who delivers pull-out vs in-class support  
- Whether they accept your child’s profile **this year**, with named pathway  
- Assessment partnerships and required psychologist / paediatric reports  
- Classroom aide policies and who pays  
- Exam access arrangements  
- Whether similar profiles have exited successfully to the next stage  
- Pass types accepted (e.g. some schools cannot enrol Student’s Pass holders)

Singapore Citizens generally need an **MOE exemption** to attend foreign-system schools; that is a separate process from foreign Dependant Pass admissions. Schools may support an exemption application — approval is MOE’s discretion.

## Parallel tracks before the plane

1. Obtain current assessments and recommendations in a portable format  
2. Shortlist 2–3 schools and get **written** SEN capacity answers  
3. Price private therapy for the first six months assuming no subsidy  
4. Align housing with school commute and therapy locations  
5. Keep a clinical + school document pack for every renewal and move
