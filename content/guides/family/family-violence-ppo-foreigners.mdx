On forums, foreign spouses and DP or LTVP holders often put off calling for help because "my pass is under his or her name." It's an understandable calculation and it's the wrong one. **Safety is not a Singpass form.** The police, NAVH and the Personal Protection Order system exist for people who are in Singapore, regardless of what a Reddit thread predicts about the sponsor cancelling everything tomorrow. Pass strategy comes after you are safe, with counsel and, where you need it, proper ICA or MOM advice.

Safety planning collides with childcare logistics more often than people expect. If you have kids, line up a trusted coverage adult from the [sick-child coverage matrix](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital) while you're seeking help. That is not a substitute for emergency services.

This is crisis orientation, not legal advice.

## If you need help right now

| If this is what's happening | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| You're in danger now | Call **999**, or SMS 70999 |
| Violence or abuse, or you aren't sure what to call it | **NAVH 1800-777-0000**, which runs 24h, or report to MSF online |
| You've been injured | Get a medical exam and keep the records. They matter later for a PPO |
| You want help getting a protection order | The Family Justice Courts, or a Protection Specialist Centre: PAVE, TRANS SAFE, or Care Corner Project StART |

## What a PPO is, and what it isn't

A Personal Protection Order restrains a family member from committing family violence against you, or against other family members, where the court is satisfied that an order is necessary. It often travels with related orders: an Expedited Order, domestic exclusion, stay-away, no-contact. MSF and Family Assist publish the full lists.

The distinction worth understanding in the moment is between the PPO itself and an Expedited Order. A PPO is the longer protective order that comes out of the court process. An Expedited Order is faster interim protection while the PPO application is still pending, and it's the one to ask for when the threat is ongoing rather than historical. Domestic exclusion and stay-away orders are the housing and proximity tools, and they get harder to obtain when the respondent has nowhere else to go.

On who counts as family for filing purposes, Judiciary guidance covers spouses and former spouses, parents, siblings, in-laws, children including step and adopted children, and other relatives the court regards as family. **Unmarried partners sit outside that standard table.** It's a common miss on Reddit and it lands hardest on cohabiting couples. If that describes you, ask counsel or a Protection Specialist Centre about alternative safety rails rather than assuming there's nothing.

A PPO is not an immigration pass. It does not automatically create a new LTVP, whatever anyone tells you. What it does create is a court record and a set of safety tools while you work through housing, children and status with specialists and lawyers. PPO hearings and divorce filings run on separate tracks, and one does not replace the other.

### If a PPO is filed against you

You are the respondent. Attend every mention, because default orders happen to people who stop showing up, and they're far harder to undo than to avoid. [Judiciary — respond to a PPO application](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/family/respond-personal-protection-order-application) sets out the process. Pass-renewal spite and PPO filings collide constantly in forum threads; both belong with counsel rather than in a WhatsApp group.

## Pass dependency: plan, but don't freeze

The pass fear is real, and it's also the thing most likely to keep someone in place. It's worth naming each version of it plainly.

If you're on a DP riding on a spouse's EP, the trap is the sponsor losing their job, or cancelling the pass out of spite. The conversation to have with counsel or a PSC is about a new pass and about STVP timing, and [between jobs on an EP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore) explains how that bridge actually works.

If you're on an LTVP sponsored by a citizen or PR spouse, the trap is fear of withdrawal or non-renewal. That's an ICA reality question, and it runs alongside the shelter and legal-aid path rather than instead of it. Start with [LTVP](/guides/ltvp-singapore).

If you're on a visit pass, or waiting on something still pending, the trap is overstay panic. Don't overstay. Use the published extension and departure rails instead: [visit-pass extension](/guides/short-term-visit-pass-extension).

Immigration officers and Protection Specialist Centres have seen sponsor-abuse patterns before; yours will not be the first. AWARE, the shelters and family lawyers routinely work with foreign spouses who were told "you will be deported if you report this." Treat that line as what it is, which is control rather than law, and check it with a professional.

Children and cross-border flight risk sit on the divorce and Hague guide, [divorce and custody](/guides/divorce-custody-foreigners-singapore). Workplace harassment is an entirely separate rail and goes to [TAFEP](/guides/workplace-harassment-tafep-ep).

## If you're the neighbour who can hear it

Call 999 when someone may be in danger. Otherwise NAVH. What you should not do is file it away as an MCST noise complaint, which is a different problem with a different answer: [condo noise guide](/guides/condo-noise-mcst-neighbours).

## A checklist worth having before you need it

1. Save 999, 1800-777-0000 and a PSC number somewhere you can reach offline.
2. Keep passports, FIN and pass cards, cash, medication and your children's documents somewhere you can pick up in one go.
3. Tell one person you trust what your plan is.
4. Apply for a PPO with PSC support if that's what you're advised to do.
5. Only then sequence the pass, the housing and any divorce filing with counsel.

Arrange the mental-health follow-up too, rather than leaving it for later: [resources](/guides/mental-health-resources) and [emergencies and mindline](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies).
