The argument in most expat households isn't really about whether Mandarin matters. It's about Tuesday at 8pm, when English comprehension, a Mother Tongue worksheet and a science journal all land on the same dining table at the same time. Parents in MOE primary and in bilingual international tracks describe the identical spiral on r/askSingapore every term: the child melts down, a parent starts Googling tutors at midnight, enrichment brands multiply, and everyone's sleep quietly disappears.

We keep the enrichment product maps in [Mandarin enrichment](/guides/mandarin-enrichment-expat-kids-singapore), and the local-system fee and MTL framing in [local schools for expats](/guides/local-schools-for-expats). This guide is the nightly operations layer: what the week actually looks like, and how to stop it eating the house.

## Two systems, two homework shapes

In MOE primary, the evening load is English plus Mother Tongue plus everything else, with a spelling and composition cadence that doesn't really let up. The trap for expat parents is assuming that "school Chinese" adds up to fluency at home. It generally doesn't, and the gap surfaces in a mid-year report rather than in the nightly worksheets, which is why it arrives as a surprise.

In international primary, the load is English-heavy and the Chinese homework is often optional and varies a great deal by campus. The trap runs the other way here: buying MTL-intensity tutoring as though your child were sitting PSLE, when nobody at the school is asking for anything close to that.

## Rules that survive two working parents

If your household is weak in the Mother Tongue, run one language block per evening and no more. Two-hour bilingual marathons don't build fluency, they teach avoidance, and the avoidance outlasts the vocabulary by years.

Split the roles honestly. The parent coaches process, which means showing up, sitting down and finishing, while a tutor owns content when neither parent speaks the language. What happens otherwise is a shame spiral, with a non-Chinese-speaking parent attempting to teach characters at 9pm and both of them ending the evening feeling worse than they started.

Cap enrichment at **one MTL product per term**. Stacking Berries plus a tutor plus two apps plus a worksheet pack isn't a strategy, it's an admission that nobody has decided what this particular child actually needs.

Protect sleep ahead of extra drill before a test. An exhausted child fails the next day's English as well, which is how a Chinese problem quietly turns into a general one.

And name the adult who owns homework nights in advance, out loud. Dual-career households drift into whoever-got-home-first, and that's exactly how nights get missed: [dual-career spouse](/guides/dual-career-spouse-singapore).

Grandparents can genuinely help by sitting with readers, as long as the house rules are agreed before they arrive rather than negotiated afterwards. See [grandparents long-stay caregiving](/guides/grandparents-long-stay-caregiving-singapore). Babysitters are for pickup and logistics, not Mother Tongue teaching, which is a different hire entirely: [part-time babysitter](/guides/part-time-babysitter-nanny-singapore).

## Tutor, enrichment, or you

| Option | Best for | Where it goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School plus light home reading | Mild gaps, with strong school support | Ignoring MTL until the mid-year report lands |
| Small-group enrichment | Social contact and a routine | Brand hopping every term |
| 1:1 tutor | Targeted composition or oral work | The tutor becomes the only relationship and parents check out |
| Apps only | Drill between human sessions | Fake progress metrics with no writing output |

If learning support or attention issues are sitting underneath the language fight, open [special needs support](/guides/special-needs-support) before you buy another worksheet pack. More drill on top of an undiagnosed problem only makes the evenings longer.

## A weekly template for a weak-MTL home

1. Mon/Thu: a 25–40 min MTL block, oral work and reading aloud.
2. Tue: English reading and comprehension only. Nothing stacked on top.
3. Wed: catch up on school worksheets, with no new brand added.
4. Fri: light oral games, stopping before the meltdown rather than after it.
5. Weekend: one cultural input — a show, the library, a call with a grandparent — rather than a three-hour tuition double.

Then guard that shape against holiday camps and extras, which expand to fill whatever term plan you've built: [kids activities](/guides/kids-activities-holiday-camps).

## What usually fails

- Treating enrichment logos as a substitute for sleep.
- Non-speaker parents running character drills after a day of overtime.
- Switching schools mid-year purely to escape Chinese, without first reading the new campus's Chinese track.
- Parallel English tuition, MTL tuition and piano, all on school nights.
- Waiting for the first red report before hiring any help at all.

Bilingual homework is a calendar design problem before it's a shopping problem. Design the week first, then buy the smallest thing that fits inside it.
