**Short answer:** Most expats on employer plans should register a **private panel GP** in week one, use **995 / A&E** only for true emergencies, and treat polyclinics as optional — foreigners usually pay **unsubsidised** public rates. Know your insurer’s hospital list and how to request a **Letter of Guarantee** before any planned admission.

New drivers and stress-related headaches show up in GP queues during licence season — see [teen driving independence](/guides/teen-driving-independence-singapore).
Singapore mixes public institutions and a large private sector. Citizens and PRs see heavier subsidy logic in polyclinics and public hospitals; EP households live in panel networks. Knowing which door to open at 10pm matters more than memorising hospital brands. If the injury happened **at work**, open a WICA file the same day — [work injury compensation](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep) — not only an insurance claim.

## Day-to-day primary care

| Option | Fit for many EP families | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private GP (panel clinic) | Speed, English-first, insurance direct billing | Confirm panel list before you attach emotionally |
| Polyclinic | Fine for vaccines / routine when queues work | Foreigners often pay **unsubsidised** rates close to private GP — compare cash before “saving” |
| Telehealth | Simple scripts and MC where allowed | Not for red-flag symptoms |

Register a nearby GP in week one. Keep vaccination cards and chronic meds lists in your phone. For kids, see [finding a pediatrician](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore).

## A&E vs urgent care vs wait-until-morning

| Situation | Door |
| --- | --- |
| Life-threatening | **995** / nearest appropriate A&E (know public vs private routing your insurer prefers) |
| After-hours but stable | 24h clinics / private hospital urgent care if covered |
| Ordinary fever/cough daytime | GP, not A&E |

Insurance panels shape behaviour: a “preferred hospital” list is worthless if you have never opened the app. Save the insurer hotline and pre-authorisation rules for admissions. Dial guide: [995 vs 999 emergencies](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies).

## Hospital deposits and Letters of Guarantee (LOG)

Foreigners without MediSave often face a large **admission deposit** unless the insurer issues a **Letter of Guarantee** to the hospital first. Reddit LOG threads fail when:

- HR / broker is asked **after** the admission desk quote  
- The LOG cap is far below the procedure estimate (or ward upgrade triggers pro-ration)  
- The letter’s ward class / Medisave-first wording is misread as “insurer pays nothing”  

Ask the hospital financial counsellor for a written estimate, then ask the insurer for an LOG matching that hospital and procedure **before** elective admission. Deposits are usually offset or refunded against the final bill — but cashflow still matters.

## Hospitals

Private hospitals (e.g. Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles, Raffles Hospital) offer speed and familiarity for many expats. Public clusters are excellent clinically and may matter for complex care or when coverage points you there. Tour the nearest A&E driveway once — jet-lagged night emergencies are bad times to navigate GPS.

## Cost realism without subsidies

Foreigners without citizen/PR subsidies should assume **private GP visits and unsubsidised public bills** can surprise. Pair this guide with [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) before you decline employer cover to “save” on premiums.

Teeth are a separate budget line — corporate plans often carve out dental, and EP holders generally have no Medisave. See [dental care for foreigners](/guides/dental-care-foreigners-singapore).

Bringing ADHD, anxiety, or pain meds from overseas is an **HSA** problem before it is a clinic problem — check controlled-drug approval rules in [bringing personal medication](/guides/bringing-medication-singapore-hsa) before you fly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should expats use a polyclinic or a private GP?

**Default to private panel GP** if your insurance covers it. Use polyclinics when the service (e.g. certain vaccines) fits and you have compared the unsubsidised cash price.

### When is A&E the right door?

Chest pain, severe breathing trouble, major trauma, stroke signs, uncontrolled bleeding — **995 / A&E**. Midnight mild sore throat is a 24h clinic problem.

### What is a Letter of Guarantee?

Insurer paperwork that tells the hospital they will cover (up to a cap). Request it **before** elective admission with a written estimate so you are not wiring a five-figure deposit at 11pm.
