**Short answer:** Register with a private panel GP in your first week, save 995 and A&E for things that are genuinely emergencies, and treat polyclinics as optional — as a foreigner you'll usually pay unsubsidised public rates there anyway. Before any planned admission, know which hospitals your insurer likes and how to ask them for a **Letter of Guarantee**.

If you've got a teenager in licence season, that comes with its own small wave of GP visits for nerves and stress headaches; we cover the family side of that in [teen driving independence](/guides/teen-driving-independence-singapore).

Singapore runs a public system and a large private sector side by side. Citizens and PRs get the heavier subsidy logic in polyclinics and public hospitals, while EP households mostly live inside insurance panel networks. Which means the thing worth learning isn't hospital brand names, it's knowing which door to open at 10pm. One exception to everything below: if the injury happened **at work**, open a WICA file the same day, not just an insurance claim. That's a separate rail with its own deadlines, and we've written it up at [work injury compensation](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep).

## Day-to-day primary care

| Option | What it's good for | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private GP (panel clinic) | Speed, English-first consults, direct billing to your insurer | Check the panel list before you get attached to a clinic |
| Polyclinic | Fine for vaccines and routine care when the queue suits you | Foreigners usually pay unsubsidised rates that land close to private GP cash prices. Compare before you assume the public option is the cheap one |
| Telehealth | Simple scripts and MCs where they're allowed | Not for anything with a red flag attached |

Register with a GP near home in your first week, while you still have the bandwidth to choose rather than the panic to settle. Keep vaccination cards and a list of chronic medications in your phone, because you will be asked for both at the least convenient moment. If you have kids, start with [finding a pediatrician](/guides/finding-pediatrician-singapore).

## A&E, urgent care, or wait until morning

Anything life-threatening, you call 995 or go to the nearest appropriate A&E, and it's worth knowing in advance whether your insurer would rather you landed in a public or a private one. After hours but stable is a 24-hour clinic or private hospital urgent care, assuming you're covered for it. An ordinary daytime fever or cough is a GP problem, and taking it to A&E just means a long wait behind people who are much sicker than you.

Insurance panels shape all of this, and a preferred-hospital list is worthless if you've never opened the app. Save the insurer hotline in your phone now and read the pre-authorisation rules for admissions while nothing is wrong. For which number to actually dial, we've got [995 vs 999](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies).

## Hospital deposits and Letters of Guarantee (LOG)

Foreigners without MediSave are often asked for a large admission deposit unless the insurer has already sent the hospital a Letter of Guarantee. The Reddit LOG horror stories almost always fail in one of three ways. HR or the broker gets asked only after the admission desk has already quoted. The LOG cap sits well below the procedure estimate, or a ward upgrade triggers pro-ration nobody mentioned. Or the letter's ward class and Medisave-first wording gets read as "the insurer is paying nothing," when it means something narrower.

So run it in this order: ask the hospital's financial counsellor for a written estimate, then ask your insurer for an LOG matching that hospital and that procedure, **before** you go in for an elective admission. Deposits are usually offset or refunded against the final bill, so the money isn't lost. The cashflow gap while you wait is still real.

## Hospitals

Private hospitals like Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles and Raffles Hospital are fast and feel familiar to most expats, which is why they're the default. The public clusters are clinically excellent, and they're where you may end up for complex care or simply because your coverage points you there. Whichever you're likely to use, do one small thing while everyone is healthy: drive past the nearest A&E entrance once so you know which driveway it is. Two in the morning, jet-lagged, with someone unwell in the back seat, is a bad time to be arguing with GPS.

## What it actually costs without subsidies

Without citizen or PR subsidies, assume that both private GP visits and unsubsidised public bills will be higher than the number in your head. Read this alongside [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) before you decline employer cover to save on premiums, because that's the decision people regret.

Teeth are their own budget line. Corporate plans often carve dental out entirely, and EP holders generally have no Medisave to fall back on either. See [dental care for foreigners](/guides/dental-care-foreigners-singapore).

And bringing ADHD, anxiety or pain medication from home is an HSA problem before it is a clinic problem. Check the controlled-drug approval rules in [bringing personal medication](/guides/bringing-medication-singapore-hsa) before you fly, not once you've landed with it in your suitcase.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should expats use a polyclinic or a private GP?

If your insurance covers a private panel GP, default to that. Use a polyclinic when the specific service fits, certain vaccines being the obvious case, and you've actually compared the unsubsidised cash price rather than assuming public means cheaper.

### When is A&E the right door?

Chest pain, severe trouble breathing, major trauma, stroke signs, uncontrolled bleeding. That's 995 and A&E, no deliberating. A mild sore throat at midnight is a 24-hour clinic problem.

### What is a Letter of Guarantee?

Paperwork from your insurer telling the hospital they'll cover the bill up to a cap. Ask for it before an elective admission, with the written estimate in hand, so you're not wiring a five-figure deposit at 11pm with a card that may not even have the limit.
