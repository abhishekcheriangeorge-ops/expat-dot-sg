The marriage threads on r/askSingapore split into two failure modes. There are EP couples who assume ROM is a one-click Singpass form and find out it isn't, and there are citizen-plus-foreigner couples who skip the Pre-Marriage LTVP Assessment (PMLA) and only meet the housing and pass friction after the wedding, when their options have narrowed. Civil marriage here is genuinely doable. It's the paperwork that's the project.

Once the paperwork is behind you, the softer landing happens at building level. We've written that up separately in [condo and block parent networks](/guides/condo-block-parent-networks-belonging-singapore), which matters more once kids arrive.

## Who needs whose approval?

| Your status | MOM approval needed before marrying an SC or PR? | What that means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment Pass / EntrePass / S Pass | Generally no, not for ROM itself | You still have to satisfy ROM's own stay and document rules |
| Current or former Work Permit holder | Yes, and the Controller of Work Passes has to approve first | It applies whether you marry in Singapore or overseas, and religious or customary marriages are included |
| Tourist or short-term visitor | No MOM Work Permit approval | If you're marrying an SC and plan to stay long term, ICA's PMLA is strongly encouraged |

Most of the confusion you'll read online comes from Work Permit rules being quoted at EP holders by people who mean well. The two situations are not the same, and the difference is worth checking at source.

## The 31-day stay rule

Where either of you is not a Singapore citizen or PR, one of you must have been physically present in Singapore for **31 continuous days**, not counting the day you arrived, before the notice of marriage can be submitted. The part people miss is that the stretch doesn't have to be the 31 days immediately before you file. A single earlier continuous trip can qualify, so go through old stamps and entry records before you assume you need to sit here for a month waiting.

<OfficialSource>
Published process overview: [Marriage.gov.sg — Overview of Marriage Process](https://www.marriage.gov.sg/Overview_of_Marriage_Process_Oct_2024.pdf). Statutory framing: [Women’s Charter](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/WC1961?ProvIds=P13-) notice/licence provisions.
</OfficialSource>

## Fees and timing, which you should still check live

| Item | Published figure (Oct 2024 overview) |
| --- | --- |
| Application fee when at least one of you is SC or PR | S$42 |
| Application fee when you are both foreigners | **S$380** |
| Notice window | File at least 21 days before solemnisation, and the application is typically valid up to 6 months |

You'll need two witnesses aged 21 or over, with ID, either NRIC or passport. And if one of you has no Singpass, or foreign documents are in the mix, plan for an in-person declaration at ROM rather than the fully remote path your colleagues will describe to you.

## Documents that stall foreigner couples

- Valid passports and immigration passes for the appointment
- Divorce or death certificates if either of you has been married before
- A letter of no impediment, or capacity to marry, from your home authorities where ROM or your embassy asks for one. This is the item with a queue behind it, because it often needs MFA or consulate authentication, or apostille sequencing, before ROM will look at it ([apostille guide](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore))
- Certified English translations for anything not already in English

Getting your home country to recognise a Singapore marriage certificate is a separate step again, handled by your embassy or home registry rather than by ROM. Ask your mission how they treat it before you assume the ROM certificate is the final word for a visa application or a surname change abroad.

## Marrying a Singapore citizen: run PMLA early

If one of you is a citizen and you intend to live here long term after the wedding, ICA strongly encourages the Pre-Marriage Long-Term Visit Pass Assessment before you solemnise. Passing it doesn't guarantee a later LTVP, and nobody pretends otherwise. But skipping the assessment is the recurring regret in these threads, because the foreign spouse's right to stay then rests on an ICA decision taken after the marriage, at the point where walking away from the plan is no longer a real option. The pass itself is covered in [LTVP Singapore](/guides/ltvp-singapore).

The forum heat around [relocating a citizen's foreign spouse](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1qvnbml/relocating_sc_spouse_to_singapore/) lands on much the same sequence:

1. PMLA while you're still overseas, or at least before ROM
2. ROM, once the documents and the 31-day rule are cleared
3. LTVP, often with a PLOC so the foreign spouse can work without needing a separate EP. Check MOM's live PLOC eligibility rather than trusting a thread
4. LTVP+ later, for inpatient subsidy access, which commonly comes after ≥3 years of marriage or an SC child. Pregnancy on its own is not a reliable LTVP+ trigger

Hold off on planning Baby Bonus or leave entitlements until you know whether the child will be a citizen. We cover the split in [Baby Bonus for mixed-status families](/guides/baby-bonus-cda-mixed-status) and [paternity and shared parental leave](/guides/paternity-shared-parental-leave-foreigners).

## After the certificate

1. Download and store the digital copy, keep a physical one, and order spares while you're still in the country
2. Sequence the spouse pass, whether that's a DP, an LTVP or a PLOC, with the main pass holder's employer or with ICA — [Dependant's Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore)
3. Update the banks, the insurance, and the wording of your will and CPF nomination, which we walk through in [wills and CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore)

ROM itself is logistics. The Women's Charter questions, meaning domicile and which country's courts would hear a divorce, are legal strategy, and they're worth paying Singapore family counsel for if either of you might leave soon after marrying here. If separation is already on the table, go straight to [divorce and custody for foreigners](/guides/divorce-custody-foreigners-singapore) rather than crowdsourcing Hague timelines.
