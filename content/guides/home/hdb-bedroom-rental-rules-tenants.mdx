Heartland room rents look wonderful on a spreadsheet, right up until you find out the setup is an unauthorised bedroom rental, a plywood-partitioned living room, or an "owner" who actually lives in JB and turns up on Sundays. HDB's rules are considerably stricter than private-condo roommate culture, and the person who gets caught in the cleanup when HDB revokes approval is you, not the owner.

## Whole flat versus bedroom, side by side

| Question | Whole-flat rental | Bedroom rental |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who may rent out? | Generally Singapore Citizen owners after MOP; PR owners cannot rent out the entire flat | SC or PR owners of 3-room+ flats (verify live, and Plus/Prime nuances exist) |
| Does the owner have to live in? | No, they rent the whole unit out with HDB approval | Yes. Owners **and** authorised occupiers must be in physical, continuous occupation alongside you |
| Your pass | EP, S Pass, WP, Student's, DP, LTVP and so on, with enough validity left (commonly ≥6 months at application, confirm live) | Same eligibility stack |
| Non-citizen quota | The neighbourhood or block quota can block non-Malaysian foreigners | The quota doesn't bite bedroom rentals the same way |
| Minimum lease | Typically ≥6 months, and short-lets are not a loophole | Same minimum-stay culture; there's no "Airbnb month" |

## The continuous-occupation trap

This is the one that quietly breaks tenancies. HDB is explicit that locking a room "for the owner" while the owner actually lives somewhere else, whether that's Malaysia, another flat or a hotel, is not continuous occupation. If the owners stop living in the flat, the bedroom-rental approval can be **revoked automatically**, and what you thought was a legitimate room rental gets treated as an unauthorised whole-flat rental.

That's why the question matters even though it feels rude to ask. The [owner stays but visits weekly](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1mzsjy6/inquiry_about_renting_hdb_with_owner_stay/) threads all follow the same arc. Ask in writing where the owner sleeps most nights, and treat a Sunday drop-in as a red flag rather than a charming quirk.

## Illegal partitions and creative rooms

Living rooms chopped up with plywood and rented as bedrooms are a recurring r/askSingapore nightmare, and the [partition room legality](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1kaidl9/isit_ever_legal_in_singapore_to_rent_out_a_hdb/) threads explain why. Fire and escape requirements plus HDB alteration rules often make the setup unlawful outright. There's usually no proper room key either, so the landlord comes and goes as they please, which is both a quiet-enjoyment problem and a safety one.

The part people underestimate is what happens if it goes to a dispute. A contract that purports to lease an illegal room can fail badly at the Small Claims Tribunals or higher, and illegality arguments cut both ways, including against you. If a large deposit is involved, get advice before you sign rather than after.

The pre-LOI tells are visible on a viewing if you know to look: no window, no real door or lock, a kitchenette carved out of the living room, and more unrelated adults in the flat than the flat type allows.

## What to insist on before you pay anything

The single document that matters is the **HDB approval or registration letter naming you** as an authorised tenant. Owners can apply online and the outcome for bedrooms is often immediate, so "we'll sort it later" is a choice, not a constraint.

Beyond that, ask for a TA clause making the tenancy conditional on valid HDB approval for the whole term, and a written owner-occupation statement if it's a bedroom rental. Hand over pass copies with enough validity left, and don't let anyone invent a tourist bridge to cover a gap; we cover why that fails in [short-term vs long-term](/guides/short-term-vs-long-term-rental). Finally, stamp the TA. "There's no stamp duty on rooms" is a classic scam tell, and the [renting process](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits) guide sets out how stamping actually works.

HDB also notes that tenants can check whether owners listed them as authorised occupants after approval. Use that if the agent starts stalling.

## If you've already moved into a bad setup

Start by documenting it while you're still there. Photograph the layout, the partition, the locks, and who else is sleeping in the flat. Then ask the owner for the current HDB approval PDF, current being the operative word.

If the approval is missing, the owner isn't really living there, or the "bedroom" is a partition, push for written mutual termination with full deposit return. If a salesperson marketed the arrangement to you, a CEA complaint is available and worth making. And if the deposit is simply refused, go to the [SCT and diplomatic-clause guide](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) once you've got your documents together. WhatsApp threats achieve nothing except a worse paper trail.

None of this is legal advice. It's also worth knowing that unauthorised subletting can end with HDB telling occupants to leave on short notice, so budget for a lawful backup stay early rather than assuming you'll have months.
