r/askSingapore rental threads recycle the same shock: “My tenant agent wants half a month again on renewal” and “Do I need an agent at all?” Singapore has **no fixed legal commission rate**. What you owe is whatever you put in a **CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement** — or nothing, if you never appointed anyone and only dealt with the landlord’s agent.

## Two different people on WhatsApp

| Role | Who usually pays | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landlord’s agent | Landlord (market norm) | Lists, views, drafts TA for the owner |
| Your tenant agent | **You**, if you signed an agency agreement | Searches, negotiates, represents you |
| Dual commission | **Illegal** for one agent to collect from both sides of the same deal | Walk away if someone asks you to “top up quietly” |

CEA is explicit: agents cannot collect commission from more than one party in the same transaction. Pay the **agency**, not cash to an individual’s personal PayNow, and get an official receipt.

## Before anyone “helps you find a place”

1. Decide if you need a tenant agent at all — many expats view via PropertyGuru / 99.co and deal with listing agents.  
2. If you want representation, insist on a **CEA prescribed Estate Agency Agreement** (exclusive or non-exclusive) **before** viewings pile up.  
3. Fill in commission amount, GST, and — critically — **renewal / extension** language. Blank fields are a red flag.  
4. Exclusive agreements can make you liable even if you later close “yourself” with a party the agent introduced during the validity window — read the exclusive vs non-exclusive table on CEA’s consumer pages.

Market practice often lands around **half a month to one month** of rent for a successful tenant-side placement, but that is custom, not law. Negotiate.

## The renewal commission trap

Forum heat spikes when year-two renewals arrive with a surprise invoice. Typical failure modes:

- Estate agency agreement already says **½ month per 12 months** (or similar) on renewal — you signed it on day one  
- Agent claims “industry standard” with **no written clause** — push back; CEA says rates are negotiable and must be agreed  
- You renew directly with the landlord to save fees while still bound by a written renewal clause — that is a contract fight, not a CEA morality play  

If you want free renewals, strike the renewal commission line **before** you sign the agency agreement. Do not wait until month 22.

## Stamp duty is not “agent commission”

Lease duty to IRAS is separate. Agents may help stamp and then bill **reimbursement** — CEA notes agents must not collect the stamp fee from clients **before** stamping. Keep the Stamp Certificate. Full rates: [renting process / stamp duty](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits).

## When an agent still earns their fee

- First landing, school radius, and HDB eligibility confusion  
- Multi-offer weekends where LOI hygiene matters  
- Landlords who rewrite TAs away from [CEA templates](https://www.cea.gov.sg/real-estate-professionals/agreements-and-checklists/)  

An agent is not a lawyer and is not your ongoing repair desk after handover — inventory and fair-wear fights live in the TA: [inventory / fair wear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) and [diplomatic clause / deposits](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause).

## Practical checklist

| Do | Don’t |
| --- | --- |
| Verify CEA registration | Pay “friends of friends” unlicensed finders |
| Get commission + renewal in writing | Assume landlord’s agent works for you |
| Pay the agency with a receipt | Dual-pay the same agent “under the table” |
| Stamp the TA within IRAS clocks | Treat stamp duty as optional |
