Open Electricity Market threads on r/askSingapore fall into two piles: people hunting a cheaper kWh, and people who have just hit a promo cliff or an exit fee halfway through a lease. This guide is about the switch itself. It isn't a blackout playbook, and it isn't for the "my bill doubled" argument that follows an estimated meter read.

## Who actually does what

Three parties have a hand in your electricity, and only one of them sets the price.

SP Group owns the network and the meter, opens and closes accounts, and runs a lot of the billing you actually see. They aren't who you're switching away from, which is why a retailer switch doesn't interrupt your supply.

The OEM retailer owns your price plan. The unit rate, the promo terms, the exit fee, and the customer service line you'll be calling when the rate looks wrong are all theirs.

Your landlord only enters the picture if the account stays in their name. Then they pick the plan and you're reimbursing their choice, which is a different problem with a different fix.

## Before you click "switch"

1. Find out **whose name is on the live SP account**. That one answer decides whether the rest of this list applies to you at all.
2. Pull the last three months of kWh, not just the dollar totals. Plans are priced per unit, and comparing dollars against a different tariff structure tells you almost nothing.
3. Compare on the official tool. Ignore the Telegram accounts promising a guaranteed saving.
4. Read four things in the contract before you accept: contract length, promo end date, exit fee, cool-off window. In that order.
5. Screenshot the acceptance page and keep the confirmation email.

If the landlord holds the account, don't try to hop retailers from the tenant side. You can't unwind it at handover and you'll have changed something you don't control. Ask for the itemised PDF bills and a written recharge formula instead.

## How a switch actually runs

You compare and choose on the official OEM tool, matching the plan to the load you really have. Working from home all day with a dehumidifier running is a different shape of usage from an empty flat until seven in the evening, and the plan types are built around that difference. Then you apply to the retailer with whatever account number, address and ID they ask for.

There's a cool-off window, and it exists for exactly the moment you realise you misread the peak bands. Use it rather than sit on it hoping the maths works out.

When the switch completes, read that first bill properly: the unit rate, and any one-off fees hiding underneath it. And if a mid-lease exit is on the cards, read your exit fee before you sign an LOI on the next place, not after.

Sequence your move-in properly and the account will already exist by the time you're shopping retailers, which is the point of [utilities and telecom setup](/guides/utilities-telecom-setup). Fibre delays run on a completely separate rail and won't be fixed by anything on this page, so see the [NetLink TP guide](/guides/fibre-tp-netlink-renters) for those.

## Where this goes wrong for renters

The promo cliff catches the most people. Months one to three look wonderfully cheap, and by month four the rate has quietly drifted back to match or beat what you left.

Peak and off-peak products are the next one. They work beautifully if you're out of the flat all day, and all-day aircon destroys the spreadsheet you built to justify them.

An exit fee on an early lease break is worse than it looks, because it lands on top of whatever the landlord is already claiming from you. We deal with that stack in [early lease termination](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants).

If the flat is being sold, you can end up with two accounts in play at once. Confirm which one is actually live before you pay anybody to "keep the power on".

And do the switch through official channels. Keep the ticket IDs from every call, because the retailer's version of events and yours will eventually differ.

## When staying put is the right answer

Stay on the plan you've got if you're moving in under six months, if the landlord owns the account, or if the exit fee comes to more than a year of the savings you were chasing. Arguments about meter access and people letting themselves in to read it are a landlord and agent problem rather than a pricing one, and [landlord entry and quiet enjoyment](/guides/landlord-entry-quiet-enjoyment) is the page for those.

One local note: the northeast pockets along the CCL and NEL edge, [Kovan](/neighbourhoods/kovan), [Bartley](/neighbourhoods/bartley) and [Lorong Chuan](/neighbourhoods/lorong-chuan) among them, carry a lot of WFH dehumidifier load. Model your own kWh before you chase a peak/off-peak SKU on the strength of a neighbour's screenshot.
