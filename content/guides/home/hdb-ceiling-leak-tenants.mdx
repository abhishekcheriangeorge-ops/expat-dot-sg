The HDB leak threads on r/askSingapore all follow the same arc. Someone showers upstairs, your bedroom ceiling starts dripping, your landlord says he's waiting for HDB, and you spend the next few weeks living around a bucket. The useful thing to know early is that under HDB's guidance a ceiling slab leak is a flat-owner problem. Your job as a tenant is to document it, notify people in writing, and keep steady pressure on your landlord. It isn't to become the unpaid coordinator between the upstairs owner's renovation contractor and the Town Council.

## Work out where the water is actually coming from

Before anyone starts arguing about rent, figure out which of four situations you're in, because each one has a different owner.

The classic case is a worn waterproofing membrane or screed in the floor slab between your flat and the one above — the toilet or kitchen ceiling that drips whenever upstairs runs the shower. HDB treats that as shared work: the upper and lower flat owners arrange the investigation and the repair together, agree on method and cost, then split it. None of that process routes through you. Take photos and video with timestamps, tell your landlord the same day, and ask them to contact the upstairs owner and HDB.

If the water is coming from common property, meaning the corridor, the roof, or a common pipe riser outside your unit, that's Town Council territory. You or your landlord reports it through Town Council channels, and whoever does it needs to keep the case numbers. "We reported it" with no reference number goes nowhere.

A burst or concealed pipe inside your own flat, a failed water heater, aircon condensate or an appliance that overflowed is a different animal again. That one lands on your tenancy agreement's repair clauses, which usually separate minor repairs, the S$150–S$300 cap you've probably signed, from major works the landlord owns. Read that clause before anybody turns up to fix things and invoice you, and photograph the state of it first.

And if it's upstairs renovation, jet-washing or a contractor's temporary mess, it still runs through the owners rather than straight to the contractor. Report it the same day, and if slab damage is suspected, push for HDB to be notified.

HDB's Goodwill Repair Assistance can co-pay 50% of eligible wear-and-tear slab repairs where both owners agree to the work, with the remaining 50% shared equally between them. Be clear about what that scheme is, though. It's **not a tenant subsidy**, and it does nothing for your ruined furniture or for rent you paid on a room you couldn't sleep in.

## Your first 72 hours

1. Contain it. Move electronics clear, bag up soft furnishings, and keep a dry path through the room so you aren't growing mould on top of everything else.
2. Build an evidence pack. Wide shots and close-ups, a short video of the drip actually running, and a note of when upstairs showers if you can tell.
3. Put it in writing the same day. Message your landlord and the listing agent together with the date, the photos, and an explicit request for urgent owner-side coordination with HDB and the upstairs flat. Verbal reports evaporate. A timestamped message doesn't.

**Don't commission waterproofing on the slab yourself** without your landlord's authority. You can end up owning the bill and still not have fixed anything, because the membrane that's failing is on the other side of your ceiling and belongs to somebody else's floor.

Do ask for temporary remedies, in writing: a dehumidifier, rent abatement on a room you can't use, hotel nights if the only toilet is out of action. You're far more likely to get them while the landlord still wants the problem handled quietly.

Forum consensus lines up with the tenancy logic in CEA-style templates here. Structure, ceiling and major water ingress sit with the landlord's duty to keep the place tenantable. Your leverage is documentation and timelines, not shouting at the upstairs tenants, who are quite possibly renting as well.

## When the clock stretches

| Situation | What to do next |
| --- | --- |
| Landlord has gone quiet for days while the drip continues | Resend with a read receipt, copy the agency, and say plainly that you'll escalate to the HDB branch or Town Council as applicable |
| The upstairs owner won't cooperate | Your landlord, not you, is the one who should pull HDB into a mediation or GRA conversation |
| The unit is unliveable for weeks | Negotiate a rent reduction or an early exit under your TA wording. See [the diplomatic clause and deposit guide](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) |
| A deposit fight afterwards over "stains you caused" | Your inventory plus the leak photo timeline beats anyone's memory. Our guide to [fair wear and tear and inventories](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) covers the handover |

The SCT is a last resort for deposit or rent disputes within tribunal limits, and it's worth remembering what it isn't: a waterproofing contractor. It won't get your ceiling dry. If the humidity starts doing secondary damage, run the leak alongside [aircon, mould and pest](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals).

If your block is going into Essential waterproofing works, read [HIP for tenants](/guides/hdb-hip-tenants). One last thing if you have kids. A week of chickenpox isolation is a miserable time to discover a wet bedroom ceiling, so chase the leak down before the school outbreak calendar decides your timing for you. Nobody should be convalescing under an active drip.
