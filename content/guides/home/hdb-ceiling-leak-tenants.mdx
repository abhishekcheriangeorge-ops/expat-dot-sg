r/askSingapore’s HDB leak threads follow one arc: shower upstairs → your bedroom ceiling drips → landlord “waiting for HDB” → you live under a bucket for weeks. Ceiling slab leaks are a **flat-owner** maintenance problem under HDB guidance. As a tenant your job is **document, notify in writing, and keep pressure on your landlord** — not to become the unpaid coordinator between upstairs reno contractors and Town Council.

## Diagnose the source before you argue about rent

| Likely source | Who usually owns the fix | Tenant move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wear-and-tear waterproof membrane / screed in the **floor slab** between flats (classic toilet/kitchen drip when upstairs showers) | **Upper + lower flat owners** jointly arrange investigation and repairs; cost-sharing is the HDB default theme | Photo/video with timestamps; notify your landlord **the same day**; ask them to contact upstairs owner / HDB |
| Leak from **common property** (corridor, roof, common pipe risers outside your unit) | **Town Council** | Landlord / you report via Town Council channels; keep case numbers |
| Burst / concealed pipe **inside your flat**, failed water heater, AC condensate, appliance overflow | Usually **your TA’s repair clauses** (tenant minor-repair cap vs landlord major works) | Read the S$150–S$300 “minor repairs” clause; photograph before anyone “fixes and invoices you” |
| Upstairs reno / jet-wash / temporary contractor mess | Upstairs owner / their contractor — still routed through owners | Same-day report; demand HDB notification if slab damage is suspected |

HDB’s home-care guidance treats slab leaks through the floor between flats as **shared owner responsibility**: owners arrange a contractor together, agree method and cost, then split. HDB’s **Goodwill Repair Assistance (GRA)** can co-pay **50%** of eligible wear-and-tear slab repairs when both owners agree, with the remaining 50% shared equally — it is **not** a tenant subsidy and does **not** automatically cover your damaged furniture or lost rent.

## Renter playbook (first 72 hours)

1. **Contain** — move electronics, bag soft furnishings, keep a dry path for mould control  
2. **Evidence pack** — wide shots + close-ups, short video of active drip, note upstairs shower timing if known  
3. **Written notice** — email/WhatsApp landlord **and** listing agent the same day: date, photos, “request urgent owner-side HDB / upstairs coordination”  
4. **Do not** hire random waterproofing on the slab without landlord authority — you can own the bill and still not fix the membrane upstairs  
5. **Ask for temporary remedies** in writing: dehumidifier hire, room rent abatement, hotel nights if the only toilet is unusable  

Forum consensus matches the TA logic in CEA-style templates: structure, ceiling, and major water ingress sit with the landlord’s duty to keep premises tenantable. Your leverage is documentation + timelines, not shouting at upstairs tenants who may also be renting.

## When the clock stretches

| Situation | Practical next step |
| --- | --- |
| Landlord silent > a few days while drip continues | Re-send with read receipts; copy agency; state you will escalate to HDB branch / Town Council as applicable |
| Upstairs uncooperative | Landlord (not you) should pull HDB into mediation / GRA discussion |
| Unit uninhabitable for weeks | Negotiate rent reduction or early exit under TA wording — see [diplomatic clause / deposit](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) |
| Deposit fight after you leave over “stains you caused” | Inventory + leak photo timeline beats vibes — [fair wear & inventory](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) |

SCT is a last resort for deposit / rent disputes within tribunal limits — not a waterproofing contractor. Pair leak ops with [aircon / mould / pest](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals) if humidity secondary damage starts.
