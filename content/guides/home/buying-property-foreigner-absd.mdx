The r/askSingapore threads on this are blunt, and they're right to be. For most nationalities, buying a Singapore condo as a foreigner isn't a mortgage-maths problem. It's an Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty problem. From 27 April 2023, foreigners generally pay 60% ABSD on any residential purchase, on top of the ordinary Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) that everyone pays. No bank is going to finance that tax for you.

## The rates, as they currently stand

| Buyer profile | ABSD on/after 27 Apr 2023 (residential) |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore Citizen — 1st residential | Not applicable |
| Singapore PR — 1st residential | 5% |
| Foreigner — any residential | **60%** |
| Entities | 65% |

<OfficialSource>
Always re-check [IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer's-stamp-duty-(absd)) before you treat any agent WhatsApp as gospel. Rates and counting rules for “how many properties you already own” are IRAS-defined, not broker folklore.
</OfficialSource>

## Where the cash actually goes

Run it once and the shock makes sense. Take a condo at around S$900k. The 75% loan ceiling means you're finding roughly S$225k of downpayment in cash, which is the part most people have already mentally budgeted for. Then the 60% ABSD arrives at roughly S$540k, and that is **cash, not something a bank will lend against**. On top sit BSD, legal fees, renovation, and the exit risk if your pass ends before your time horizon does. Those figures are illustrative rather than advice, but the shape of them doesn't move.

That arithmetic is why "rent and invest the difference" wins nearly every non-FTA thread. ABSD is doing exactly what it was designed to do. It's a deterrent, not a rounding error you can optimise your way around.

## FTA remissions are narrow, but they're real

Under the published FTA remission rules, certain buyers get Singapore Citizen-equivalent ABSD treatment. That covers nationals of the United States, and nationals or permanent residents of Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland.

In practice that usually means 0% ABSD on a first residential property, with the SC ladder rates applying to anything you buy after that. It remains subject to IRAS conditions and the stamping process, so treat it as a remission you apply for rather than a status you simply possess.

## What you generally can't buy

HDB flats for your own occupation are citizen, PR and family-nucleus products. They aren't an open market for EP holders hunting something affordable and vaguely landed, however the listing photos read. If your household includes an SC spouse, confirm live [HDB eligibility](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/flat-grant-and-loan-eligibility/couples-and-families) rather than assuming, because the non-citizen spouse schemes are narrow and the grant and loan rules differ from the standard case. Once you have PR yourself, or you're an SC with a foreign spouse, our [HDB for PRs and mixed-status couples](/guides/hdb-for-pr-and-mixed-status) guide covers the 3-year SPR wait, the HFE letter and the SPR quota.

Landed residential, in many cases, still runs through special approval routes. Spotting a semi-D listing on a portal is research. It isn't permission.

Private condos and some commercial products are the usual foreigner conversation, and ABSD still applies on the residential side of it.

## So should you buy, or rent?

If you're here on a work pass for 2–5 years and want to live well, rent. That's the honest answer for most people reading this, and [condo vs HDB for expats](/guides/condo-vs-hdb-expat) covers how to rent well.

If you want to park capital and stay long term, price in the ABSD and the exit SDL/tax properly, then ask whether waiting for PR changes the sum. An SPR first property attracts 5%, not 60%, which is a different universe of number.

If you're a US or EFTA national buying a first home, don't dismiss purchase before you've run the IRAS remission math. You're in one of the few groups where the answer might genuinely be yes.

And if the plan is a speculative flip while you're on an EP, the policy is deliberately designed to make that unattractive. It works.

PR isn't a property hack — it's a residency decision carrying NS and other implications for your family. Read [PR application strategy](/guides/pr-application-strategy) on its own terms, well before it becomes a line in a purchase spreadsheet.

## Questions worth putting to any agent

1. What ABSD rate applies to my nationality on this OTP date?
2. Is ABSD remittable under an FTA in my case, and who actually files the remission?
3. What cash falls due **within 14 days** of OTP or agreement, counting both the BSD and ABSD clocks?
4. Will the bank exclude ABSD from the loan quantum? (Almost always yes, but make them say it out loud.)
5. If my EP is cancelled in year three, what does the forced-sale cost stack look like?

Before you option anything, [financial planning year one](/guides/financial-planning-year-one) has the buffer maths you want settled first. If you're still bridging housing while you decide, read [short-term vs long-term rental](/guides/short-term-vs-long-term-rental).

One family note to finish on. A dual-career household with rest-day coverage falling apart is a poor backdrop for an argument about ABSD numbers. Settle who owns childcare before you book another Sunday of viewings.
