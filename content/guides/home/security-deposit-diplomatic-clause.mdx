On r/askSingapore, diplomatic clause threads are some of the angriest rental posts on the board, and they're nearly always written after the fact. Someone resigns, gets reassigned, or loses a pass, reads the clause properly for the first time, and discovers it doesn't say what the agent implied it said. The wording is negotiable in the **Letter of Intent**. It stops being negotiable once the Tenancy Agreement is in front of you and everyone around the table is calling it a formality.

## Security deposits (norms, not law)

Market practice sets the number, not statute. A 1-year lease commonly attracts around 1 month's rent as security, and a 2-year lease around 2 months'. In both cases the deposit is there to cover unpaid rent and damage beyond fair wear and tear, and that's the whole of its job.

Because it's contractual rather than a government-fixed tariff, what settles the argument at exit is a joint inspection and dated inventory photos. Deposits go unreturned because the documentation was missing, not because somebody lost the WhatsApp debate. The make-good detail is in [inventory and fair wear and tear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear). Ambush deductions after the keys are back, and chasing an overseas landlord, are in [deposit return after handover](/guides/deposit-return-handover-disputes).

Stamp duty, agent fees and who pays which of them belong in the [renting process guide](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits). This page is about break risk.

## What a diplomatic (break) clause actually is

It's a clause that lets **the tenant**, not the landlord, end the lease early after a minimum stay, on notice or with rent in lieu, if one of a listed set of triggers happens. The usual list runs: an employer-initiated permanent transfer out of Singapore, cessation of employment or a pass cancellation that ends your right to stay, and an ordered departure from Singapore.

The triggers that matter are the ones written in your TA, not the ones in that sentence. "I found a better job in London" and "I resigned because I hate the role" fail a narrowly drafted clause, and landlords know exactly why they draft it narrowly. Refusing anything that reads as a voluntary-resignation exit ramp is entirely standard behaviour, not a sign of a difficult landlord.

The commercial shape, illustrative rather than legal advice, is usually a minimum stay of 12 months on a 24-month lease, notice of 1–2 months or the equivalent rent in lieu, and documentary evidence in the form of a termination or transfer letter, or the pass cancellation notice.

## The five negotiation mistakes

1. Assuming the clause is standard, and therefore still in the TA. Plenty of landlord drafts strike it out, and agents will sometimes soft-sell that it can be added later.
2. Accepting verbally that resignations count. If it isn't in the document it doesn't count, however sincerely it was said at the viewing.
3. Leaving the trigger limited to embassy and diplomatic staff. The name is a historical leftover, and an EP or DP household needs wording that actually covers an EP or DP household.
4. Agreeing to a replacement-tenant obligation. Some TAs make you find a substitute before they'll release you, which can work out worse than simply paying cash.
5. Having no clause at all. Early exit then defaults to forfeiting the deposit and, depending on how the TA is drafted, further claims for the remaining rent. Read the SCT limits further down before you decide litigation is your answer.

## Who has the leverage, and when

Leverage moves with the market, and it's worth knowing which side of it you're standing on before you start asking for things.

In a soft rental market, with the unit sitting empty for weeks, you're the one with the position. Insist on the diplomatic clause at LOI stage rather than hoping for goodwill later. If there are three other LOIs on the table that weekend, the landlord has it, and you should expect a minimum stay and tightly drawn triggers as the price of the flat.

A project or contractor role with a known end date puts you back in a decent spot: ask for a shorter lease outright, or a break tied explicitly to the contract end. A family with school deposits already committed also has more room than it feels like at the time. Align the lease end with the school year, and keep the clause anyway.

## Deposit return playbook

1. Photograph every room on move-in and move-out, with timestamps and meter readings.
2. Attend the joint inspection. Leaving the keys with the agent and flying out is how surprise deductions happen.
3. Agree every deduction in writing before it touches your refund.
4. Keep the stamped TA and the LOI as PDFs somewhere offline. Email threads vanish when you change jobs, which is often exactly when you need them.

## If you're already stuck without a clause

Your options narrow to commercial negotiation: a surrender fee, a replacement tenant if the landlord will wear one, or walking away, forfeiting the deposit and working out what else the TA exposes you to. The longer version of that conversation, covering the "you just lose the deposit" myth, mitigation and the SCT ceilings, is in [breaking a lease early](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants). What this page isn't is a DIY court strategy. Read your contract, and for large sums get proper advice.

One HDB shock worth naming: **a landlord selling mid-lease is not the same thing as your diplomatic clause firing**. The private-market folklore about "sale with tenancy" often falls apart on HDB's vacant-possession completion, so use the dedicated playbook at [HDB landlord selling](/guides/hdb-landlord-selling-tenants). A bedroom rental that was never properly approved is a different deposit fight again, and that one's at [HDB bedroom rules](/guides/hdb-bedroom-rental-rules-tenants).

## Small Claims Tribunals (deposit fights)

Deposit threads reach "just file SCT" within about three replies. The jurisdiction is narrower than Facebook makes it sound.

| Gate | Published rule of thumb (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| Claim type | Residential tenancy dispute, such as a deposit refund |
| Lease length | Contract for lease of residential premises not exceeding 2 years |
| Claim size | Up to S$20,000, or S$30,000 with a signed Memorandum of Consent |
| Time limit | File within 2 years of the cause of action, often when the refund became due or was refused |
| Service | The respondent generally has to be servable in Singapore |

Three failure modes show up repeatedly, and they match both the Judiciary's guidance and recent SCT decisions. Back-to-back renewals and continuous occupation can be treated as a single lease running over 2 years, at which point the SCT may discontinue the claim. An overseas landlord with no workable Singapore service address can stall a filing even when the TA names a local notice address. And winning an order is not the same as being paid: enforcement is a separate problem with its own timeline.

Bring the stamped TA, the inventory photos, the deduction spreadsheet and the whole WhatsApp and email trail. And note that an agent who handled a dual representation badly can still face a CEA complaint, even in cases where the SCT turns out to be the wrong forum entirely.
