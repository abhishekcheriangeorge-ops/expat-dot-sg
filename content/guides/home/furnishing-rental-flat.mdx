"Unfurnished" in a Singapore listing doesn't mean what it meant wherever you came from. New arrivals hit the same wall: plenty of condos come with a washer, a fridge and maybe blinds, and that's it. No bed. Pin down the inventory annex before you build a budget, and well before you fly in with only a suitcase. It matters at the other end too, because deposit arguments at checkout are nearly always inventory arguments, and [fair wear and tear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) is where those get settled. Furniture you bought yourself that turns out to be defective is a different route entirely: [CASE and the Lemon Law](/guides/case-lemon-law-consumers-singapore).

## Decoding the listing language

| What the listing says | What it usually means | What it does to your budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fully furnished | Beds, sofa, dining, the basics. Quality swings wildly | Higher monthly rent, far less friction on the way out |
| Semi or partially furnished | Appliances, and maybe one bed frame | Mattresses, desks and storage are on you |
| Bare or unfurnished | Empty rooms, appliances optional | The biggest upfront spend; earns its keep on 2+ year stays |

Ask the landlord's agent for a room-by-room inventory with recently dated photos. "Partially furnished" on PropertyGuru is not a promise that you can sleep there on night one.

## Buying versus renting the furniture

| How long you're staying | What most people settle on | Why it goes that way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| &lt;12 months | A furniture rental pack, or cheap IKEA topped up from Carousell | Resale hassle and disposal fees eat whatever you saved |
| 1–3 years | Buy mid-range, and plan the Carousell exit from the start | The monthly premium on a furnished unit usually outruns the upfront spend |
| 3+ years, or you're on a PR path | Buy things that survive the humidity | Teak or better particle board beats the disposable stuff |

Long-term furniture rental does exist here, but it's a thinner market than you may be used to. What happens more often is that a household buys cheap, and then discovers that getting rid of it costs more than giving it away. That's the entire explanation for the "free, must collect" posts that flood Carousell in the weeks before leases end.

## What's worth the money here

- **A dehumidifier**, or more than one. Humidity in Singapore is a maintenance problem, not an atmosphere, and it will find your wardrobes. Pair it with [disciplined aircon servicing](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals)
- A mattress bought new, with a breathable protector. Frames are perfectly fine second-hand; the mattress is a hygiene call
- Blackout curtains on anything west-facing. Your electricity bill and your sleep both improve
- The kitchen kit you'll genuinely use every week, not the full wedding registry
- A cordless drill if you're assembling IKEA. Landlords rarely stock tools

One thing the local threads agree on: the cheapest particle board fails fastest near windows. If you're staying past year one, step up a tier on wardrobes and desks.

## Humidity, mould and dehumidifiers

The r/askSingapore mould threads aren't really about how it looks. They're about the smell that gets into a wardrobe, the cost of aircon chemical washes, and the deduction waiting for you at checkout.

| Tactic | Where renters land on it |
| --- | --- |
| Aim for indoor RH somewhere around **40–60%** | Sitting at ~70% RH is still comfortable territory for mould in a lot of wardrobes |
| Buy a hygrometer before you buy anything else | Guessing at humidity just burns electricity |
| One dehumidifier per damp room, rather than walking a single unit around for 2 hours at a time | Matters most in ground-floor and pool-facing units |
| Run the aircon in dry or fan mode after cooling | Helps dry the coil; pair it with [aircon servicing](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals) |
| Charcoal or desiccants inside closed cabinets | A machine in the room never reaches a sealed wardrobe |
| Photograph mould at move-in and tell the landlord early | Months of silence turn into "tenant negligence" at checkout |

Broadly, structural leaks and mould that predates you push responsibility toward the landlord, while failing to ventilate or failing to report it after move-in pushes it toward your deposit. Keep the invoices for chemical washes and dehumidifier purchases in case you end up at the SCT. If you're already into paint-over arguments, asking for rent abatement, or assembling an evidence pack, we have a dedicated [mould and humidity playbook for tenants](/guides/mould-humidity-rental-tenants).

## Working Carousell and Marketplace

Expat churn is your inventory. The good stuff surfaces when someone is clearing a whole flat on a three-day clock — sofas and dining sets routinely land below what shipping the equivalent from JB would cost.

A few rules that save regret:

- Prefer self-collection, with the dimensions measured against your lift before you commit
- Photograph the condition before any money moves
- Never wire a deposit to a stranger who wants to hold something in a storage unit for you
- Budget for a Lalamove or a small mover for anything that won't fit in a Grab

Hock Siong, Nitori, Courts, Castlery, Fortytwo and the mall furniture floors fill the gaps Carousell can't. The neighbourhood cheap shops are genuinely hit or miss, and the common advice is to stay with known retail for anything load-bearing.

## What not to buy

- A full dining set if you eat out most nights
- A sofa that fails the service-lift test. Measure first, buy second
- Built-in modifications the landlord will charge you to reverse
- White goods that are **already on the inventory**, because a duplicate is a deposit fight waiting to happen
- Anything you couldn't sell or give away across two weekends, which is roughly the notice you get when the diplomatic clause fires

## Leaving without orphan furniture

Start listing 4–6 weeks before the keys go back, not in the final week. Bundle whatever's left for one buyer; a single person taking six pieces is worth more to you than six separate handovers in your last fortnight. What doesn't sell goes to donation or a free listing, and paying for disposal is the last resort rather than the plan. Photograph the empty rooms once everything is out, so a "where's the landlord's chair" claim has nowhere to go.

From here it's either [moving within Singapore](/guides/moving-within-singapore) if you're shifting units, or the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook) if the assignment is ending.

One note for families: when PSI spikes, a working aircon and blackout curtains earn their keep in a way another decorative lamp never will. Furnish for the indoor days.
