“Unfurnished” in Singapore listings is not a global standard. Forum threads from new arrivals land on the same shock: many condos arrive with **washer + fridge + maybe blinds**, not beds. Clarify the inventory annex before you budget — and before you fly with only a suitcase. Deposit fights later are inventory fights: [fair wear & tear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear). Defective new furniture you bought yourself: [CASE / Lemon Law](/guides/case-lemon-law-consumers-singapore).

## Decode the listing language

| Label you see | What it often means | Budget implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fully furnished | Beds, sofa, dining, basics — quality varies wildly | Higher monthly rent; less exit friction |
| Semi / partially furnished | Appliances + maybe one bed frame | You still need mattresses, desks, storage |
| Bare / unfurnished | Empty rooms; appliances optional | Highest CapEx; best for 2+ year stays |

Ask the landlord agent for a **room-by-room inventory** and photos dated recently. “Partially furnished” on PropertyGuru is not a promise that you can sleep on night one.

## Buy vs rent furniture

| Horizon | Practical default | Why forums converge here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| &lt;12 months | Furniture rental pack **or** cheap IKEA + Carousell | Resale pain and disposal fees eat savings |
| 1–3 years | Buy mid-range; plan Carousell exit | Monthly furnished premium often exceeds CapEx |
| 3+ years / PR path | Buy pieces that survive humidity | Teak / better particle-board beats disposable |

Long-term furniture rental exists but is less common than in some expat markets. Many households buy cheap, then discover **disposal costs more than giving away** — which is why Carousell “free / must collect” posts spike before lease ends.

## High-ROI purchases (Singapore-specific)

- **Dehumidifier(s)** — humidity is a maintenance problem, not a vibe. Pair with [aircon servicing discipline](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals)
- **Mattress + breathable protector** — buy new for hygiene; frames can be second-hand
- **Blackout curtains** on west-facing rooms — electricity and sleep both improve
- **Kitchen kit you will use weekly** — not a full wedding registry
- **Cordless drill** if you assemble IKEA — landlords rarely stock tools

IKEA humidity tip from local threads: the cheapest particle board near windows fails fastest. Step one tier up on wardrobes and desks if you are staying past year one.

## Humidity, mould, and dehumidifiers

r/askSingapore mould threads are less about aesthetics and more about wardrobe smell, aircon chemical washes, and deposit deductions.

| Tactic | Forum / renter consensus |
| --- | --- |
| Target indoor RH roughly **40–60%** | ~70% RH is still mould-friendly for many wardrobes |
| Buy a hygrometer first | Guessing humidity wastes electricity |
| One dehumidifier per damp room beats rotating one unit for 2 hours | Especially ground-floor / pool-facing units |
| Aircon **dry / fan** mode after cooling | Helps dry the coil; pair with [aircon servicing](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals) |
| Charcoal / closet desiccants for closed cabinets | Machines alone do not reach sealed wardrobes |
| Photograph mould at move-in and message landlord early | Late silence becomes “tenant negligence” at checkout |

Structural leaks and pre-existing mould push toward landlord responsibility; failure to ventilate / report after move-in pushes toward tenant deductions. Keep invoices for chemical washes and dehumidifier purchases if you end up in SCT.

## Carousell / Marketplace playbook

Expat churn is your inventory. Search for whole-flat clearances when someone is leaving on a three-day clock — sofas and dining sets often price below shipping from JB.

Rules that avoid regret:

- Prefer **self-collection** with dimensions measured against your lift
- Photograph condition before you pay
- Never wire deposits to strangers for “storage unit holds”
- Budget a Lalamove / small mover for anything that does not fit a Grab

Hock Siong, Nitori, Courts, Castlery, Fortytwo, and mall furniture floors fill gaps Carousell cannot. Neighbourhood “cheap” shops are hit-or-miss on quality — forum consensus often prefers known retail for anything load-bearing.

## What not to buy

- Full dining sets if you eat out most nights
- Oversized sofas that fail the **service-lift** test
- Built-in hacks landlords will charge to reverse
- White goods already listed on the inventory (duplicate = deposit fight)
- Anything you cannot sell or donate in two weekends when the diplomatic clause fires

## Exit without orphan furniture

Start listing **4–6 weeks** before keys return. Bundle remaining pieces for one buyer. What does not sell goes to donation / free Carousell — pay for disposal only as last resort. Photograph empty rooms after removal so “missing landlord chair” claims do not stick.
