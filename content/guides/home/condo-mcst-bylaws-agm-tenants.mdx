Condo marketing sells you the pool. Around month three, the MCST by-laws turn up: renovation blackouts, a cap on visitor parking, a pet veto, and AGM notices your landlord forgets to forward. Treat this as tenant orientation to strata living rather than a full noise playbook, which we've put in [condo noise and MCST neighbours](/guides/condo-noise-mcst-neighbours), or the eligibility comparison over in [condo vs HDB](/guides/condo-vs-hdb-expat).

## By-laws vs your tenancy agreement

Two separate rulebooks govern your life in a condo, and a third one lands in your inbox every few weeks.

| Document | What it controls for you |
| --- | --- |
| Tenancy agreement | Rent, deposit, landlord access, who pays which utilities |
| MCST by-laws and house rules | Use of common property, pets, renovation hours, moving-in bookings, BBQ and facility bookings |
| Managing agent circulars | Short-term operations: tank cleaning, lift works, the AGM date |

Where the TA and the by-laws disagree about pets or renovation, assume the stricter rule wins in practice until the owner sorts it out with the council. You're the one living in the building, and the guard at the gate enforces the by-laws, not your lease.

## What to ask before you sign the LOI

All of this costs nothing to ask and is expensive to discover later.

1. The current by-laws or house-rules PDF. Ask for the actual document, not the agent's summary of it.
2. The pet, renovation, moving-in and lift-booking, and visitor-parking clauses, confirmed in writing.
3. Who the managing agent is, and how residents actually submit a ticket.
4. A photo of the lobby notice boards on viewing day. Upcoming works tell you more about the next year than the brochure does.
5. An expectation, said out loud to the landlord or agent, that AGM notices and circulars get forwarded to you.

## AGMs without a vote

The AGM is where budgets, by-law changes and council elections get decided, and as a tenant you usually don't get a vote. That sits with the owner as subsidiary proprietor.

You can still do three useful things. Ask your landlord for the AGM pack when a motion touches something you live with, like pets, EV chargers, or major works above your unit. If you need to speak to a motion, ask the owner for a written proxy or attendance note rather than turning up with a resident pass and hoping. And keep the circulars, all of them. Deposit arguments are full of "you never told me about the façade project dust", and a saved circular ends that conversation quickly — see [deposit return and handover disputes](/guides/deposit-return-handover-disputes).

## Escalation ladder

Work it in order. Raise an MA ticket with photos and dates, tell your landlord or agent in writing the same day, then ask the owner to raise it with the council as SP or at the next meeting. If the breach is ongoing, start building a quiet-enjoyment trail, which we cover in [landlord entry and quiet enjoyment](/guides/landlord-entry-quiet-enjoyment).

One thing not to do: **don't withhold rent** to protest a silent managing agent. It turns their problem into your breach.

Belts like [Eunos](/neighbourhoods/eunos), [Guillemard](/neighbourhoods/guillemard) and [Dakota](/neighbourhoods/dakota), where condos sit right on the HDB edge, mix older MCSTs with newer inserts, so by-law quality varies block to block. Walk past the notice board before you LOI.
