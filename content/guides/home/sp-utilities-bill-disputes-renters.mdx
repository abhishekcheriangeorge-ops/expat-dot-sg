The “my SP bill exploded” threads on Reddit are almost never about a haunted meter. Nine times out of ten it comes down to three things: whose name is on the account, whether that month was billed on an estimate or an actual read, and whether a dehumidifier, a second aircon, or a retailer plan step quietly changed the arithmetic. This guide is the billing-dispute rail. It isn’t blackout response, and it isn’t about PUB shutting off a street pipe for works.

## Start here, on the day the bill lands

| Signal | Likely cause | First move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bill ~2× after a quiet quarter | Actual read after estimates | Ask SP for read type history; photograph meter |
| Water line spikes, electricity flat | Leak / running cistern / dripping heater | [PUB leak tips PDF](https://www.pub.gov.sg/-/media/Images/Feature/Content-Pages/Resources/Publications/Pdfs/Tips_on_Finding_Leaks.pdf) + landlord |
| Electricity spikes in monsoon | Dehumidifier / aircon runtime | Log RH and hours; see the [mould playbook](/guides/mould-humidity-rental-tenants) |
| Price per kWh jumped, usage flat | OEM retailer plan / promo end | Pull plan PDF from retailer portal |
| Only your unit vs whole stack | Internal fault or account mix-up | Compare with neighbour WhatsApp; call SP |

## Whose name is on the account

If the landlord keeps the SP account and recharges you, ask for the **itemised SP or retailer PDF** every single month. Not a number in a message. A PayNow caption that says “utilities $280” is exactly how a disputed month becomes a deposit fight at the end of the lease, because by then nobody can reconstruct what was actually consumed and you’re arguing from memory. If the tenancy agreement is vague about this, set the expectation in writing at LOI stage, while you still have leverage.

If the account is in your own name, you get the good version of this. You open and close it on your own key dates, you’re the one who can lodge a dispute, and you control the timing of the final read at move-out. Photograph the meter on move-in day and again at handover. It’s the same habit, for the same reason, as the [inventory and fair wear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) walkthrough.

Dual accounts during a sale or a mid-lease transfer get messy fast. Before you pay anyone “to keep the lights on”, confirm whose name the live account is actually under. Whatever you get told about cut-off dates belongs in writing to the agent and the landlord, not in a rent strike.

## Estimated reads, and the catch-up bill

An estimated bill smooths the line until an actual read lands, and then the actual read does all of the correcting in one month. That’s why the spikes cluster after a long trip, or after a stretch where the gate was locked and the meter reader couldn’t get in. The electricity was always being used. It just wasn’t being billed yet, and now it all is.

Three questions for SP, in this order:

1. Which months were estimated and which were actual?
2. When was the previous actual read?
3. Can they schedule or confirm the next actual read?

Don’t pay a “friend who knows someone at SP” to “fix” the meter. Keep the whole thing on the official app or the hotline, because what you want out of this is ticket numbers, and ticket numbers are what CASE or Small Claims will ask for if it ever goes that far.

## When it’s the plan, not the meter

If you switched retailers under the Open Electricity Market, the bill can move because your plan changed rather than because anything went wrong with the metering. Check the live offers on [OEM price comparison](https://compare.openelectricitymarket.sg/#/home) before you assume a fault. The usual culprits are a promo or discount period quietly ending, a peak/off-peak product that punishes all-day work-from-home cooling, bundled “free months” that push the cost into later periods, and exit fees if you go chasing a better retailer mid-contract.

SP still handles the network and metering side of a lot of these journeys, but the argument about the unit rate sits inside your retailer contract. That’s who you take it up with.

## What to have in hand before you dispute

1. Bill PDFs for the disputed month and the three months before it
2. A phone photo of the meter showing the digits and the meter ID, on the bill date if you can manage it
3. A short usage diary — guests, dehumidifier, the week of the aircon chemical wash
4. The TA clause on who pays utilities, plus any WhatsApp recharge history
5. The ticket or chat reference from SP or the retailer

Then ask for a re-read, a leak check, or a plan clarification, in that order. It’s worth checking your [aircon servicing weeks](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals) while you’re at it, because a “mystery” spike is quite often a second unit left running after a chemical wash.

## Four things not to do

**Never withhold rent** over a utility line. The bill might well turn out to be wrong, but you’ll have stacked a breach of your own on top of it, and you argue from a much worse position after that ([early exit and breach risk](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants)). Don’t pay cash to a “meter agent” who messaged you on Telegram. Don’t ignore a final bill after handover, because closing reads follow the FIN holder rather than the address. And don’t confuse a PUB street shut-off with an SP billing dispute; those are different problems, handled by different people.

The central-east light-industrial and residential belts — [MacPherson](/neighbourhoods/macpherson), [Tai Seng](/neighbourhoods/tai-seng), [Paya Lebar](/neighbourhoods/paya-lebar) — carry a lot of work-from-home plus dehumidifier load in older walk-ups, and meter access problems and estimate true-ups show up in the same threads over and over. If you’re still in week one, run the [utilities and telecom sequence](/guides/utilities-telecom-setup) first, so the account is in the right name before the first big bill ever arrives.
