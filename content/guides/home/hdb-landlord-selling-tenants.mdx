The angriest HDB-tenant threads on r/askSingapore all open the same way. You signed a 2-year whole-flat TA three months ago, and now the agent is on WhatsApp saying the owner is selling and you may need to move. Most of what you'll read next about sale with tenancy comes from the private-condo market, and it doesn't transplant cleanly onto HDB resale.

## HDB and private aren't the same rail

| Rail | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| Private condo or landed | A buyer can take the unit with the existing tenancy if the contracts allow it, so your TA can survive a change of landlord |
| HDB resale | Sellers generally have to deliver **vacant possession** at [resale completion](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/selling-a-flat/process-for-selling-a-flat/resale-flat-completion). Buyers turn up to the HDB appointment expecting empty keys |
| Your TA | Still a private contract with your seller-landlord. The sale doesn't magic away rent, deposits or early-exit clauses, but the pressure of the HDB process usually forces a commercial unwind anyway |

The forum consensus, best captured in [the thread about an owner wanting to sell mid-lease](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1lidib5/renting_a_hdb_with_2yrs_contract_but_owner_wants/), lands in a sensible place. Don't assume that MOP rules on the buyer somehow cancel your lease tomorrow. Do assume the seller needs you gone by completion, unless they negotiate something unusual, and HDB-compliant, with the buyer.

<OfficialSource>
HDB’s resale completion guidance: sellers must vacate before the completion appointment so buyers can take possession. Temporary extension-of-stay after completion is a **seller–buyer** arrangement for sellers/authorised occupiers — not a free pass for the seller’s former tenants ([extension of stay](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/selling-a-flat/process-for-selling-a-flat/resale-flat-application/request-for-temporary-extension-of-stay)).
</OfficialSource>

## Read your TA before you panic-post

Four things in the document decide how much leverage you actually have, and it's worth knowing which ones you've got before you reply to the agent.

The first is a sale or landlord-break clause. Some tenancy agreements let the landlord terminate on a sale with a stated number of months' notice and compensation. Plenty of drafts don't mention a sale at all, which cuts both ways: no automatic right for the landlord, and no automatic payout for you.

The second is quiet enjoyment and viewing access. Reasonable access for viewings is standard and you should expect it. An agent letting themselves in with keys whenever it suits them is not the same thing, and it isn't automatic.

The third is your diplomatic or tenant break clause. That's your early-exit tool for when your own circumstances change. It is not a shield against your landlord selling, and people confuse the two constantly.

The fourth is any agent commission reimbursement clause. Those usually exist to protect the agency's fee, not to cover your moving costs, so read what it actually says before you count on it.

If the TA is silent on sale, you're in straight commercial negotiation: notice length, moving costs, a temporary hotel, overlapping rent, and when your deposit comes back. Where the sums are large, pay for written advice rather than running on forum bravado.

## Viewings while the flat is listed

Viewings are the second flashpoint, right after "when do I have to leave?"

| Reasonable | Push back on |
| --- | --- |
| Written notice, often 24–48h, for daytime viewings | Same-day surprise walk-ins |
| A limited number of slots, with the frequency capped in writing | An open house every evening |
| You or a trusted adult present | Keys left with the listing agent "for convenience" |
| Photos of common areas you've already agreed to | Bedrooms photographed without your consent |

Document every visit: date, how long, who came in. Harassment, changed locks or cut utilities to push you out aren't marketing, and they're worth escalating in writing, to CEA if an agent is involved, and to the police if anyone's safety is at stake. If the deposit fight comes afterwards, it still runs on the usual [security deposit and SCT](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) rules.

## A negotiation playbook that survives completion week

1. Ask in writing for the OTP and the expected completion window. HDB timelines often run a matter of weeks after acceptance, so verify the current position rather than assuming
2. Propose a mutual termination letter covering the move-out date, rent pro-rating, the deposit return clock, and who pays the movers
3. Price your ask from real numbers: overlapping rent on the next place, the mover's invoice, temporary accommodation, the school-bus disruption. A penalty figure you picked because it felt fair won't survive the conversation
4. **Keep paying rent until a written deal says otherwise.** Withholding rent shifts leverage away from you, not towards you
5. Photograph the inventory on the way out, and avoid leaving the keys with an agent and flying if you possibly can

## What not to believe

"Sale with tenancy always applies to HDB" is private-market folklore until a lawyer or agent shows you an HDB-compatible structure in writing. "MOP means the buyer can't make us leave" gets it backwards: MOP constrains what the buyer can do with the flat, and it does nothing to relieve your landlord of delivering vacant possession at completion. And "the agent fee clause in my TA refunds my moving costs" is, in almost every draft we've seen, simply not what the clause does.

Pair this with [moving within Singapore](/guides/moving-within-singapore) for the lift-booking and MCST logistics on your next unit, and with [HDB bedroom rental rules](/guides/hdb-bedroom-rental-rules-tenants) if you were in a room rental, where the approval and owner-occupation risks work differently.

One family note. A landlord sale notice landing during orientation week is a genuinely bad week for a dual-career household. Get the tenancy paper trail in order before term-one fittings, not in the middle of them.
