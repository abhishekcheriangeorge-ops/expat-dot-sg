Humidity isn't a vibe here, it's a maintenance schedule. Let it slide and it comes back as mould, a blocked condensate drain dripping down the wall, a landlord who wants all of it out of your deposit, and eventually your own Reddit thread titled “am I getting scammed by aircon servicing?”

## What “basic servicing” should actually include

Forum consensus and property-manager advice line up reasonably well on what a normal clean covers. Filters cleaned. The blower or fan coil properly washed, which usually means it comes out and gets rinsed rather than foam-sprayed for 90 seconds while you watch. Drain tray cleaned and the drain pipe vacuumed. Outdoor condenser checked, where the technician can actually reach it.

Honest **general cleaning** tends to land around **S$25–35 per indoor unit** in community pricing, though minimum job sizes vary. Chemical and deep washes are an occasional thing, annually or when you can see mould, rather than something that belongs on every visit. Some vendors also offer a steam wash that sits between basic and full chemical.

| Red flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Every single visit needs a “gas top-up” | Refrigerant doesn't just evaporate — if it's low, there's a leak, so ask to see the manifold gauge readings |
| A “chemical wash” where the unit never gets opened | You paid for theatre |
| Hard-sell annual chemical contracts on the first visit | Standard upsell, and optional for plenty of homes |
| Cash only, no invoice, no company details | That's the paperwork you'll want when the deposit fight starts |

Keep the invoices. Plenty of tenancy agreements put regular servicing on the tenant and major repairs or compressor failure on the landlord, but the wording genuinely varies, so read yours instead of assuming. At move-in, photograph the serial numbers and count the units.

## How often is often enough

Running the aircon most nights through the humid months, every **3–4 months** is the usual rhythm. Lighter use in a clean flat and **6 months** can work fine. Live near construction or a highway and the dust shortens the interval regardless of what the calendar says. If a unit smells musty or starts dripping indoors, book it now — that one isn't a scheduling question.

## Pests, cockroaches mostly

Tropical pests are ordinary. They aren't a verdict on your housekeeping. What's worth finding out early is whether your condo already has a contracted pest schedule, and what goes to the managing agent versus what you handle yourself with a private technician. Dengue season and standing water on your balcony are household hygiene too, not purely an “estate problem.” And document anything that looks like an infestation the day you notice it, because end-of-lease arguments get considerably worse when there's no record.

The r/askSingapore cockroach threads keep landing on the same handful of renter tactics.

| Tactic | Why renters swear by it |
| --- | --- |
| Tape or cover the rubbish chute edges during estate fogging | Fogging drives roaches up the shared chutes and into kitchens; the full playbook is in [dengue and chute fogging](/guides/dengue-cluster-living-renters) |
| Bait stations rather than panic spray | Spray scatters them; bait gets carried back to the nest |
| Photograph the entry gaps (pipes, balcony doors, chute) at move-in | Heads off the “you caused the infestation” conversation later |
| Escalate shared-chute infestations through the managing agent | Unit boundaries mean nothing to tropical pests |

Mould on fan coils and bathroom silicone is a humidity problem, not a servicing failure, so pair your servicing cadence with a [dehumidifier strategy](/guides/furnishing-rental-flat) and the [mould and landlord remediation playbook](/guides/mould-humidity-rental-tenants). It isn't a reason to buy another chemical wash. In dengue cluster weeks you get mosquito habitats stacked on top of the kitchen pests, so empty the drip trays and check the NEA map before you decide it's “just cockroaches.”

## Bed bugs, and the point where pests become a lease fight

Bed bugs are a different animal from kitchen cockroaches, and they need a different response. NEA notes they hide in sleeping areas: mattress seams, bed frames, headboards, electrical outlets, night tables. The bites itch and can trigger allergies, although bed bugs are **not** known disease vectors. DIY spray rarely ends an infestation on its own. NEA's advice is to engage a pest management company and combine vacuuming, hot laundering, steam or heat, removing harbourages, and proper insecticides.

Here's the renter playbook that matches where the forum heat actually is, habitability exits after something serious included — see this [termites and an uninhabitable unit](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1e9w12r/contract_termination_in_condo_termites/) thread for the flavour of it.

| Move | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Photograph the bites and the harbourages on day one | The timeline is what protects you when the landlord decides you brought them in |
| Read the repair and pest covenants in your TA | That's what tells you who pays for the first treatment and who pays for recurrence |
| Tell the landlord or MA in writing before any major spend | Reimbursement fights love silence |
| Quarantine luggage and second-hand sofas | The classic introduction vector, after travel or a karang guni furniture find |
| Don't stop paying rent while you negotiate | It just creates a second breach; see [early termination](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants) |

A mutual early exit is a negotiation when the unit is genuinely uninhabitable and repairs have stalled. It isn't an automatic statutory eject, whatever the thread told you. Keep your [inventory evidence](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear) clean so a deposit fight doesn't end up stacked on top of the pest drama.

## Renewals

Start the renewal conversation **2–3 months** out. Turn up with comparable rents, your own payment record, and the repair goodwill you've earned by keeping the aircons healthy. The deeper playbook, including the fact that there's no statutory cap on rent increases and how to use URA and HDB comps, is in [lease renewal and rent increases](/guides/lease-renewal-rent-increase). If you're leaving instead, use the [diplomatic clause calendar](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) deliberately rather than emotionally, and read [breaking a lease without a clause](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants) before you write off the deposit and hope. For the viewing pressure that arrives in the last couple of months, there's [landlord entry and quiet enjoyment](/guides/landlord-entry-quiet-enjoyment).

From here: [the renting process](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits) for TA annex clarity, and [furnishing](/guides/furnishing-rental-flat) if you're restocking after a humid first year.

One note if you have kids. Outbreak weeks and recovery weeks are the worst possible moment to discover a failed chemical wash or a fresh pest flare, so book servicing before the HFMD and hospital calendar goes sideways, and keep one vendor's number in the family shared notes.
