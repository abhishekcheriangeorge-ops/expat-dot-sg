Every agent describes their listing as quiet residential. Six months later a piling rig on the empty plot next door starts at 7am, and you find out what that phrase was actually worth. Where you take the complaint depends entirely on what's making the noise, and getting that wrong costs you weeks of being politely bounced between agencies.

If it's a neighbour renovating their own unit, that's a building-management matter and it runs on the [MCST and CDRT rail](/guides/condo-noise-mcst-neighbours). If it's an external site — a BTO build, a condo going up, road or MRT works — it's NEA's, with its own hotline and its own limits. Same misery when you're trying to hold a call from the spare room, completely different complaint path.

## Who regulates what

| Noise source | First rail | Not this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbour’s condo reno / late parties | MCST → CMC → CDRT | NEA construction desk |
| HDB neighbour reno | HDB / Town Council channels | “Call NEA for upstairs tiling” |
| Adjacent construction site (BTO, condo build, road/MRT works) | NEA, by call or OneService or myENV, while it is still loud | Police as primary (they often bounce you) |
| Landed A&A works | Often the NEA reno/construction regime | Condo by-laws |

## The limits renters actually end up quoting

Where a residential building sits within &lt;150 m of a site, NEA publishes caps that tighten in steps after 7pm and again after 10pm. The tables change by schedule, so check the live ones rather than a number you read on a forum. The uncomfortable part is that daytime work can be genuinely awful and still perfectly lawful, because it's sitting under the daytime limit. Loud is not the same thing as illegal, and that distinction is why some reports go nowhere.

The rule with real teeth is the no-work window for sites within &lt;150 m of residences or certain noise-sensitive premises, unless NEA has permitted otherwise: **10pm Saturday to 7am Monday, and 10pm on the eve of a public holiday to 7am the day after**. That's the one worth knowing by heart, because it's the one a contractor can't argue their way around with a decibel reading.

Even then, quiet, public-interest and safety-critical permits exist. Hearing drilling on a Sunday is good evidence for a report. It isn't proof of anything until NEA has checked what permits the site holds.

The reporting pattern that people say works, judging by threads like [this one on night construction past 2am](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1g3vf5r/where_to_report_loud_ongoing_construction_that/) and [this one on a BTO beside the block](https://www.reddit.com/r/SingaporeRaw/comments/1reztv8/bto_construction_noise_beside_my_block_driving_me/), goes roughly like this. Call NEA on 6225 5632 or file on OneService or myENV while the noise is actually happening, so an officer has something to hear. Note what's on the site signboard: developer, contractor, contact. Keep a log of dates with a few short videos, and get neighbours to file separately, because one complaint reads as one sensitive resident and five reads as a site problem. What you should expect at the end of that is monitoring equipment or a warning, not permanent silence.

## Tenancy reality check

Here is the part nobody wants to hear. A standard residential TA almost never lets you walk away rent-free because a BTO site opened up nearby. An agent who somehow didn't mention the empty plot next door has handed you a misrepresentation argument and some negotiating leverage, not a statutory eject button.

| Move | When it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-LOI map check (URA SPACE / Google timeline / empty plots) | Before you pay the holding deposit |
| Ask in writing: “Any known adjacent construction?” | Creates a paper trail |
| Rent reduction / temporary WFH hotel | Only if the landlord agrees in writing |
| Early exit | Needs a TA break clause or mutual termination — [diplomatic clause / SCT](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) |
| Mitigation | Heavy curtains, foam seals, indoor drying (dust), air purifier. Helps you sleep, gives you no legal remedy |

## Before you sign, if you're hunting in the heartlands

Walk the block at 8am on a weekday rather than judging it from a Sunday afternoon viewing, when half of Singapore's construction is asleep. Look around for boarded plots, cranes, HDB project boards and MRT works. Search the town's upcoming BTO and infrastructure news once, properly, then stop — this is a one-evening job, not a doomscrolling habit. And if you work from home, an upper-floor unit facing a courtyard is worth more to you than a slightly larger one facing the site.
