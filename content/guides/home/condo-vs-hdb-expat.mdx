Almost everyone who arrives for a job defaults to a condo, and that's a reasonable first move when you're choosing a home off photos from another time zone. What happens later is more interesting. Families who stay a few years often work out that a well-located HDB estate gives them noticeably more space for the same rent, provided the rules actually fit them. The lifestyle and budget side of that is in [heartland living for expats](/guides/heartland-living-for-expats).

## What a condo actually buys you

The pool, the gym and the guard post aren't extras bolted on. They're a maintenance culture you're paying into every month, and the rent per square foot reflects it. Condos also sit thickest along the international school corridors, Bukit Timah and East Coast among them, which is usually the real reason a family ends up in one. The agency process will feel familiar too, because it has been shaped around expat tenants for decades.

Pets are where the "condos are easier" assumption breaks. There is no national condo rule. Your dog lives or dies by the MCST by-laws plus your landlord's own view, and those two can disagree with each other. More on that in [pets daily life](/guides/pets-daily-life-singapore).

## Renting an HDB flat

HDB owners can rent out spare bedrooms or the whole flat, but only within HDB's eligibility rules, minimum occupation periods, and nationality restrictions, and all of those change over time. So check the rules against the specific flat rather than against what a colleague managed two years ago.

Then budget for heartland operations. Town Council bulky waste collection, the fogging waves when pests come up the chute, and the classic: your upstairs neighbour's shower reaching you as a drip on the ceiling. What to do when that happens is in [HDB ceiling leaks for tenants](/guides/hdb-ceiling-leak-tenants).

On pets, HDB is now the clearer of the two. Limited cats and one approved small dog are allowed under the current Animals Rules and Cat Management Framework. Your landlord can still say no, and you still need AVS licensing.

## How the trade-offs land

| Factor | Condo | HDB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Space per dollar | Lower | Higher |
| Facilities | Strong | Usually limited |
| Neighbour density | Moderate | Higher |
| Expat peer density | Higher in core belts | Mixed |
| Leak / slab disputes | MCST / private contractor path | Owner joint repair + possible HDB GRA |
| Pets | By-laws lottery | Clearer national limits, landlord veto still wins |

Choose for commute and schools first, finishes second. That sounds obvious and gets ignored weekly by people who fell for a kitchen. And if pets or humidity maintenance are going to dominate your first year here, treat them as hard filters while you're still shortlisting, not as week-four surprises you're negotiating over WhatsApp with a landlord who has already banked your deposit.

If you're tempted to buy rather than rent, read [buying property as a foreigner (ABSD)](/guides/buying-property-foreigner-absd) **before you sign anything carrying an option to purchase**. For most nationalities the rent-versus-buy maths simply collapses under 60% ABSD.
