Singapore water is completely reliable, right up until the morning your shower coughs air and the building WhatsApp group fills with "no water again?". Power cuts are a different utility rail entirely. Flash floods are drainage weather. This guide is about PUB pipe works and supply disruption, from a renter's side of it.

## Planned works versus a surprise dry tap

| What's happening | Check this first | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A lift notice or SMS about pipe replacement | Dates, which floors, where the temporary supply points are | Stock jugs, shift your showers, warn whoever is expecting you on a call |
| Only your flat is dry and the neighbours are fine | Your internal stopcock, and anything recently renovated or DIY'd | This is the landlord's plumber, not the PUB mains desk |
| The whole stack or block is dry, with no notice | Neighbours first, then PUB's outage channels | Photograph the notice boards; report through the official contacts |
| Brown or yellow water once supply restarts | Run the cold taps; skip the laundry | Escalate if it doesn't clear |
| A wet ceiling during mains works | Photos, then the landlord | [Ceiling leak playbook](/guides/hdb-ceiling-leak-tenants) |

## Getting through pipe-replacement week

1. Fill clean bottles and pots the night before a published shut-off. On the morning itself the tap is already off, which is not the moment to remember this.
2. Time showers and dishwasher runs around the window rather than hoping they'll overlap neatly.
3. Have a bucket-flush plan for the toilet if the outage runs to hours. It's the part everyone forgets until they need it.
4. Screenshot the PUB and town notices for your landlord file. Dates are what any later conversation turns on.
5. **Don't open the main stopcock** while contractors have the supply isolated, however tempting it is to check whether it's back.

## Yellow water, laundry and filters

Sediment moves when the mains reconnect, and cold water can come back tinted for a while. Run the taps until it drains clear, and put the white laundry off for a day. A pitcher filter helps with taste; it does nothing about a burst main. If the water still looks wrong after hours rather than minutes, take it to PUB's water-quality contacts instead of whatever chlorine folklore is circulating in the Facebook group.

## Who actually owns the problem

- PUB street and riser works belong to the utility authority, and their notices are the record
- Anything inside your leased unit is the landlord's or agent's plumber
- A condo's common pump or tank is the MCST or managing agent, though you still loop the landlord in
- Meter or billing confusion is an SP Group account matter, and the path for that is in [utilities and telecom setup](/guides/utilities-telecom-setup)

Any quiet-enjoyment conversation needs dates in writing, which is the real reason for all the screenshots ([landlord entry and quiet enjoyment](/guides/landlord-entry-quiet-enjoyment)). And **withholding rent in protest** is still a bad idea, because it hands your landlord a breach of yours to point at ([early lease termination](/guides/early-lease-termination-tenants)).

## A flood week is a different problem

Heavy rain and PUB's flash-flood maps are covered in [flash floods for renters](/guides/flash-floods-singapore-renters). A dry tap during pipe works is supply isolation; a flooded driveway is drainage. They turn up in the same building chat group, but they aren't the same problem and they don't have the same owner.

Central-east and north-east estates on older mains see the most of this. [Kallang](/neighbourhoods/kallang), [Bendemeer](/neighbourhoods/bendemeer), [Potong Pasir](/neighbourhoods/potong-pasir) and [Toa Payoh](/neighbourhoods/toa-payoh) all generate steady forum chatter when a replacement programme rolls through. Walk the lobby notice board before you sign an LOI on what looks like a quiet week.
