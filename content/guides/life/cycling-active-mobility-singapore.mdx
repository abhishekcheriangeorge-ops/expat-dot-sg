**Short answer:** You don't need a licence for an ordinary pedal bike, but on the road a helmet and lights aren't optional. E-bikes are a different story. A PAB has to be LTA-registered and you have to pass a theory test before you ride one, and it's grey imports and footpath riding that get people fined.

The same questions come round on r/askSingapore every few weeks, from tourists and from EP holders who've just landed. Do I need a licence? Am I allowed on the road? Why are e-bikes so restricted here? Singapore's Active Mobility Act answers all three, and it answers them with device standards, path rules and fines that actually get issued. This isn't a "just be careful out there" culture.

## Ordinary pedal bikes

| Rule | Practical takeaway |
| --- | --- |
| No driving licence for pedal bikes | Still obey traffic signals on roads |
| Helmet compulsory on roads | Encouraged on paths |
| Front white + rear red lights / reflectors at night | Mandatory when riding on roads |
| Use cycling paths / PCNs where provided | Give way to slower path users; keep left |
| No expressways / tunnels | Plan Park Connector Network (PCN) routes instead |

If you're finding your feet, East Coast Park and the bigger PCNs are the forgiving places to do it. Where shared use is allowed for pedal bikes, ride as though the pedestrian owns the path, because as far as the etiquette goes they do.

## Power-Assisted Bicycles (PABs / "e-bikes")

This is where newcomers get caught, almost always by buying the bike first and reading the rules afterwards. A legal PAB typically has to:

- Meet EN15194, with continuous assist of no more than 250 W and the assist cutting off by 25 km/h
- Weigh 20 kg or less, under the published technical criteria
- Be type-approved, sealed and registered at an LTA-authorised inspection centre, with a visible registration plate
- Be ridden by someone aged 16 or over who has passed the **mandatory PAB theory test**
- Stay on roads and cycling paths, **not** footpaths

The reason to take that list seriously is the size of the penalties. Riding without a valid theory certificate, or riding a non-compliant PAB, carries published first-time penalties in four-figure and jail ranges. A seller describing a bike as "EU spec" is telling you nothing about whether it clears the LTA bar, so a Carousell listing is not evidence of compliance, however confidently it's worded.

## Personal Mobility Devices (PMDs)

Motorised PMDs, meaning e-scooters and their relatives, have been squeezed hard since the mid-2010s free-for-all. Assume public paths are off limits to them, and assume there's a registration and insurance regime attached, unless you've checked the current LTA device class rules for your exact model. Shipping over the scooter you enjoyed on holiday and reasoning your way to legality from a 2018 forum post is not a plan.

## What cycling here is actually good for

Heat and the afternoon storms will beat heroics, so most people who cycle here do it in hops — bike to the station, then MRT the long leg ([MRT literacy](/guides/mrt-bus-transport-literacy)). Before you buy anything large, work out where it's going to live, because condo bike rooms and HDB yellow boxes fill up and estate rules differ. Cargo bikes especially.

Don't buy one as a car substitute either. Own-car economics still lose for most households ([car vs sharing](/guides/car-ownership-vs-sharing-singapore)), but that's an argument for not buying a car, not an argument that a bike replaces one. A legal bike is a lifestyle tool, not a COE substitute.

Ride the published rules, not the Facebook group's "never been stopped."

One small family note: water bottles and PE kits go missing at speed once kids are biking to enrichment. Name and phone number on the bottle, before the Active Mobility lecture.
