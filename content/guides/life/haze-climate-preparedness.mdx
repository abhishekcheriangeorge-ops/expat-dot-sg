**Short answer:** Use haze.gov.sg or myENV rather than a random AQI widget. The 1-hour PM2.5 reading tells you what to do now; the 24-hour PSI is what you plan tomorrow around, and it's what MOH's advisory bands key off. Stock N95s and a bedroom purifier if anyone in the house is sensitive. And treat **heat as the year-round risk**, not something that only turns up in haze weeks.

Transboundary haze is intermittent but memorable, which is why it dominates the conversation about weather here. Heat stress is annual, and nobody posts about it. Flash floods are the third climate surprise renters tend not to check for ([flood and rental site checks](/guides/flash-floods-singapore-renters)), thunderstorms and high-floor lightning anxiety are the fourth ([lightning and thunderstorm living](/guides/lightning-thunderstorms-singapore)), and dengue-cluster weeks are the fifth ([cluster and fogging ops](/guides/dengue-cluster-living-renters)).

New arrivals routinely confuse third-party AQI apps with Singapore's official PSI and 1-hour PM2.5 readings. Those apps often run on different sensors and different scales, so you end up either cancelling a fine morning or going for a run on a genuinely bad one.

## Two numbers, two jobs

| Metric | Use it for | Orientation bands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-hr PM2.5 (µg/m³) | Deciding now: the run, the CCA, lunch outside | Normal 0–55 · Elevated 56–150 · High 151–250 · Very high ≥251 |
| 24-hr PSI (and the haze-season forecast) | Planning tomorrow: school and training decisions | Good 0–50 · Moderate 51–100 · Unhealthy 101–200 · Very unhealthy 201–300 · Hazardous &gt;300 |

The reason for the split is that the scientific health evidence is largely built on 24-hour exposure. That is why MOH advisories key off PSI bands even while you're watching the hourly PM2.5 chart twitch.

## What to do when PSI rises

Orientation from the MOH tables, which you should verify live. If you're a healthy adult, reduce prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion from Unhealthy (101–200), avoid it at Very unhealthy, and minimise outdoor activity when it hits Hazardous. Children, the elderly and pregnant people tighten earlier, minimising strenuous outdoor activity from Unhealthy. Anyone with chronic heart or lung disease should avoid strenuous outdoor exertion from Unhealthy, and avoid outdoor activity altogether at the higher bands.

N95-style masks help when you have to be outside for a longer stretch during haze. Indoors, with the windows closed and filtration running, they're usually unnecessary. If you have a heart or lung condition, ask a doctor before any prolonged masked exertion.

## Household kit

- Correctly sized N95-style masks for each person in the household
- At least one decent purifier for sleeping rooms, if anyone is haze-sensitive or asthmatic
- Hydration norms for kids' outdoor CCAs in the heat, haze or no haze
- myENV and haze.gov.sg bookmarked, so you're never relying on a foreign AQI widget alone

When PSI spikes, reschedule the outdoor sport and don't make a production of it. This is ordinary regional weather politics meeting biology, not a personal failure of tropical toughness.
