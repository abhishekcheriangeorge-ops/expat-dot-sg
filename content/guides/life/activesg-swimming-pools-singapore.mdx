Fitness threads on the forums swing between "is ActiveSG even open to foreigners?" and "which pool actually has proper lap lanes?" The gym half of that question lives in [sports and fitness](/guides/sports-fitness-singapore). This page is the swimming half: what you pay, how booking works, how not to irritate people in a shared lane, and when a public pool beats paying rent for condo water.

## What it costs and who can get in

You can swim. ActiveSG pools are open to citizens, PRs, pass holders and visitors alike, and the only real difference is which price column applies to you.

| Topic | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| Who can enter | Citizens, PRs, pass holders and visitors. Non-SC/PR pay the Standard Rate column |
| Payment | MyActiveSG+ once you have Singpass. Before that, ring the centre and ask how walk-up payment works there |
| Multi-entry packs | Check membership eligibility live. Facebook screenshots saying "EP holders can buy X" age badly |
| Towels and lockers | Bring your own towel. Locker arrangements differ from centre to centre |

## Lap lanes versus family splash

Not every ActiveSG pool is a serious swimming venue at 7pm on a weeknight. Before you adopt one as your regular, look at:

- When the lane ropes go in, and when the pool flips to free swim
- How busy it gets with school and CCA sessions
- Whether it's a competition pool or a town-centre leisure basin
- How crowded the showers and the deck are after office hours

If you're still deciding where to live, the heartland shortlists around [Queenstown](/neighbourhoods/queenstown), [Bedok](/neighbourhoods/bedok) and the central-east pockets near [Kallang](/neighbourhoods/kallang), [Bendemeer](/neighbourhoods/bendemeer) and [Potong Pasir](/neighbourhoods/potong-pasir) usually win on travel time if you already live west, east or central-east. Map the actual centre you would swim at rather than the town name on the brochure. The commute gap between the two is usually what decides whether you keep going.

## Lane etiquette, so nobody has to say anything

1. Pick the lane that matches your pace. Don't park in the fast lane to chat.
2. Circle swim when the lane is shared, and rest in the corner rather than mid-wall.
3. No diving where the signs say no diving.
4. A waterproof phone is not a deck speaker.
5. Kids on floats belong in family hours, not under the peak lap ropes.

## Heat, haze and thunderstorms

Outdoor decks cook at midday, so swim early or after dusk and save yourself the argument with the sun. On haze weeks, read [haze preparedness](/guides/haze-climate-preparedness) before you commit to a hard outdoor set. Lightning risk means centres clear the outdoor water, and there is no negotiating with the whistle. If the approach road is underwater on a storm evening, that's a [flash flood](/guides/flash-floods-singapore-renters) problem rather than a refund conversation.

## Condo pool versus ActiveSG, honestly

Condo pools win on convenience and on toddlers. ActiveSG wins on lap length, on cost per swim, and on **not paying rent every month for a facility you barely use**. Plenty of year-one expats in HDB flats or suburban condos without proper lanes swim at ActiveSG 2–4× weekly and skip the athletic-club fees entirely.

Keep MyActiveSG+ next to Singpass in your [apps stack](/guides/apps-that-matter-singapore).
