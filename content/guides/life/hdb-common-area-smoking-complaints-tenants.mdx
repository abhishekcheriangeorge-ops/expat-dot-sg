HDB corridor smoke stops being an abstract public-health topic the moment it's your corridor. It's an 11pm argument in a block WhatsApp group about who owns the shared air outside your door, and it generates a lot of complaint volume for exactly that reason. This guide is about the common-area version: corridors, stairwells, void-deck benches. Smoke drifting in from inside the flat next door is a different problem on a different track, and vaping is different again ([vaping & tobacco laws](/guides/vaping-tobacco-laws-foreigners)).

## Which problem do you actually have

| What's happening | Where it routes |
| --- | --- |
| Someone smoking on the corridor, the staircase, or a void-deck bench | OneService and the Town Council common-area path |
| Smoke coming into your flat through a neighbour's windows or door | Neighbour dispute, landlord, quiet-enjoyment writing |
| Vapes or illegal products | Tobacco and vape law enforcement, a different offence class |
| Condo common property | Your MCST and its by-laws, not an HDB Town Council |

Work out which row you're in before you file anything, because the row decides who has the power to act. A Town Council can do something about a bench in a void deck. What happens inside someone else's flat isn't theirs to fix, however far the smoke travels, and a misrouted report just buys you a polite closure notice.

## An evidence pack that actually routes

1. Time and location. Block, floor, and which staircase or void-deck bay.
2. A photo or short video showing the act in the common area. Not a collage of faces bound for a Telegram group.
3. A frequency log. Three nights on record beats one furious message.
4. The OneService submission itself, with a pin drop, and keep the reference number.
5. Optionally, one polite note to the block WhatsApp. Once. Then stop arguing.

It's the same reporting muscle you'd use to pin a [dengue breeding site](/guides/dengue-cluster-living-renters), which is a decent argument for installing the app in week one with the rest of the stack: [apps that matter](/guides/apps-that-matter-singapore).

## What not to do

Don't take it into the corridor. The versions of this that end in assault or property damage all began as a reasonable conversation someone had at the wrong hour. Don't withhold rent because the staircase smells, either. [Quiet enjoyment](/guides/landlord-entry-quiet-enjoyment) is a written-notice route rather than a rent strike, and stopping payment turns your neighbour's behaviour into your problem with your landlord. Fake “HDB ban” posters printed off a meme page help nobody. Neither does quoting condo MCST rules at an HDB corridor; they're separate regimes and the people you're quoting them to know it.

## Estate texture matters

Mature northeast stacks around [Kovan](/neighbourhoods/kovan), [Bartley](/neighbourhoods/bartley) and [Lorong Chuan](/neighbourhoods/lorong-chuan) mix older corridors with condo-edge living. If smoke sensitivity is a hard constraint for your household, walk the staircase at night before you sign the LOI rather than after. Ten minutes then is worth a year of complaints later. And if it ever becomes a safety question, fire or violence, that's [995 or 999](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies). **OneService is not an emergency line.**
