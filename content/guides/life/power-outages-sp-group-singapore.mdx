Singapore's grid is genuinely reliable, right up until the afternoon your lift dies, the freezer starts beeping, and the block chat fills with "is it just my unit?". Outages here are usually short and usually localised to one block or one stack. That is less comforting than it sounds when you live high up, so it's worth knowing roughly what you'll do before it happens.

## Your first five minutes

1. Look at the corridor lights and your neighbours' doors. If they're dark too, this is a block or stack problem rather than yours, and that changes who you call.
2. Only touch your main switch or RCD if you genuinely know what you're doing. If you don't, leave it and wait for someone who does.
3. Open SP Group's outage channels and app notices. Save the contact page to your phone before you need it, not while you're squinting at a dark screen.
4. If you smell burning, see sparks, or hear breaker chatter along with smoke, leave and call **995** ([emergencies](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies)). That is a fire call, not a utility call.
5. Text your landlord or the managing agent once you know the blast radius.

## Lifts, and why floor 30 is a different experience

Treat the lift as unavailable until someone confirms it isn't. Stair fitness stops being an abstraction somewhere around level 30. Keep a torch or a power bank somewhere you can actually find it in the dark, which is not the same as somewhere you know it is.

If anyone in the household relies on a medical device or has mobility needs, arrange assistance with the building staff in advance. The first outage is the worst possible moment to be introducing yourself to the management office. Car park barriers and garage doors can strand vehicles too, so if you were planning to drive somewhere, walk and check first rather than joining the queue at the boom gate.

## Food, heat, and getting work done

Singapore's heat makes fridge and freezer clocks matter more than they would in a cooler climate. Keep the freezer closed, resist the urge to keep checking, and decide early whether you're cooking and eating the things that are thawing rather than gambling on restoration. If you work from home, know which mall, library, or office nearby has power before you're up against a deadline. A sealed bedroom with no aircon is a health question rather than just an uncomfortable night, so ventilate sensibly ([climate and haze habits](/guides/haze-climate-preparedness)).

## Who actually fixes what

Block feeder faults belong to SP Group, or to the building's electrical contractor working alongside SP. A trip inside your unit after you've run five adaptors off one socket is yours, which in a rental means your landlord's electrician rather than the utility hotline.

The other thing people get wrong: switching retailers on the Open Electricity Market changes **who bills you, not who repairs the network**. The same crews still dig up the same feeder ([utilities setup](/guides/utilities-telecom-setup)).

## When it overlaps with something else

Flash-flood evenings sometimes arrive with trips and pump failures attached — see [flood renter checks](/guides/flash-floods-singapore-renters). Haze weeks still demand ventilation discipline when the aircon dies. If the cut follows renovation works next door, document it for your landlord, but don't assume the contractor owes you grid restoration.

## Being a decent neighbour about it

Share the SP update in the group chat once. Repeating it every ten minutes just makes the chat unreadable for whoever is trying to coordinate. Offer stair help to the neighbours who'll need it. And skip the candles: they're a real fire risk in an HDB corridor, so use torches instead.
