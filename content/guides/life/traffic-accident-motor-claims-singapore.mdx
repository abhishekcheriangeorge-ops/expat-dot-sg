**Short answer:** SPF investigates offences. Your insurer settles claims. A minor scrape between local plates usually needs no police report, but it still needs an insurer report under the Motor Claims Framework, commonly **within 24 hours**. Foreign plates, injury, hit-and-run, or damage to government property all mean you lodge with SPF. Don't do the WhatsApp cash settlement.

Two posts turn up on r/askSingapore over and over. "Do I need a police report for a scratch?" and "A Malaysian plate rear-ended me and my insurer says there's nothing they can do." Both come from the same confusion. Traffic Police investigate offences; insurers settle claims. They are separate processes running on separate clocks, and it's the insurer's clock people miss, because it's the one nobody mentions to you at the roadside.

## Do you need the police, or just your insurer?

| Situation | Traffic accident report to SPF? | Insurer / workshop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor scrape, no injury, local plates only | Usually not required | Yes. Exchange particulars and report under the Motor Claims Framework, commonly within 24 hours |
| Fatality | Required | Immediate |
| Damage to government property | Required | Immediate |
| Foreign-registered vehicle involved | Required | Immediate |
| Pedestrian or cyclist involved | Required | Immediate |
| Hit-and-run, with or without injury | Required | Immediate |
| Ambulance to hospital, or 3 days or more of outpatient MC | Required | Immediate |

The rule SPF publishes is straightforward: if none of the mandatory criteria apply, you exchange particulars and take it to your insurer under the Motor Claims Framework. The police are not going to run your body-shop claim, and filing a report you didn't need won't make the workshop move faster.

## What to actually do at the scene

Same list whether it's your own car or a car-share.

1. Hazards on, move to safety if the vehicles can still move, and call 995 if anyone is hurt.
2. Photograph the plates, the damage, the road markings and the weather, and save the dashcam clip.
3. Exchange name, contact, NRIC/FIN/passport number, insurer, and plate.
4. Call your insurer's 24h hotline, or the rental or GetGo incident line, before anyone starts proposing a cash settlement over WhatsApp.
5. Lodge the SPF report if any of the criteria above apply. Don't wait to see whether the other driver stays reasonable about it.
6. Report to your insurer or authorised workshop inside their stated window even if the two of you have privately "sorted it". **A late insurer report can wreck your NCD and your claim rights**, and by then there is nothing anyone can do for you.

That last point is the one that costs real money. The private agreement feels like the calm, adult option at the scene, and it holds right up until the other driver's story changes a week later and you have no report, no assessment and no insurer on your side.

Car-share fleets will often send an assessor to your door and push you towards a TP report. Sometimes that's right, particularly where third parties or hit-and-run criteria are in play. Check it against SPF's list anyway rather than taking the assessor's script as the law.

## Malaysian and other foreign plates

Cross-border recovery is slow and awkward. GIA documents how claiming against foreign motorists works; the pattern people describe on the forums goes like this. Your insurer may want you to make an own-damage claim first and then recover from the foreign insurer, which means your excess sits with them for **months, sometimes years**, while that plays out. Always get the police record when a foreign vehicle is involved, and note that you don't have a choice about it in any case: it's on SPF's mandatory list.

See [GIA's guidance on claiming against foreign motorists](https://gia.org.sg/motor-insurance/22-premium-renewal-of-policy/361-claiming-against-foreign-motorists.html) for the mechanics. If you drive up to JB at weekends, check your policy's geographic wording covers it before you need it to. And if all of this is making you reconsider the car entirely, the ownership maths is in [COE versus car sharing](/guides/car-ownership-vs-sharing-singapore).

## Injury and medical

If you end up needing hospital care, say plainly that it was a traffic accident. It changes how the bill gets routed. Private bills for foreigners still depend on how your motor cover, personal accident cover and employer medical cover stack up, which we cover in [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat).

There's one wrinkle worth knowing. An injury sustained while you're on company transport may also touch [WICA](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep). Ordinary private commuting usually doesn't.

## Licence and demerit points

Once you hold a Singapore licence, the local demerit rules apply to you like anyone else, and the conversion route is in [licence conversion](/guides/converting-foreign-driving-licence).

Worth bracing for: a TP investigation can run alongside an insurer dispute, on its own timetable. Closing one doesn't close the other, so don't read a settled claim as the end of the matter.
