Expat drivers (and GetGo users) hit the same panic loop on r/askSingapore: “Do I need a police report for a scratch?” and “Malaysian plate rear-ended me — insurance says no.” Traffic Police investigate **offences**; insurers settle **claims**. Mixing the two clocks is how people miss the Motor Claims Framework window.

## Police report vs insurer report

| Situation | Traffic accident report to SPF? | Insurer / workshop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor scrape, no injury, local plates only | Usually **not** required | **Yes** — exchange particulars; report per Motor Claims Framework (commonly **within 24 hours**) |
| Fatality | **Required** | Immediate |
| Damage to government property | **Required** | Immediate |
| **Foreign-registered vehicle** involved | **Required** | Immediate |
| Pedestrian or cyclist involved | **Required** | Immediate |
| Hit-and-run (with or without injury) | **Required** | Immediate |
| Ambulance to hospital, or ≥**3 days** outpatient MC | **Required** | Immediate |

SPF’s published rule: if none of the mandatory criteria apply, exchange particulars and follow the **Motor Claims Framework** with your insurer — police do not run your body-shop claim.

<OfficialSource>
Use [SPF — Reporting traffic accidents and offences](https://www.police.gov.sg/Knowledge-Hub/Traffic/Traffic-Matters/Reporting-Traffic-Accidents-and-Offences). Online lodging needs **Singpass**; without Singpass, lodge at an NPC/NPP.
</OfficialSource>

## Scene checklist (own car or car-share)

1. Hazards on, move to safety if vehicles can move, call **995** if anyone is hurt.  
2. Photos of plates, damage, road markings, weather, and dashcam clip.  
3. Exchange name, contact, NRIC/FIN/passport number, insurer, and plate.  
4. Call your insurer’s 24h hotline (or the rental / GetGo incident line) **before** private “cash settlement” WhatsApp deals.  
5. Lodge SPF report when criteria above apply — do not wait for the other driver’s mood.  
6. Report to insurer / authorised workshop within their stated window even if you “agree” privately; late reports wreck NCD / claim rights.

Car-share fleets often send an assessor to your door and pressure a TP report — that can be correct when third parties or hit-and-run criteria apply. Still verify against SPF’s list rather than the assessor’s script alone.

## Malaysian / foreign plates

Cross-border recovery is slow and awkward. GIA documents claiming against foreign motorists; practical forum pattern:

- Your insurer may require an **own-damage** claim first, then recover from the foreign insurer  
- Excess cashflow can sit for **months to years**  
- Always get a police record when a foreign vehicle is involved — it is on SPF’s mandatory list  

See [GIA claiming against foreign motorists](https://gia.org.sg/motor-insurance/22-premium-renewal-of-policy/361-claiming-against-foreign-motorists.html) and keep JB weekend driving inside your policy’s geographic wording — ownership maths: [COE vs sharing](/guides/car-ownership-vs-sharing-singapore).

## Injury and medical

If you need hospital care, say it is a traffic accident. Private bills for foreigners still hinge on motor + personal accident + employer medical stacks — [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat). Work-commute injuries on **company transport** may also touch [WICA](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep); ordinary private commuting usually does not.

## Licence and demerit side-effects

Foreign licence conversion and local demerit rules still apply once you hold a Singapore licence — [licence conversion](/guides/converting-foreign-driving-licence). A TP investigation can run in parallel with an insurer dispute; closing one does not close the other.
