**Short answer:** E-vaporisers are **illegal** here, for visitors and pass holders alike. If you're caught, expect the device to be seized and a composition notice to follow. Repeat offences, and anything involving etomidate (K-pods), can escalate to pass revocation, deportation, and a re-entry ban. This is a briefing on what it does to your pass. It isn't advice on how to get away with it.

The r/askSingapore threads about someone being caught with a vape or a K-pod on a DP aren't hypothetical trivia. The part forums consistently under-weight is the immigration knock-on. For a local this is a fine and a bad afternoon. For you it can be the end of the posting.

## What the public guidance says (check it live)

gov.sg's [Stop Vaping — higher penalties](https://www.gov.sg/stopvaping-penalties/) page, updated into 2026, summarises the Tobacco and Vaporisers Control Act (TVCA) framework and sets out what happens to foreigners specifically:

| Situation | Published consequence theme |
| --- | --- |
| A foreigner found with an e-vaporiser | Device seized, plus a Notice of Composition |
| An STVP holder who re-offends | Banned from re-entering Singapore once they depart |
| Long-term pass holder (EP, S Pass, WP, Student's Pass, LTVP, DP) on a third ordinary vaping offence | The pass or immigration facility may be revoked, with deportation and a re-entry ban possible |
| Etomidate vaporisers (K-pods), or a positive etomidate test | Pass may be revoked, with deportation and a re-entry ban; appeals are handled case by case |
| Import or supply | Far harsher criminal penalties under TVCA and related regimes |

Please don't file "first offence = just S$500" under lifestyle costs. Composition amounts and the escalation rules both change, and in any case the fine was never the expensive part of this. The pass is.

## The myths that show up in the comments

"Tourists are exempt." They aren't. Visitors sit under exactly the same prohibition as everyone else.

"It's only CBD, it's nicotine-free." Marketing labels don't create an exemption. A prohibited vaporiser product stays prohibited whatever the packaging claims about wellness.

"My kid's DP won't affect my EP." Household outcomes are fact-specific, and this isn't a thing to gamble a family's status on. Student's Pass and DP holders also face school discipline on top of national enforcement, which is two separate processes running at once.

"I'll pay the fine and keep the device." You won't. Devices are seized.

"Medication import rules cover it." They don't, because that's a different rail entirely. Bringing approved medicines in under HSA rules is covered in [HSA medication](/guides/bringing-medication-singapore-hsa). A vape is not personal medication.

## If you already use nicotine

Quit before you fly, or switch to legal tobacco products sold under Singapore's tobacco controls, which means age limits, duty-paid retail, and smoking-room rules. Don't post vaporisers to yourself. Don't ask a friend to bring a couple over for the flight.

For quit support, the gov.sg page has historically pointed to the HPB QuitLine on 1800 438 2000. Confirm the live programmes before you rely on any of it, because the QuitVape programme's status changed in 2026 according to that same page.

## Nightlife and the share-a-puff problem

Bars and clubs don't create a legal bubble. Peer pressure on a night out is exactly how Student's Pass holders and younger DP holders end up in the threads above. [Nightlife](/guides/nightlife-social-scene) is social, not a waiver.

If you're already in a police or HSA process, get counsel and notify your employer or school as required. One thing to be clear about: pass cancellation timing only interacts with [STVP between jobs](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore) once employment has ended for other reasons. There's no such thing as a "vape STVP".

A note for families. If you're having this conversation with teenagers over foodcourt dinner, treat it as zero tolerance. The "just a vape outside" folklore isn't worth testing, and foreigner penalties here aren't a mall-uncle rumour.
