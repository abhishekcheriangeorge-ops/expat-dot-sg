**Short answer:** Once you're through the terminal, compare Grab's upfront fare against a metered taxi plus the LTA Changi location surcharge, which is S$8 between 5pm and 11:59pm and S$6 at every other hour. Both options run overnight. Take the MRT only if your bags are light and nobody is waiting on you. And you don't need a wad of SGD cash, as long as you've got a card loaded in Grab or you're in a taxi that takes plastic.

The jet-lagged forum threads will tell you it's always Grab, or always the taxi queue. Neither is right, because which one wins moves around by the hour and by how long the queue is. What actually works is about 30 seconds of fare arithmetic at the kerb.

## Which one to take

| Situation | Prefer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy luggage, kids, rain, or a hard check-in time | Taxi queue or Grab | Door to door, no platform transfers |
| Late night (midnight–6am) | Taxi or Grab | MRT service thins out, and taxis add 50% late-night hiring on the meter (LTA) |
| Light backpack, daytime, hotel in the CBD or out east | MRT plus a short walk or bus | Cheapest, and you start building [SimplyGo habits](/guides/mrt-bus-transport-literacy) early |
| App is showing 1.8× surge and the taxi queue is short | Street-hail taxi | A fixed location surcharge beats an opaque multiplier |
| Taxi queue is 20+ minutes deep | Ride-hail | You're paying for time, not taking sides |

## What's actually on the taxi meter

Street-hailing from Changi is still the classic path. Follow the Taxi signs after baggage claim and join the official queue rather than going with whoever approaches you in the lobby. Stick to the standard liveries too, unless you genuinely mean to pay a premium flag-down for one of the unmarked "premium black" cars.

If you have no SGD cash, ask "Card OK?" before your bags go in the boot. It's a ten-second question, and a much worse problem to discover in your driveway at 1am.

What you'll pay is flag-down plus distance and waiting time, then the Changi location surcharge on top, and possibly a peak (25%) or late-night (50%) hiring charge as well. Those percentages stack on the metered fare, not on the flat location surcharge, which is worth holding in your head when you're eyeballing an app quote against it.

| Changi taxi location surcharge (LTA) | When |
| --- | --- |
| S$8 | 5pm to before midnight, any day |
| S$6 | All other times |

## Grab and the other apps

Download Grab before you fly, and add Gojek or TADA if you want a second quote to compare against. Put your foreign card in while you're on home Wi-Fi, because the OTP fight is far better had on your own sofa than in the arrivals hall on a dying phone.

Then, on the day:

1. Clear immigration and collect your bags first. Booking while you're still airside just means a driver circling.
2. Open the app on airport Wi-Fi or your new [prepaid +65 SIM](/guides/first-week-sim-singpass-bank).
3. Pick the exact pickup pin the app gives you for your terminal or Jewel door. They're specific, and guessing turns into a phone call in bad signal.
4. Read the upfront total before you confirm. Surge and any temporary operator fees are already baked into that number.
5. Walk to the pin. Ignore anyone at the kerb offering to "take Grab cash", because that isn't Grab.

Grab's own Changi transfer pages lean hard on upfront fares and private-hire options precisely so you aren't bargaining with touts, so treat the in-app number as the contract: [Grab's Changi Airport transfers](https://www.grab.com/global/airport-rides/changi-airport/).

## When the MRT is the grown-up choice

One roller bag and daylight? Public transport is genuinely fine, and Changi connects into the network. Tap in with a foreign contactless card, which carries a SimplyGo admin fee of S$0.60/day, or buy a stored-value card instead. The full set of habits is in [MRT and bus literacy](/guides/mrt-bus-transport-literacy). Budget for one extra transfer, or a short Grab for the last kilometre, if you're heading to a walk-up condo.

## Cash, declarations, and the rest of landing day

Keep your bridging cash under the CBNI threshold where you can. If you're carrying more than S$20,000 in physical or bearer value, the declaration has to be filed **within 72 hours before** you arrive, which is the bit people miss because they assume it's something you do at the airport. That's all in the [CBNI guide](/guides/cbni-cash-declaration-singapore).

The SG Arrival Card is a separate obligation with its own window, and still mandatory: [SGAC](/guides/sg-arrival-card-expats). After that it's temporary keys, via the [serviced apartment bridge](/guides/first-housing-serviced-apartment), and then HR on issuance.

Once you're on hotel Wi-Fi, work through the [day-7 checklist](/journeys/arriving/day-7). The hard part was the decision at the kerb, not the map.

One for families: public holiday long weekends make a Changi run tempting, but in a dual-career household someone still has to be the named adult at home when one parent flies. "We'll both half-travel" isn't a plan.
