**Short answer:** Dial **995** for fire, ambulance and medical emergencies (that's SCDF) and **999** for the police. 1799 is the ScamShield helpline, and 1800-777-0000 is the national line for family violence. Your embassy can help with a lost passport and a welfare check. It can't pay your hospital bill or make a fine go away.

## Numbers to know cold

| Number | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 995 | Fire, ambulance and civil defence medical emergencies (SCDF) |
| 999 | Police emergencies |
| 1799 | ScamShield helpline, 24/7: [scams & Singpass](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats) |
| 1800-777-0000 | National Anti-Violence and Sexual Harassment Helpline (NAVH): [family violence](/guides/family-violence-ppo-foreigners) |

Do the boring part now rather than in the moment. Teach your children and your helper the block address in clear English, unit and street and postal code, so they can say it to an operator who has never been to your estate. Condo security can walk responders in from the gate, but only once somebody knows which door. And confirm the current guidance for whatever your own situation is, because none of this is a substitute for the agency's own page on the day.

## Dengue and NEA home inspections

Singapore maps dengue clusters actively and acts on what the map shows. If your estate goes yellow or red, NEA officers may knock and ask to look at bathrooms, kitchens, plant trays and anywhere else water sits still. That applies to condos exactly as much as it applies to HDB blocks, whatever your neighbours tell you. Reddit threads treat the knock as a fine-hunting expedition, which it mostly isn't: the legal frame is vector control under the Control of Vectors and Pesticides Act, and the officer is looking for larvae rather than for you.

A few habits keep it a non-event.

1. Check [NEA's dengue clusters](https://www.nea.gov.sg/dengue-zika/dengue/dengue-clusters) and your myENV alerts when neighbours start mentioning fogging.
2. Empty plant saucers, pails and aircon drip trays on a rhythm you'll actually keep. Hanging it off your [aircon and pest renewals](/guides/aircon-pest-renewals) works well.
3. Verify the officer's ID before anyone comes in, and make sure your helper knows the same rule for the days you're at work.
4. If larvae are sampled, don't pour them away. Officers collect vials for lab confirmation, and any fine that follows arrives in the post rather than as cash at your door.

Living with a cluster day to day is its own topic, from colour changes to OneService breeding reports to fogging in the chute: [dengue cluster living](/guides/dengue-cluster-living-renters).

## Embassies

This is where expectations and reality part company. Consular services help with lost passports and welfare checks. They don't override Singapore law, they can't cancel an NEA fine, and they won't pay your medical bills. Better to know that now than while you're standing at a hospital counter assuming someone will step in. Save your mission's contact details alongside your insurer's and your company's emergency line, so the whole set is in one place on a day when you aren't thinking clearly. And if someone dies, here or overseas, next of kin still have certificates, NEA permits and embassy repatriation sequencing to work through: [death & repatriation](/guides/death-repatriation-foreigners-singapore).

## Frequently asked questions

### 995 or 999, which do I dial?

995 is medical, fire and SCDF. 999 is the police. If you aren't sure and someone is badly hurt, dial 995 first; the operators can cross-route you.

### Can the embassy fix a dengue fine or a hospital deposit?

No. They can advise you and help with documents. The money and the legal outcome stay with you, your insurer and the Singapore agency involved.

### What if a “police officer” calls about Singpass?

Hang up. Real enforcement doesn't ask you to read out OTP codes. Call 1799 or verify through the official apps, and read the [scam playbook](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats) before it happens to you rather than after.
