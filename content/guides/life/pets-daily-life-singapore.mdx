**Short answer:** Daily life with a pet here comes down to three things: your lease, your licence, and your logistics. HDB and AVS ownership limits are not landlord permission, a PALS licence is not condo approval, and public transport is mostly closed to you, so plan around GrabPet, the park connectors, or a car.

Import paperwork gets you through Changi. Then the actual living starts, and that's a different set of problems. The pet threads on r/askSingapore in the weeks after arrival cluster on the same three surprises: the HDB rules changed for cats, the condo MCST still says no, and the MRT is not a pet bus.

For quarantine schedules and AQC booking, stay with [importing pets](/guides/importing-pets-singapore). This guide picks up once the crate is empty.

## Get it into the tenancy agreement, not just the conversation

| Housing type | AVS / HDB ownership limits (verify live) | Where the friction is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HDB | Up to 2 cats plus 1 approved small-breed dog per flat under the current HDB Animals Rules and Cat Management Framework, and no Specified (restricted) dog breeds | The landlord still decides. Plenty refuse pets even where the rules allow them |
| Private condo or landed | Up to 3 cats and dogs combined per premises, with the Specified dog breed rules applying on top; anything more needs case-by-case AVS approval | MCST by-laws plus the landlord's own clause. **A PALS licence is not permission to keep the animal at that address** |

AVS says this plainly: a cat or dog licence does not approve the premises. So disclose the breed, the weight and the number of animals in the LOI, not at handover when the agent is already holding your deposit. The forum pattern is consistent — pet households often pay an extra month's deposit, or simply lose viewings to faster applicants. Budget search time, not just rent.

## Licences, and the household admin behind them

1. Do the AVS Pet Ownership Course, then apply through PALS
2. Microchip cats, which the framework requires, and put the licence renewals somewhere you'll actually see them come round
3. Mesh the windows and the balcony. Falls from height are a genuine welfare and licensing risk in a high-rise, and it's the thing new arrivals underestimate most
4. Update your PALS address when you move flats. Changing your own address against a FIN or pass is a separate admin track, and doing one does nothing for the other

## Getting around with them

Public transport here is generally not open to dogs and cats, assistance animals aside, so you plan around it rather than through it. What people actually end up using:

| Option | When it works |
| --- | --- |
| GrabPet or a pet taxi | Ideal on paper. Drivers are often scarce in practice, so book early and message ahead to confirm they'll take the animal |
| A regular Grab or taxi with a carrier | Small pets in a secure carrier, and ask the driver before the trip starts rather than at the kerb |
| Your own or a shared car | Households out by the park connectors often justify a car on vet and boarding runs alone — [the car ownership maths](/guides/car-ownership-vs-sharing-singapore) |
| Walking and park connectors | East Coast, West Coast and the inland PCNs are the daily exercise grid |

Vets, boarding and grooming cluster around the family belts, which is why East Coast and the west-coast corridors keep turning up on expat shortlists. See [East Coast](/neighbourhoods/east-coast) and [Clementi and West Coast](/neighbourhoods/clementi-west-coast).

## Neighbour diplomacy

Noise complaints and corridor accidents end leases faster than any AVS fine will. Keep litter sealed, leash in common areas, and learn your condo's pet lift and pet path rules on day one rather than after the first complaint lands. The same etiquette applies in the heartlands: [heartland living](/guides/heartland-living-for-expats).

Before you ship crates, get the housing rules pinned down in the [renting process](/guides/renting-process-loi-ta-deposits), and compare [condo versus HDB](/guides/condo-vs-hdb-expat) with pets as a hard filter rather than a soft preference. It's one of the few criteria that genuinely narrows a shortlist.

One family note while we're here. HFMD weeks are human-virus weeks rather than pet ones, but still wash hands before handling the animals, and don't outsource outbreak childcare to a pet-heavy playdate without asking the other household first.
