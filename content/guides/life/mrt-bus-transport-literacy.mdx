**Short answer:** You can ride the MRT and buses on day one with a foreign contactless Mastercard or Visa, but SimplyGo adds a **S$0.60 per day** foreign-card admin fee on top of whatever FX your bank charges. Once your local salary account opens, switch to a Singapore-issued contactless card, or that same card in Apple or Google Pay, and use one credential for both tap-in and tap-out.

For a lot of expats here, public transport quietly becomes the default way of getting around, including plenty who owned a car back home and assumed they'd own one here too. The map isn't the confusing part. How you pay in the first week is.

## Paying for it: the week-one decision

| Method | Best for | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singapore-issued contactless Visa, Mastercard or Amex, or that card in Apple/Google Pay | Residents who already have a local bank card | Use the same physical card or wallet token going in and coming out |
| Foreign-issued contactless Mastercard or Visa | Tourists and brand-new arrivals | **S$0.60 Foreign Bank Card Admin Fee per day of use** through SimplyGo, plus your bank's FX and overseas fees |
| SimplyGo EZ-Link or stored-value card | Kids, visitors who'd rather not deal with FX, heavy riders with no local bank yet | The card cost or deposit, and the faff of topping up |
| Tourist and concession products | Short stays, or students who qualify | Sold at specific SimplyGo counters, and you'll need the eligibility documents |

Adult fares come out the same across eligible contactless and stored-value travel, on the usual distance-based structure. The foreign-card admin fee sits on top of that rather than being baked into it. And do use the exact same credential for entry and exit: tap in with the physical card and out with the Apple Pay version of the same card and you've created an orphan tap, which gets charged at the maximum fare.

## How the first few weeks usually go

1. Landing week: a foreign contactless card is fine for a few days, as long as you've made peace with the admin fee and the FX
2. Once the Singapore bank account is open: add the local card to your phone wallet and retire the foreign one for transit
3. If you've got school-age kids: check the SimplyGo concession card rules, because a Student's Pass or FIN is often required for foreign students
4. Get SimplyGo and a myTransport-style app on your phone for journey planning, and for checking trip history when a fare looks wrong

Peak hours here are genuinely peak. The school run and the CBD stack both need a buffer built into the plan rather than optimism. Buses fill in where the MRT doesn't reach, and the arrival boards in Google Maps, Citymapper or SimplyGo will tell you far more than standing at the stop hoping.

## Etiquette that stops you looking brand new

Stand on the correct side of the escalator, let people off before you get on, keep your voice down, and don't block the doors with luggage during rush hour. Priority seats are for the people who need them, not for your bag.

## When Grab still wins

Landing day from Changi is its own decision, and we've laid that one out separately in [Grab vs taxi vs MRT](/guides/changi-airport-grab-taxi). Settle that before you start optimising your daily habits.

Beyond that, the honest list is short: late at night on a thin corridor, three kids and a stroller in wet weather, or a time-critical ICA or MOM appointment where a missed train costs you more than a Grab does. There's no virtue in refusing to take a car, and nobody is scoring you on it. Most households that stay a while end up using both without thinking about it.
