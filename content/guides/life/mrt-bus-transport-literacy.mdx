**Short answer:** You can ride MRT and buses on day one with a **foreign contactless Mastercard/Visa**, but SimplyGo adds a **S$0.60/day** foreign-card admin fee plus your bank’s FX. After a local salary account opens, switch to a **Singapore-issued** contactless card (or the same card in Apple/Google Pay) and keep **one** credential for tap-in and tap-out.

Singapore’s public transport is the default operating system for many expats who never owned a car — and for many who did. The confusing part is not the map; it is **how you pay** in the first week.

## Payment options (week-one decision)

| Method | Best for | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Singapore-issued** contactless Visa/Mastercard/Amex (or the same card in Apple/Google Pay) | Residents with a local bank card | Same physical card / wallet token in **and** out |
| **Foreign-issued** contactless Mastercard/Visa | Tourists and brand-new arrivals | **S$0.60 Foreign Bank Card Admin Fee per day of use** (SimplyGo) + your bank’s FX / overseas fees |
| **SimplyGo EZ-Link / stored-value card** | Kids, visitors who hate FX noise, heavy daily riders pre-local bank | Card cost / deposit; top-up logistics |
| Tourist / concession products | Short stays or eligible students | Sold at specific SimplyGo counters; eligibility docs needed |

Fares for eligible contactless and stored-value adult travel follow the same distance-based adult structure; the foreign-card admin fee is **extra**. Use the **exact same** payment credential for entry and exit — swapping Apple Pay tokens mid-journey creates orphan taps and maximum fares.

## Practical arrival sequence

1. Landing week: foreign contactless is fine for a few days if you accept the admin fee + FX  
2. After a Singapore bank account: add the local card to your phone wallet and retire the foreign card for transit  
3. Families with school-age kids: check SimplyGo concession card rules (Student’s Pass / FIN often required for foreign students)  
4. Download SimplyGo / myTransport-style apps for journey planning and trip history  

Peak hours are genuinely peak — school-run and CBD stacks need buffers. Buses fill MRT gaps; the bus arrival board in Google Maps / Citymapper / SimplyGo is more reliable than guessing.

## Etiquette that marks you as local-adjacent

Stand on the correct side of escalators, let passengers alight first, keep voices down, and do not block doors with luggage during rush hour. Priority seats are for those who need them — not for bags.

## When Grab still wins

Landing day from Changi is its own decision table — [Grab vs taxi vs MRT](/guides/changi-airport-grab-taxi) — before you optimise daily MRT habits.

Late-night sparse corridors, three kids + stroller + wet weather, or a time-critical ICA/MOM appointment. Do not moralise the mix — most long-stay households use both.
