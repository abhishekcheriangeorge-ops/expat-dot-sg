**Short answer:** Lemon Law and the CPFTA protect consumers buying from Singapore businesses, so your EP or PR status is mostly beside the point. Write to the seller, then take it to CASE for mediation, then to SCT if it is still unresolved. Expect repair or replacement before anyone talks about a refund, and keep your proof. No pass type unlocks a faster track.

The consumer threads on r/askSingapore — the ghosted telco promo, the furniture that never arrived, the dealer who insists Lemon Law doesn't cover COE cars — mix up three different tools. Citizenship doesn't unlock any of them. Buying as a consumer from a Singapore business does. What decides your case is whether you can show what you bought, what you were told, and when you complained.

## Three layers, and the order you use them in

| Layer | What it does | Enforcement power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing to the seller | Fixes most defects and non-delivery | Contract hygiene, nothing more |
| CASE (Consumers Association of Singapore) | Negotiates and mediates consumer-to-business disputes | Soft power, not a court |
| Small Claims Tribunals (SCT) | Binding order for eligible claims | An actual court process |

CCCS administers the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act (CPFTA), but its job is persistent unfair traders rather than your individual refund. CASE is the first contact for individual redress, with STB covering tourists. Starting at CCCS over a one-off sofa refund mostly costs you weeks.

## Lemon Law in one table (goods, not vibes)

Singapore's Lemon Law, the part of the CPFTA dealing with non-conforming goods, gives you remedies when goods are not of satisfactory quality or do not match the contract at delivery. The order matters: repair or replacement first, and only if that fails or is unreasonable do you reach price reduction or rescission. Most people want the sequence to start at "refund". It doesn't.

| Covered | Not covered |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer goods bought in Singapore (electronics, furniture, apparel, motor vehicles, etc.) | Real property (houses, land, fixtures sold as property) |
| Second-hand and discounted goods, with quality judged against age and price | Services, so telco install labour, tuition and spa packages sit outside it |
| Business to consumer sales | Business to business, and peer-to-peer Carousell sales |

You generally lose those remedies if you damaged or misused the item, DIY-repaired it into a worse state, were told about the fault before you bought it, simply changed your mind, or what you are complaining about is ordinary wear and tear. That last category covers more ground than people expect.

One dealer myth worth killing outright: **COE versus PARF status does not cancel Lemon Law**. A used car sold by a business to a consumer can still be a lemon if a latent defect existed at the point of sale. Your evidence and the six-month presumption window decide that, not the registration jargon in the showroom.

## What CASE actually does, and where it stops

CASE assists with consumer-to-business disputes over personal or domestic purchases from registered Singapore companies. Hotline and walk-in advice can be free. If you want CASE to chase the merchant on your behalf, paid membership or fees usually apply.

The limit is the part to understand before you invest weeks in it: CASE cannot force a refund. That is why the Reddit instinct to go straight to SCT when the paper trail is strong isn't unreasonable. Tourists with retailer disputes may be pointed to STB pathways instead; residents use CASE.

Photograph the unboxing, keep the invoices and the WhatsApp threads, and video the serial numbers on the day things arrive. That habit wins more claims than writing "Lemon Law" in a subject line ever has.

## When SCT is the better move

Go to SCT when the seller has stonewalled and your claim fits the Judiciary's eligibility rules on claim type, amount caps and time limits. Tenancy deposit fights carry extra lease-length gates, which we cover in [security deposits and SCT](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) and [inventory and fair wear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear). Furniture that never arrived and phones that never worked properly are classic consumer SCT lanes once CASE has stalled.

## Where expats specifically come unstuck

Buying on a tourist visit, before the EP is issued, is the first one. You are still a consumer and the law still applies, but collection and SCT logistics get much harder once you have left the country.

Putting the purchase on a company card is the second. It can make the transaction look like a business purchase, and Lemon Law is a **consumer** statute.

Buying from a scam "seller" is the third, and it is a different problem entirely. CASE cannot resurrect a mule account, so that one belongs on the [ScamShield and Singpass phishing](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats) track.

Telco free-gift promos are the fourth. These are usually contractual or advertising disputes rather than defective goods, so document the advertised terms first, then take it to CASE or SCT.

For large or cross-border disputes, where SCT ceilings or a company defendant make doing it yourself unrealistic, our [legal counsel directory](/directory/legal/family-law-expat) is where to start looking.

One last thing, gently. Friendly evening chat at the supermarket is not a free CASE consult. Keep defective-goods claims on receipts rather than aisle gossip.
