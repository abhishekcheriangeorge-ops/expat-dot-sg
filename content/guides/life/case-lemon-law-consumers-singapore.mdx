**Short answer:** Lemon Law / CPFTA protect **consumers buying from Singapore businesses** — EP/PR status is mostly irrelevant. Write the seller → **CASE** mediation → **SCT** if needed. Expect **repair/replace** before refund; keep proof. Pass type does not unlock a faster track.

r/askSingapore consumer threads (“StarHub promo ghosted,” “furniture never delivered,” “dealer says Lemon Law doesn’t cover COE cars”) mix three different tools. Citizenship does not unlock them — **buying as a consumer from a Singapore business** does. Pass type is mostly irrelevant; proof and timelines are not.

## Three layers, one order of operations

| Layer | What it does | Enforcement power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Talk to the seller** in writing | Fix most defects / non-delivery | Contract hygiene |
| **CASE** (Consumers Association of Singapore) | Negotiate / mediate C2B disputes | Soft power — not a court |
| **Small Claims Tribunals (SCT)** | Binding order for eligible claims | Court process |

CCCS administers the Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act (CPFTA) for persistent unfair traders. CASE (and STB for tourists) remain the **first contact** for individual redress. Do not start at CCCS for a one-off sofa refund.

## Lemon Law in one table (goods, not vibes)

Singapore’s Lemon Law (CPFTA Part on non-conforming goods) gives consumers remedies when goods are **not of satisfactory quality / do not match the contract** at delivery: typically **repair or replacement** first; if that fails or is unreasonable, **price reduction or rescission**.

| Covered | Not covered |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer goods bought in Singapore (electronics, furniture, apparel, motor vehicles, etc.) | Real property (houses / land / fixtures as property sales) |
| Second-hand and discounted goods (quality judged against age/price) | **Services** (telco install labour, tuition, spa packages) |
| Business → consumer sales | Business → business, or **Carousell peer → peer** |

You generally lose Lemon Law remedies if **you** damaged or misused the item, DIY-repaired it into a worse state, were told about the fault before purchase, simply changed your mind, or the issue is ordinary **wear and tear**.

Dealer myth to kill: **COE vs PARF status does not cancel Lemon Law**. A used car sold by a business to a consumer can still be a lemon if a latent defect existed at sale — evidence and the six-month presumption window matter more than registration jargon.

## CASE: what foreigners should expect

- CASE assists **consumer-to-business** disputes for personal/domestic purchases from registered Singapore companies.  
- Hotline / walk-in advice can be free; **paid membership / fees** usually apply if you want CASE to chase the merchant for you.  
- CASE **cannot force** a refund. Reddit is right that many people escalate straight to SCT when paper trails are strong.  
- Tourists with retailer disputes may be pointed to **STB** pathways — residents use CASE.

Photograph unboxing, keep invoices/WhatsApp, and video serial numbers the day items arrive. That habit wins more claims than citing “Lemon Law” in a subject line.

## When to go SCT instead

Use SCT when the seller stonewalls and your claim fits Judiciary eligibility (claim type, amount caps, time limits). Tenancy deposit fights have **extra** lease-length gates — see [security deposits & SCT](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause) and [inventory / fair wear](/guides/rental-inventory-fair-wear-tear). Furniture non-delivery and phone defects are classic consumer SCT lanes when CASE stalls.

## Expat-specific failure modes

- **Buying on a tourist visit before EP issuance** — still a consumer, but collection and SCT logistics get harder once you leave.  
- **Corporate purchase on a company card** — may look B2B; Lemon Law is a **consumer** statute.  
- **Scam “sellers”** — CASE will not resurrect a mule account; use [ScamShield / Singpass phishing](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats) paths.  
- **Telco “free gift” promos** — often contractual / advertising disputes; document the advertised terms, then CASE or SCT.

Large or cross-border disputes: [family & employment counsel category](/entities/family-law-expat) when SCT ceilings or company defendants make DIY unrealistic.
