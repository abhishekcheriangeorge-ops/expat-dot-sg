Every r/askSingapore secondhand-smoke thread contains the same defeated sentence: "NEA said they cannot do anything because he is smoking in his own home." For private indoor smoking that is often legally accurate. It also isn't the end of the road, because there's a real playbook underneath it. It just doesn't start with NEA.

## Lawful smoking versus an actual breach

| Situation | Where to start | Where not to start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smoke from the neighbour's living room or their own balcony | Mitigation, then a polite ask, then CMC if it continues | Expecting NEA to fine indoor home smoking |
| Smoking in HDB common corridors, void decks or stairwells | OneService, the Town Council, or NEA smoking prohibition where it applies | The condo MCST |
| Smoking in condo common corridors, sky gardens or playgrounds | MA or MCST by-laws, plus the estate's own no-smoking rules | HDB Branch |
| Cigarette butts thrown from height, or litter | OneService, or NEA littering | Opening with a CDRT email |
| Illegal vaporiser use | A separate criminal and composition rail — [vaping laws](/guides/vaping-tobacco-laws-foreigners) | Treating it as neighbour smoke mediation |

The line that decides everything is whether the smoking happened somewhere a rule already prohibits it. On common property there is something for an authority to enforce, so you get a fast answer. Inside the neighbour's own flat there generally isn't, which is exactly why the first rail here is mitigation and mediation rather than a complaint.

## Mitigate first, escalate second

Close your windows around the smoker's schedule, and run an exhaust fan or a purifier in the affected room. Send one polite written note, and date it. Then stop, because repeated corridor confrontations make the mediation stage harder and the living situation worse. Keep a log of dates, times, and where the smoke actually gets in, whether that's the service yard, a bedroom window or the bathroom vent.

If you have young children or someone in the house with asthma, this feels urgent, and emotionally it is. It is still worth being clear-eyed: it doesn't create a statutory indoor-smoking ban next door. What it does do is make the log you are keeping matter more later.

## HDB and condo run on different rails

In an HDB block, common-area smoking and butt litter go to [OneService](https://www.oneservice.gov.sg/) or the Town Council, while drift from inside a private unit goes into the mediation stack instead.

In a condo, what you want is a letter from the MA citing the by-laws or the house rules. Smoke on common property is the easy win there. Balcony-to-balcony drift is the genuinely hard case, and it behaves much like the problems covered in [condo noise and MCST](/guides/condo-noise-mcst-neighbours).

## What mediation and CDRT are actually like

CMC mediation is the middle rail the system was designed around, and it works when both households turn up. That is also its weakness, because attendance is not something you can compel at that stage.

CDRT is for unreasonable interference, and smell and smoke appear as an explicit example in judiciary guidance. Expect cost, expect time, and expect real evidence standards. Bring a dated log covering weeks, not one memorably bad evening.

## Two things to keep straight

Your neighbour's cigarettes are not your immigration problem. **Your** vaporiser is — that runs on a different track entirely, set out in [vaping and tobacco laws](/guides/vaping-tobacco-laws-foreigners). Haze weeks stack outdoor PSI stress on top of the balcony smoke, which is covered in [haze preparedness](/guides/haze-climate-preparedness). And medical emergencies stay on **995**, whatever else is going on ([emergencies](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies)).

Dense older towns such as [Geylang](/neighbourhoods/geylang), [Toa Payoh](/neighbourhoods/toa-payoh) and [Ang Mo Kio](/neighbourhoods/ang-mo-kio) produce the most service-yard drift threads, which is what you would expect when blocks sit that close together. Northwest family towns like [Bukit Panjang](/neighbourhoods/bukit-panjang) and [Boon Lay](/neighbourhoods/boon-lay) aren't immune either, not if your windows face the prevailing breeze.
