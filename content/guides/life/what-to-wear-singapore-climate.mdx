**Short answer:** Dress for the **AC–outdoor swing** — breathable fabrics outside, a light layer for Arctic offices, modest coverage for religious sites, and covered shoes for clubs.

Outdoor heat meets indoor Arctic aircon. Layering is not a winter metaphor here — it is a cardigan in your bag. r/askSingapore expat weather and office-sweat threads ([expat weather](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/khue33/as_an_expat_how_are_you_experiencing_the_weather/), [office wear + MRT sweat](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1fb0quq/singapore_men_wearing_office_wear_and_taking/)) converge on one truth: **cotton that works in dry heat fails in humidity**, and mall AC will freeze you if you dress only for the walk.

## Practical wardrobe

| Context | What usually works | What fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor / commute | Linen, lightweight cotton, technical wicking; breathable shoes that survive sudden rain | Heavy jeans + non-breathable synthetics; “fashion” sneakers that soak |
| Office / MRT | Light outer layer in the bag; change or wipe-down on arrival | Single heavy formal shirt with no spare |
| Religious sites / some government buildings | Shoulders + knees covered; mosque/temple-specific norms | Shorts, sleeveless, see-through fabrics |
| Clubs / smarter bars | Covered shoes, long pants or neat dress — see [nightlife](/guides/nightlife-social-scene) | Slippers, gym shorts at door-policy venues |

Skip shipping an entire temperate wardrobe. Humidity will vote wool and thick denim off the island. Buy locally once you feel the AC–outdoor swing for a week.

## Office sweat stack (forum-tested)

1. Leave early enough that you are not sprinting in humidity  
2. Undershirt or quick-dry base if you blot through shirts  
3. Body wipes / deodorant at the desk; cool under a vent before meetings  
4. Keep a spare shirt at work for client days  

Uniqlo Airism / Heattech debates rage on Reddit — treat them as tools, not religion. Test what your body actually needs in **your** commute.

## Respectful coverage

Mosque visitor desks (e.g. [Sultan Mosque](https://www.sultanmosque.sg/visitors-info/)) often provide loan robes and expect sleeved tops and long bottoms. Hindu and some Buddhist temples ask for modest coverage and shoe removal — see [religious communities](/guides/religious-communities-singapore). When unsure, cover more and ask staff.
