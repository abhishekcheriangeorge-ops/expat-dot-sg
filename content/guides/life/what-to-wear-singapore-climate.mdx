**Short answer:** Dress for the AC–outdoor swing rather than for the weather. Breathable fabrics outside, a light layer in your bag for Arctic offices, modest coverage at religious sites, and covered shoes for clubs.

Outdoor heat meets indoor Arctic aircon, and the gap between the two is what you're really dressing for. Layering here isn't a winter metaphor. It's a cardigan in your bag. The r/askSingapore threads on [expat weather](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/khue33/as_an_expat_how_are_you_experiencing_the_weather/) and [office wear and MRT sweat](https://www.reddit.com/r/askSingapore/comments/1fb0quq/singapore_men_wearing_office_wear_and_taking/) keep landing on the same point: cotton that behaves perfectly well in dry heat gives up in humidity, and mall aircon will freeze you if you've dressed only for the walk from the station.

## What actually works

| Context | What usually works | What fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor / commute | Linen, lightweight cotton, technical wicking; breathable shoes that survive sudden rain | Heavy jeans and non-breathable synthetics; "fashion" sneakers that soak |
| Office / MRT | A light outer layer in the bag; a change or a wipe-down on arrival | One heavy formal shirt and no spare |
| Religious sites and some government buildings | Shoulders and knees covered, plus any mosque or temple-specific norms | Shorts, sleeveless tops, see-through fabrics |
| Clubs / smarter bars | Covered shoes, long pants or a neat dress; see [nightlife](/guides/nightlife-social-scene) | Slippers or gym shorts at door-policy venues |

Don't ship an entire temperate wardrobe over. Humidity will vote wool and thick denim off the island, and you'll just be paying to store things you never put on. Buy locally once you've lived with the AC–outdoor swing for a week and know what your own body does in it.

## The office sweat stack

This is the version forum regulars converge on. Leave early enough that you aren't sprinting in humidity. Wear an undershirt or a quick-dry base if you blot through shirts. Keep body wipes and deodorant at the desk, and cool down under a vent before a meeting rather than walking straight into the room. Keep a spare shirt at work for client days, because the day you need it is never the day you planned for.

The Uniqlo Airism and Heattech debates rage on endlessly on Reddit. Treat those as tools rather than religion, and test what your body actually needs on your commute.

## Respectful coverage

Mosque visitor desks, [Sultan Mosque](https://www.sultanmosque.sg/visitors-info/) among them, often lend robes and expect sleeved tops and long bottoms. Hindu and some Buddhist temples ask for modest coverage and shoe removal, which is worth remembering before you put on complicated footwear — see [religious communities](/guides/religious-communities-singapore). When you're unsure, cover more and ask the staff.
