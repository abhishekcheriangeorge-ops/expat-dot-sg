Opening a salary account is week-one infrastructure. A local **credit** card is a different underwriting problem: zero Singapore credit history, higher income floors for foreigners, and banks that want payslips you do not have yet.

## Income floors are not the citizen ones

Major retail banks commonly set a **higher** minimum gross annual income for foreigners than for citizens/PRs. A concrete, verify-live example from DBS:

| Applicant | Typical DBS principal-card income floor (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Singaporean / PR (≤55) | S$30,000 |
| Foreigner | S$45,000 |

Eligible pass types on the same DBS checklist include EP, S Pass, PEP, LTVP with LOC, Tech.Pass, and International Organisation Pass — confirm the live list before you apply.

<OfficialSource>
Eligibility and document tables: [DBS card eligibility](https://www.dbs.com.sg/personal/support/card-application-eligibility.html) and [application checklist](https://www.dbs.com.sg/personal/cards/cards-application-checklist.page). Other banks publish their own foreigner thresholds — always check the issuer you are applying to.

## Document stack that gets people rejected

| Timing | What banks typically want (foreigner, salaried) |
| --- | --- |
| Employed &gt; 3 months | Passport + work pass (≥6 months validity) + latest ~3 months’ SGD payslips + address proof if you are a new bank customer |
| Employed &lt; 3 months | Passport + pass + latest payslip **or** employer salary letter (dated within ~3 months) + address proof |
| Variable / self-employed | Notice of Assessment often preferred; freelancers get rejected more |

r/askSingapore patterns that match bank checklists:

- Apply week one with **zero** payslips → frequent decline  
- Spray applications across five issuers the same day → more friction, not more luck  
- Salary bank with GIRO credit history → smoother path than a cold application elsewhere  

Use Singpass pre-fill when you have it; new bank customers may still need branch passport verification.

## Secured cards and debit stopgaps

If unsecured credit is declined:

1. Ask your salary bank about a **secured** card backed by a fixed deposit (conversion to unsecured later is often opaque — budget a branch visit).  
2. Use multi-currency debit (YouTrip / Wise / bank FX debit) for overseas spend while you wait.  
3. Build 3–6 months of on-time local repayment on a low-limit starter card before chasing premium miles cards.

Singapore’s bureau logic is not a US FICO clone. “No history” is treated as risk; a tiny limit you repay in full is the usual bootstrap.

## Practical sequence (month 1–3)

1. Finish [bank account opening](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) and get salary credited.  
2. Wait for at least **one** computerised payslip (three is safer).  
3. Apply first at the bank that already sees your payroll.  
4. Keep FX spend on a fee-aware debit until the local card arrives.  
5. Only then optimise cashback vs miles — see [year-one finance](/guides/financial-planning-year-one).

A credit card is optional infrastructure. Payroll, PayNow, and rent GIRO are not.
