Opening a salary account is week-one infrastructure. A local credit card is a different animal, and it catches people out: you land with no Singapore credit history whatsoever, the income floors for foreigners sit above the ones you'll see quoted, and every issuer wants payslips you haven't been paid yet.

## The income floor isn't the one you read about

Most retail banks set a higher minimum gross annual income for foreigners than for citizens and PRs. DBS publishes its numbers, which makes it the easiest worked example, though you should check the live page before you apply:

| Applicant | Typical DBS principal-card income floor (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Singaporean / PR (≤55) | S$30,000 |
| Foreigner | S$45,000 |

The same DBS checklist lists eligible pass types: EP, S Pass, PEP, LTVP with LOC, Tech.Pass and International Organisation Pass. That list changes, so confirm it rather than trusting a screenshot.

And clearing the floor is not the same thing as getting approved. **Meeting the minimum only means you were allowed to apply.** The bank still has to form a view on default risk and on whether your spending will earn it anything, which is why people sitting exactly on the published minimum with no local history get declined so routinely.

## The paperwork, and where it falls apart

| Where you are | What a bank typically wants from a salaried foreigner |
| --- | --- |
| Employed &gt; 3 months | Passport, work pass with ≥6 months validity, your latest ~3 months of SGD payslips, and address proof if you're new to the bank |
| Employed &lt; 3 months | Passport, pass, and either your latest payslip **or** an employer salary letter dated within the last ~3 months, plus address proof |
| Variable pay, self-employed, or on a stipend | A Notice of Assessment is usually preferred, and stipends that never reach IRAS get rejected more often |

The patterns people report on r/askSingapore line up neatly with those checklists. Apply in your first week with no payslips and you'll almost certainly be declined. Fire applications at five issuers on the same day and you get five lots of friction rather than five chances. The bank that already watches your salary land by GIRO is the softest target you have, by some distance.

As for the "GX scored me as no credit history" posts, that's not a conspiracy about your passport. A thin file reads as risk to an underwriter, and the fix is to build history rather than to argue with it.

Use Singpass pre-fill where you can. If you're new to the bank, expect to verify your passport in branch anyway.

## If you get declined

Ask your salary bank about a secured card backed by a fixed deposit. Peer anecdotes usually put the deposit around S$10k, and your limit is normally tied to it. Converting to an unsecured card later is the murky part, so plan on a branch conversation rather than an automatic upgrade.

Otherwise, wait. Go back to the same bank after two or three clean payslips instead of spraying other issuers in the meantime. Multi-currency debit (YouTrip, Wise, or your bank's own FX debit) covers overseas spend perfectly well while you wait. And once a starter card does arrive, run three to six months of on-time local repayment on a small limit before you go anywhere near the premium miles cards.

Singapore's bureau logic isn't a clone of US FICO. No history is treated as risk rather than as a clean slate, and a tiny limit you clear in full every month is how almost everyone bootstraps out of it.

## What the first three months should look like

1. Finish [opening the bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) and get your salary credited there.
2. Wait for at least one computerised payslip. Three is safer.
3. Apply first at the bank that already sees your payroll, which is often the same one you'll use for [Multiplier, 360 or One](/guides/high-interest-salary-accounts-foreigners).
4. Keep foreign spend on a fee-aware debit card until the local card lands.
5. Only then start optimising cashback against miles — see [year-one finance](/guides/financial-planning-year-one).

A credit card is optional infrastructure. Payroll, PayNow and rent GIRO are not.
