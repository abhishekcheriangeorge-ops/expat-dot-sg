Year one is for scaffolding, not for optimisation cosplay. The expensive mistakes nearly always take the same shape: an illiquid status purchase made before you've worked out how volatile your own position here actually is. A condo carrying 60% ABSD. A fresh COE car. Neither of those unwinds gracefully if the pass wobbles.

## Build this first

| Buffer | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| 3–6 months of essential expenses in accessible cash | Go higher if your pass is volatile, or if the household runs on one EP income |
| Clarity on your insurance deductibles | You want to know where the inpatient gaps are before your first A&E visit, not during it. See [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) |
| Rent and utilities on autopilot | Bounce fees and GIRO surprises are entirely avoidable, and embarrassing |
| A remittance plan that doesn't strand you before payday | [Remittances](/guides/remittances-singapore) |
| Exit cash that assumes IR21 | Final pay can freeze while tax clearance runs. See [between jobs and STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore) |

Size the emergency fund off the things you genuinely have to pay every month: rent, school, insurance, food. Not off an aspirational savings rate you read somewhere.

## What can wait until year two

Buying residential property as a non-FTA foreigner means **60% ABSD**, which is a very large cheque to write against a pass you haven't renewed yet. Read [buying property and ABSD](/guides/buying-property-foreigner-absd) before you go anywhere near an OTP. COE car ownership belongs in the same bucket unless your package genuinely funds it, and [car versus sharing](/guides/car-ownership-vs-sharing-singapore) walks through the arithmetic.

Then there's the stuff that finds you rather than the other way round: complex offshore structures pitched at networking events, interlocking whole-life products offered in your first ninety days, and the suggestion that you should be maxing SRS immediately. On that last one, understand the exit tax treatment first ([SRS for foreigners](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner)). Stability first. Sophistication later, once you know you're staying.

## FX and banking hygiene

Open a local operating account as soon as you have address proof ([bank opening](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat)). Keep a home-currency runway until the first Singapore payroll actually clears, because "the salary lands on the 25th" and "the money is in my account on the 25th" are not the same statement in month one. Don't convert the whole relocation lump sum on day one of a strong SGD week without a plan behind it. Bonus-interest products can wait until your salary credits are being recognised by the bank ([high-interest salary accounts](/guides/high-interest-salary-accounts-foreigners)).

## What you're really buying in year one

Optionality, mostly. A lease length that plays nicely with a diplomatic clause, school deposits you could actually stand to forfeit, and a job-search buffer in case COMPASS or a renewal goes sideways. Spreadsheets that assume ten uninterrupted EP years are fiction until you have some renewal history behind you.
