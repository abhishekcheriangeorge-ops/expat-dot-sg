Once payroll starts landing, the question on the forums shifts from "which bank will open an account for an EP holder?" to "why is my Multiplier, 360, or One still paying base rate?" The answer is almost always the same. These aren't magic savings accounts, they're category engines, and they only pay you when they can see the categories firing. Foreigners are perfectly eligible for all of them. What trips people up is that the bank doesn't recognise the salary credit, or that they've gone chasing the insurance and investment categories before the income one is even working.

Treat this as orientation rather than a rate sheet. Rates, floors, and caps all move, so check the bank's own page in the month you actually care about.

## Who can open one (DBS Multiplier as the example)

DBS lists Singaporeans, PRs, and foreigners on an EP, S Pass, DP, LTVP, or Student's Pass among the people who can open a Multiplier, from age 18+, usually through Singpass. You can also carry on without Singpass and upload your passport and FIN documents instead, which is slower but works.

Opening a Multiplier is a separate exercise from opening your first [salary account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat), and it's worth keeping the two apart in your head. Plenty of people bank somewhere else for months and only move payroll once they understand what the bonus rules actually want.

Three things have to be true before any of this pays off. You need a lawful pass and a FIN, since that's the KYC gate and Singpass Face Verification is the fast lane through it. You need an SGD deposit account that can receive payroll, because bonus interest sits on the product balance but income is only recognised from salary-shaped credits. And you need patience for month one, because the first payroll run often lands before the category logic has caught up with you.

## The three products EP holders actually compare

| Product (verify live) | Salary-shaped credit cues | Typical pairing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DBS Multiplier | GIRO, FAST, or PayNow carrying SAL or PAY codes, or SALARY / PAYROLL / COMMISSION / BONUS descriptions | Income plus card and PayLah! spend, loan, insurance, or investment categories above the published floors |
| OCBC 360 | GIRO-SALARY via GIRO or PayNow via GIRO, or FAST and PayNow via FAST carrying SAL, SALARY, SALA, or SAL. The floor people usually quote is S$1,800 a month | Salary bonus stacked with the save and spend categories on OCBC's live board |
| UOB One | GIRO or FAST marked SALA, or PayNow marked PAYNOW SALA. Usual quoted floor is S$1,600 a month, or 3 GIRO debit transactions as the published alternative | Eligible UOB card spend, commonly S$500 a month, paired with either salary or the 3× GIRO route |

Cheques, cash deposits, ordinary fund transfers, and inbound remittance wallets are the usual exclusions across all three. That last one catches a lot of people who assumed money arriving is money arriving.

## The categories in plain English (Multiplier-style)

1. Income, meaning salary, dividends, or annuities credited in the specific way the bank defines as salary
2. Card and wallet retail spend
3. Home-loan instalments, which almost no new EP arrival has
4. Insurance bought after you open the product, since existing policies are often ignored
5. Investments, again bought after you open the product

Parking cash and doing none of the above earns you the base rate, which is the position most people are quietly in when they complain the account isn't working.

## Salary credit: the EP failure mode

| Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Salary arrives but the product shows no income | Ask HR to use the bank's salary GIRO path (peers cite GIRO code 22 on some corporate files) or the exact SAL, SALA, or GIRO-SALARY description that bank publishes |
| You're paid from overseas HQ in foreign currency | Get it converted and credited as local payroll into the SGD account the product watches. Remittance wallets are not salary |
| Freelance or DP LOC income | OCBC, UOB, and DBS may all reject non-employer credits. UOB's 3× GIRO route is the usual workaround when salary codes won't fire |
| Joint-account confusion | Read the issuer's joint rules first, because combined income can attribute in ways you don't expect |
| Insurance not recognised for a foreigner | The ID on the policy has to match bank records. Passport versus FIN mismatches are a well-known Multiplier trap |

The thread running through all of these is that **the bank is matching a pattern, not reading your intent**. Once you see it that way, the fix is usually a five-minute conversation with payroll rather than a new account.

## A sensible year-one sequence

1. Open a working [salary account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) and get one clean payslip through it
2. Only then open Multiplier, 360, or One at the bank that already sees your payroll
3. Add [card spend](/guides/credit-cards-foreigners-singapore) as a category, not peer transfers
4. Leave the insurance and investment category farming alone until you understand [exit tax and SRS](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner)
5. Re-check the rates when your balance gets near the published bonus-interest cap, because everything above it earns base

High-interest cash is a payroll hygiene project more than an investing one. Do it after your rent GIRO and [PayNow](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore) are working, not before. Chasing basis points while your landlord's standing instruction is broken is the wrong order.
