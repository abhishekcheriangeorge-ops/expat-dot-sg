Once payroll lands, Reddit pivots from “which bank opens for EP?” to “why is my Multiplier still on base rate?” Bonus-interest products (DBS Multiplier is the most-cited example) are **category engines**, not magic savings accounts. Foreigners are eligible — the failure mode is almost always **unrecognised salary credit** or chasing insurance/investment categories before income clears.

This is orientation, not a rate tip sheet. Live rates and caps change; verify the bank page the month you care.

## Who can open (example: DBS Multiplier)

DBS lists Singaporeans, PRs, and foreigners on **EP / S Pass / DP / LTVP / Student’s Pass** among Multiplier openers, age **18+**, often via Singpass — or continue without Singpass and upload passport / FIN docs. Opening a Multiplier account is separate from opening the first [salary account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat); many people already bank elsewhere and only move payroll once they understand the rules.

| Prerequisite | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Lawful pass + FIN | KYC; Singpass Face Verification is the fast path |
| SGD deposit account that can receive payroll | Bonus interest sits on the Multiplier balance, but **income** is recognised from salary credits into DBS/POSB |
| Patience for month-one recognition | First payroll often hits before category logic “sees” you |

## Categories in plain English

Multiplier-style products typically sum monthly eligible activity across buckets such as:

1. **Income** — salary / dividends / annuities credited to a personal or joint DBS/POSB account  
2. **Card / PayLah! retail spend**  
3. **Home-loan instalments** (rare for new EP arrivals)  
4. **Insurance** bought **after** Multiplier open (existing policies often ignored)  
5. **Investments** bought **after** Multiplier open  

Bonus interest usually needs income **plus** one or more other categories above a published dollar floor — or a young-adult spend-only path. Parking cash alone earns the **base** rate.

## Salary credit — the EP failure mode

Banks look for payroll shaped like payroll. DBS’s published cues include GIRO / FAST / PayNow credits with codes **SAL** / **PAY** or descriptions such as **SALARY** / **PAYROLL** / **COMMISSION** / **BONUS**. Manual transfers labelled “Abhishek rent share” do not count.

| Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Salary arrives but Multiplier shows no income | Ask HR to use the bank’s salary GIRO code (peers cite **GIRO code 22** for corporate files) or a SAL/PAY description |
| Paid from overseas HQ in FX | Convert/credit as local payroll into the SGD account the product watches — remittance wallets are not salary |
| Joint-account confusion | Read the issuer’s joint rules; combined income may attribute oddly |
| Insurance not recognised (foreigner) | ID on the policy must match bank records (passport vs FIN mismatches are a known Multiplier/Manulife trap) |

## Sensible year-one sequence

1. Open a working [salary account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) and get one clean payslip  
2. Only then open Multiplier (or rival category account) at the bank that already sees payroll  
3. Add [card spend](/guides/credit-cards-foreigners-singapore) / PayLah! retail — not peer transfers  
4. Skip insurance/investment category farming until you understand [exit tax and SRS](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner)  
5. Re-check rates when balance approaches the published bonus-interest cap  

High-interest cash is a **payroll hygiene** project. Treat it after rent GIRO and [PayNow](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore), not before.
