**Short answer:** A local account is what unlocks salary credit, GIRO and PayNow, and the usual way it goes wrong is arriving on an IPA, living in temporary housing, and having no proof of local address to hand the bank. Line up an address letter from the serviced apartment or from HR, get a +65 number, finish issuance and Singpass, then open the payroll account. Most personal accounts expect lawful local presence and a FIN.

That address-proof loop is the one everybody hits. You can't get the account without an address, and you can't get a lease signed and stamped in your first week, so you sit in the gap while payroll asks for bank details. There's a second failure mode worth naming too, because it's the one people argue about hardest: trying to open a personal Singapore account with no FIN and no local status at all.

## The documents banks ask for

| Document | What the bank is checking |
| --- | --- |
| Passport | That you are who you say you are |
| Work or Dependant Pass, or an IPA under some programmes | The basis on which you're living and working here |
| Proof of address | Local presence. A TA, an employer letter, or an accepted temporary-housing letter |
| Employment confirmation | Why you need a salary account at all |
| Local mobile number | App onboarding and OTPs, which is not optional in practice |

What each bank actually wants varies, and it varies again depending on whether you're salaried, a dependant, or incorporating. Check the live checklist on the bank's own site in the week you apply, not the version someone posted last year.

## Breaking the address chicken-and-egg

There are four moves that keep working, and you can usually stack them. Ask your serviced apartment or hotel for a letter confirming your stay address, since licensed serviced residences are the bridge banks are most used to seeing. Ask HR for a letter on company letterhead stating your temporary or permanent address. Use a signed tenancy agreement or stamped lease the moment you have one. And ask which bank payroll already partners with, because friction drops noticeably when HR introduces you rather than when you turn up cold.

Some banks are considerably pickier than others about IPA-only openings. If payroll is only days away, escalate through HR instead of arguing your case alone in a branch queue. HR has done this before and has a phone number you don't.

## “I don’t live in Singapore, which bank will open a personal account?”

The honest answer, from both forum consensus and how bank KYC actually works, is that **most retail personal accounts expect a lawful local presence**, which for many products means long-term status plus a FIN. Owning a Singapore company doesn't quietly unlock a personal FIN-based salary account either; those are different rails. Premium and Treasures-style pitches aimed at non-residents still have to run AML and KYC, so plan on an in-person branch visit, a source-of-funds narrative you can actually evidence, and a genuine chance of being refused anyway.

What people use as a bridge in the meantime: an international bank where you already hold a relationship, HSBC or some regional banks, with a cross-border introduction from your existing branch; or a MAS-licensed remittance or MPI wallet such as Wise to handle FX while you wait, which is **not a payroll substitute** ([remittances](/guides/remittances-singapore)). Founders have a separate corporate account path, with stricter KYC again.

One thing not to improvise: don't fly in with a suitcase of seed capital above S$20k without reading [CBNI declaration](/guides/cbni-cash-declaration-singapore) first.

## Singpass timing

Once you have a FIN and the pass is issued, or MOM registration has created Singpass for you, both banking and government workflows get dramatically easier. The catch that people report over and over is that **IPA-only Singpass registration often fails until issuance**, so don't build a week-one plan around digital KYC you can't yet do. Treat getting Singpass live as a priority the moment the pass is issued rather than as administrative busywork you'll get to eventually.

## High-interest accounts wait for clean payroll

Multiplier-style products pay on recognised salary credits, not on a healthy balance. Open the ordinary salary account first, let a payroll run go through, and chase [bonus-interest categories](/guides/high-interest-salary-accounts-foreigners) once HR's payroll description has cleared the bank's income rules. Doing it in the other order just produces a product you don't qualify for yet.

## US persons and FATCA friction

US citizens and green-card holders usually face extra forms and slower onboarding. Peers sometimes report smoother US-person processes at particular banks, though policies change often enough that it isn't worth treating any recommendation as durable. Budget an extra branch visit and bring your tax IDs with you.

## Founders versus salaried EP

| Profile | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Salaried EP | A personal salary account is the default path and usually the easy one |
| Dependant | You may need the principal's employment documents, and product eligibility varies |
| Founder or EntrePass | Corporate KYC: constitution, shareholding, and a narrative for the business activity |

Corporate KYC is meaningfully stricter than a personal salary account. Turn up with a clean ownership chart and you'll save yourself a round trip.

## The first 30 days

1. Confirm payroll's preferred bank with HR before wheels-down.
2. Get the temporary address letter lined up before you need it.
3. Open the personal account, then set up GIRO for levy and utilities where relevant.
4. Register PayNow, preferring your FIN over a recycled prepaid mobile number, since recycled numbers can still receive someone else's payments. [PayNow setup](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore).
5. Only then go looking at multi-currency and investment products.

Banking here is infrastructure, not a lifestyle brand choice. Optimise for payroll landing on time, and upgrade the products later.
