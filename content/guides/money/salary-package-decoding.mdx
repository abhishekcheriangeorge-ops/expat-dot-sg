Reddit’s r/askSingapore is full of “is S$X enough?” threads, and the headline salary is never the useful answer. What you want is **fixed monthly cash, after tax, after rent, after school**, with honest assumptions about whether your spouse will be able to work. That last assumption quietly decides more of these threads than the salary does.

## Foreigners and CPF

Most Employment Pass holders do not receive CPF contributions the way Singapore citizens and PRs do. So when you’re comparing a Singapore offer against a local package, don’t mentally import a ~17% employer CPF top-up into yours. It isn’t there, unless HR has explicitly structured something different and lawful, and usually they haven’t. The full picture is in [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners).

<OfficialSource>
CPF rules for citizens/PRs live on the [CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/). EP eligibility and salary definitions live on [MOM’s Employment Pass pages](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass) — allowances that are not fixed monthly salary may not count toward the EP floor or COMPASS salary factor.
</OfficialSource>

## Fixed monthly salary vs AWS and bonus, as MOM defines it

For EP floors and COMPASS, **fixed monthly salary means basic monthly salary plus fixed monthly allowances**. That is the whole definition. It does not include AWS (13th month), bonuses, commissions, overtime, variable allowances, reimbursements, in-kind benefits, board fees, or stock and dividends. Which is why a package that looks generous in a recruiter’s deck can still sit under the floor when MOM does the arithmetic.

| Counts toward EP fixed salary? | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Yes, if it is genuinely fixed and monthly | Basic pay; a fixed food or housing allowance that doesn’t vary |
| No | AWS or 13th month; performance bonus; commission; overtime; variable transport; equity |

There’s a second trap underneath that one. MOM may still reject a filing where fixed allowances are a major share of the total and clearly should have been basic salary, so loading the allowance side to clear a threshold can backfire. Get the **fixed monthly salary figure that will appear on the EP application** in writing, from someone who understands what that phrase means. Not a “CTC including 2 months bonus” slide.

## Model the monthly cash before you sign

Do this once, on paper, while you can still walk away:

1. Start with the fixed monthly salary, which is the pass-relevant number
2. Subtract the estimated tax cadence. Resident progressive rates apply once you qualify, and non-resident treatment is different; [IRAS tax residency](/guides/iras-tax-residency-filing) sorts out which one you are
3. Subtract rent and utilities for the neighbourhood you will actually live in, not the cheaper one you have been telling yourself about
4. Subtract the insurance gaps: outpatient, maternity, dental
5. Subtract school and childcare, where that applies
6. Whatever is left is lifestyle, savings, remittances and flights home

Bonuses and RSUs are real money, but they’re lumpy money, and landlords don’t accept lumps. Singapore generally taxes share awards at vest and options at exercise, and there’s a deemed-exercise trap waiting for you on the way out. [RSU and ESOP tax](/guides/rsu-esop-tax-singapore) goes into it properly.

## What the forums get right about “comfortable”

These are the order-of-magnitude patterns that keep resurfacing in askSingapore threads. Treat every one as a placeholder to be replaced with your own quotes.

| Household shape | Recurring community signal |
| --- | --- |
| Dual-income couple, no kids | Combined ~S$10k+/month is often called “comfortable” if the rent isn’t CBD-prestige |
| Single earner ~S$120k/year with a spouse job-hunting | Frequently described as tight once a mid-range condo rent lands, often ~S$4–5k |
| Family with international schools | School fees can dominate everything; plenty of locals will tell you a package without housing or school support shouldn’t bring kids |

These are peer heuristics rather than official thresholds. Use them to stress-test your assumptions, then rebuild the real number with [cost of living by household](/guides/cost-of-living-by-household).

## The negotiation levers that move a household

Push on the things that change monthly cash or reduce risk. A better title costs the company nothing and does nothing for your budget.

| Lever | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- |
| The split between fixed salary and allowance | It drives pass thresholds, COMPASS, and tax treatment |
| Cash housing allowance vs a company lease | Cash housing is usually taxable employment income; a company lease shifts the negotiation burden but is still not “free rent” as far as IRAS is concerned |
| A school fee cap, and which children it covers | Waitlists mean a cap existing is not the same as a seat existing |
| Spouse career support | Coaching, agency introductions, a real dual-career policy; see [trailing partner careers](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore) |
| Shipping and temporary housing days | They stop you making a condo decision under pressure in week one |
| Notice, probation and relocation clawback | Your household is mid-move, so early-exit risk falls much harder on you than on them |
| The insurance schedule | Private hospital bills with no cover behind them undo the whole “comfortable” calculation |

## A negotiation script, with specific asks

Put these to HR or the hiring manager in writing. Verbal reassurance in an offer call doesn’t survive a reorg, and you may need to point at something later:

1. Confirm the fixed monthly salary figure that will appear on the EP application
2. Itemise housing, school, flights and shipment as cash, reimbursed, or in-kind, because the three are taxed and felt very differently
3. Ask whether school support means corporate nomination seats or only fee reimbursement, since a subsidy is not a seat
4. Ask for a benefits PDF setting out outpatient, specialist and hospital caps, and whether dependants are on it
5. If a spouse is relocating without a job, ask for a career coaching stipend or introductions. It is far cheaper for the company than a failed dual-career posting, and they know that
6. Clarify tax equalisation or protection, which is common in some MNCs and rare on local packages

## COMPASS belongs in the compensation conversation

Raising fixed salary can be the difference between a clean COMPASS pass and a messy appeal, which makes it an offer-design constraint rather than something HR tidies up afterwards. Have that conversation before you resign overseas, not after. Treat [COMPASS](/guides/compass-framework-explained) accordingly. The age-banded EP floors belong in the same discussion, starting from **S$5,600** for non-FS roles and **S$6,200** for FS at the youngest band in the current tables. More on the comparison in [EP vs S Pass](/guides/ep-vs-s-pass-comparison).

## Job-hop cashflow: IR21 applies even when you stay

Changing Singapore employers without leaving the country still usually means the leaving employer files **Form IR21** and withholds monies due to you until IRAS clears your tax. Starting the new job immediately doesn’t erase that clock. What it does is open a path to ask IRAS to continue GIRO once you have the new IPA and contract in hand. So budget rent for the freeze window instead of assuming the two paydays join up neatly, because they often don’t. The operational sequence is in [changing jobs on EP](/guides/changing-jobs-employment-pass), the detail in [tax clearance when leaving](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving), and the official version in [IRAS’s employee tax-clearance FAQs](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/employees/scenario-based-faqs-for-working-in-singapore-and-abroad/tax-clearance-for-non-singapore-citizen-employees).
