Living across two currencies leaks money, and it leaks quietly. Nobody sends you an invoice for a bad exchange rate. The other thing that catches new arrivals is that three things which sound related behave nothing alike: PayNow moves money inside Singapore only, cross-border remittance is a separate product with its own failure modes, and physically carrying cash is governed by a different regime again.

## Compare the all-in rate

A "free transfer" with a poor exchange rate isn't free. You paid the fee inside the rate instead of on the receipt, which is precisely why it feels cheaper. Compare the whole picture:

| Lever | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Mid-market vs offered rate | The silent fee |
| Upfront fee | Flat or a percentage |
| Speed / cut-off | Same-day marketing vs when the money actually lands |
| Recipient bank charges | Correspondent fees on SWIFT |
| Limits / KYC | How much friction the first large transfer hits after you open the account |

Specialist FX providers usually win on rate if you're sending money home every month. Your Singapore bank may win on convenience for the occasional one-off wire. Whichever you settle on, re-price it every six months, because spreads move and the provider that was obviously best when you arrived often isn't the one that's best now.

The r/askSingapore inward-remittance threads keep converging on the same stack, and it's a sensible one. A transparent mid-market specialist such as Wise for the recurring FX. A bank telegraphic transfer when the counterparty only accepts SWIFT. And never a suitcase for an amount that would trigger a [CBNI declaration](/guides/cbni-cash-declaration-singapore).

## PayNow vs international rails

PayNow moves SGD between participating Singapore banks and wallets using your NRIC or FIN, your mobile number, or a UEN as the proxy. Once your FIN is linked it's the easiest way to pay rent, your helper, and local vendors, and you'll use it constantly.

Two things go wrong at arrival: recycled prepaid SIMs, and the assumption that having a mobile number means PayNow registered itself. It does not. Our [PayNow setup guide for foreigners](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore) walks through the steps.

What PayNow won't do is cross a border. Leftover salary going home, school fees abroad, supporting family, all of that still needs an FX product or a bank telegraphic transfer.

Receiving money on PayNow doesn't by itself create a tax event. What matters is what the money actually is, whether that's salary, a gift, or business income, because that's what IRAS and your home tax authority care about rather than the pipe it travelled down. If you fire a payment at the wrong proxy, we've written up what to do next in [PayNow and e-payment recovery](/guides/wrong-paynow-epayment-recovery).

## Wise and other MPIs aren't "a Singapore bank"

Peer advice on this is usually "just open Wise," and as far as it goes that's fine. In Singapore these specialist providers are typically licensed as Major Payment Institutions, or under a similar MAS category. That makes them genuinely good at FX and at holding balances. It does **not** make them a substitute for a local bank salary account, for GIRO, or for some kinds of credit underwriting. Use them as a bridge and still finish [opening a bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) for payroll and long-term admin.

## Inward remittance and source-of-funds

Money arriving in bulk — a large gift, property sale proceeds, family returning cash you previously remitted home — clears through a bank or a licensed remittance provider with no separate ICA CBNI form. That form covers cash in your bag, not wires. What the bank or provider can still ask for is paperwork: a relationship or gift letter, the sale contract or statements behind the money, and a plain account of what the funds are for, such as family maintenance, investment, or living costs.

That's AML hygiene, not an IRAS "remittance tax." Taxability follows the source of the income, not the pipe it came down, and [foreign income](/guides/foreign-income-and-tax) goes into how that works. Physical cash or bearer instruments above **S$20,000** when you fly are the other regime entirely, covered in the [CBNI guide](/guides/cbni-cash-declaration-singapore).

## Habits that help

Batch your transfers monthly rather than panic-sending on a bad mid-market day. Keep a small buffer in your home currency for the commitments that don't move, like a mortgage, parents, or subscriptions.

Document the large transfers while the documents are still easy to lay hands on: contracts, sale proceeds, gift letters. You may want source-of-funds clarity later for a mortgage, private banking, or PR paperwork somewhere else, and filing it now is a great deal easier than reconstructing it then.

Finally, line your remittance timing up with your [tax residency](/guides/iras-tax-residency-filing) cashflow. The first IRAS bill often lands the year after you arrive, so money that felt spare in year one is sometimes needed here in year two.
