**Short answer:** Singapore doesn't cap how much physical cash you can carry in or out. What it does is require a report. If your total Physical Currency and Bearer Negotiable Instruments (CBNI) come to more than **S$20,000** or the foreign-currency equivalent, you file Form NP 727 online within 72 hours before you arrive or leave. Bank wires aren't this form. Since 13 May 2024 a successful e-filing is enough on its own, so keep the acknowledgement somewhere you can find it at Changi.

"What's the cash limit into Singapore?" comes round on r/askSingapore more or less monthly, and the answer catches people out in both directions. There's no legal ceiling on the amount you may carry. There is a hard reporting duty once you go past the threshold, and it's the reporting, not the carrying, that gets prosecuted.

## What counts as CBNI

If you enter or leave Singapore carrying CBNI worth more than S$20,000 in total, you owe the Singapore Police Force a full and accurate Form NP 727 report. That total isn't only your own money: it covers what you carry on behalf of someone else, whether you're travelling alone or with others.

CBNI means notes and coins, plus bearer instruments — traveller's cheques, some bearer cheques, money orders, promissory notes and their relatives. What it doesn't mean is money moving electronically. A bank wire or a licensed remittance credit into your Singapore account runs on entirely different rails with its own paperwork, and no NP 727 anywhere in it.

| What people believe | What's actually true |
| --- | --- |
| "S$20k is the maximum you can bring" | No. Nothing caps the amount; the duty is to declare above S$20k |

| "S$15k feels risky, I'll declare anyway" | The duty starts above S$20k. Carrying less isn't an offence you need to atone for |

## Filing it, and the clock that matters

Start by counting everything. All the physical cash and bearer instruments you will take across the border, including anything you're carrying for somebody else. Then submit online within 72 hours before you arrive or depart, which is a narrower window than most people assume: a fortnight ahead is too early, and the departure gate is too late.

Save the summary page and the acknowledgement email somewhere you can reach on a phone with bad airport wifi. If your dates or amounts change after you file, void the old declaration and submit a fresh one rather than letting a stale form ride. **Inaccurate reporting is an offence in the same way non-reporting is**, and "the number changed after I filed" is not a conversation you want to be having at a checkpoint.

At the checkpoint, be ready to show the acknowledgement if an officer asks for it. Since 13 May 2024, lodging a paper NP 727 at the Red Channel is no longer the default path.

The form wants your personal particulars, the CBNI type, currency and amount, the source of the funds, and the recipient if you're carrying for someone else. Keep the source-of-funds proof with you rather than in a shipping container: sale proceeds, a gift letter, a bank withdrawal slip.

## Why "just don't declare" is terrible advice

Failing to report CBNI above S$20,000, or reporting it inaccurately, is an offence under the Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes (Confiscation of Benefits) Act. Published enforcement reminders cite fines up to S$50,000, imprisonment up to three years, or both, and the undeclared money itself can be seized or confiscated on top of that. The deterrence messaging has been noticeably louder from mid-2024 onwards, and that is deliberate rather than accidental.

## Suitcase or wire

For a household move or a large family gift, the wire wins almost every time. Once your KYC is open, send it as a licensed remittance or a bank wire into your Singapore account; [our remittances guide](/guides/remittances-singapore) covers which product to use. If you genuinely have to fly with cash or bearer instruments above S$20k, declare early and travel with the documentation rather than with an explanation.

What you shouldn't do is split the money across several travelling companions so that each of them stays under S$20k. SPF and ICA guidance already addresses carrying for others and travelling with others, so the aggregate picture is the one that counts, and arranging yourself around a threshold reads exactly as badly as it sounds.

Wiring the money isn't the end of the questions either. A large digital inflow can come back to you months later when a bank starts asking about source of funds for a mortgage, a private banking relationship, or a routine AML review. Clearing the airport cleanly doesn't substitute for a paper trail that explains how the [foreign income](/guides/foreign-income-and-tax) was characterised.

## Landing with seed money

If you're arriving before a bank account exists, carry enough cash to cover the first stretch of living costs and keep it under the threshold where you can. Get from [Changi to the hotel](/guides/changi-airport-grab-taxi) without a suitcase of notes, open banking quickly ([opening a bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat)), then wire the rest across once the account is live. Suitcase financing is a last resort, not a flex.
