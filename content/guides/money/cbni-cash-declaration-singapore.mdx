r/askSingapore keeps asking “what’s the cash limit into Singapore?” The answer that trips people: **there is no legal ceiling on how much physical currency you can carry**. There **is** a hard reporting duty once the total exceeds **S$20,000** (or foreign-currency equivalent).

## What must be declared

If you enter or leave Singapore carrying **Physical Currency and Bearer Negotiable Instruments (CBNI)** exceeding **S$20,000** in total value — for yourself or on behalf of someone else, alone or with others — you must submit a full and accurate **Form NP 727** report to the Singapore Police Force.

CBNI includes notes and coins plus instruments such as traveller’s cheques, some bearer cheques, money orders, promissory notes, and similar bearer instruments. Bank wires and licensed remittance credits into your Singapore account are **not** this form — different channel, different paperwork.

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| “S$20k is the maximum you can bring” | Wrong — declare above S$20k; amount itself is unrestricted |
| “Bank transfer also needs CBNI” | Wrong — CBNI is **physical / bearer** movement across the border |
| “S$15k is risky so I should declare anyway” | Declaration duty starts **above** S$20k; under-threshold cash is not the offence |
| “I can skip it if I’m just transiting” | Check live ICA/SPF guidance for your itinerary — the duty attaches to movement into/out of Singapore |

## How to file (clock that actually matters)

Submit the CBNI declaration **online within 72 hours before** arrival in or departure from Singapore. If travel dates or amounts change, **void** the old declaration and file a new one — stale forms are how accurate-reporting offences happen.

You will typically need:

- Personal particulars  
- CBNI type, currency, and amount  
- Source of the funds  
- Recipient (if carrying for someone else)

Keep source-of-funds proof handy (sale proceeds, gift letter, bank withdrawal slip). Officers may ask; declaration ≠ interrogation theatre, but unexplained bulk cash invites questions.

## Penalties (why Reddit “just don’t declare” advice is bad)

Failure to report, or inaccurate reporting, of CBNI above S$20,000 is an offence under the Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes (Confiscation of Benefits) Act. Published enforcement reminders cite fines up to **S$50,000**, imprisonment up to **three years**, or both — plus possible confiscation of undeclared amounts. Enhanced deterrence messaging from mid-2024 onwards is intentional.

## Suitcase vs remittance (practical choice)

For household moves and large gifts:

1. Prefer a **licensed remittance / bank wire** into your Singapore account once KYC is open — see [remittances](/guides/remittances-singapore)  
2. If you must fly with cash or bearer instruments above S$20k, **declare early** and carry documentation  
3. Do not structure multiple companions each under S$20k as a clever workaround if the true picture is aggregated carriage — SPF/ICA guidance covers carrying for others and travelling with others  

Large digital inflows can still trigger **bank source-of-funds** questions later (mortgage, private banking, AML). Declaration compliance at the airport does not replace clean paper trails for [foreign income](/guides/foreign-income-and-tax) characterisation.

## First-arrival habit

If you are landing with seed money before a bank account exists, budget a short window of cash for living costs **under** the threshold where possible, open banking quickly ([opening a bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat)), then wire the rest. Suitcase financing is a last resort, not a flex.
