Relocation packages feel like free money right up until IRAS season. Before you spend the lump sum on furniture, split the package into three buckets in your own head: reimbursements made against receipts, cash allowances, and ongoing benefits such as housing, a car, or school fees. They don't all get treated the same way, and the one that stings later is usually the cash you already spent.

## Ask HR these five questions

Ask early, and ask for the answers **in writing**. A recruiter's verbal reassurance doesn't help you much once an assessment lands.

1. Is the relocation cash an allowance, which is likely taxable, or a receipted reimbursement?
2. Which flights, hotels, and temporary housing does the company pay directly, and which do you pay and claim back?
3. Are you on tax equalisation, tax protection, or neither? Those are three different promises.
4. Will the relocation items appear on your IR8A / Appendix 8A?
5. Who owns year-one filing support: a Big Four firm, in-house mobility, or you on your own?

## Where each piece usually lands

| Item | Usually ask |
| --- | --- |
| Lump-sum “settling-in” cash | Taxable unless HR documents otherwise |
| Receipted moving invoices paid by employer | Often cleaner if policy-compliant |
| Ongoing housing allowance | Benefit / income characterisation matters |
| Home-leave flights | Check benefits-in-kind rules |

None of this is a reason to panic. It's a reason to keep a buffer, because if the allowance is later classified as taxable, the Notice of Assessment turns up long after the sofa did.
