**Short answer:** Call **your bank** immediately and open an **erroneous transaction** case. Do **not** WhatsApp the stranger for a peer refund. If you received the money by mistake, let the banks reverse it — never PayNow a stranger “back.” Recovery is a MAS process duty, not a same-day guarantee.

r/askSingapore’s weekly “I PayNow’d the wrong number” post has one correct first move and one dangerous instinct.

## What “erroneous transaction” means

MAS [E-Payments User Protection Guidelines](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/e-payments-user-protection-guidelines) (EUPG) Section 6 covers payment mistakes from a **protected account** to the **wrong recipient** (PayNow / FAST-style transfers — not the same as every card dispute). When you notify your FI and your FI notifies the recipient’s FI, **both FIs should make reasonable efforts** to recover the sum.

That is a **process duty**, not a guaranteed same-day clawback. If the recipient refuses, banks escalate; police reports enter the picture when recovery stalls.

## If you sent the money

1. Screenshot the transfer (proxy, name shown, amount, time, reference)  
2. Call **your** bank’s hotline from the number on the card/app — not a Google ad  
3. Ask explicitly for an **erroneous / mistaken transfer** recovery under the EUPG process  
4. Provide the info the bank requests (amount, date/time, recipient proxy, your account)  
5. Wait for bank-to-bank contact — weeks happen when the other side is slow  
6. If the recipient ignores the bank, follow the bank’s advice on a **police report**

Do **not** send a second “correct” transfer until the first case is logged. Do **not** pay a “recovery agent” fee — [scam hygiene](/guides/scams-singpass-phishing-expats).

## If you received the money

1. Tell **your** bank you received an unexpected PayNow  
2. Tell any stranger who DMs you to go through **their** bank  
3. Consent to the **bank-led reversal** when your bank asks (letter / branch / recorded call)  
4. Never reverse by manually PayNowing a stranger who claims they are the sender  

Forum consensus and bank letters agree: peer-to-peer “I’ll just send it back” is how money-laundering and impersonation traps start.

## Prevention habits for FIN / new arrivals

| Habit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Read the **registered name** on the PayNow confirm screen | Proxies reuse numbers after SIM changes |
| Prefer **NRIC/FIN** proxy for rent / helper salary once set up | Fewer digit typos than mobile |
| Small test transfer for new landlords | Cheaper than a S$4k mistake |
| Separate **remittance** rails for overseas family | PayNow is domestic — [remittances](/guides/remittances-singapore) |

PayNow setup for foreigners still needs a local bank account first — prefer **FIN** over mobile, and assume prepaid numbers may still be linked to a prior owner: [PayNow setup](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore), [apps / PayNow](/guides/apps-that-matter-singapore), [opening a bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat).
