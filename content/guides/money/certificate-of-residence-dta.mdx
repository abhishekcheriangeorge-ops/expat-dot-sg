Multi-country households all hit the same wall eventually. A broker, a payroll team, or a foreign tax office asks you to prove you're a Singapore tax resident before you can claim anything under an Avoidance of Double Taxation Agreement (DTA). What they want is one specific letter: an IRAS Certificate of Residence, or COR. What they won't accept, however tempting it is to send, is your EP card, your FIN, or your IR8A. Those show you work here. They don't certify where you're tax resident, and the person processing your claim knows the difference.

## What a COR is actually for

IRAS issues a COR to certify your Singapore **tax residency**, so that you can hand it to a foreign tax authority or a withholding agent when you're claiming treaty relief. In practice that usually means reduced withholding on dividends, interest or royalties, or another DTA benefit that only residents of a treaty partner get to claim.

| You need a COR when… | You don't need one when… |
| --- | --- |
| A foreign broker or company wants Singapore residency proof before applying a treaty rate | You only have Singapore-sourced salary and no foreign claim to make |
| You're reclaiming foreign withholding under a DTA | You're still a non-resident under IRAS's tests |
| An advisor back home asks for evidence of Singapore residency | You just want "proof of address" for a bank KYC form |

The right-hand column is the one worth pausing on, because non-residents don't get DTA resident benefits at all. Applying before your residency facts support it isn't a head start, it's a wasted cycle. Settle residency first: [IRAS tax residency](/guides/iras-tax-residency-filing).

## How to apply if you're a foreign employee

IRAS splits its COR page by profile, which is why the advice a colleague gives you is often right for them and wrong for you. Foreign employees with Singpass use the online COR application for DTA countries and jurisdictions. Without Singpass, IRAS points you to a downloadable COR application spreadsheet submitted by email. SC and PR employees have a separate QR and form path. And if a foreign tax form requires IRAS endorsement on an original overseas document, that's the physical application packet posted to Revenue House as published, not the online route.

Processing runs about **2 to 3 weeks**. On digital notices you'll get an SMS or email when the letter is ready on myTax Portal; on paper notices it arrives by post to the address IRAS has on record.

## Where this goes wrong

1. Applying while you're still a non-resident. That's a wasted cycle, so fix the dates and any straddling-year facts before you file.
2. Assuming EP means tax resident forever. Residency can be reviewed at IR21 clearance when you leave, which is the point at which you have the least room to manoeuvre.
3. Mixing COR up with foreign-income folklore. Singapore generally doesn't tax most foreign-sourced income received here by individuals in the way new arrivals fear: [foreign income](/guides/foreign-income-and-tax).
4. Leaving RSU or dividend withholding unplanned. [RSU and ESOP](/guides/rsu-esop-tax-singapore) positions often need the treaty paperwork sorted **before** the pay date.

Keep a PDF of the COR in your year-end tax folder. It won't reduce your Singapore bill, and it doesn't replace home-country filing advice. It only tells a foreign authority, in a form they'll accept, where you're resident.
