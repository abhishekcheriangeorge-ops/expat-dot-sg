Mobile careers generate overlapping tax questions: remote work for a foreign employer, equity vesting from a parent company, rental income abroad, consulting on the side. Forum threads keep colliding two statements — “overseas income is not taxable” and “I am physically working in Singapore” — as though they settled the same question. They don’t.

## The distinction that actually matters

| Situation | Usual IRAS orientation (verify on your facts) |
| --- | --- |
| Employment or services exercised in Singapore, even if paid by an overseas company into an overseas account | Generally Singapore-sourced, so taxable here |
| Genuine foreign-sourced income received into a Singapore bank, the classic remittance case | Often not taxable for individuals from 2004 onward, with listed exceptions |
| An overseas posting where services are rendered wholly outside Singapore | May sit outside Singapore tax; this is IRAS FAQ territory |
| Travel overseas that is incidental to a Singapore employment | Often still tied to the Singapore employment |

The r/askSingapore DP and remote-work threads keep landing on that first row, and it’s the one people least want to be true. Living here on a family pass while billing a foreign employer doesn’t make the income “overseas” for IRAS, because what’s being asked is where you did the work. MOM’s work-rights rules then sit on top as a separate compliance layer, so satisfying one of them tells you nothing about the other.

## Remittance isn’t a free pass, and it isn’t a trap either

How you move the money decides nothing. Wise, PayNow, a wire, it makes no difference to the answer. **Source** is what decides: where the work is done, or where the right to the income arises. The mirror image holds too: leaving money offshore doesn’t erase Singapore-source employment income, it just leaves it somewhere else.

## Equity, directors’ fees and dual filing

RSUs and options from a foreign parent have to be characterised before you can do anything sensible with them, as an employment benefit or as capital. Get the documents out of your employer or their tax vendor early, rather than in the week of the vest.

Director’s fees from a Singapore company carry their own IRAS treatment, and that holds even when you spend most of the year travelling.

If you’re a US person, or otherwise taxed at home on worldwide income, you’re still filing at home as well. Singapore’s treatment of something is not your home country’s treatment of it, and the two won’t reconcile themselves for you.

## A practical stance

Keep clean records of foreign income, of remittances, and of days worked by location. The last one sounds fussy right up to the moment somebody asks you to prove it.

Align employer equity reporting before the first vest after you arrive, not after it has happened.

And engage a cross-border adviser if you have material foreign assets, dual filing, or a “remote for a foreign company while on DP or LTVP” structure. Those are the situations where guessing gets expensive.
