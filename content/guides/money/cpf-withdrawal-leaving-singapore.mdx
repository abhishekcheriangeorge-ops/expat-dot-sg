The "can I move my CPF into Aussie super or a US 401k?" posts on r/askSingapore all rest on the same wrong premise, which is that moving abroad unlocks the balance. CPF Board's design works the other way round. Ordinary CPF exists for Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents, so a full cash-out needs you to be **no longer SC or PR**, followed by a formal account closure. Geography isn't the trigger. Status is.

## Who this page is for

| Your status | What CPF withdrawal actually looks like |
| --- | --- |
| EP or S Pass foreigner, never PR | You sat outside ordinary CPF the whole time, so there's usually nothing to withdraw beyond contributions made by mistake |
| Singapore PR keeping status while overseas | Your savings stay in CPF. Letting the REP lapse doesn't cash anything out, it just creates a different problem |
| Ex-PR or ex-SC, renunciation completed | Now you're eligible to close the CPF account and have the savings transferred to a registered bank account |

If you want the baseline myths and how PR contribution rates phase in, that's [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners). The immigration side of the exit is [renouncing PR](/guides/renouncing-pr-singapore).

## The sequence that matches the Board's rules

1. Finish the ICA renunciation, or the loss of PR or citizenship, first. While you're still SPR or SC, CPF won't treat you as leaving permanently no matter how permanent it feels
2. Close before you leave Singapore if you can. The bank letter and Singpass route is considerably cleaner than doing it from another time zone
3. Apply online, or use Form CPF-CA with overseas-mission certification if you're already gone
4. Budget roughly **12 weeks** of processing on average, because the Board runs thorough checks before it disburses anything
5. Receive the funds into a bank account registered with the Board, and then decide for yourself whether to feed a foreign retirement product with them

There is no CPF-to-superannuation or CPF-to-401k direct pipe, and no amount of asking will produce one. Once the money is disbursed it's yours to move wherever you like, subject to the receiving country's tax and contribution rules rather than Singapore's.

## The four things people get wrong

The first is treating residency as status. Living overseas doesn't make you non-SC or non-PR, and CPF only cares about the second thing. The second is assuming REP expiry acts as a withdrawal button. The [Re-Entry Permit](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas) governs your mobility as a PR, and it has nothing to do with releasing your CPF savings.

The third is the belief that years on an EP quietly built a nest egg. Foreigners generally aren't in ordinary CPF at all, so there's no pot there. If you're thinking of something you genuinely did contribute to, it's more likely an MNC's own scheme or [SRS](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner), which run on their own rules. The fourth is planning a straight transfer into Australian super. You close CPF first, and only then deal with the foreign fund's inbound rules, which are a separate negotiation.

## Before you renounce just to get the cash

Model the whole trade rather than the balance. MediShield, housing, and your options for coming back later all go when PR goes. And if the balance is modest and there's any chance you re-apply, note that CPF says returning ex-SC and ex-PR members can face refund-with-interest obligations on amounts they previously withdrew. Read the live Important Notes on the closure form before you commit, because that's where the current version of this lives.

While you still have a CPF account, the nomination and estate side is covered in [wills and CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore), and the wider exit logistics sit in the [leaving Singapore playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook).

The short version: CPF cash-out is a status change plus a Board process, not a remittance product. Once you've renounced, work through the [CPF account closure checklist](/guides/cpf-account-closure-after-renouncing-pr-singapore).
