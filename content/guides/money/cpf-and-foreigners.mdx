CPF is Singapore's mandatory social security system for citizens and PRs in covered employment. If you're here on a work pass, you sit outside it. Employers generally **must not** contribute CPF for foreigners, which means this isn't something a sympathetic HR manager can arrange for you.

## The short answer

| Status | CPF contributions? |
| --- | --- |
| EP, S Pass or Work Permit foreigner | No. Foreigners sit outside ordinary CPF, and employers aren't permitted to contribute |
| Singapore PR | Yes, at rates that phase in under prevailing PR rules |
| Singapore Citizen | Yes |

## Why the myth persists on Reddit

Job ads and lunchtime conversation blur local and expat packages together. Your Singaporean colleagues see employer CPF, roughly a 17% band depending on age and wage rules, as money on top of salary, so foreigners hear the number and assume they should be getting the same in cash. It doesn't work like that.

Your gross offer is usually structured differently from a local package in the first place. You also keep the employee-side CPF slice in cash, which locals never see. What you don't get are the Medisave and CPF medical rails, so medical cover becomes something you have to buy deliberately rather than something that happens to you in the background. We go through the options in [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat).

Ask HR to state CPF treatment explicitly in the offer, and read [salary package decoding](/guides/salary-package-decoding) before you compare two numbers that aren't measuring the same thing.

## "Fake CPF" and shadow pension schemes

Some MNCs run voluntary shadow savings plans, employee deferral with an employer match, and these are not CPF. Plenty of them are genuinely good. The catch is that they vest, or vanish, on schedules that only become interesting in the week you resign. Get the plan PDF and the vesting rules in writing rather than a thumbs-up in Slack.

## After PR

Once you convert, you move into the CPF contribution regime at prevailing rates: graduated through SPR years 1–2 by default, full rates from year 3, with the usual age and wage bands. Joint applications can accelerate that.

Two things then happen at once. Your take-home pay drops compared with your EP years, and you start building the housing and retirement and medical architecture you previously lacked, including MediShield Life and CareShield premiums funded from MediSave once contributions start. Model the cashflow before you celebrate the approval. The day-one list is in [after getting PR](/guides/after-getting-pr-checklist), and the strategy context sits in [PR application](/guides/pr-application-strategy).

A few things are worth doing in the first weeks:

- Tell HR your SPR conversion date immediately, so the correct contribution table applies from the right month.
- File a CPF nomination. Without one, your savings default to the Public Trustee and the delay that comes with it ([wills and CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore)).
- Expect DPS auto-enrolment after your first valid working contribution. Opting out is a deliberate act, not a default.
- Treat Re-Entry Permit renewal as mobility insurance if you travel ([REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas)).
- Understand that a full CPF cash-out is only for people who are **no longer** SC or PR. You renounce or lose the status first, then close the account, and the Board process runs around 12 weeks. There's no direct pipe into a foreign super fund or a 401k ([CPF withdrawal when leaving](/guides/cpf-withdrawal-leaving-singapore)).

## SRS is not CPF

Foreigners can often use the Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS) voluntarily for tax-timed savings. It's a different product with its own rules, and it isn't a stand-in for a Medisave or housing CPF account. The overview is in [SRS for foreigners](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner).

## Offer-letter script

Ask for it in these words: "Confirm that as an Employment Pass holder I will not receive CPF contributions, and state any shadow pension, gratuity or SRS-adjacent benefit in writing, with vesting."

If the recruiter can't answer that, you don't understand the package yet. Quite possibly neither do they.
