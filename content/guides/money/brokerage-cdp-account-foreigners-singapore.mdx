**Short answer:** A Singapore salary account **is not a brokerage account**, and one doesn't quietly turn into the other. To hold SGX-listed shares you'll normally need your FIN and pass documents, a Singapore bank account linked for CDP's Direct Crediting Service, and a broker that has finished its own KYC on you. US persons should expect extra friction. SRS is optional and sits to one side of all of this.

The year-one pattern is familiar. You open DBS, OCBC or UOB for payroll, you get PayNow working, and you assume you can buy an STI ETF tomorrow. Broker and CDP onboarding are their own queues, and they fail for the same reasons bank accounts fail: IPA-only status instead of an issued pass, address proof nobody will accept, or a nationality and tax-residency combination the provider has decided not to take on.

## What you actually need

| Piece | What it does for you |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore bank account | Salary in, SGD settlement, and the bank link CDP wants |
| FIN / issued pass | Your identity for both CDP and broker KYC |
| Singpass | Faster digital onboarding wherever it's offered |
| CDP account | Central holding for many SGX securities |
| Broker | Order routing, research, access to overseas markets |
| SRS (optional) | A tax-deferred pocket, not a prerequisite for any of the above |

If the bank account or PayNow is still the thing that's blocked, fix that first with [opening a bank account](/guides/opening-bank-account-expat) and [PayNow setup](/guides/paynow-setup-foreigners-singapore), then come back here.

## CDP and your broker are not the same thing

The mental model worth having: CDP holds many Singapore-listed securities in your own name at the central depository, while your broker is the thing that executes trades. Some broker flows settle into CDP, others hold your shares in a nominee structure instead, so read your broker's SGX settlement notes rather than assuming which one you've signed up to.

Where this bites is when money is meant to arrive. Dividends and corporate-action credits usually need a correctly linked bank account through the Direct Crediting Service, so a link you never finished setting up is a link you find out about on your first payout date.

Apply with the same patience payroll KYC needed. Bring the passport, the pass card and bank proof, and don't expect hotel invoices on their own to clear every provider.

## If you're a US person, or another restricted nationality

FATCA, the W-9 paperwork and product-level rules mean some MAS-licensed brokers will not onboard US citizens or US tax residents at all, valid EP or not. Ask before you wire anything, and ask for the answer in writing. It isn't a judgement about you; it's a compliance decision the firm has taken about an entire category of client, and no amount of explaining your situation moves it.

If local onboarding is refused, keep your existing overseas brokerage for US-listed names. What you shouldn't do is let the frustration push you toward the unregulated "expat trading" Telegram clones, which are a [MAS investor alert](https://www.mas.gov.sg/investor-alert) problem rather than a clever workaround.

## Tax, and the two exceptions that catch people

Casual long-term share investing by an individual is often outside Singapore income tax as capital gains, though that's worth verifying against the live IRAS pages rather than against what someone told you over dinner.

Two things sit outside that comfortable story. Employment equity — RSUs, ESOPs, options — follows a different set of rules entirely, and we cover those in [RSU and ESOP tax](/guides/rsu-esop-tax-singapore). And SRS investing carries foreigner withdrawal withholding, which is the detail that turns a tax-deferral into a surprise if you leave; that one's in [SRS as a foreigner](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner).

## A sensible order for year one

1. Issued pass, salary account, emergency buffer. That's the base, and it's covered in the [year-one plan](/guides/financial-planning-year-one).
2. Start CDP and broker KYC only once payroll is stable.
3. Confirm the broker's US-person and nationality policy in writing before you move any money.
4. Keep cash investing, SRS and your employer's share plan mentally separate. They are three different things with three different rulebooks.
5. Ignore the leverage and crypto-app FOMO until the boring stack works.

The decision rule is narrow and it's worth holding to: you're ready to trade when KYC, the bank link and product access are all genuinely real. Not when PayNow turns green.
