The Hash is a social trail run, or walk, with a stubborn sense of humour attached. Singapore has several kennels and they don't all move at the same pace or share the same culture. When the “how do I make friends here” threads come round again on r/askSingapore, hashing still gets named alongside Meetup sports and volunteering, largely because it's a weekly ritual that costs almost nothing, which matters a lot in a city where the country club alternative looks absurd.

## Why newcomers take to it

The appeal isn't complicated. It's a ritual that happens every week whether or not you organised it, there's no entrance fee to agonise over, and the pack is genuinely mixed by nationality and age in a way most expat social scenes aren't. Plenty of kennels welcome walkers, so fitness is optional and, frankly, so is consistency. Friendliness isn't. Then there's the circle after the trail, which is where the actual social hour happens.

It isn't for everyone. The humour can be crude, and if that's not your tone, that's fine. Go once as a guest before you judge the whole movement either way.

Cultures differ more than a table can show, so try two kennels before you declare a home pack. Check Facebook or the kennel's own site for this week's start location, because hashes move. If you also want passport-cluster events on the nights you aren't running, pair this with [nationality groups](/guides/nationality-groups-singapore).

## Guest etiquette

1. Turn up on time, and introduce yourself as a virgin or a visitor if that's the kennel's language
2. Bring cash for the run fee and for whatever you drink afterwards
3. Wear shoes that can take a trail and kit that can take the humidity ([what to wear](/guides/what-to-wear-singapore-climate))
4. Hydrate before you start. Singapore heat isn't a European spring 10k and it will find you out
5. Stay for the circle at least once, because that's where the names stick

Treat one kennel as a hub rather than your whole social plan. Pair it with [hobby sports communities](/guides/hobby-sports-communities-singapore), [sports & ActiveSG](/guides/sports-fitness-singapore), and [find my people](/guides/find-my-people-singapore). And if the humour turns out not to be your tone, leave without guilt. There are other weekly sports here, and [volunteering](/guides/volunteering-networks-singapore) reaches a different crowd again.
