**Short answer:** AIP is not PR yet. Finish the ICA formalities, tell HR so CPF can start, re-register Singpass on your new NRIC, update the banks, and remember that the thing which gets you back into the country is a valid **Re-Entry Permit**, not the blue card on its own.

The "I just got PR, what now?" threads aren't lifestyle fluff. The first fortnight is genuinely an identity swap: FIN becomes NRIC, your EP-world insurance gives way to the MediShield rails, and your take-home pay suddenly has CPF taken out of it. Skip the admin and you get bounced Singpass logins, unpaid premiums, and a payroll system that still believes you're on an EP.

## Before the blue card: the AIP formalities

"Approved in principle" isn't the finish line, and the gap between the IPA email and the card in your hand routinely runs into weeks. Medical upload, ICA biometrics appointment, SingPost collection. Treat the portal checklist as a small project with dates rather than a list you'll get to.

1. Book the ICA appointment the day you're able to. Slots can sit 3–5+ weeks out, and that is the single biggest thing standing between you and everything else here.
2. Complete the medical, plus any MediSave or MediShield enrolment steps ICA lists against your IPA.
3. Bring the documents the appointment letter names, including your passport and your existing pass card.
4. Once biometrics are done, download or print the Entry Permit and REP, then book IC collection.
5. On collection day, re-register Singpass on the new NRIC. It does not always auto-migrate from your FIN, and you'll find that out at the worst possible moment otherwise.
6. Answer the REP occupation and income fields truthfully. Being unemployed or between jobs at take-up can mean a shorter first REP, which is annoying but survivable. Lying about it is considerably worse than a 1-year stamp. More on that in our [REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).
7. Until the formalities are finished you are not a PR for travel purposes. If you need to leave and come back in the meantime, do it on your existing work pass rules, not on "almost-PR" reasoning.

One practical note on the identity swap: in day-to-day banking and KYC terms, FIN to NRIC behaves like a new identifier, whatever forum pedants say about the number being yours forever. Every institution still holding a G or F FIN needs updating.

## What to do in week one, in order

| Order | Action | Why it bites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tell HR and payroll, with the Entry Permit or temporary IC | CPF can't start until your employer knows the SPR conversion date |
| 2 | Re-register Singpass on the new NRIC | Most e-services are still keyed to your old FIN and will simply fail |
| 3 | Update banks, insurers, brokers, and the telco | Cards and KYC freeze when the FIN on file no longer matches your NRIC |
| 4 | Expect MediShield Life mail or an e-notice | Cover is automatic for citizens and PRs, with premiums drawn from MediSave |
| 5 | Read the CareShield Life rules for your birth year and PR grant date | Foreigners who became PR from 1 Oct 2020 face mandatory rules that older cohorts may treat as optional |
| 6 | Watch for the DPS enrolment letter after your first CPF contribution | Term cover with premiums from your OA. Opting out is a deliberate act, not the default |
| 7 | Keep the Re-Entry Permit valid before any trip out | PR isn't a souvenir stamp, as the [REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas) explains |

## CPF: remodel your take-home before you celebrate

Once you're an employed PR, you're in CPF. Graduated rates apply by default for your first two SPR years, with full citizen-equivalent rates from the third. Age and wage bands change the picture, so work from the live tables rather than from what a colleague remembers.

Here's the directional story for many private-sector SPRs under 55 on graduated rates, which you should sanity-check against the [1 Jan 2026 PDF tables](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/content/dam/web/employer/employer-obligations/documents/CPFcontributionratesfrom1Jan2026.pdf):

| SPR year | Typical story people quote | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | Lower employee and employer percentages than full rates | The cashflow shock is milder, but MediSave may still be thin when the first MediShield premium lands |
| 2nd | Steps up | Recalculate what you can carry on mortgage and school fees |
| 3rd+ | Full rates, e.g. the 20% employee and 17% employer bands for many under-55 wage cases | This is the point where people write the "my take-home collapsed" post |

You and your employer can jointly apply to contribute at higher rates earlier, either full/full or full-employer with graduated-employee. Do it in writing through CPF Board's own process. A handshake with your manager isn't a mechanism.

## MediShield Life is not your old corporate plan

MediShield Life is automatic for every citizen and PR, and it takes you with pre-existing conditions, which is a genuinely good deal by international standards. What it isn't is a replacement for the corporate cover you're leaving behind. The benefits are sized for subsidised B2 and C ward public-hospital care, so a private or Class A bill still leaves you with a large gap to cover in cash or from MediSave. Premiums auto-deduct from MediSave, so top the account up if the early months of graded CPF have left it short.

Most new PRs then price an Integrated Shield Plan on top if they want private-hospital or higher-ward cover. Compare those on MOH's official table rather than on the screenshot an agent sends you over WhatsApp.

## CareShield Life and DPS: the letters worth opening

CareShield Life covers long-term care and severe-disability payouts. The trap for new PRs is that **foreigners granted citizenship or PR from 1 Oct 2020 generally have mandatory participation**, with disability rules that differ by grant date. The "it's optional" advice you'll read online was written by and for an older cohort, and it doesn't apply to you.

DPS is the term life piece, covering death, terminal illness and total permanent disability, and it's administered by Great Eastern. It's auto-extended on your first valid working CPF contribution if you're a citizen or PR aged 21–65, with premiums coming out of your CPF OA. You can opt out, but that has to be something you actively choose rather than something you drift into.

## The identity admin people skip

Start with the paperwork that travels. Store the Entry Permit somewhere you can find it, and get into the habit of checking REP before you book anything long-haul. When you next renew your foreign passport, you'll need to run ICA's Transfer REP process, which is covered in [transfer REP to new passport](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport). Your driving licence particulars need updating at Traffic Police when prompted, because FIN to NRIC isn't always automatic there either. And learn the replacement path and fees for a lost blue IC before your first JB weekend, not during it — [lost NRIC](/guides/lost-replace-nric-pr-singapore) has the detail. Stop carrying the old FIN card "as backup" while you're at it.

Then the changes you have to report. Residential address changes go to ICA via eCOA within 28 days, overseas postings included, and the sticker goes on the blue IC. Our [eCOA playbook](/guides/ecoa-nric-address-change-singapore) walks through it. If you're mid-deed-poll, PRs change the foreign passport name first and then ICA particulars, in that order, which [change of name on NRIC](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore) explains. NRIC re-registration only applies if ICA notifies you at 30 or 55 under the 10-year rule, so diary it rather than worrying about it — see the [re-registration guide](/guides/nric-reregistration-30-55-singapore) and fold it into your [annual life admin](/guides/renewing-life-admin-annually).

Family and money come next. A newborn or under-21 child is not automatically PR because you are; you sponsor them through ICA's child path, and if you have sons you should read the NS implications properly first, which [PR for child or newborn](/guides/pr-for-child-newborn-singapore) sets out. File a CPF nomination once contributions start, because the Public Trustee default is slow and your family will feel it ([wills and nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore)). If citizenship IPA lands later, treat [SCJ after IPA](/guides/singapore-citizenship-journey-scj) as its own project rather than a formality.

Finally, the benefits worth understanding before you assume them. PR doesn't magically unlock every HDB path, so read [HDB for PR and mixed status](/guides/hdb-for-pr-and-mixed-status) before you start viewing. On upskilling, **SkillsFuture Credit, the dollar wallet, is for Singapore Citizens only and PRs don't get it**. Many course-fee subsidies on MySkillsFuture do still list PRs, and sometimes LTVP+ holders, but usually at lower bands than the mid-career enhancements citizens get, so check each course's own funding table before you budget on "PR means free training". The soft perks are real but modest: NLB membership and NHB museum free-admission rules shift in your favour ([arts and culture](/guides/arts-culture-singapore)). Nice, but not why you applied.

## Decision rule

Treat the PR formalities week as a project rather than a celebration dinner. The one thing worth chasing hard: if HR hasn't confirmed your SPR conversion date in payroll within a cycle, escalate. Every month of delay is a month of shallower MediSave and contributions running off the wrong table.
