The "do I re-register at 30 if I replaced my IC last year?" threads on r/askSingapore tangle three separate things together: the compulsory age windows, loss replacements, and a stubborn bit of folklore about collecting abroad. ICA's actual rule is narrower than the group chat thinks it is. If you're out of the country when your window lands, the return clocks are on [NRIC re-registration overseas return](/guides/nric-reregistration-overseas-return-singapore).

## Who has to re-register

The Identity Card is a compulsory document, and both SC and PR holders have to re-register it at two points: at age 30, before the 31st birthday, and again at age 55, before the 56th. That's the whole of the age rule.

Then there's the carve-out that explains most of the confusion on the forums. **If you were issued a replacement IC within the last 10 years, the age window doesn't force another card.** Someone who lost their IC in their mid-twenties simply skips the "turn 30" ritual. It's also why a lot of PRs never get a birthday SMS and then worry they've missed something. They haven't.

Don't confuse this path with a [lost or stolen IC](/guides/lost-replace-nric-pr-singapore), which carries different fees, or with a [name or particulars change](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore), which is the S$60 change-of-particulars route.

## Notice, then file, then collect

1. Wait for ICA's digital notice, which comes by SMS or through the Singpass app. Once it arrives, eligible people get a subsidised window of **one year from the notice date** to file online.
2. Log in to ICA's e-Service with Singpass and upload a passport-sized digital colour photo taken within the last three months, along with any name or marriage documents the form asks for.
3. Pay the subsidised fee ICA publishes: S$10 if you're a citizen, S$50 if you're a PR.
4. Wait about seven working days, longer in peak periods. A rejected photo or thin supporting documents are what usually stretch that out.
5. When approval lands by SMS and email, book your collection **within three months**. Collection needs identity authentication **in Singapore**, and embassies don't hand over NRICs.

### What you'll be uploading

If you're a citizen the list is short: a recent photo, plus religious-name, deed-poll or marriage documents only if you're changing particulars at the same time.

If you're a PR, add the bio page of your current foreign passport. The order matters when you're also renaming, because the new name has to be on the passport first. A marriage certificate goes in as well if you're adding a married name.

PRs renaming mid-re-registration walk into exactly the trap we describe on [name change](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore).

## Overseas when your window lands

If you're living abroad when the re-registration period comes round, ICA requires you to re-register within one year of returning to Singapore. The hope you'll see aired on forums, that you can quietly collect at a consulate, runs straight into ICA's published requirement for fingerprints and facial matching here. So plan the trip around collection rather than hoping collection will fit around the trip. The detail sits on [overseas return clocks](/guides/nric-reregistration-overseas-return-singapore), and REP discipline while you're away stays on [REP](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).

## If your address changes mid-application

If your re-registration, or any IC replacement, carries a new residential address, ICA still posts a 6-digit PIN mailer to that address and won't finish the IC file until you verify the PIN on the e-Service. That catches people who assume the address is just another field on a form. You then get a new IC with the new address printed on it, not a sticker on the old one. More on [eCOA and address change](/guides/ecoa-nric-address-change-singapore).

## What to actually do

Diary 30 and 55, then wait. File only after ICA's notice arrives, or after you've satisfied yourself that you're inside the 10-year carve-out. Use the subsidised fee path, assume a photo reject is the thing most likely to delay you, and book a Singapore collection slot inside three months. And if your card is already cracked, don't laminate it and hope that turning 55 will bail you out. If loss fees already apply, they apply.
