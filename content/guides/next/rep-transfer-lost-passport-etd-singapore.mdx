The "passport stolen in Bangkok, am I still PR at Changi?" threads on r/askSingapore usually blur three separate things: the replacement book your mission issues, the emergency travel document it might issue instead, and ICA's re-entry permit transfer. They're three different steps, and only the last one decides how your arrival goes. If your passport simply expired and you renewed it the normal way, you want [transfer REP to a new passport](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport) instead. This one is for when the book goes missing mid-trip.

Here's what trips people up. Your blue NRIC doesn't carry a passport number, but your Re-Entry Permit does. So a new book is a new travel document as far as ICA is concerned, and that includes many of the temporary and emergency documents a mission will hand you, right up until you move the remaining REP validity across onto it.

## The order that hurts least

1. Report the loss or theft the way your mission and the local police require. That paperwork is what unlocks a replacement or an ETD in the first place.
2. Get the replacement passport or ETD from your home mission. ICA's transfer needs a travel-document number to attach the permit to, so nothing else can move until this exists.
3. Run ICA's Transfer of Re-Entry Permit / Update Travel Document e-Service with Singpass. This is the step that shifts your remaining REP validity onto the new document.
4. Print the updated REP and carry it with the new document. If you end up in secondary inspection, that printout is your script.
5. Only then assume you'll be cleared as a PR on the way back. **Visa-free entry on your nationality is not the same thing as PR status**, and the officer is reading the permit, not your history here.

## ETD or a proper replacement book

A full replacement passport is the clean case. The mission replaces the lost or expired book, you get a new passport number, and you transfer onto that number before you expect PR clearance on the way in.

An emergency or temporary travel document is messier. It's typically one-way or short-validity, just enough to get you home or back to Singapore, and it leaves you with two questions rather than one. Ask the mission what they're actually issuing, and separately check whether ICA's transfer e-Service accepts that document type at all. If the transfer isn't available for it, you're relying on officer discretion and you should plan for secondary inspection rather than be surprised by it. What you should not do is lean on the "an NRIC selfie is enough" folklore, because it isn't.

There's a rarer third case: the same passport number with new pages, the sort of extension sticker a handful of countries still issue. Check with ICA whether they want an update anyway. Most PR cases involve a new number, so this one probably isn't you.

ICA's published transfer fee for a new travel document is typically free, and processing is commonly cited at around three working days. Verify that live, because it's exactly the kind of detail that drifts. What won't happen is the clock shrinking because your trip fell apart, so start the transfer the moment the new document exists rather than the night before your flight.

## Transfer, renew and lost NRIC are three different rails

If the REP is still valid and only the document number has changed, you want a transfer. That's this guide, plus [the routine version](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport) if the passport was replaced on schedule rather than in a panic.

If the REP itself is near expiry or already gone, transferring won't save you. That's a renewal, and running one from abroad has its own discipline: [REP from overseas](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).

And if what you lost was the blue NRIC rather than the passport, that's a separate ICA process entirely, covered in [replacing a lost NRIC](/guides/lost-replace-nric-pr-singapore).

## Decision rule

Mission first, ICA second, Changi third. Get the replacement document or ETD, transfer the REP onto it, and only then start thinking about the flight home. Routine passport renewals stay on [transfer REP](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport), and the wider habit of keeping a permit healthy while you're living abroad sits on [REP overseas](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).
