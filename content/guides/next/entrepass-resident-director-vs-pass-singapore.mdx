Founders on r/askSingapore keep asking which visa lets them start a company, as if ACRA and MOM were one form with two pages. They aren't. The incorporation mechanics are in [incorporating as a foreigner](/guides/incorporate-company-foreigner-singapore), and the founder-pass gates are in [EntrePass](/guides/entrepass-singapore). This guide is the comparison layer, because being a resident director, holding an EntrePass and having permission to work are three separate things, and people routinely buy the first while assuming they've acquired the third.

## Two jobs, two agencies

| Job | Agency | What clears it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owning shares and registering a Pte Ltd | ACRA, and foreigners go through a CSP | Name reservation, constitution, at least one ordinarily resident director, company secretary rules |
| Living here and actually running the business | MOM | EntrePass, an employer-sponsored EP, ONE Pass or PEP (watch the PEP shareholder-director ineligibility), or a DP business LOC. A Bizfile listing is not work rights |

Anyone in the world can own shares in a Singapore company. Running that company from inside Singapore without a work pass is a different matter, and it's an offence rather than an oversight.

## When EntrePass is genuinely the right answer

EntrePass fits when you hold **at least 30%** of a venture-backed or innovative-technology business and you can clear at least one of the published innovator tests: funding, an incubator, a prior exit, registered IP, or a research collaboration in Singapore. Before you relocate a household on the strength of it, model the renewal ladder, meaning the TBS and local workforce requirements, because renewal is where this pass gets harder rather than easier.

One thing founders discover late: a shelf company older than 12 months doesn't buy you a head start. It gets assessed against the renewal criteria instead, so a quiet dormant Pte Ltd you picked up last year won't unlock a founder IPA.

And if a properly capitalised sponsor could clear you through an ordinary EP role, that path is usually cleaner. Constructing an EntrePass story around what is really normal employment costs more time than it saves.

## Where resident-director arrangements go wrong

Nominee directors are real directors under the Companies Act. The cheap "name only" packs sell you the appearance of compliance while the named human carries genuine personal exposure, which is exactly why the serious providers charge what they charge.

If you're an EP holder taking a resident director seat, ACRA registration doesn't legalise it on its own. A commercial board seat may still need a secondary directorship LOC and your primary employer's consent. Appearing in Bizfile is not the same as being allowed to moonlight.

PEP is its own trap here, because it lists shareholder-directors, freelancers and sole proprietors as ineligible. Entrepreneurial work therefore points at EntrePass or ONE Pass, not PEP. We've put the three side by side in [PEP, ONE Pass and Tech.Pass](/guides/pep-one-tech-pass-chooser-singapore).

## The rule to hold onto

**ACRA first for the company, MOM second for the human.** If your plan depends on a founder pass, read EntrePass end to end before you pay anybody for a "guaranteed pass plus nominee director" package.
