r/askSingapore workplace-injury threads split into two myths: “EP means no WICA” and “HR must buy insurance or I get nothing.” Wrong on both. **WICA covers eligible employees including EP holders**; mandatory **WIC insurance** is a narrower employer duty. High-earning non-manual EPs can still claim — the employer pays even if they never bought a policy for that salary band.

## Coverage vs mandatory insurance (the trap)

| Question | Official answer (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| Are EP holders covered under WICA? | **Yes** — MOM FAQ is explicit |
| Who is covered generally? | Local/foreign employees under a **contract of service**, regardless of salary, age, or citizenship |
| Who is **not** covered? | Independent contractors / self-employed, **domestic workers**, uniformed services |
| When must the employer buy WIC insurance? | All **manual** workers (any salary) + non-manual earning **≤ S$2,600**/month |
| High-salary non-manual EP injured at work? | Still covered by the Act; employer compensates whether or not insured |

Do not confuse WICA with **Foreign Worker Medical Insurance** (Work Permit / S Pass inpatient floors) or your private group hospital plan. Those stacks can overlap on a bill; they are not substitutes for a work-injury claim.

## What counts as a work injury

MOM’s eligible-claims page covers accidents **arising out of and in the course of employment**, including many scenarios Reddit argues about:

- Travelling **during work for work purposes** (meeting venue, client site) without a personal detour  
- Company transport between home and workplace (not ordinary public transport)  
- Overseas assignment while employed by a local employer  
- Approved **WFH / FWA** duties that injure you while performing work  
- Certain occupational diseases and exposure-related conditions  

Ordinary commute on the MRT is **not** automatically WICA. Overseas leisure travel is not. When HR says “night hours / outside workplace so no claim,” ask them to cite the eligible-claims scenarios — then escalate if they stall.

<OfficialSource>
Read [Eligible claims under WICA](https://www.mom.gov.sg/workplace-safety-and-health/work-injury-compensation/eligible-claims) before you accept a verbal denial.
</OfficialSource>

## Day-of playbook

1. Get **Singapore-registered** medical care and say it is a workplace injury — WICA medical expenses generally require Singapore doctor/dentist bills (JB clinic first is a recurring claim-killer).  
2. Notify **employer / HR / safety** in writing the same day with time, place, witnesses, photos.  
3. Keep copies of every MC, light-duty note, receipt, and WhatsApp. Give originals/digital MCs as HR requests.  
4. Ask HR to confirm the **iReport / insurer notification** — for accidents from **1 Sep 2020**, the claim is generally deemed made once the employer has notice; processing starts when MOM/insurer is notified.  
5. Do **not** resign mid-claim just to “start fresh” without reading pass clocks — cancellation does not erase eligibility, but cashflow and hospital LOGs get uglier. See [between jobs / STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

## What you can claim (caps matter)

| Benefit | Orientation (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| Medical leave wages | Working days on MC / light duty / hospitalisation leave, up to **1 year** from accident |
| Medical expenses | Treatment within **1 year**, capped at **S$53,000** for accidents from **1 Nov 2025** (was S$45,000 before) |
| Permanent incapacity / death | Lump-sum tables with min/max; higher limits from **1 Nov 2025** |

Eligible medical lines include consultation, ward, treatment, meds, ambulance, dental where prescribed, and certain rehab / TCM when Singapore-registered practitioners prescribe them. Private hospital upgrades and non-work illness are usually **insurance / LOG** problems — pair with [expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat) and [hospital LOG deposits](/guides/healthcare-gp-hospital).

You choose **WICA or common law**, not both. Withdrawal forms exist if you pursue civil suit instead.

## When HR goes quiet

- Confirm in writing that MOM / the designated insurer was notified  
- If medical leave wages or bills sit unpaid, contact the **designated insurer** and then **MOM**  
- Salary disputes that are *not* work-injury belong at [TADM](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom)  
- Pass-cancellation threats during hospitalisation are a separate immigration/employment mess — keep embassy and [emergencies](/guides/emergencies-995-999-embassies) numbers handy  

Most routine claims resolve without a lawyer. Permanent incapacity assessments and contested causation are where counsel earns their fee.
