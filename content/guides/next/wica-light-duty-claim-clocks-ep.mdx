**Short answer:** Same-day **written notice to HR**, treat **light duty as a paid shortfall**, and diary the **1-year** medical / PI / civil-fork clocks — do not wait for a separate MOM claim form.

r/askSingapore injury threads stall on three clocks: **did I file?**, **does light duty pay full salary?**, and **WICA or sue?**. Coverage myths (“EP = no WICA”) live on [WICA for EP holders](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep). This companion is the **light-duty + deadline** layer for accidents from **1 Sep 2020** onward.

## Notify ≠ “file a claim form”

For accidents from **1 September 2020**, MOM’s published rule is that the WICA claim is **deemed made once your employer has notice** of the accident. You generally do **not** open a separate “claim with MOM” first.

| Step | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Report the accident to **employer / HR / safety** ASAP (written) | You |
| Employer reports to MOM when you get hospitalisation, MC, or **light duty** | Employer (incident report clock is their problem — usually **10 days** from notice) |
| Claim processing starts when MOM / designated insurer is notified | System |
| Opt **out** of WICA if you want common law instead | You — withdrawal form via insurer / MOM |

If HR never notifies, chase the **designated insurer** and MOM in writing — silence is not a legal waiver of your notice to the employer.

## Light duty is compensable — with maths

**Light duty** means a Singapore doctor says you can work with activity limits. Under WICA (accidents on/after 1 Sep 2020), medical-leave wages cover the **shortfall** between what you actually earn on light duty and the published AME band:

| Period from accident | MC pay orientation | Light-duty shortfall orientation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First **14 days** (outpatient / light duty band) | Full **AME** | Actual wages + shortfall so you reach **full AME** (whichever reading is higher for you) |
| Day **15 → 1 year** | **2/3 AME** | Actual wages + shortfall toward **2/3 AME** |

Hospitalisation leave uses the hospitalisation wage table on MOM’s types-of-compensation page — verify live. CPF still applies to **actual wages** paid while on light duty.

Hard edge: if you are issued light duty and **stay away without leave**, you can lose WICA medical-leave wage entitlement for that absence.

## Hard clocks peers miss

| Clock | Published orientation |
| --- | --- |
| Medical-leave wages / medical expenses | Up to **1 year** from accident date (medical expenses also hit the **S$53,000** cap for accidents from **1 Nov 2025**, whichever comes first) |
| Permanent incapacity application memo | Complete/submit within **1 year** of accident if you want PI under WICA |
| Choose WICA vs common law | Up to **1 year** from accident to decide the path — **not both** |
| Withdraw WICA before NOA/NOC | Generally anytime before notice of assessment / computation |
| Withdraw after NOA/NOC | Tight windows (MOM publishes **14 / 28 days** depending on dispute) — read the letter |

## Decision rule

Same-day written notice to HR + Singapore medical notes beats forum “wait and see.” Treat light duty as a **paid shortfall claim**, diary the **1-year** medical / PI / civil-fork clocks, and do not resign mid-stabilisation without reading [between jobs / STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).
