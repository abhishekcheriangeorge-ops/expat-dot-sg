**Short answer:** Only your employer can appeal, and there is a published 3-month window from the rejection to do it in. Get the advisory text in writing, add facts that weren't in the first file, and **don't resign because someone said "we'll appeal"**.

The full map is in [EP rejection, appeal and IPA issuance](/guides/ep-rejection-appeal-ipa-issuance). This is the candidate's half of it: the part Reddit tends to compress into "HR said they'd appeal, so I resigned."

## The appeal isn't yours to file

MOM's appeal page is written for the employer or their agent, not for you. A candidate emailing MOM directly doesn't get a case ID out of it, which is why those emails feel like they vanish. The published window is 3 months from rejection, and appeals that genuinely engage with the advisory tend to come back in about 6 weeks. So the pressure worth applying is on HR, early, and in writing.

| What gets repeated | What's closer to true |
| --- | --- |
| "SAT said 90, the appeal will fix it" | The Self-Assessment Tool is planning input, not a veto you can overrule |
| "I'll email MOM myself" | Candidate enquiries aren't the appeal channel |
| "Appeal pending means I keep working" | No IPA, or a cancelled pass, is not work rights while you wait |
| "Resign now, appeal later" | A weak appeal and no job leaves you on visit status |

## Before you burn anything down

Ask HR for the rejection advisory text itself — the EP eService screen or the letter — rather than a verbal summary of it. The summary is where the useful detail usually dies, and you can't answer a reason you've only heard paraphrased.

Then map each line of that advisory to a new fact someone can actually put in front of MOM: a higher fixed salary, the COMPASS inputs, proof of which campus your degree came from, the FCF ad ID or the exemption being relied on, the employer's own tax hygiene. An appeal that restates the original file is just the original file again, and it gets read that way.

With that in hand, have the honest conversation with HR about whether an appeal is even the right move. A fixed-salary uplift plus a completed [FCF clock](/guides/fcf-mycareersfuture-14-day-clock-ep-singapore) often gets further than a thin appeal that adds nothing.

Whatever happens, don't resign on "we'll appeal" alone, especially from overseas. Keep a lawful stay and a way back intact until something is actually approved. And if the pass never issues, issuance clocks simply don't apply to you yet; they start mattering once there's an approval, at which point the sequencing in [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) takes over.

## The decision rule

No advisory and no new facts means there's no appeal worth filing. Either fix the file so the appeal has something to say, or leave your current job and stay plan where they are.
