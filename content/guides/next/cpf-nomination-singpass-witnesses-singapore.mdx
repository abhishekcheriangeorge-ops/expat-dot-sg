The "I nominated but it failed" threads on r/askSingapore are nearly always about witness hygiene rather than any mysterious hostility from CPF Board. Somebody picked the wrong witness, or picked a good one who then didn't click anything for a week. The wider estate picture, meaning how a Singapore will, probate and CPF interact, is on [wills, probate and CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore). This one is the narrower Singpass witness and amendment layer, for PRs who are already contributing.

It's easy to file this under "not our problem". EP and S Pass holders generally have no CPF at all, so the instinct is to skip the topic entirely. But a PR spouse in the same household very often does have an account, and that's exactly the household where nobody has looked at the nomination.

## What has to be true for an online nomination

| Rule | Published orientation (verify live) |
| --- | --- |
| Nominees | Up to 15 online |
| Witnesses | Two individuals aged 21+, each with Singpass |
| Witness conflict | A witness cannot also be a nominee on that nomination |
| Confirm clock | Each witness has to confirm within **7 days** of your submission |
| If a witness misses the clock | The nomination typically fails or expires, and you start again |

Brief your witnesses before you click submit, not after. The trap that catches most people is obvious in hindsight: your spouse is usually a nominee, which means your spouse cannot be one of the two witnesses on the same online nomination. If you've mentally cast them as both, you're one click away from a wasted submission.

## Online or paper

| Path | When people use it |
| --- | --- |
| Online via Singpass | The default if you're resident here with two cooperating Singpass witnesses |
| Paper or counter | Witnesses abroad without Singpass, complicated share splits, or edge cases the Board directs you to |

Paper is slower and much easier to fill in wrongly, and a form that comes back for correction costs you weeks. Go online unless you have a concrete reason you can't.

## Life events worth a re-check

| Event | Why revisit the nomination |
| --- | --- |
| Marriage, divorce, remarriage | Your old nominees may no longer match what you actually intend, and changing your will does not rewrite your CPF nomination |
| Birth or adoption | Add the children as nominees if that's the plan |
| A nominee dies or loses capacity | Stale shares can end up routed through Public Trustee paths |
| Renouncing PR, or leaving for good | Cash-out rules live on the CPF leaving pages, but the nomination still matters right up until the account closes |

Worth separating out: planning for who can act on your behalf while you're alive but incapacitated is [LPA and AMD](/guides/lasting-power-attorney-amd-foreigners). That's a different rail entirely from nomination, and one doesn't cover the other.

## What happens with no nomination

Without a valid nomination, CPF Board sends the monies through the Public Trustee under statutory distribution rules. People consistently underestimate both the fees and the months it takes, and the timing is unkind, because the family is usually already waiting on probate for the non-CPF assets while the Singapore bank accounts sit frozen. Making a nomination is free. Sorting out the alternative is not.

## The rule of thumb

Treat this as annual life admin rather than a one-off. Brief two Singpass witnesses in advance, submit, get both of them to confirm inside the **7 days**, screenshot the success page, and look at it again after a marriage, a divorce, or a child. The parent estate map stays on [wills and probate](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore), and the PR exit cash-out sits on the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook).
