The threads that start with "my kid's school wants proof we're Singaporean" usually end with someone waving a birth certificate, a pink IC and a scan of something that expired years ago. ICA has an actual product for this: a digital Confirmation of Singapore Citizenship Status letter. It costs S$20 and typically takes 3 working days once your documents are complete. If the thing asking is a foreign authority or a FormSG submission, the edges are different enough that we handle them separately in [the confirmation letter for foreign desks](/guides/confirmation-citizenship-letter-foreign-authority-singapore).

## Who can apply

ICA's eligibility line is wider than most people assume:

> Any person who is a current or former NRIC/FIN holder, or who has a Singapore birth certificate, may apply.

That covers someone clarifying status for a foreign university, someone part-way through another country's citizenship process, and the "am I still Singaporean after missing ORAL?" conversation. Be clear about what the letter does in that last case, though. It reports your status as ICA holds it today. It does not rewrite the [ORAL](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21) clock.

## What you get, and what you don't

| What ICA publishes | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Format | Digital only |
| Presentation | ICA states the letter has no signatures, stamps, or QR codes |
| Fee | S$20 per application, by card or PayNow |
| Processing | About 3 working days once the required documents are in |

The presentation row is the one to read twice. There is no wet-ink certificate with a crest on it, so don't go looking for one. If a foreign authority takes a look and rejects it as an unsigned PDF, **ask them what format they will accept before you pay the S$20 a second time**. And if what they really want is an attested copy of some other document, that's a different stack altogether — see [apostille and notarisation](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore).

## Documents, and who can ask on whose behalf

ICA typically asks you to upload the applicant's foreign passport, birth certificate, Singapore citizenship certificate or Singapore IC. Who is allowed to make the request depends on age. An appointed proxy needs an authorisation letter plus the requestor's Singapore IC. Children under 16 go through a parent, or through a proxy the parent has appointed. From 16 onwards they can request as themselves.

Follow the live form's document list rather than this paragraph. An incomplete file is the usual reason a three-day clock turns into something longer.

## When families actually need this

| What you're trying to do | The product that fits |
| --- | --- |
| Prove citizenship to a foreign school or mission | The confirmation letter, which is this guide |
| Under-21 citizen clearing Singapore checkpoints on a foreign passport | [ROE](/guides/right-of-entry-roe-under-21-singapore) |
| Keep citizenship past 21 when dual by descent or registration | [ORAL](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21) |
| PR adult applying to become a citizen | [Citizenship application](/guides/singapore-citizenship-application) |

Former citizens asking what ICA still has them down as can use the letter too. It answers that question and nothing beyond it, and it doesn't reopen a renunciation.

## The short version

Buy the confirmation letter when a third party needs ICA's status wording in writing. Use ROE when the problem is a young citizen clearing a checkpoint on a foreign passport, and ORAL when the problem is the retention cliff at 21. Those are three separate clocks, and one PDF doesn't stand in for the other two.
