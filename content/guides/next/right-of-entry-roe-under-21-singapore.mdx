The dual-passport kid threads on r/askSingapore tend to blur the pink IC, the Singapore passport, the foreign passport and ORAL at 21 into one airport-queue panic. ICA actually publishes something much narrower for this situation. It's called Right of Entry, and it exists so that a Singapore citizen under 21 who also holds a foreign passport can clear Singapore immigration on the foreign book. If you're renewing that book and need the FormSG route, we've covered it in [transferring ROE to a new passport](/guides/roe-transfer-new-foreign-passport-singapore).

Be clear about what it isn't. **ROE is not permission to hold dual citizenship past 21.** Retention still runs entirely through [ORAL](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21). And if what you need is a status letter for a school or a foreign mission, that's a different product again, covered in [confirmation of citizenship status](/guides/confirmation-singapore-citizenship-status).

## Who can get one

ROE is for a Singapore citizen under 21 who also holds a foreign passport. There's no fee. When the documents are complete, the outcome usually comes back in about 3 working days, which by ICA standards is quick enough that you shouldn't need to plan a trip around it — though you should still not leave it until the week you fly.

Who files depends on age. For a child under 16, a parent submits, or a proxy the parent has appointed. From 16 to 20, the child can file as the requestor themselves.

The ROE is issued on the foreign passport itself. Once ICA confirms issuance, that passport can be used for immigration clearance entering and leaving Singapore at Singapore checkpoints.

## Documents, and the name-match trap

On the child's side it's straightforward at a high level: the foreign passport, plus a deed poll or religious certificate if the name has changed at any point.

The rule that actually trips families up is the name match. The name on the foreign passport has to match ICA's records before ROE will be issued on that passport, and there's no discretion in it. If the child already holds a Singapore IC and the two names have drifted apart — a middle name dropped on one document, a spelling normalised on the other — fix the ICA particulars first through the IC unit or the [name-change path](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore). Applying for ROE and hoping the mismatch gets waved through just costs you the wait twice.

On the requestor side, proxies need a letter of authorisation, and where ICA lists it, the requestor's own Singapore IC.

## When the foreign passport is renewed

The ROE doesn't migrate to the new book on its own. ICA's published instruction is to submit a fresh request to transfer the ROE onto the new travel document, and the detail is in [ROE transfer via FormSG](/guides/roe-transfer-new-foreign-passport-singapore). Think of it the way PR families think about the [Transfer REP](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport) habit: new passport first, immigration annotation second, and only then book the long-haul flight.

## What ROE is not

| What people believe | What's actually true |
| --- | --- |
| "ROE means they can keep both citizenships forever" | It doesn't. The ORAL and adult renunciation rules still land at 21 |
| "ROE replaces a Singapore passport" | It only makes clearance work on a foreign passport. It isn't a substitute for citizenship documents |
| "The embassy can issue ROE overseas, like a visa sticker" | Follow ICA's online request flow and whatever instructions are live at the time, rather than mission folklore |
| "The confirmation letter is the same thing" | Different product entirely. The [confirmation letter](/guides/confirmation-singapore-citizenship-status) is a digital status letter costing S$20, and it does nothing at a checkpoint |

If you have a son in this position, National Service is the other clock running in the background. Get across [NS and the Exit Permit](/guides/ns-exit-permit-pr-families) before any travel plan assumes the foreign passport keeps making things easy indefinitely.

## The rule to hold onto

If an under-21 Singapore citizen in your family travels regularly on a foreign passport, get ROE onto that passport and transfer it whenever the passport is renewed, so that what the checkpoint expects matches what you're holding. Then diary ORAL at 21 as a completely separate exercise. ROE buys convenience at the border. It never buys dual citizenship past the published retention window.
