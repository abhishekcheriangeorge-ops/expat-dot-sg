People renounce PR for ordinary reasons: a permanent relocation, or the end of a roots experiment that didn't take. Then there's the version that fills the loudest Reddit threads, which is trying to exit NS mid-stream. ICA's side of this is bureaucratic and survivable. MINDEF's side is the part the forums under-warn about.

If your situation is NS-shaped — pre-enlistment versus NSF versus after ORD, collateral damage to the family that sponsored you, or letting a REP lapse instead of filing properly — the decision companion is [renouncing PR and NS liability traps](/guides/renouncing-pr-ns-liability-traps-singapore).


One check before you go further. **Singapore citizens use a completely different pack**, covered in [renouncing Singapore citizenship](/guides/renouncing-singapore-citizenship). This page is about Permanent Residence only.

## When renunciation is the path, and when it isn't

ICA's framing is that a PR may renounce if they intend to relocate or leave Singapore permanently. That intention matters, because it also decides who gets pulled along with you.

If you're the main applicant and you renounce, the dependants who got PR under that sponsorship (spouse and children under 21) generally have to renounce together with you. It isn't an individual decision, however individual it feels while you're filling in the form. Children who are 21 or over and got PR under a parent are treated separately and need their own application.

There's also a case where you don't need to file at all. If you already lost PR by staying overseas without a valid REP, ICA says you're not required to file a renunciation. Worth confirming before you start assembling documents, because people do file unnecessarily.

And renunciation isn't automatic. The government may reject or withhold it where you have unresolved matters with other agencies, or outstanding NS obligations.

## What to prepare

Mandatory uploads are PDF, JPEG, or PNG, each one ≤1MB, which is smaller than a phone photo and worth sorting out before you sit down to file. The pack typically includes:

- Annex A, the Letter of Request setting out your reasons
- Annex B, the MINDEF Acknowledgement Form where it applies, for NS-liable male dependants and Foreign Student Scheme PRs, and for sponsors where the table requires it
- Your passport biodata and signature pages
- Name-change proofs, and divorce, custody, or death certificates where relevant
- A loss-of-IC police report or statutory declaration if the blue IC has gone missing
- A clearance letter from MINDEF, SPF, or SCDF, which is compulsory for serving NSFs

The application runs through ICA's online portal and takes about 20 minutes. Processing is often up to around 4 weeks, longer if they come back with clarifications. Incomplete packs simply stall, so the twenty minutes is worth spending properly.

### While you wait

If you're in Singapore, stay until it completes. On approval you'll receive a 30-day Visit Pass and a renunciation letter by email, so check your spam folder, because it's exactly the kind of mail that gets filtered.

Then return the blue IC within 14 days, either through an Overseas Mission or by registered mail to ICA's published address. If it's lost, a statutory declaration or police report stands in its place.

## NS-liable males: read this before you trust a Reddit walkthrough

ICA's own PR page is blunt about this. Renouncing or losing PR without serving or completing full-time NS has a serious adverse impact on later applications to work or study here, and on long-term immigration facilities generally. **That reaches your family members' sponsorship, REP renewals, and any future citizenship bid** — the consequence doesn't stay neatly with the person who renounced.

The r/NationalServiceSG threads that map out "tell your PC, MINDEF interview, ICA renunciation, leave" are describing a process. A process is not a consequence-free outcome, and those threads tend to end at the airport, which is years before the consequences would surface.

Serving NSFs need the clearance letter, and approval is discretionary. Whatever else you do, don't go AWOL while the paperwork is pending.

## Money and estate after renunciation

CPF is where the sequencing bites. Full account closure and withdrawal for non-citizens only happens once you are no longer SC or PR, so the status change has to come first and the account closure after it. We cover the mechanics in [CPF withdrawal when leaving](/guides/cpf-withdrawal-leaving-singapore), the official position sits on the [CPF Board leaving page](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/cpf-asset-management/on-leaving-singapore), and the usual EP-holder myths are in [CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners).

Wills and nominations want updating once your status and your accounts have both actually changed, rather than in anticipation. See [wills and probate](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore).

Your REP stops mattering the moment you're no longer a PR. What you shouldn't do is treat a lapsing REP as a quiet alternative to renouncing, because that's accidental loss and it isn't the same thing at all. The [REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas) sets out the difference.
