**Short answer:** A lost blue PR or pink SC NRIC goes to ICA's Loss of IC e-Service, not MOM, and you want to be filing **within 14 days**. Fees are typically S$100 for a first loss and S$300 for later ones, so verify live. Singpass Digital IC keeps daily life moving but it doesn't waive the replacement. Losing an EP card is a different rail entirely.

The "I lost my IC" threads on r/askSingapore run two different situations together: the blue NRIC that PRs and citizens carry, and the FIN work-pass plastic. Different agency, different fees, different clocks, and using the wrong one burns days you don't have. If what's gone is an EP, S Pass or DP card, you want [lost work pass card](/guides/lost-replace-work-pass-card). This page is about Identity Cards, meaning the blue PR card or pink SC card ICA issues once your formalities are done. If the card isn't lost but the details on it are wrong, or it's damaged, that's ICA's change-of-particulars path instead — [change of name on NRIC](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore).

## What to do in the first day or two

1. Report the loss to ICA through the Loss of IC e-Service with Singpass, and apply for the replacement. ICA asks you to do this **within 14 days** of the report.
2. Use Singpass Digital IC for day-to-day identity wherever it's accepted. It's genuinely useful, and it is not a reason to put off the filing.
3. If the card went with a stolen wallet or bag, file a police report as well. ICA says a police report isn't required for the IC replacement itself, but banks and insurers usually ask for one, and any later appeal reads better with it attached.
4. If you're overseas, report when you can. ICA's published path is to complete the replacement on your next return to Singapore through the same e-Service, because collection still needs your identity authenticated here. The full sequence is in [lost NRIC overseas](/guides/lost-nric-overseas-return-singapore).

## What ICA charges (verify live)

| Event | Published fee |
| --- | --- |
| First loss | S$100 |
| Second and subsequent losses | S$300 |
| Processing | About 7 working days, longer in peaks; photo or supporting document problems stretch it |
| Collection window | Book collection within 3 months of approval, and watch for the SMS or email |

Once you've submitted a lost-IC replacement, ICA issues a new card. There's no refund if the old one turns up in a coat pocket the following week, which is worth remembering before you file in a panic on day one. Reddit stories about getting the S$300 knocked down do surface, usually attached to a solid theft police report and a hardship case. Treat that as discretion someone else was granted, not as policy you can plan around.

## Losing it versus re-registering at 30 or 55

These two get confused constantly, and they're unrelated processes.

| Situation | Path |
| --- | --- |
| Card missing or stolen, or you simply can't produce it | [Loss of IC](https://www.ica.gov.sg/documents/ic/loss) |
| Turning 30 (before your 31st birthday) or 55 (before your 56th), with no replacement IC in the last 10 years | The re-registration route: [NRIC re-registration at 30 / 55](/guides/nric-reregistration-30-55-singapore), subsidised at S$10 for citizens and S$50 for PRs once ICA's digital notification arrives |
| Living overseas when re-registration falls due | Re-register within one year of your return to Singapore |

Plenty of adults who took up PR and collected a blue IC in their late twenties never receive a "you're turning 30" notice and assume something has gone wrong in the system. Usually nothing has. ICA's 10-year rule is the reason, and you're simply not due. Wait for the official SMS or Singpass notification rather than forcing an attempt you aren't eligible for.

## Address stickers and moving house

Changing flat, or posting overseas, isn't the same as losing the card, but it runs through the same identity stack. The full playbook is in [eCOA address change](/guides/ecoa-nric-address-change-singapore). IC holders, citizens and PRs alike and including those residing overseas, must report a new residential address within **28 days**. A PIN mailer goes to the new address, and then a sticker goes on the back of the IC, or you get a new IC if you happen to be mid-replacement anyway. The forum advice about putting a Malaysian address down because it looks better for REP is how people torch their renewal credibility, so don't: see [REP](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).

## Work-pass plastic versus the blue IC

| | MOM work-pass card | ICA NRIC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | EP / S Pass / DP and peers | SC / PR |
| Agency | MOM, and your employer usually files | ICA, and you file it yourself with Singpass |
| Digital bridge | SGWorkPass | Singpass Digital IC |
| Typical first-loss fee | MOM's published EP card fees | S$100 |

After PR formalities, your FIN card world ends. Go through wallets, travel pouches and the [annual life-admin](/guides/renewing-life-admin-annually) checklist so you're not still carrying both plastics "just in case", because the one you hand over at a counter should never be a coin flip.

## The rule to hold onto

Report the loss quickly, replace it on ICA's clock, and keep Digital IC and your passport as the travel pair. Paying S$100 once is a considerably better afternoon than explaining an unreported lost identity document to a bank, a club door, or an enlistment admin desk.
