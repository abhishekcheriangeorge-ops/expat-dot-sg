The "HR says my annual leave eats my notice" threads on r/askSingapore mix up three different MOM rails, and the mixing up is what costs people money. There's encashing unused leave, there's taking annual leave during notice with approval, and there's a separate mutual deal to bring your last day forward. They aren't the same thing and they don't pay the same. Floor entitlements sit in [Employment Act leave and notice](/guides/employment-act-leave-notice-ep). This page is the exit math layer on top of them.

## Three exit patterns, and why you shouldn't collapse them

| Pattern | What it usually means | Who has to agree |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Encashing unused AL | Accrued, unused statutory or contract leave is paid out on termination, misconduct carve-outs aside | The employer pays it, and you don't spend those days on the calendar |
| Taking AL during notice | You're still serving notice while you're on approved leave, and your last day stays where it is | The employer approves when you take the leave |
| Offsetting notice with AL | Your last day is brought forward, so leave days replace calendar notice days | Both of you. It's a **mutual agreement**, not something HR can announce at you |

The third one is the one that gets presented as policy when it's really a negotiation, so it's worth a polite written question rather than a nod in a meeting. MOM's termination-with-notice page is the primary text for all of this. Screenshot your leave balances before you resign rather than after, because a WhatsApp "we'll sort the leave out later" is exactly how encashment fights start.

## MC during notice still counts on the clock

If you're covered by the Employment Act, sick leave taken during notice counts toward the notice period, and it counts whether the sick leave is paid or unpaid. Your employer **can't** stretch the notice period to make up for those days, and can't claim short-notice damages over them either. A contract clause that says otherwise is arguing with published MOM guidance, which is why it's worth keeping the MC and the MOM link in the same email thread instead of in two separate conversations.

Unpaid leave during notice is the different case. There the employer can extend notice, but only if you agree to it.

## Where this bumps into your pass

Leave math doesn't pause any immigration clock, so the two have to be sequenced together rather than one after the other.

1. Get the new IPA in hand before you burn the old job, which we lay out in [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) and the [IPA-before-resign calendar](/guides/ipa-before-resign-calendar-ep-singapore)
2. A retrenchment exit still owes you unused AL and notice pay on the last day — [retrenchment benefits](/guides/retrenchment-ep-benefits)
3. If the encashed leave comes up short, that's a salary claim rather than a HR disagreement, and it goes to TADM. See [salary disputes](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom)
4. Line up IR21 and final pay against the last day that actually applies after any offset, not the one you first wrote down — [tax clearance](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving)

## The rule worth holding on to

Encashment, offsetting and leave-during-notice are three different things with three different outcomes for your bank balance and your last day. Ask HR in writing which one they mean, keep the balances and the MOM page in that same thread, and don't trade away a clean last day for a verbal promise to pay the leave out later.
