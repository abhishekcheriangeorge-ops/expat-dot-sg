The r/askSingapore version of this question is usually “I got laid off but my spouse is Singaporean, do I still only get 30 days?” The answers get tangled because four different products end up mashed into one panic: STVP, Dependant’s Pass, spouse LTVP, and graduate LTVP. They aren’t variations on a theme. They have different sponsors, different issuing agencies, and different rules about work, and working out which lane you’re in is most of the job.

This guide covers one situation specifically: the main EP has died, and you’re married to a Singapore citizen or PR who can sponsor you for an ICA spouse LTVP. If you want the hour-by-hour version, we keep a same-week checklist at [EP cancel with an SC or PR spouse](/guides/ep-cancel-spouse-ltvp-same-week-checklist-singapore).

The generic between-jobs choreography, meaning the up-to-90-day STVP request, the DP cascade, and IR21, stays on [between jobs on an EP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore). The deeper spouse LTVP and PMLA mechanics live on [spouse LTVP and PMLA](/guides/spouse-ltvp-pmla-singapore).

## Which product you actually need

| What you want | What that means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| A short buffer to pack or interview while you’re still here | An employer-requested STVP when the EP is cancelled. MOM publishes up to 90 days, and HR often defaults to something shorter unless somebody asks |
| A longer stay as the foreign spouse of an SC or PR | An ICA spouse LTVP, sponsored by your local spouse |
| To work while you hold that LTVP | A PLOC, or an employer-held LOC. See [PLOC and LOC](/guides/ploc-loc-ica-ltvp-singapore) |
| To stay and job-hunt as a local IHL graduate, with no SC or PR spouse sponsoring you | [Graduate LTVP](/guides/graduate-ltvp-job-hunt), which is an entirely different eligibility row |
| Permanent status | [Spouse-sponsored PR](/guides/pr-for-spouse-singapore), which does not become automatic just because your EP died |

One thing that surprises people: Dependant Passes hang off your EP, so when the EP goes, **the DPs generally go with it**. If your trailing spouse is on a DP and you’re assuming that pass will quietly carry them while you sort yourself out, check that assumption before cancel day, not after. We cover it properly in [Dependant’s Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore).

## Sequencing that actually works

Start before cancel day, while you still have leverage and while HR still has your file open. Get three things confirmed in writing: the cancellation date, whether they’ll request the longest STVP MOM allows, and how they’re handling IR21 and the withholding of your final pay. That last one catches people who were expecting a normal payday ([tax clearance when leaving](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving) explains why the money stops).

If you’re already married to a Singapore citizen, the spouse LTVP file can run in parallel with your notice period and any STVP. PMLA is worth understanding here mostly so you don’t chase it: it only helps if you completed it before the marriage. It isn’t a post-layoff shortcut, however often it gets suggested as one.

If you’re married to a PR, there’s no PMLA path at all. You file the spouse LTVP on the published PR-sponsor clock, which is orientationally around 6 weeks when the documents are in order. The words “when the documents are in order” are doing real work in that sentence.

If you aren’t married yet, be realistic. ROM plus an LTVP is a multi-week project, so the STVP length you can get and your plan for leaving and re-entering matter far more than the WhatsApp advice to just marry next week. Work Permit holders also still need MOM marriage approval where that’s published.

Then work rights, which is the step people skip. An LTVP is permission to stay, not permission to earn. Tick the PLOC box when you file the LTVP, or request it afterwards, and use an employer-held LOC for the restricted occupations. Living here legally while working illegally is a much worse position than the one you started in.

Last, the household logistics: the lease, the school places, the helper. An STVP is a bridge, not a housing plan, and if the honest answer is that the family is leaving rather than staying, switch over to the [leaving Singapore playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook) and stop paying for optionality you won’t use.

One nuance worth knowing. Some people do file the spouse LTVP while still on their EP, attaching the termination letter and an explanation. That happens, and it can work, but treat it as case-by-case ICA practice rather than a published rule that says you must wait for the STVP. Whatever your outcome letter says is what governs you.

## Myths that cost people money

| What you’ll hear | What is actually the case |
| --- | --- |
| “STVP lets me keep working until day 90” | An STVP after cancellation is visit stay. It carries none of your EP work rights |
| “PLOC comes automatically with a spouse LTVP” | It is optional and assessed, so you have to ask. See the [PLOC guide](/guides/ploc-loc-ica-ltvp-singapore) |
| “Graduate LTVP and spouse LTVP are the same thing” | Different ICA rows. Graduate stay-to-hunt is not a spouse product and does not come with PLOC |
| “Losing the EP starts the spouse PR process” | PR stays discretionary either way, as we explain in [spouse PR](/guides/pr-for-spouse-singapore) |

The retrenchment money question, meaning notice pay and TADM, runs on a separate track from all of this: see [retrenchment on an EP](/guides/retrenchment-ep-benefits).

## What to do the day you get the news

Run two clocks at once. The first is HR’s STVP request, where you push for the full 90 days rather than accepting whatever they’d have filed by habit. The second, if your spouse is a citizen or PR, is the spouse LTVP file, with PLOC attached if you’ll need to work.

The mistake that costs the most is waiting: letting a 30-day default run down before your spouse opens the ICA e-Service, as though the shorter pass has to expire before the longer one can start. It doesn’t. And a visit pass buffer, however long you negotiate it, is still a buffer and not a plan.
