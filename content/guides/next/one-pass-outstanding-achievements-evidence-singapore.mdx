## The two tracks aren't a blend

ONE Pass commonly offers a high fixed-monthly salary path or an outstanding-achievements path. Those are alternatives, not ingredients. The forum version of this — "I'm almost at S$30k and I did a TED talk" — is exactly where IPA disappointments begin, because neither half of it is a complete story on its own. And if you're really here because of the spouse Letter of Consent, that hangs off a genuine principal approval and nothing less. We go into it in [spouse LOC depth](/guides/one-pass-spouse-loc-depth-singapore).

## Building an evidence pack that holds up

Decide first whether you're a salary-track candidate or an achievements-track candidate, and then tell that one story properly. An application that hedges reads as weak on both.

Lean on things other people made about you rather than things you wrote about yourself: patents, peer-reviewed work, wins in competitions your field recognises, a funded exit. A well-written bio is not evidence of anything except that you can write a bio. Date-stamp all of it, too, because glory from a decade ago rarely carries a live filing on its own.

If you're anywhere near the salary band, keep the fixed-monthly documentation and employer letters ready regardless. It costs you nothing to have them and it's the obvious fallback if the achievements framing doesn't land.

And don't resign on the strength of a feeling about how your achievements will be read. Have a sponsored [EP](/guides/employment-pass-singapore) Plan B that you'd actually be willing to take.

## The decision rule

**If you can't document outstanding achievements with dated, third-party proof, stay on the salary track or take an employer EP.** Inventing a ONE Pass achievements story out of LinkedIn folklore costs you a filing fee and several months you'd rather have back.
