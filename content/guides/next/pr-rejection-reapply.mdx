**Short answer:** ICA doesn't publish a scorecard, so there's nothing to reverse-engineer. Appeal only through the official e-Service, and reapply when your profile has genuinely changed rather than on a schedule. A long "pending" is not a good sign, and it isn't a bad one either.

PR threads on r/askSingapore cluster around three moments: waiting forever, being rejected with no reasons given, and working out what to change before attempt number two. ICA doesn't publish a scorecard, and no amount of forum archaeology will produce one. What it does publish is how to appeal, how to check your status, and the fact that a fresh application only makes sense once your circumstances have actually moved.

## Pending is not approval, or rejection

ICA's published orientation is that applications are generally processed within 6 months when the documents are in order, and that some take longer. On the forums you'll find waits of 12–27 months reported as routine. A long wait means your file hasn't been decided yet. That is all it means. People read it as evidence the case is being taken seriously, which is a comforting story with nothing behind it.

| The thing people panic about | A safer reading |
| --- | --- |
| "Pending" for more than 6 months | Still undecided. Email ICA with the reference if you want to confirm nothing was requested and missed |
| The upload button disappears | Often means an outcome is near. Still not a guarantee either way |
| Your reference changes from PRE-… to PRS-… | Usually an ICA-side refile or reconsideration path. Read it as "still live", not as approved |

You can check status through MyICA with Singpass, or on the e-Service with your reference ID and FIN or UIN. Outcomes arrive by email, or by post for older submissions made before 26 June 2024.

## Telling ICA about changes while you wait

A salary jump, a new employer, a marriage, a birth, a property purchase, a degree finally conferred. All of these are worth reporting. The Reddit standby of "just email ICA_PR@…" is folklore. Use the upload channel inside the live application when ICA has opened one, and if it hasn't, write to ICA with your application reference and attach the evidence. What you must not do is start a second concurrent PR application.

Changing jobs mid-pending is the anxiety that comes up most. Update ICA once the change is real. Don't put a career on hold out of superstition, and don't go quiet on the file either.

## If it comes back rejected

ICA does not explain individual refusals in its public FAQs. There is no letter telling you which box you missed. The published assessment themes still apply, both on appeal and on a refile: family ties to Singaporeans, economic contributions, qualifications, age, family profile, length of residency, ability to integrate, and commitment to sinking roots. That last one is vaguer than most applicants would like, and it is genuinely part of the assessment.

### The appeal path, and it is the e-Service only

You can appeal a rejected SC, PR or LTVP application through ICA's dedicated e-Service. Who submits it matters.

- The original sponsor submits, using Singpass. If there was no sponsor, the main applicant does.
- If the original application wasn't filed with Singpass, use the same email address as the first filing.
- ICA states that it does **not accept appeals in person, by email, or by post**. Take that literally; the email route still circulates on forums and goes nowhere.
- If your application is still pending, wait for the outcome. The appeal e-Service is not a status nudge.

Each appeal is assessed on its own merits, processing times vary, and the outcome comes by email and in your online status.

What evidence belongs on an appeal as opposed to a fresh refile, and how to avoid ending up with two live PR files, is covered in [PR rejection: appeal evidence vs reapply](/guides/pr-rejection-appeal-evidence-reapply-singapore).

### Starting again with a fresh application

ask.gov.sg's ICA answer is that if you're unsuccessful, you should consider a fresh online application when circumstances change. Community folklore about a hard six-month cooldown is widespread; check your rejection letter and the live ICA pages for any cooling-off language rather than taking the forums as your clock.

Reapply when something material has actually moved:

- A sustained higher fixed salary, or a clearer economic contribution
- Longer residency, or a stronger family profile: length of marriage, SC or PR children, local schooling
- Credentials completed, meaning a degree conferred rather than "almost done"
- Documented community roots that weren't there the first time

Sending the same documents back six weeks later costs you the fee, which is a non-refundable **S$100** per application taken at submission, and more expensively it costs you another year of waiting. No agency can buy you an outcome. ICA explicitly disclaims commercial "PR specialists".

## When a local sponsor's application is refused

Threads about a citizen or PR sponsoring a spouse tend to land on short marriage length, thin shared roots, or the sponsor's income. Length of marriage and household stability do come up constantly in peer anecdotes. They are not published pass/fail thresholds, which matters, because treating them as thresholds leads people to sit and wait for an arbitrary anniversary. Plan a refile around real changes — a second child, a property, more years genuinely living together — rather than calendar superstition about applying in January.

## NS still belongs in the decision

A rejection doesn't take National Service out of the equation. If the next attempt includes sons, model that before anything else.
