**Short answer:** Put a recurring Singapore life-admin week in the calendar. Once a year you sit down and go through passes, passports, REP, NRIC re-registration and address, insurance, the lease, school fees and embassy lead times, before any of them turn into a problem.

Almost all the forum panic you'll read about passport appointments, EP renewals, REP surprises and school re-enrolment comes from someone who skipped this. None of it is difficult on its own. The trouble is that every one of these clocks runs quietly and independently of the others, so nothing warns you until something has already expired.

## Review annually (or every September)

- Your work and family passes. Note the expiry dates on the EP, S Pass, DP and LTVP, and check where you now sit against COMPASS and the current salary thresholds, because those move. [EP renewal](/guides/ep-renewal-strategy) has the strategy, and while you're at it confirm SGWorkPass is still installed and working ([lost pass card](/guides/lost-replace-work-pass-card)).
- PR and citizenship ID. Check the blue or pink NRIC is physically where you think it is, that the Digital IC is live in Singpass, and that you know the loss fees before you need to know them ([lost NRIC](/guides/lost-replace-nric-pr-singapore)).
- Name and particulars. If you're mid deed poll, the order matters. PRs update the foreign passport first, then pay ICA S$60 to update particulars. [Change of name on NRIC](/guides/change-name-nric-singapore).
- Passport and REP sync. A new foreign passport means you need a free ICA Transfer REP. [Transfer REP](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport).
- Children and family PR. Newborn or under-21 sponsorship goes through [PR for child](/guides/pr-for-child-newborn-singapore), spouse through [PR for spouse](/guides/pr-for-spouse-singapore), parents through [aged parents](/guides/aged-parents-pr-ltvp-singapore), and the student exam and IP timing question sits on [student PR](/guides/pr-for-student-singapore).
- Spouse LTVP and work rights. Stay for an SC or PR spouse is [spouse LTVP + PMLA](/guides/spouse-ltvp-pmla-singapore). The right to work is a separate stack on [PLOC / LOC](/guides/ploc-loc-ica-ltvp-singapore).
- Extra EP documents. If MOM asks for a NOA partway through a renewal, [EP NOA / documents](/guides/ep-renewal-noa-documents) explains what they're after.
- NRIC re-registration. The age 30 and age 55 windows apply if ICA has notified you and your card is at least 10 years old. Diary the letter when it arrives rather than filing it. [NRIC re-registration](/guides/nric-reregistration-30-55-singapore).
- Residential address. Report a move within **28 days**, local or overseas, through eCOA. This is the one the September review catches most often, usually for someone who moved mid-year and forgot. [eCOA address change](/guides/ecoa-nric-address-change-singapore).
- Citizenship IPA and SCJ. Holding a citizenship IPA? Diary portal day +3 and the SEV / CSS steps on [SCJ after IPA](/guides/singapore-citizenship-journey-scj).
- Re-Entry Permit. PRs renew before travelling, not after booking. Being unemployed or spending long stretches overseas often means a shorter stamp than you were expecting. [REP](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).
- Passports. Schools and airlines want 6–12 months of validity, and the real constraint is embassy lead times rather than your own diary. [Passports and documents](/guides/passports-and-documents-overseas).
- Dual-nationality clocks. For children who are SC by descent or registration, put the 21→22 ORAL window in the calendar now rather than meeting it by surprise. [ORAL](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21).
- Insurance. Beneficiaries, outpatient caps and maternity waiting periods all quietly stop matching your actual life. [Expat health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat).
- Housing. The lease renewal and diplomatic clause windows are dates worth keeping in a calendar rather than in your head. [Deposits and diplomatic clause](/guides/security-deposit-diplomatic-clause).
- School. Re-enrolment fees, bus contracts and camp deposits each run on their own schedule.
- Estate. A will and guardianship arrangement that recognises your cross-border reality, rather than one drafted for a family that lives in one country.
- Condo or HDB. Emergency contacts at security and at school should still be current.
- Banking. FIN→NRIC after PR, PayNow proxies, and the standing instructions that broke when you changed phone number. [After PR checklist](/guides/after-getting-pr-checklist).
- Exit readiness. Know what IR21 and CPF closure would involve if you ended PR, even if you have no plans to. [Leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook) and [CPF withdrawal](/guides/cpf-withdrawal-leaving-singapore).

## Passports and embassies

Your Singapore pass or NRIC is **not** your passport, which sounds obvious right up until the year your passport quietly ages out underneath you. Missions often want appointments booked weeks ahead, and some make you come back a second time to collect. Start from [MFA overseas missions](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/Overseas-Missions) for your nationality's Singapore embassy pages.

## PR traps the September review catches

There's no such thing as renewing permanent residence. What you renew is the REP. The "PR renewal" folklore sends people hunting for a product that doesn't exist, and it wastes a surprising amount of time.

Putting an overseas address on the IC is legal, and you're supposed to report it. Just expect a harder REP conversation afterwards if your Singapore ties start looking thin on paper.

If you lose the IC while you're overseas, replace it once you're back. Don't let it drift until a club night or an enlistment letter forces the issue for you.

## Boring systems are kindness

A shared password vault, scanned marriage and birth certificates, and a one-page household "in case of emergency" sheet are all thoroughly unglamorous, and they are what prevents 2 a.m. chaos. Families stay kinder to each other under stress when the admin is already done.
