The parent guide covers what money might be owed and the STVP ask on cancellation: [retrenchment on EP](/guides/retrenchment-ep-benefits). This one picks up at the dispute fork. When does unpaid quantum go to TADM, when does MOM point non-unionised staff at TAFEP instead, and what happens to your pass while all of that grinds on.

## Which door you're actually knocking on

Establish first whether the money you're chasing is contractual or aspirational, because that decides everything after it.

If your contract or a collective agreement names a retrenchment benefit and HR hasn't paid it, that's a contractual money claim and it goes to TADM like any other. The filing windows once you've left employment are tight, often 6 months from your last day, though read the live TADM text rather than trusting a number in a guide. A tight window and a job hunt are a bad combination, which is why this fork gets decided early rather than after the dust settles.

If there's no contractual provision and the real complaint is that your employer sits below the TAMEM advisory norms, you're somewhere else entirely. Negotiate in writing, and expect MOM's FAQ to steer many non-unionised employees towards TAFEP engagement rather than towards a statutory cheque that was never going to exist.

Unpaid salary, notice pay, and unused annual leave are a separate question from retrenchment benefit, and they go to TADM regardless of what the retrenchment letter says. We cover those in [salary disputes](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom) and [AL encashment](/guides/annual-leave-encashment-notice-offset-ep-singapore).

If the selection itself looks discriminatory, or "performance" has been pinned on what was plainly a redundancy, that's TAFEP or counsel. It's a different kind of fight from arguing about a number.

The thing worth internalising: **retrenchment benefit is usually contractual or negotiated, not a universal Employment Act payout.** Read the letter and the handbook the night you get them, not the week after.

## Releases and waivers

Get the package in writing before you sign a release that waives salary, leave, or benefit claims. A release does exactly what the word suggests, and it does it to claims you haven't valued yet.

"Sign today or the offer expires" is a negotiation tactic. It may still be a real deadline, but screenshot the draft and MOM's FAQ before you decide either way.

And if the letter reaches for misconduct-dismissal language when the facts are a redundancy, that's a different fight. Counsel earns their fee when the letter smells wrong.

## Mediation does not extend your EP

Neither TADM nor TAFEP pauses your pass cancellation. The employment dispute and the immigration clock run on separate tracks, and only one of them cares that you're in the middle of something.

1. Ask HR in writing for the longest STVP MOM allows on cancel — see [between jobs](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore)
2. File while you're still in Singapore if the left-employment clock is tight
3. Remember you can't work on an STVP, so you need a new IPA before you start anything: [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore)
4. Start IR21 early rather than late: [tax clearance](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving)

## The rule worth remembering

A named contractual benefit that goes unpaid is a TADM clock. An advisory-norm gap is a negotiation, with TAFEP engagement per MOM's FAQ. Salary, leave, and notice arrears go to TADM either way. Keep the pass and STVP calendar on its own track, because the quantum fight will outlast your pass.
