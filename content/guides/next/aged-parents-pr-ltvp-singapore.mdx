The "bring Mum over" threads on r/askSingapore treat social visit passes, LTVP and PR as one form with different boxes ticked. ICA publishes two genuinely different products, and which one is open to you depends on your own status rather than on how much your parent needs to be here. Aged-parent PR is listed **only for parents of a Singapore citizen aged 21+**. Parent LTVP is the one open to parents of an SC or a PR, with sponsor rules that turn on the child's age. And **parents-in-law aren't on the parent LTVP row at all**. If you're already past the application and into renewals, the clocks and sponsor-change rules sit on [aged-parent LTVP renewal](/guides/aged-parent-ltvp-renewal-sponsor-change-singapore).

## Which row is actually open to you

| What you want | The ICA product | Who sponsors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent residence for a parent | PR, aged parent of an SC | A Singapore citizen child aged 21+, logging in with Singpass |
| A longer stay, without PR | LTVP, parent of an SC or PR | If the child is 21+, that SC or PR child sponsors. If the child is under 21, you need a local SC or PR sponsor aged 21+ |
| In-laws | Nothing on the parent LTVP row | Don't file under "parent" folklore |

So if you're a PR rather than a citizen and you want your parents here for care visits, the realistic starting point is an LTVP, or plain social visits, and not a PR application on a row that ICA's eligibility table doesn't list for you. It isn't that your case would be weak. The row simply isn't there.

## "Aged" has no public number on the PR page

ICA's live PR page says "Aged parent of a Singapore citizen of at least 21 years old." Read that carefully, because the 21 belongs to the child, not the parent. ICA doesn't publish a fixed parent age anywhere on that eligibility table. Not 55, not 60, not retirement age. So when a forum tells you that ICA only accepts parents over some threshold, that's peer folklore rather than anything you could cite back at them. If you need clarification for your own case, call or write to ICA, and don't pay an agent for an invented cutoff.

Approval stays discretionary whatever your parent's age. ICA's published assessment themes still apply: family ties, economic contributions, qualifications, age, family profile, length of residency, integration, roots. Which means a dependent retiree with no Singapore history of their own can be perfectly eligible to file and still be refused. "Eligible to apply" and "likely to succeed" are two different sentences, and the gap between them is where most of the disappointment sits.

## Fees and clocks (verify live)

### Parent PR, with an SC sponsor

| Stage | Published fee / clock |
| --- | --- |
| Submission | S$100 per application |
| Formalities | S$20 Entry Permit, S$50 for the 5-year REP, S$50 for the IC when it's issued |
| Processing | Around 6 months when the documents are in order, and some run longer |

The PR checklist PDF carries an additional aged-parent document block, including paperwork about children who aren't applying. Work from the live PDF rather than an agency binder, because a binder is only ever a snapshot of what the rules looked like when someone last printed it: [checklist](https://www.ica.gov.sg/docs/default-source/ica/ipses/pr-document-checklist.pdf).

### Parent LTVP, with an SC or PR sponsor

| Stage | Published fee / clock |
| --- | --- |
| Submission | S$45 per application |
| Issuance | S$60 for the LTVP, plus S$30 for a Multiple Journey Visa if it applies |
| Processing | Most land within 6 weeks when the documents are in order, and some run longer |
| Renewal | Apply **at least 3 months before expiry**. The renewed validity starts from issuance, and leftover validity on the old pass **isn't carried forward** — the deep dive is on [parent LTVP renewal / sponsor change](/guides/aged-parent-ltvp-renewal-sponsor-change-singapore) |

Every fee on those tables is non-refundable. And an approval isn't the finish line: after IPA, your parent still has to complete ICA's issuance formalities in person when they're told to.

## Housing, money, and medical realism

Neither an LTVP nor an aged-parent PR **turns your parent into a subsidised citizen**, and this is where household arithmetic usually goes wrong.

Private hospital bills without a workable insurance plan are the obvious one, and they are the reason people end up making decisions under pressure. Housing is the second: parents on an LTVP generally can't buy HDB the way SC and PR households can, so whatever you imagined about a small flat nearby needs checking before it becomes a plan. The third is the one nobody wants to sit with, which is what happens to your parent's right to stay if you, the sponsor, die, emigrate or lose your own status.

Budget the insurance and the caregiver capacity before you treat an LTVP as a soft landing. Orientation on expat medical cashflow lives on [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat), and we're not going to invent a premium quote for you here.

## Citizenship for a parent who already holds PR

A parent who already holds PR can later apply for citizenship under ICA's citizenship eligibility rows, including the aged-parent themes where those are published. It is a separate discretionary file rather than a continuation of the first one, and we cover it on [citizenship application](/guides/singapore-citizenship-application). Rejections after decades of PR are a well-documented forum pattern, so don't read "eligible to apply" as "will get the pink IC."

## Agents and appeals

ICA says plainly that it has no affiliation with commercial immigration specialists for PR or LTVP, which is worth remembering when someone quotes you a success rate. If you're refused, the [appeal e-Service](https://www.ica.gov.sg/reside/reside-appeals) paths are the route. Refiling an identical parent PR application every quarter just burns fees without adding any new substance to the case, and that pattern is what we unpack on [rejection and reapply](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply).

## So which one is yours

If you're an SC aged 21+ and you want permanent status for a parent, the aged-parent PR row is yours to file, as long as you go in clear-eyed about how hard and how discretionary it is. If you're a PR, or you need your parent here without PR, the parent LTVP row is the path, and in-laws are excluded from it. Either way, price the medical risk and the housing first. Having the emotional "Mum should get PR too" conversation after you've done that arithmetic is a lot easier than having it before.
