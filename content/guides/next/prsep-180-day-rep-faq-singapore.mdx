The r/askSingapore threads since 1 December 2025 have mashed four different things into one panic: an expired REP, a visit pass, "reinstatement," and PRSEP. They aren't the same thing, and confusing them is how people lose PR by accident. The orientation guide is [Re-Entry Permit overseas](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas). This page is the scenario companion, working through the published answers in ICA's revision FAQ and MHA's notice rather than repeating the renewal how-to.

## When the 180-day clock starts

From 1 December 2025, if you're physically outside Singapore without a valid REP, you have 180 days to apply for one before PR is lost. ICA's FAQ sets three different start dates, depending on how you ended up in that position.

| Situation | Clock starts |
| --- | --- |
| You left without a valid REP | Date of departure |
| You left with a valid REP that expired while you were overseas | Date of REP expiry |
| You were already overseas without a valid REP on 1 Dec 2025 | 1 December 2025, under MHA's transition rule |

Read "apply" literally. **Meeting the deadline means you filed inside 180 days, not that ICA has to approve.** That distinction does a lot of work further down this page.

## What the FAQ says, versus what the threads say

The most common panic comes from people sitting in Singapore with an expired or expiring REP, convinced they've already lost PR. They haven't. If you're in Singapore, an expiring or expired REP doesn't by itself cancel your PR. What it does is pin you here, so renew before you leave, because leaving is the event that starts everything else.

The mirror image is flying out on a nearly-expired REP and planning to be back before the date. You do retain PR if you return before expiry. It still isn't a plan: renew before the next trip rather than running the same gamble twice.

Then there's the belief that causes the most damage, which is "I came back inside 180 days, so I'm fine." Returning doesn't stop the clock. The clock is about filing, not about where you're standing. Fail to apply inside the 180 days and PR is lost the day after the window ends, and being back in Singapore at the time changes nothing about that.

PRSEP attracts its own folklore. It is not a new multi-year REP. It's a single-entry checkpoint pass that gets you back in during the window, and your Entry Permit still governs your stay. Entering on one doesn't discharge anything either: ICA's FAQ says to apply for a new REP inside the 180 days even after you've returned on a PRSEP.

And then the expensive one. If you miss the window or your application is refused, there is no reinstatement — MHA and ICA are both clear on that. The only route back is a fresh PR application if you're eligible, not an appeal to have the old status restored.

## What PRSEP actually is

During the 180-day prescribed period you remain a PR. Subject to checkpoint assessment and public-health rules, ICA may issue you a Permanent Resident's Single-Entry Pass so you can re-enter without a valid REP.

The FAQ is specific about the limits. It's valid for one entry, on the date stated on the pass. It doesn't carry a separate multi-day "visit validity" the way tourist-visa folklore imagines, because while you're still a PR your stay runs off your Entry Permit. And it doesn't stand in for renewing or obtaining a proper REP before your next departure.

There's a sharp edge here worth naming. If your in-window REP application is refused after you entered on a PRSEP, PR is lost the day after the outcome is sent, and ICA says you're then issued a Visit Pass. You can be sitting in Singapore, having come home on the pass ICA itself gave you, and still lose PR.

So don't build a career move or a school year around PRSEP. It's an emergency re-entry bridge while you regularise, and that's the whole of it.

## Outcomes after you apply, or don't

| Path | Published outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Applied inside 180 days, approved | You remain PR, subject to Entry Permit and REP conditions |
| Applied inside 180 days, still pending | You generally remain PR until the outcome |
| Applied inside 180 days, refused | PR lost the day after the outcome is sent, even if you are in Singapore |
| Never applied inside 180 days | PR lost the day after the period ends, whether you are overseas or already back |
| Lost PR under this process | No appeal for reinstatement; a fresh PR application only |

ICA does say you'll get a notification when PR is lost. Diary the window yourself anyway. A notification arrives after the fact, and by then it only confirms something you can no longer do anything about.

## The rule worth remembering

Renew or obtain an ordinary REP before you leave, every time you can. If you're already overseas without one, count 180 days from the published start date for your situation, file inside that window, and treat any PRSEP you're issued as a single re-entry rather than as travel permission.

If PR is lost, the honest next steps are [fresh PR strategy](/guides/pr-application-strategy) and [rejection and reapply](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply) — or a deliberate [renunciation](/guides/renouncing-pr-singapore), if leaving was the plan anyway.

One last trap. A passport number change still goes through [Transfer REP](/guides/transfer-rep-new-passport), and that only works on a valid REP. Transfer is not a 180-day rescue.
