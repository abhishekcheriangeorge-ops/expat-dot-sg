Everyone who leaves Singapore has a version of the same story, and it resurfaces on r/askSingapore and the Singapore Expats Forum every few weeks. At some point your employer stops paying you. Not out of spite. IR21 tax clearance obliges them to, so **your final salary, and usually your bonus and leave pay with it, gets frozen** at exactly the moment you owe a landlord, a moving company and an airline.

This guide is the money map. For the operational running order, who you tell and when, use the [leaving Singapore playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook).

## The IR21 cash freeze

Where tax clearance applies, IRAS expects your employer to do three things, and none of them are things your HR contact can bend for you as a favour.

They file Form IR21 at least one month before your cessation or qualifying departure. They withhold all monies due to you from the date they know you are leaving — salary, bonus, overtime, leave pay, allowances, gratuities and lump sums, whichever of those apply in your case. And they release nothing until IRAS issues the relevant clearance or payment directions.

Electronic cases usually move faster than the paper-era stories suggest. Even so, plan for **1–3+ weeks of constrained cash** after your last working day, and longer if your paperwork is untidy or the IR21 needs amending after the fact.

### Buffers people wish they had built

The fix is unglamorous and it works. Park 2–3 months of essential expenses in a liquid account **before resignation day**, because resignation day is when the withholding obligation starts, and after that your own cash is the only cash you control.

Don't book non-refundable international movers on the assumption that your final pay arrives the same week. Ask HR in writing which components will be withheld and when they expect to file the IR21, so that later on you are discussing a date rather than a feeling. And if you resign with short notice, expect the process to compress rather than disappear.

## Line-item exit budget (build your own)

| Cost bucket | What to model | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income tax on clearance | Final-year employment income | Could be nil, could be material. IRAS computes it from the IR21 |
| Rent overlap | Notice period vs flight date | Diplomatic clause notice windows matter |
| Deposit risk | 1–2 months’ rent disputed | Cleaning, aircon and “fair wear” fights delay refunds |

| International shipping | 20–40ft container vs air vs sell-down | Sell-down often wins financially |
| Storage | Bridge weeks if destination housing lags | The line everyone forgets |
| Helper exit | MOM cancellation, airfare, agency fees | If you employ an MDW |
| School | Withdrawal notice, fee forfeiture | Handbook-driven, so read the handbook |
| Flights + hotels | Peak season premiums | Align with pass cancellation windows |
| Living float | Food, Grab, last-month utilities | This is what runs while salary is frozen |

## There is no "IR21 fee"

People search for one, and it isn't a thing. There's no consumer application fee you pay the way you'd pay for a visa. What it actually costs you is the withholding, plus whatever tax gets assessed, plus the opportunity cost of money you can see on a payslip and can't spend. Shipping GST and customs, on either the origin or the destination side, is a separate problem — see [shipping out](/guides/shipping-out-household).

## SPR and other special cases

Singapore PRs who are not leaving permanently can fall outside IR21 in some scenarios, and employers often collect a Letter of Undertaking instead. Overseas postings and genuine permanent departures are treated differently again. Check your own situation against IRAS rather than against a WhatsApp group, because the person answering confidently in the group left under their own set of facts, not yours.

## Worked stress-test (illustrative)

Say your last fixed salary was S$12,000/month. Your final month plus a pro-rated bonus gets withheld, the movers quote S$8,000, your rent notice overlaps your flight date by 6 weeks, and the deposit refund lands 4 weeks after handover. Put that on a calendar and you can be cash-outflow positive for 6–8 weeks even if your eventual tax bill turns out to be small.

Which is the whole point. An exit needs a treasury plan, not just a packing plan.
