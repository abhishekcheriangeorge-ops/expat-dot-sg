Citizenship threads on r/askSingapore circle the same four questions forever. Am I eligible after one year of PR? How long does ICA take? Do agencies help? What happens to my other passport? ICA publishes eligibility categories, the fees, and a processing orientation of within 12 months. What it doesn't publish, and never will, is your odds of approval.

This guide is about the application process itself. National Service liability for sons, and what happens to an ex-PR who goes back onto an EP, are a different and heavier conversation — we've written that one up in [citizenship and NS](/guides/citizenship-ns-implications). If you're an adult looking to leave Singapore citizenship rather than take it, you want [renouncing Singapore citizenship](/guides/renouncing-singapore-citizenship), not this page.

## Who ICA says can apply

You can apply if you fit one of ICA's published paths. At a high level, they look like this.

| Path | Published floor |
| --- | --- |
| Adult PR (apply with PR spouse + unmarried children under 21 in marriage / legal adoption) | PR ≥ 2 years, aged 21+ |
| PR married to a Singapore citizen | PR ≥ 2 years and marriage ≥ 2 years (SC spouse sponsors with Singpass) |
| Unmarried child under 21 of an SC (marriage / legal adoption rules) | SC parent sponsors |
| PR student track | Residing in Singapore >3 years (of which ≥1 year as PR) and passed at least one national exam (PSLE / GCE N/O/A) or in IP |
| Aged parent of an SC aged 21+ | SC child sponsors |

Clearing a floor gets your application looked at. It doesn't get it approved. Beyond the published minimums, ICA weighs your family ties to Singaporeans, your economic contributions, qualifications, age, family profile and length of residency — it's assessing whether you can contribute, integrate, and put down roots here. **Being eligible and being approved are two different things**, and a lot of disappointment comes from conflating them.

Three forum beliefs worth letting go of. The first is that if the e-Service let you click through at year one, the two-year rule must be dead. It isn't; an open form field is not a grant, and the published floors still drive the assessment. The second is that some agency somewhere knows the racial quota. ICA explicitly disclaims commercial "citizenship specialists", which should tell you what that knowledge is worth. The third is that a long pending period means you're doing well. ICA's published orientation is within 12 months, some cases run longer, and nothing about that delay is a score.

## What it costs and how long it takes

| Stage | Adult PR / most children paths (as published) |
| --- | --- |
| At submission | S$100 per application (non-refundable) |
| At completion of formalities | S$70 Citizenship Certificate + S$10 Identity Card (new citizens aged 15+) |
| Processing | Generally within 12 months; overseas-born children of SCs often within 1 month when documents are in order |
| Outcome | Email + MyICA / e-Service status |

One practical trap on the Singpass side. Every applicant named on the form has to review and declare for themselves, and ICA recommends getting Singpass registration done first, which takes 2–5 working days. Start the family filing before everyone has Singpass and you'll sit there waiting on a teenager's registration while the form is half-submitted.

## After the IPA: the Citizenship Journey and the formalities

A successful application gets you an In-Principle Approval, which is genuinely worth celebrating and is genuinely not citizenship. **You are not a citizen until the formalities are finished.** If you're between 16 and 60, that includes the mandatory Singapore Citizenship Journey programme, which ICA requires before completion.

Run the SCJ and the formalities as a project, with the same discipline you'd have applied to [PR AIP formalities](/guides/after-getting-pr-checklist).

1. Diary the SCJ modules and sessions the moment the IPA lands. Stalling here delays your pink IC and your passport, and nobody else is going to chase you.
2. Finish any document or renunciation steps your IPA checklist names before the completion appointment, not on the morning of it.
3. Pay the published completion fees: S$70 for the certificate plus S$10 for the IC for new citizens aged 15+, as in the table above.
4. Once the pink IC is in hand, go back through Singpass, your banks and your employer records. Your identity rails change again, and things quietly break if you don't.
5. Only unlock SC-gated schemes once the status is actually live, [Baby Bonus and the CDA](/guides/baby-bonus-cda-mixed-status) for SC children being the obvious one.

If your child already holds SC by descent or registration, none of this applies to them. They're on a separate retention clock that bites at 21, and that's the [ORAL guide](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21), not this checklist.

## Dual nationality and what your home country thinks

Singapore's adult citizenship model is not a relaxed two-passport arrangement, whatever you've read. Many new citizens have to renounce a prior nationality to complete the formalities, and plenty of others run into home-country rules that forbid dual status past a certain age anyway. The "I kept both, it's fine" stories you'll find online are usually either incomplete or specific to a jurisdiction that isn't yours.

Get advice in both countries before you file, and get answers to four things. What exactly must you renounce, and by when? Does renouncing affect inheritance, property ownership, or your ability to get a visa back home? Have you modelled your sons' NS obligations ([citizenship and NS](/guides/citizenship-ns-implications), [Exit Permit](/guides/ns-exit-permit-pr-families))? And if a child already holds SC by descent or registration, who in the family owns the [ORAL at 21](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21) calendar?

That last cluster is where the real damage happens. A male citizen who later tries to renounce SC without having discharged his NS liabilities can find that **renunciation is simply withheld**. And losing SC or PR without completing full-time NS attracts language from ICA and MINDEF about serious adverse impact on future work, study and family sponsorship. It's the same trap family as [renouncing PR](/guides/renouncing-pr-singapore), and it's the one that follows a young man for decades.

## Agencies and "profile building"

No paid immigration consultancy can buy you an outcome. ICA states it has no affiliation with commercial specialists and does not endorse them, which is about as clear as a government agency gets. Spend the money where it actually moves something: certified translations, accurate and complete documents, and proper dual-nationality advice if your other passport is complicated. Not on a cover letter that invents volunteer hours.

If an earlier citizenship application was refused, treat the re-file the way you'd treat a [PR rejection and reapply](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply). Something material about your circumstances needs to have changed, or you wait until it has. Resubmitting the same package a few months later costs you the fee and another year of low-grade anxiety, and changes nothing.

## When to actually file

File when your household is ready for permanent political membership of this country, National Service for liable males included, because that is what you are signing up for. Not because a Reddit timeline told you year three is lucky. If you're still keeping exit optionality on the table, you're not there yet — stay with [long-term stay vs exit](/guides/long-term-stay-vs-exit) and keep your [REP](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas) discipline tight while you're still PR.
