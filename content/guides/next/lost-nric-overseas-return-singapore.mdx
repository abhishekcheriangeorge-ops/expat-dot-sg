The "I lost my blue card in Bangkok" and "my wallet went in London" threads all arrive at the same wrong idea: that the embassy can hand you a temporary IC. It can't. **Missions do not replace Singapore Identity Cards**, and the sooner you accept that, the fewer days you burn chasing consular appointments that were never going to help. Fees, the 14-day report clock, and Digital IC habits live on [lost / stolen NRIC](/guides/lost-replace-nric-pr-singapore). This companion is the narrower case: you're overseas, so report, return, collect.

If what you lost is the plastic EP, S Pass, or DP card rather than an NRIC, that's MOM's rail and not this one: [lost work pass card](/guides/lost-replace-work-pass-card).

## What to do from wherever you are

1. Report the loss through ICA's Loss of IC e-Service with Singpass, as soon as you reasonably can. ICA asks you to report immediately and then apply for the replacement within 14 days of that report.
2. Keep travelling on your valid passport, plus the REP print-out if that's the travel stack your household uses as PRs. The IC was never your boarding pass; it's the passport and REP rules that decide whether you get home: [REP](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas).
3. Use the Singpass Digital IC wherever the counterparty accepts it. It's genuinely useful day to day, and it does not waive the physical replacement filing. People assume it does, then find out months later that nothing was ever filed.
4. Finish the replacement and collection on your next return to Singapore. That's ICA's published path for people residing overseas, and collection is in person, here.

If you can't hit the 14-day window because you're stuck abroad, use the ICA IC unit contact path named on the live Loss page. Don't go inventing a mission workaround; there isn't one waiting to be found.

## What embassies will not do

- Issue a temporary Singapore IC, or a "consular NRIC letter" that stands in for ICA replacement
- Collect a new IC for you or courier it to you overseas. Collection stays in Singapore.
- Convert a foreign police report into an ICA waiver of the e-Service report

A local police report is still worth making if the wallet was stolen, because banks and insurers will ask for one. ICA itself says a police report is not required for the IC replacement.

## Return-week checklist

1. Before you fly, check the status of the Loss e-Service report and the replacement application if you already filed from abroad.
2. Book collection with ICA, or SingPost if your notice offers it, within three months of approval. That window is tighter than it sounds if you travel again soon after landing.
3. Bring the identity documents ICA's collection page lists, typically your passport plus whatever slip or email the notice names.
4. Once you've submitted, a new IC is issued. If the old card turns up in your suitcase the following week, there's no refund. That's the price of filing on time, and it's the right trade.

Fees stay on the parent guide: typically S$100 for a first loss and S$300 for subsequent ones, though verify against the live page.

## The rule worth keeping

Report on ICA's e-Service from wherever you are, fly home on your passport (plus REP if you're a PR), then replace and collect in Singapore.
