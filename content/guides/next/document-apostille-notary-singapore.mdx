Expat document admin falls over in the same place every time, and Reddit records it well. One family gets quoted S$2–3k to notarise, apostille and translate a thick marriage file overseas. Another family, with much the same paperwork, pays embassy rates measured in tens of dollars a page. The gap is almost never the documents. It's which route they went down, and whether anyone asked the receiving authority what it actually wanted first.

In Singapore, paper heading abroad usually runs Notary Public first, then an Apostille from the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL). Paper coming the other way, where ICA or MOM want home-country proof for an LTVP, runs on a different path entirely. Mixing the two up is how people end up paying twice.

## Paper travels in two directions

| Direction | Typical path |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore-issued documents for use overseas (birth and marriage certificates, court papers, notarised affidavits) | SAL legalisation or Apostille for public documents; Notary first, then SAL authentication, for private ones |
| Foreign documents for use in Singapore (LTVP common-law evidence, school forms, home-country affidavits) | Usually notarise or apostille in the issuing country, or an embassy letter plus an English translation. SAL can't apostille a foreign original as though it were Singapore-issued |

## The SAL Apostille, for Singapore documents

Singapore has been in the Apostille Convention since 16 September 2021, and SAL is the competent authority. It affixes an Apostille, and any receiving country that is also a Convention party then accepts the document's origin without the old embassy legalisation chain. If your memory of this process involves three stamps and a courier, that's the chain you're remembering, and you probably don't need it any more.

A few practical things from SAL's published guidance:

- Bring originals where you can. A laminated original may need a certified true copy or extract from the issuing agency instead.
- Digitally issued government documents usually have to be printed before counter legalisation.
- You can send someone else to submit on your behalf, and an appointment generally isn't required for authentication.
- Legalising a public document is published at around S$10.70 each, including GST. Verify live.

## Private documents need a notary first

Private papers — affidavits, true copies of your passport, commercial packs — generally need a Singapore Notary Public before anything else happens. SAL then authenticates the notary's certificate with an Apostille. Both fees usually get settled at the notary's office: the notarisation fee itself, which is capped under the Notaries Public Rules, and the SAL authentication fee, published at around S$87.20 including GST. Ask to see the notarisation schedule rather than taking a number on trust, and verify the SAL figure live.

You can find practitioners through the [SAL Notary Public directory](https://legalisation.sal.sg/Directory). US university forms and US litigation signatures usually work fine with a Singapore notary. The thing to confirm before you start is whether the receiving party also wants embassy consularisation on top, because discovering that afterwards means running the whole errand again.

## The LTVP and ICA document panic

For common-law spouses applying for an LTVP, MOM's [documents required](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/long-term-visit-pass/documents-required) page accepts **any one** of three things:

- A common-law marriage certificate, or
- An affidavit from both partners that the relationship is recognised under your home country's law, notarised in that home country or region, or
- A letter from your embassy acknowledging the common-law relationship under that country's law

One of them, not all three. It's worth reading that twice before you start commissioning documents you don't need.

Anything not in English needs the original and the English translation uploaded together as a single file. On translation itself, the forum consensus is sound: ask your embassy in Singapore for its approved translator list before you accept an overseas quote priced by the character, because character-based pricing on a thick file is where the S$2–3k horror stories come from. Read this alongside the [LTVP guide](/guides/ltvp-singapore) and the [common-law spouse evidence pack](/guides/ltvp-common-law-spouse-evidence-pack-singapore).

## How to keep the bill down

1. Ask the receiving authority (the school, the court, ICA or MOM, the overseas university) exactly what it wants: an Apostille, an embassy stamp, a certified translation, or all three. Everything else follows from that one answer.
2. For Singapore-origin documents, use a Singapore notary and SAL. **Don't ship originals overseas** for a second apostille.
3. For foreign-origin LTVP packs, price the embassy route against a home-country notary before you accept a S$2k translation quote.
4. Keep PDFs of every stamped page. Renewals and PR files have a real appetite for repeat paperwork.
