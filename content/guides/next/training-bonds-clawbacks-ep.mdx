r/askSingapore fills up with "is this EP bond even legal?" screenshots. Repay one month if you leave within a year. Repay **the rest of a 24-month salary** if you resign after probation. Hand back the sign-on bonus. The first thing worth saying is that sponsoring your pass doesn't hand an employer a special foreigner penalty regime; you're under the same contract law as everybody else. What actually decides it is whether the clause is a genuine pre-estimate of the employer's loss, which tends to be enforceable, or a penalty built to frighten you into staying, which often isn't. Whether HR can simply take the money out of your last paycheque is a separate question again.

This is orientation, not legal advice. A contested bond needs counsel.

## The clauses you'll actually see

| Pattern | Usually about | Where it goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign-on / relocation clawback | Prorated repayment if you leave early | Common; negotiate carve-outs for employer-initiated exits |
| Training cost repayment | Named courses, exams, overseas training spend | Much stronger if the amounts map to real invoices |
| "Pay remaining contract salary" | Scarecrow retention after probation | Often looks like a penalty rather than liquidated damages |
| Probation exit fee (e.g. 0.5 month) | Early churn cost | Common for sponsored hires, and still negotiable before you sign |
| Non-compete plus garden leave | Competitive moves | Enforceability turns on reasonableness; see [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) |

Scholarship and MOE Tuition Grant bonds are a different animal with statutory liquidated damages attached, and we cover those in [the TG bond guide](/guides/moe-tuition-grant-bond). Don't reason from those rules to a private SME's "foreigner bond", or the other way round.

## What MOM actually constrains

You can resign. Serve your notice or pay salary in lieu, and that is that — an employer cannot "reject" a lawful resignation, however the reply email is worded. [MOM's FAQ](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/termination-of-employment/what-is-termination) says so in plain language, and it's worth having the link to hand.

Salary deductions are tightly limited under the Employment Act. HR cannot casually deduct a disputed bond from your final salary; a deduction has to fit an allowed category, with your written agreement where that's required. [MOM's salary deductions page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/salary/salary-deductions) sets out what is and isn't allowed, and unpaid wages get escalated through [TADM](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom).

The EP cancellation clocks that start when employment ends run on their own track, entirely separately from whether a civil claim for damages exists. That side is in [cancel and STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

Which brings us to the WhatsApp message saying "we will report you to MOM if you don't pay the bond". That's a WhatsApp message. It is not a court judgment, and it proves nothing except that somebody was willing to send it. Keep everything in writing.

## Negotiate before the IPA, not after

Once you've flown, resigned from the old job and started, your leverage is gone. Everything you want changed has to change before you accept.

Ask for the full employment contract, not just the offer letter's highlight reel. The bond usually lives in a clause the letter never mentions. Strike or cap any "remaining term salary" line, and if they insist on something in its place, push them onto a named training cost schedule, because a number tied to real invoices is easier to interrogate and much harder to inflate later.

Carve out the clawback for the situations where they end it: termination without cause, retrenchment, or a failure to secure or renew your pass. That isn't a cheeky ask. It's the exact scenario where repaying a bond would hurt most and deserve it least.

Then line the notice period, any garden leave, and the bond clock up with the [new-employer IPA first](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) sequence. **Never resign into a bond fight without a landing IPA.** And check the bonus and AWS eligibility dates as a separate exercise, because performance-bonus clawbacks tend to hide in policy letters rather than in the EP clause you're busy reading.

## If they chase you for payment after you've left

Ask for a written calculation tied to actual costs, or to the schedule you signed. Round numbers have a habit of shrinking once somebody has to itemise them.

Don't ignore formal demand letters. Silence escalates matters, and it reads badly later if this ever gets in front of anyone who matters. Do think about whether the sum being claimed is a penalty or genuine liquidated damages, which is precisely the question private counsel or a legal clinic can answer against your facts rather than in the abstract.

Meanwhile, keep your pass, IR21, and final-pay trails clean. Immigration hygiene and a civil dispute run on parallel tracks, and letting the first slip because you're consumed by the second is how a money argument turns into a status problem.

## What to get in writing before you sign

| Ask in writing | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Exact clawback trigger dates | "Within 12 months" and "before anniversary of confirmation" are not the same date |
| Whether an employer-initiated exit waives repayment | Retrenchment and PIP exits |
| GST and training invoice backup | Opaque "training cost" figures balloon |
| Deduction or invoice after your last day | This is what protects your final salary |
