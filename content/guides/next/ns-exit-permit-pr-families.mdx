Most families who take PR for their sons find out about Exit Permits years later, and usually at the worst possible moment: a boarding school place comes through, or an overseas degree, or a parent's secondment lands, and suddenly there's a permit standing between the family and the plane. It isn't one set of rules, either. CMPB and MINDEF treat pre-enlistees and NSmen quite differently, and Reddit threads cheerfully mix the two. The age band on your son's letter decides which half applies to you.

This is orientation, not personalised NS advice. Write to CMPB or use OneNS for anything about your actual file.

If you're past ORD, the messier NSman cases — the 12-month threshold against the 6–12 month notify rule, auto-cancel folklore, what to file if you're unemployed or freelance, ICT clashing with a permit — are on [NSman Exit Permit edge cases](/guides/ns-exit-permit-nsman-edge-cases-singapore).

## Who this actually hits

Under the Enlistment Act, male Singapore citizens and permanent residents face NS obligations unless they are exempted. ICA's PR page doesn't hedge about it: males granted PR as a foreign student or under their parents' sponsorship are generally liable. They register around 16½, enlist from 18, and university deferment is not granted. That last point is the one that catches families who assumed a degree would come first.

Exit Permit rules exist because of that liability. If an NS-liable male lives or studies overseas, the permit is what keeps the obligation attached to him rather than fading with distance. CMPB starts sending the letters when boys turn 13, which is often a household's first warning that any of this is coming.

## Before enlistment: from 13 to call-up

This is CMPB's published table. Check it live before you plan around it, because bond amounts move.

| Age band | Overseas duration | What CMPB requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 to 16.5 | 3 months to &lt; 2 years | Exit Permit |
| 13 to 16.5 | 2 years or longer | Exit Permit **plus a bond**: Banker's Guarantee of S$75,000 or 50% of both parents' combined preceding-year gross income, whichever is higher |
| Above 16.5 to enlistment | 3 months or longer | Exit Permit (check the live CMPB page for bond and documents in your case) |

The bond paperwork wants proof of the overseas study plus the parents' employment and income tax assessments, and the Exit Permit only issues after that bond process completes. So this is not a form you file the week before term starts. Everything runs through OneNS: apply, renew, extend, cancel, check status.

If he can't get back before the permit expires, apply for an extension. Those are decided case by case, so don't treat one as a formality. Staying out without a valid permit is where this stops being administrative. You risk bond forfeiture, and you're into offence territory: CMPB cites fines of up to S$2,000 in the 13–16.5 band, and that liability can reach the parent or guardian. For a liable male above 16.5 who remains overseas without a valid Exit Permit, it runs to **up to S$10,000 and/or 3 years' imprisonment**.

One more thing that trips people up. If the institution, place of study, or programme changes after a study Exit Permit has been granted, update CMPB through OneNS. A transfer is not silently covered by the old permit.

## After ORD: NSmen overseas

Operationally Ready National Servicemen run on a different clock from teenagers, and the thresholds are the whole game. An Exit Permit is required for overseas trips of 12 months or longer. For trips of 6 to under 12 months you generally don't need one, but you do need to inform your unit and keep your contact details current; the obligation doesn't disappear, it just changes shape. If call-ups such as ICT fall inside an Exit Permit period, you seek deferment approval separately, with supporting documents. And MINDEF Reserve still expects Exit Permits for trips of 12 months or longer until you reach statutory age or an exemption.

Study Exit Permits usually want the school acceptance letter — an eCOE, CAS, or I-20 style document — and, if that letter has gone stale, something newer such as the student visa or recent fee receipts. File late and you may find the permit approved only from the submission date, which leaves a gap sitting behind you. Forums are full of people who picked up IPPT or defaulter warnings in exactly that gap. Apply before you depart; a permit is not a backdated souvenir.

## An Exit Permit is not a Re-Entry Permit

| Instrument | Owner | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Re-Entry Permit (REP) | ICA | Keeps your PR immigration status alive while you travel |
| Exit Permit | CMPB / MINDEF | NS liability and overseas travel compliance |

You can easily need both at once, and they fail in different directions. Losing PR because the REP lapsed is an ICA problem. Skipping the Exit Permit is an Enlistment Act problem. Our [REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas) covers the first one.

## The questions parents actually ask

"Can my PR son finish a four-year overseas degree before NS?" Deferment is pre-tertiary only, so university studies get disrupted for enlistment once he's liable. And an Exit Permit or bond may still be needed for the schooling that happens before that point.

"We got PR under PTS — does my adult male applicant serve?" The scheme and the grant letter are what matter here, and the "first-gen exemption" meme doing the rounds is not something you can plan around. Start with [citizenship & NS](/guides/citizenship-ns-implications).

"If we renounce PR before enlistment, can he come back on an EP later?" ICA and MINDEF both warn of serious adverse impact on later work, study, and family long-term facilities. Get written CMPB advice before you treat renunciation as a loophole, because the families who discover otherwise find out with no way back. See [renouncing PR](/guides/renouncing-pr-singapore).

## If a secondment is coming

1. Confirm each son's NS liability letter and which age band he sits in
2. Diary the Exit Permit lead times against school terms and visa start dates
3. Budget the bond cash or Banker's Guarantee capacity if it's a long pre-enlistment stay
4. Keep the REP and Exit Permit calendars separate, and keep both green
5. If the household is leaning towards leaving rather than staying, read [long-term stay vs exit](/guides/long-term-stay-vs-exit) and the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook) before anyone trips into default

None of this is fine print on a PR celebration email. CMPB is the source of truth, and it's the only version that counts when a date is disputed.
