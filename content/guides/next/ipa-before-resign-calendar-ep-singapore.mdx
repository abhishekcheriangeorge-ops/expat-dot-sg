MOM's rule fits in one sentence: the new employer files a fresh pass, and you stay in the old job until you're holding the new IPA. Almost everything that goes wrong when people switch jobs on a work pass comes from running those two steps in the wrong order. The parent overview is [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore), and if there's a [training bond](/guides/training-bond-resign-after-ipa-ep) in your contract, read that one before you do anything at all. This guide is the calendar: the part where the "I resigned on a verbal offer" threads go to die.

## Roughly how the weeks go (orientation, not a promise)

| Phase | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet search | Interview while your current EP or S Pass is still live | Tell the market when you're planning to resign |
| Offer diligence | Ask the new HR where they actually are on [FCF advertising](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep) and the COMPASS or S Pass gates | Hear "we'll sort the pass out later" and treat that as a start date |
| **New IPA in hand** | Now you resign and start serving notice, and not a day before | Resign on a verbal, or on "the IPA should come through next week" |
| Notice or garden leave | Line your last day up with IPA validity and the new start date; remember [MC still counts toward notice](/guides/employment-act-leave-notice-ep) | Assume garden leave quietly pauses your bond or non-compete clocks. Read the clause |
| Cancel, then issue | The old employer cancels once employment genuinely ends, and the new pass issues after that | Assume the old pass dies by itself the moment the new one is approved |

Two things surprise people here. Moving to a subsidiary of the company you already work for is still usually a fresh application rather than a transfer, even when it's the same group, the same building and the same payroll system. And your old employer doesn't get to hold your cancellation hostage: once you've resigned with proper notice, they're expected to cancel lawfully, whatever the mood in the exit meeting.

## The collisions people miss

The nastiest one is a long notice period running up against IPA validity. If your contract has you serving three months and the new employer was hoping to start you sooner, that's a conversation to have before you hand in the letter, while you still have the option of not handing it in. Asking in week six of notice is asking too late.

Leave is the next trap. Encashing your annual leave and using it to bring your last day forward are two different deals with different consequences, and people conflate them constantly. We've split them apart in [annual leave encashment and notice offset](/guides/annual-leave-encashment-notice-offset-ep-singapore).

Then there's the household. DP and LTVP holders track your main pass, so a new IPA that doesn't come through isn't only your problem. Budget for card re-issuance, and look at where the gap would fall in the school term before you decide how brave to be.

If you're job hunting because you've been retrenched, the sequencing is tighter again. While you're serving notice you're still employed, and that notice period is your legal window to work. After the pass is cancelled, an STVP lets you stay in the country but it isn't permission to earn. The detail is in [retrenchment on an EP](/guides/retrenchment-ep-benefits) and [between jobs on an STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

## The one rule that matters

**No new IPA, no resignation letter.** Everything else, bonds and leave encashment and IR21 and the family's cards, is a parallel checklist that only starts to matter once that gate has cleared.
