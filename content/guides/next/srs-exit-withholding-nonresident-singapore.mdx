**Short answer:** Work out your residency, which withdrawal path you're on, and what gets withheld, all before you touch the account. SRS on exit isn't a free remittance pot, and the early path costs real money.

The general orientation is in our [SRS investing as a foreigner](/guides/srs-investing-as-foreigner) guide. This one is about the exit math, which is the part people skip when they start treating SRS as a savings account to sweep on the way out.

## Two questions before you hit withdraw

Both of these change the number, and they change it by a lot.

First, are you withdrawing as a Singapore tax resident or a non-resident? Second, is this an early withdrawal or one of the published 50%-taxable paths? Early means a 5% penalty and usually 100% of the withdrawal being taxable. The 50% paths are the one at or after prescribed retirement age with a 10-year spread, and the foreigner lump sum where at least 10 years have passed since your first contribution. Check both against live IRAS text before you rely on either.

| Path (verify live IRAS) | Taxable portion | 5% penalty? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On/after prescribed retirement age (10-year spread allowed) | 50% | No |
| Foreigner lump sum with at least 10 years from first contribution | 50% | No |
| Early / premature withdrawal | 100% | Yes, and non-refundable |

## What gets withheld is not what you owe

For foreigners and SPRs, the SRS operator generally withholds at the prevailing non-resident rate on the taxable portion — 24% in current IRAS examples — plus any 5% penalty. There's a 15% concessionary path as well, where your withdrawals for the calendar year stay under the published cap, commonly S$200,000, and you have no other Singapore income that year. That second condition does more work than people expect, because leaving mid-year usually means you already have Singapore employment income on the books.

Withholding also isn't the final assessment. IRAS may refund part of it or assess you for more later, so model 15% against the progressive rates the way IRAS instructs for non-residents rather than treating the withheld figure as the end of it.

One more thing that surprises people: you generally can't peel "just the dividends" out of SRS. A withdrawal is a withdrawal, and it gets ordinary withdrawal treatment.

## Sequencing this against IR21 and pass cancellation

An SRS withdrawal is not IR21 tax clearance. Different process, different counterparty. What they share is timing: both land in the same month, on the same bank balance, which is the only reason to plan them together.

1. IR21 final-pay freeze first: [tax clearance](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving) and the [IR21, cancel and STVP sequence](/guides/ir21-pass-cancel-stvp-sequencing-singapore)
2. SRS operator forms and the withholding timeline
3. A liquid buffer sized against your [leaving costs](/guides/leaving-singapore-costs)

That third step is the whole point. Paying movers out of a frozen salary and a freshly taxed SRS withdrawal makes for a bad month.

[CPF withdrawal](/guides/cpf-withdrawal-leaving-singapore) is a different gate again, keyed to your status rather than to anything SRS does. Don't run them together in your head.

## The rule worth remembering

Model residency, path, and withholding rate before you contribute, then model them again before you empty the account on exit. **Year-one relief that ends up funding an early non-resident withdrawal is how the brochure math dies.**
