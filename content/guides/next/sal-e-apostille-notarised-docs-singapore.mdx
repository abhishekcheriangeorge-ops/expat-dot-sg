If you've been reading the "do I still have to queue at Adelphi for an Apostille?" threads on r/askSingapore, you're reading advice that hasn't caught up with SAL's e-Apostille rails. The basic direction-of-travel question, which is whether you're sending Singapore documents overseas or bringing foreign documents in for ICA or MOM, lives on our [notary and apostille guide](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore). This page is the narrower one: which electronic route, if any, is yours.

One thing before you spend anything. **Ask the receiving authority whether they'll accept an electronic Apostille at all**, and ask before you pay. Plenty of courts and universities still want wet ink on paper, and that is an expensive thing to discover afterwards.

## Two e-Apostille products people mix up

| Product | What it covers | Fee orientation (verify live) | How you get it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| e-Apostille for notarised documents | Kicks in after a participating Singapore Notary Public creates an e-Notarial Certificate | About S$87.20 incl. GST per Apostille, the same band as paper authentication | You request it at the notary's office. As of SAL's Oct 2025 FAQ posture this isn't a portal you drive yourself |
| Direct e-Apostille for eligible ACRA documents | Selected Accounting & Corporate Regulatory Authority business profiles and extracts, verified through Open Attestation and trustBar | About S$10.70 incl. GST per document | Upload through the [SAL Legalisation Portal](https://legalisation.sal.sg/) and the digital certificate comes back by email |

The price gap is the whole reason to check which bucket your document falls into. Paper Apostilles haven't gone anywhere either, so if the overseas recipient rejects the electronic form, that route is still open.

## When e-Apostille is the wrong first move

If the document was issued abroad and you need it here — a foreign marriage certificate, a degree, a police certificate for ICA or MOM — SAL is the wrong door. Get it apostilled or notarised in the country that issued it, or go the embassy-letter route. SAL doesn't treat foreign originals as Singapore public documents, so no amount of uploading will make that work.

If the destination country isn't a party to the Apostille Convention, a SAL Apostille on its own won't finish the job. You may still need that country's embassy in Singapore to legalise the document on top, which means budgeting for two steps rather than one.

If the recipient has specifically asked for a physical Apostille, take the paper queue and collection. Don't assume a PDF is close enough because it's obviously more convenient for you.

And if what you're legalising is a private document, such as an affidavit or a true copy, it goes to a Singapore Notary first. SAL's Apostille, electronic or paper, attaches to the notarial certificate rather than to your original.

Singapore has affixed Apostilles for outbound documents since the Convention entered into force here on 16 Sep 2021. MFA no longer legalises outbound Singapore public documents; SAL is the competent authority. Older checklists that send you to MFA are simply out of date.

## Cost-control checklist

1. Email the overseas school, court, or registry and ask one question: is an e-Apostille acceptable? Everything below turns on that answer.
2. If the document is an ACRA extract on the eligible list, price the S$10.70 direct e-Apostille before you start paying notary rates for the same result.
3. For private packs, pick a notary from the [SAL directory](https://legalisation.sal.sg/Directory) who takes part in the e-Apostille for notarised documents pilot, assuming digital delivery is what you're after.
4. Keep the verification link and the PDF filed with the underlying document. Renewals have a habit of asking for the same stamp again a year later.

## Decision rule

Ask the recipient what format they want, then pick one of three: the ACRA direct e-Apostille, the notary e-Apostille, or paper. That's the whole decision.
