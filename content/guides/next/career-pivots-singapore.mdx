**Short answer:** A pivot means a new employer files a **fresh pass**. Your old one isn't a transferable token. Stay in the job until the new IPA is actually in your hands. Industry switches are harder here because of sponsorship economics rather than anything about you, and family passes usually track the main EP.

Your pass belongs to an employer, not to you. So a pivot is really a new sponsorship story rather than a new LinkedIn headline, and almost everything that goes wrong is a sequencing problem. Reddit's version is blunter: get the new IPA before you burn the old job, because being unemployed on a cancelled pass isn't a sabbatical, it's an exit clock. And before you assume you can resign whenever you like, go and read whatever training bond or clawback is sitting in your contract ([bonds and liquidated damages](/guides/training-bonds-clawbacks-ep)). People tend to remember those on the final payslip.

## The official sequence (EP / S Pass)

If the new EP is rejected halfway through a pivot, don't resign on the strength of an appeal that's still pending. The realistic version of that situation is in the [EP rejection appeal playbook](/guides/ep-rejection-appeal-candidate-playbook-singapore).

MOM's published position runs like this:

1. The new employer submits a new EP or S Pass application. Nothing is handed over; they file from scratch.
2. You generally don't cancel the existing pass before that application goes in.
3. Approval is decided on the merits under whatever rules are current, meaning qualifying salary plus COMPASS for most EPs, and quota and levy for S Pass.
4. When the new pass is approved, the old one is **not auto-cancelled**. Your current employer cancels it after employment ends, typically within one week.
5. An employer can't weaponise "we won't cancel your pass" after you've lawfully resigned. MOM expects cancellation and can assist if they refuse.

A move to a subsidiary or a related company usually still needs a fresh application rather than a paperwork transfer, which catches out people who reasonably assumed an internal transfer would be internal.

## Why industry switches are harder

Design to data, teaching to finance: these moves do happen, but the company has to justify the hire under current EP and S Pass economics. Locals already find career changes hard, and needing sponsorship on top raises the bar again. That isn't a verdict on your ability, it's arithmetic happening on the employer's side of the table.

So market-test quietly while you're still employed and still compliant, and don't resign on a verbal EP promise, because a verbal promise clears no gates at all. In most cases the new employer also has to work through Fair Consideration Framework advertising ([FCF and MyCareersFuture](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep)) before the COMPASS maths even starts. That's calendar time, and you should be counting it.

## A practical calendar

| Step | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Search quietly while still employed | Your pass stays valid, and your leverage stays intact |
| Offer clears salary plus COMPASS or S Pass gates | Run the SAT with the new HR rather than guessing |
| New IPA in hand | Only now do you resign and serve notice. The week-by-week version is in [IPA before you resign](/guides/ipa-before-resign-calendar-ep-singapore) |
| Line up notice, garden leave, non-compete and bonds | IPA expiry and start dates collide more often than you'd think. Taking MC during notice doesn't extend the clock under the Employment Act ([leave and notice](/guides/employment-act-leave-notice-ep)), and clawbacks don't pause for a verbal offer |
| Old employer cancels, new pass issued | DP and LTVP holders in the family usually track the main pass |

## Family knock-ons

Dependant and LTVP status typically follows the main pass holder's employment, so a pivot that stalls doesn't stall quietly at home. Budget for card re-issuance timing, school term disruption, and the diplomatic clause in your tenancy if the new role doesn't come through. The second-income side of this is in [trailing partner careers](/guides/trailing-partner-career-singapore), and if the pivot is really an exit wearing a different hat, start with the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook).

## When the real problem is unpaid salary

Some "pivots" are actually final-pay theft or levy kickbacks, and those belong at TADM or MOM rather than in a LinkedIn post. Read [salary disputes for work-pass holders](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom) before you leave the country, because leaving is how an evidence trail goes cold. International graduates still serving an MOE Tuition Grant bond have another clock running when they resign into a gap, so check the TG&S terms as well: [Tuition Grant bond](/guides/moe-tuition-grant-bond).
