**Short answer:** PR is **discretionary**. There's no public points calculator and there never was one. ICA weighs family ties, economic contribution, qualifications, age, family profile and length of residency, and it doesn't publish the weights. Treat any agency "guarantee" as a reason to walk away.

That's uncomfortable if you've come from a points-based system somewhere else, where you could at least add up your own score before applying. Here you can't, and the vacuum fills with consultants implying they know the formula. They don't. What follows is what ICA actually publishes, and how to read everything else you'll be told.

## What ICA actually publishes

ICA says it takes into account factors such as:

- Family ties to Singaporeans
- Economic contributions
- Qualifications
- Age
- Family profile
- Length of residency

It uses those to assess whether you can contribute, integrate, and sink roots here. EP and S Pass holders apply through ICA's e-Service with Singpass. The published processing guidance is within 6 months when your documents are in order, though a fair number of cases run longer and nobody will explain why yours is one of them.

On money, as published on ICA's PR page at last review: S$100 per application when you submit, then formalities fees for the Entry Permit, Re-Entry Permit and Identity Card if you're approved. All submission fees are **non-refundable**, which is worth sitting with before you decide to apply on a loop.

Use [ICA — Becoming a Permanent Resident](https://www.ica.gov.sg/reside/PR) for eligibility categories, documents, fees, and Singpass requirements. ICA states it has **no affiliation** with commercial “PR specialists.”

## Who can apply

Per ICA, foreigners may apply if they are, among other categories:

- Spouse of a citizen or PR, with the sponsor logging in via Singpass. The deep dive is [PR for spouse](/guides/pr-for-spouse-singapore), and the stay bridge while you wait is [spouse LTVP and PMLA](/guides/spouse-ltvp-pmla-singapore)
- Unmarried child under 21 of a citizen or PR, within the marriage or legal adoption rules — [PR for a child](/guides/pr-for-child-newborn-singapore)
- Aged parent of a citizen aged 21+. Note the sponsor must be an SC; PR children use LTVP instead, which catches a lot of families out — [aged parents, PR vs LTVP](/guides/aged-parents-pr-ltvp-singapore)
- Holder of an Employment Pass or S Pass
- Student who has passed at least one national exam (PSLE, GCE N/O/A) or is in the Integrated Programme — [student PR](/guides/pr-for-student-singapore)
- Foreign investor through EDB's Global Investor Programme, which is a separate track with its own rules

## How to read peer anecdotes

The Singapore Expats Forum and Reddit are full of "approved on try 2 after my salary jumped" stories, and they're worth reading as illustrations of how uncertain this is rather than as policy. The pattern to watch for is someone generalising from one approval to a rule.

| Anecdote pattern you will hear | Safer interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| "Wait 2–3 years then apply" | Tenure helps you tell an integration story, but it isn't a published rule |
| "You need S$X salary" | ICA publishes no salary floor for PR; economic contribution is one factor among several |
| "Kids in local school helped" | Consistent with the integration and roots language, though not a checklist item ICA publishes |
| "The agency guaranteed approval" | Ignore it. ICA explicitly disclaims commercial agents |
| "Rejected, then approved after a promotion" | Profiles change. Re-applying without new substance rarely does much |

Don't build yourself a private scoring system out of these. And if a consultant tells you they have insider knowledge of quotas, that's your cue to leave.

## Timing that's actually a strategy

The things you can control are stable employment, clean tax filings, and a family plan that hangs together when someone reads it cold. Build those first. Then line the application up against the schooling and housing decisions you'd have to unwind if you're refused, because a refusal you planned for is an inconvenience and one you didn't is a very expensive year.

Resist the urge to apply every six months with identical documents. It doesn't help, and the fees add up. If you have sons, model the National Service liability **before** you celebrate an in-principle approval, not after. And if you've already been refused or you're stuck pending, we wrote a separate playbook for that: [PR rejection, reapply and appeal](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply). ICA runs an appeal e-Service, which is not the same thing as the email folklore you'll be offered.

## National Service: non-optional reading

Under the Enlistment Act, male citizens and PRs are generally liable for National Service unless exempted. ICA's PR page summarises the registration and enlistment timing, and it warns in plain terms that renouncing or losing PR without completing full-time NS has serious adverse effects on future work, study and long-term immigration facilities — and not only for the person concerned, but for family members too.

That's the part households tend to skip in the excitement of an approval, and it's the part that's hardest to undo. Families planning overseas schooling also need to understand Exit Permits and bonds before the first term starts: [NS Exit Permit for PR families](/guides/ns-exit-permit-pr-families).

## What changes after approval

Day-one admin is a project in itself, and we've put the full list in [after getting PR](/guides/after-getting-pr-checklist). The short version is that your money and your mobility both change.

CPF contributions typically begin once you're an employed PR, so remodel your take-home pay before you commit to a bigger lease ([CPF and foreigners](/guides/cpf-and-foreigners)). Expect MediShield Life, and often CareShield and DPS letters, because you're no longer outside the resident healthcare stack. Housing and school fee bands can shift as well, so verify the live HDB and MOE rules rather than assuming the better rate applies automatically.

Two bits of admin that people forget until they hurt. Re-Entry Permits matter the moment you travel or take an overseas secondment, so treat REP renewal as annual admin rather than a souvenir stamp ([REP guide](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas)). And once CPF contributions start, make a CPF nomination and a Singapore-aware will ([wills and probate](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore)), because CPF doesn't pass under a will from somewhere else.

PR is a roots decision, not a coupon code for cheaper international school. If you're still undecided about the next five years, read [long-term stay vs exit](/guides/long-term-stay-vs-exit) before you file anything.
