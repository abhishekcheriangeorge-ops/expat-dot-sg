The full plan audit lives in [health insurance for expats](/guides/health-insurance-expat). This one is about a narrower and nastier moment: the gap between one job's group cover ending and the next one starting, which most people discover they have while sitting in A&E.

## Group cover usually dies with the employment, not the pass

Most employer schedules end cover on your last day of employment, sometimes at midnight after the cancellation goes through. That's earlier than people assume, because they're thinking about the pass rather than the payroll. Staying on an STVP after your EP is cancelled keeps you lawfully here; it does nothing at all for medical cover, and the two get conflated constantly. More on the stay side in [between jobs and the STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore).

Worth remembering what's underneath: EP and S Pass holders sit outside MediSave and MediShield anyway, and public hospitals bill unsubsidised foreigner rates unless a specific scheme says otherwise. So there's no quiet safety net behind the group plan. When it stops, you're paying list price.

| Window | Typical cover posture |
| --- | --- |
| Employed on an EP | Employer group cover, sometimes with a personal top-up |
| Notice period or garden leave | You're usually still employed, so group cover may continue. Read the schedule rather than assuming |
| The day after employment ends, and any STVP time | Usually no group cover at all |
| Day one with the new employer | New group cover, possibly with waiting periods, and underwriting for dependants |

## Get these in writing before you resign

1. The exact date your group cover ends, from HR or the broker. Not "end of the month" — the date.
2. Whether your dependants drop off on the same day, which they often do.
3. The start date of whatever personal plan you're moving to, and its waiting periods. Maternity and pre-existing conditions are the two that bite hardest here, because a new plan won't backdate around a pregnancy you already know about.
4. A Letter of Guarantee path if any elective care is scheduled anywhere near the gap.
5. A cash buffer for deposit-heavy private care. Stack that against your [leaving costs](/guides/leaving-singapore-costs) and remember the final-pay freeze that comes with IR21 — see [IR21 sequencing](/guides/ir21-pass-cancel-stvp-sequencing-singapore).

If the injury happens at work, that runs on [WICA](/guides/wica-work-injury-compensation-ep) rails first, not on a travel policy you bought for a holiday. Nobody's travel insurance is going to quietly absorb an ICU stay.

## The rule to hold onto

**Assume your cover ends when your employment ends**, start a personal bridge plan before cancel day rather than after, and don't schedule elective care into a week when you're uninsured. Having a lawful stay and having medical cover are two different products, and one of them does nothing for the other.
