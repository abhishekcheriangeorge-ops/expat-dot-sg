The mechanics of the test itself are on [AEIS and S-AEIS](/guides/aeis-saeis-international-students), and the wider picture of what the MOE system is actually like sits on [local schools for expats](/guides/local-schools-for-expats). This page is about the decision that comes after you've sat the test: whether to hold or drop the international-school place you're paying for.

## Passing isn't the same as getting the school you wanted

AEIS and S-AEIS measure your child's performance, but they also measure something you have no control over, which is where the vacancies happen to be. MOE assigns to schools with seats. That won't necessarily be the brand-name primary local families ballot for.

So crossing the score line still leaves you with three possible mornings. If a place is offered, you register at the school MOE designates and you model the commute properly before you celebrate anything. If your child passes but there's no place, or only options you can't live with, you're back to the international hold or another cycle, with the test fee already spent. If they don't pass, the fee is gone in exactly the same way. **The test fee is non-refundable in all three cases.**

## The calendar forks that kill holds

| Path | Typical start | Hold strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AEIS | January the following year | Keep the international seat through the outcome email if the cash allows |
| S-AEIS | Apr/May the same year | Fewer seats going, so it's the worst thing to be relying on if you've already cancelled a private hold |
| P1 IS | Separate May interest window | Not AEIS at all, and age-gated at 6 to 6+ on 1 Jan of the admission year |

The thing to avoid is cancelling a paid international hold on the reasoning that you'll just do S-AEIS if this round doesn't work out. That's the round with fewer seats, not more.

## Decision rule

Budget AEIS as a lottery ticket with a fixed start date, and keep the private hold until an offer letter names a school you can genuinely commute to. Assignment beats preference here, every time. International fees only beat a cross-island Year 3 surprise if you planned and funded the buffer in advance.
