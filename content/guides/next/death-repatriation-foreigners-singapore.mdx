The r/askSingapore threads on this, the ones titled "what happens when a foreigner dies here?" and "reporting death of a foreign spouse", tangle three separate clocks together: medical certification and ICA registration, NEA's burial, cremation and repatriation logistics, and the bank and probate side. They are not the same job, they don't move at the same speed, and different people handle each one. This guide is orientation for next of kin. It isn't grief counselling and it isn't legal advice.

## Registration is mostly automatic now

Since 29 May 2022, NEA's citizen-facing guidance has been that the public generally doesn't queue at a physical counter to register a death. A doctor certifies the death online, registration follows from that, and a digital death certificate is issued, retrievable through My Legacy along the path ICA points next of kin to.

One thing to do straight away rather than later: download that certificate as soon as you have the certificate number. NEA gives a **30-day download window**. Thirty days sounds generous until you're also arranging a funeral, fielding calls from home, and working out what the employer needs from you.

With the digital certificate in hand — or, where it isn't available, an alternative such as a manual confirmation of death or a hospital printout — next of kin or an authorised funeral director applies for a Permit to Bury/Cremate on NEA's ePortal. That permit comes before you can book anything at a government or private cremation or burial facility.

| Step | Who handles it, and where |
| --- | --- |
| Certification | The doctor or hospital |
| Digital death certificate | Download promptly. NEA's window is 30 days |
| Permit to bury or cremate | NEA ePortal, or an authorised funeral director acting for you |
| Embassy or mission | Notify them for passport cancellation, consular help and home-country paperwork |
| Pass and FIN admin | Employer and ICA as relevant. Don't work to timelines you read on a forum |
| Estate and banks | Singapore probate or letters of administration. See [wills and probate](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore) |

## When a PR or long-term pass holder dies overseas

If a Singapore citizen, PR or long-term pass holder dies outside Singapore, ICA's published sequence has two steps. First, register the death with the foreign authority and obtain that country's death certificate. Second, report the death to ICA through the FormSG path on its overseas-death page, with identity documents, the foreign death certificate, and the deceased's Singapore ID or long-term pass card where you have it.

ICA charges no fee for the overseas-death report and gives a general processing time of about three working days once the documents are complete, with complex cases taking longer. Bringing remains or ashes into Singapore is a separate matter, covered on NEA's overseas-death pages rather than by ICA's immigration registry step. Keep the two straight in your head; they are different offices asking for different things.

## Repatriation or local cremation

Local cremation or burial needs the NEA permit first. After that, funeral directors handle the facility bookings, and they do this every week.

Repatriating remains is the harder path by some distance. You're dealing with airline rules, destination-country rules, coffin permits, and frequently embassy letters. Budgets and timelines vary wildly depending on where you're sending someone. Start the mission and a funeral director on the same day rather than one after the other, because this isn't a process that rewards doing things in sequence.

Ashes are usually lighter paperwork than a coffin, but not no paperwork. Expect to need both the death and cremation certificates, and to satisfy airline screening rules. The hard gate is almost always destination customs, not some imagined Singapore ban on ashes leaving the country.

Keep passport biodata pages, marriage and birth certificates and pass cards together in one folder. You will be asked for the same documents by several different organisations, and having to find them twice is a small cruelty you can spare yourself. If a foreign court needs anything apostilled or notarised, that's in [document apostille](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore).

## Survivors who were on the deceased's pass

When the main EP holder dies, a Dependant's Pass or LTVP doesn't quietly convert into a right to stay and work. MOM's cancellation path cancels the DPs along with the main pass, and can issue an **STVP of up to 90 days** for survivors still in Singapore. That is permission to stay, not permission to work, and it runs out fast. The deep dive is [EP death → DP cascade](/guides/ep-death-dependant-pass-cascade-singapore), with [Dependant's Pass](/guides/dependant-pass-singapore) and [LTVP](/guides/ltvp-singapore) alongside it. Whatever a forum thread says MOM did for somebody else, that was not an approval for you.

## This is not the same thing as probate

Banks freeze Singapore accounts until they see a Singapore grant, or a foreign grant that has been properly resealed here. For PRs, CPF follows the nomination rather than the will, which catches families out badly, because the will feels like the document that should govern everything and on this point it doesn't. Build that map while the person is still alive if you possibly can: [wills, probate, CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore) and [LPA and AMD](/guides/lasting-power-attorney-amd-foreigners).
