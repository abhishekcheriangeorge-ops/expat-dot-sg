**Short answer:** Either your employer can name the exact published exemption they're relying on, or the role runs the 14-day clock. Skipping the ad doesn't waive fair hiring, and it doesn't lower the bar on what the EP file has to show.

The parents to this one are [Fair Consideration Framework](/guides/fair-consideration-framework-ep) and the [FCF 14-day clock](/guides/fcf-mycareersfuture-14-day-clock-ep-singapore). This companion is about the edges, where a candidate hears "we're exempt" and politely stops asking questions.

## Exempt from advertising ≠ exempt from fairness

A published FCF exemption lets an employer skip the public MyCareersFuture ad clock. That is all it does. It doesn't waive fair hiring, it doesn't soften COMPASS or salary substance, and it doesn't excuse a gap between the role that was advertised and the role you were actually offered.

So when HR names an exemption, each one comes with an obvious follow-up question.

If it's a fixed monthly salary at or above S$22,500, ask whether that's the fixed monthly figure going onto the EP file. Total-package folklore, with bonus and allowances stapled together to clear the bar, is not the same number, and you're the one who wears the consequences if the filing doesn't hold up.

If it's an employer with fewer than 10 employees, ask which headcount date and which definition they're using. It's a real exemption, and it's also one that quietly stops applying as a company grows.

If it's a vacancy of 1 month or less, ask whether this is genuine short cover or a permanent role wearing a short label.

And if it's an intra-company transfer or another published ICT path, ask which exact MOM exemption category is going on the filing. "It's an ICT" on its own isn't a category.

## What to ask when HR says "exempt"

Get the exemption category in writing. Not a Slack message saying not to worry: the actual category name, in an email you can keep.

If an ad did run, screenshot it. Job Advertisement ID, dates, title, salary band. Ads come down, and you want the record while it still exists.

Line your contract's fixed pay up against what goes into EP Online and COMPASS C1. Bait-and-switch ads, where the filed role drifts from the advertised one, are the kind of thing that reads badly under audit.

"We already picked you on day two" is **not a published shortcut** past a mandatory ad window when no exemption applies. It describes what happened in the hiring process. It isn't a basis for skipping the ad.

And if the IPA does come back against you on FCF or COMPASS hygiene, the recovery path is in the [rejection playbook](/guides/ep-rejection-appeal-candidate-playbook-singapore).

## Decision rule

Name the exemption or run the 14-day clock. Fair hiring applies either way, because an exemption changes the advertising theatre, not the substance the file has to carry.
