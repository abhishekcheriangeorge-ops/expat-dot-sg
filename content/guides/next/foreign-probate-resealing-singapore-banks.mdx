Death-admin threads on r/askSingapore stall at exactly the same counter every time: **DBS, UOB and OCBC will not release Singapore balances on a foreign grant alone**. The broader estate rails — the will, CPF, repatriation — are covered in [wills, probate & CPF nomination](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore). This companion piece handles the narrow stretch in between, from a foreign grant to a Singapore bank.

Treat it as orientation rather than estate advice. A contested estate needs Singapore counsel, not a guide.

## Why the freeze happens

Nobody at the branch has any discretion here. The bank needs a court document naming who is entitled to the money, and until that document exists the account simply sits there.

| Asset | What banks and agencies usually want |
| --- | --- |
| Singapore bank or brokerage | A Singapore grant of probate or letters of administration, or a **resealed** qualifying foreign grant |
| CPF, if PR contributions exist | A valid CPF nomination. The will on its own is never enough |
| Condo or restricted residential interest | Estate counsel, plus possible Residential Property Act disposal clocks |

A Malaysian, UK or Australian grant that settled everything at home will often still need a Singapore court step before a local bank moves. That catches families out, and it tends to catch them out several weeks in, when they thought the hard part was behind them.

## Resealing, or starting again in Singapore

| Path | When people use it | Reality check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reseal a Commonwealth or otherwise qualifying foreign grant | The foreign grant has already issued, and the Singapore assets are the secondary part of the estate | Only certain grants can be resealed under the Probate and Administration Act framework, and counsel is who confirms whether yours is one of them |
| Apply for a fresh Singapore grant or LA | There's no resealable grant, or there are domicile or form problems | Expect to have to put in foreign-law evidence where the deceased died domiciled outside Singapore |
| "Just show the will" | Never | Banks want a court grant. A PDF of the will doesn't get you past the counter |

Electronic foreign grants and sealed originals each carry their own filing and verification rules under the Family Justice Courts practice directions. This is not a place to improvise from a forum post.

## What to gather first

1. The death certificate, plus passport bio pages for both the deceased and the executor or next of kin.
2. Singapore bank, SRS and brokerage statements, with the account numbers on them.
3. The original will, or the sealed foreign grant, together with certified copies.
4. Marriage and birth certificates tying the applicant to the deceased.
5. The CPF nomination screenshot, if the deceased was or is a PR contributor — see [wills & CPF](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore).

Planning for incapacity while someone is still alive is a separate rail altogether, and that one is [LPA / AMD](/guides/lasting-power-attorney-amd-foreigners). Certificates and repatriation logistics sit with [death & repatriation](/guides/death-repatriation-foreigners-singapore).

## The rule to hold on to

Singapore cash stays frozen until there is a Singapore grant or a reseal. Get counsel involved early rather than waiting for "the foreign probate to finish everything" first, because the Singapore step doesn't start moving until somebody starts it. The parent estate map stays on [wills, probate & CPF](/guides/wills-probate-cpf-nomination-singapore).
