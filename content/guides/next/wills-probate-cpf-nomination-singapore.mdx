r/askSingapore death-admin threads keep arriving at the same shock. Singapore banks freeze the accounts until someone produces **a Singapore grant of probate or letters of administration**, and a Malaysian or UK grant on its own often isn't enough to get them open. Separately, and this is the bit people get wrong in writing, **CPF never follows your will**. It follows a CPF nomination, or the Public Trustee if you never made one. Planning for a loss of capacity while you're still alive runs on a different rail again — that's [LPA and AMD](/guides/lasting-power-attorney-amd-foreigners). Certificates, NEA permits and the repatriation logistics sit in [death and repatriation](/guides/death-repatriation-foreigners-singapore).

## Three rails, and they don't talk to each other

| Rail | What moves it | Where expats get caught |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singapore bank / brokerage / condo | Singapore probate or LA, or resealing of a qualifying foreign grant | Assuming home-country probate “just works” at DBS or UOB |
| CPF (if you are or were PR) | CPF nomination pays the nominees; with no nomination, the Public Trustee distributes | Putting the CPF instructions only inside a will |
| Embassy / repatriation | Mission, then the ICA and NEA sequence | Leaving next of kin no asset list — see [death admin](/guides/death-repatriation-foreigners-singapore) |

## Make a Singapore-aware will while you still can

If you hold local bank accounts, SRS, or an interest in property here, a will that's valid under Singapore's Wills Act and names an executor who can actually act in Singapore saves your family months. Not an afternoon of paperwork. Months.

Three things are worth keeping somewhere your family can find them. A one-page asset map covering banks, insurers and whoever administers your employer equity. Digital copies of passports, marriage and birth certificates, and pass cards. And the name of counsel who understands foreign domicile affidavits, because that's what the filing turns into when the deceased lived somewhere other than Singapore.

## Foreign grants: reseal, or start again here

Some Commonwealth grants can be resealed so they take effect in Singapore, under the Probate and Administration Act framework. Where that route isn't open, you're looking at a fresh Singapore grant instead, often with evidence of foreign law attached because the deceased died domiciled outside Singapore.

There's real procedural detail underneath all this. Original wills and sealed foreign grants carry their own filing and counter-verification steps, and electronic foreign grants have specific verification rules set out in the practice directions. It's solicitor work, not a Reddit checklist, and it's worth starting long before anyone actually needs cash out of a Singapore account.

## The restricted-property clock

If a foreign person inherits an interest in restricted residential property, the Residential Property Act disposal clock can force a sale. It's commonly discussed as **5 years** from death, with possible extension or SLA approval paths. Note who counts as a foreign person here: it includes many PRs, so the household that assumes it's exempt often isn't. If you own landed property, or anything else in the restricted category, raise it early in estate planning rather than leaving your executor to find it.

## CPF nomination is free, and it's separate

If you're a PR and contributions have started, nominate now. You do it online through Singpass and you can name up to 15 nominees. You'll also need two witnesses aged 21 or over who hold Singpass and who aren't themselves nominees, and they have to confirm within 7 days.

Skip the nomination and CPF doesn't quietly pass to whoever your will names. It goes to the Public Trustee for distribution under the intestacy and inheritance rules, with fees and delay attached.

</OfficialSource>

EP and S Pass holders usually have no CPF at all, which is exactly why this section gets skipped. But the same household frequently includes a PR spouse who does have an account. "We're expats, so nomination doesn't apply to us" is how an avoidable Public Trustee file gets opened.

Witness confirmation clocks, nominee-conflict traps and the amend-after-marriage housekeeping all live in [CPF nomination, Singpass and witnesses](/guides/cpf-nomination-singpass-witnesses-singapore).

## Leaving Singapore, or giving up PR

The full CPF cash-out only happens once you're no longer a citizen or PR. If you can, close the account and move the money to a bank while you've still got Singpass working ([CPF on leaving Singapore](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/cpf-asset-management/on-leaving-singapore); [re-entry permits and PR from overseas](/guides/re-entry-permit-pr-overseas)).

None of this is morbid. It's how you stop a Singapore life from turning into a Singapore freeze.
