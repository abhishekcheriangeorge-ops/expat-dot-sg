The "how do I convert my spouse" threads on r/askSingapore tend to mash three different things into a single word: the marriage itself, an LTVP, and PR. They aren't the same product and they don't arrive on the same schedule. ICA's eligibility list is clear enough — a Singapore citizen or PR can sponsor a foreign spouse for Permanent Residence through the ICA e-Service. What sponsorship gives you is the right to apply. It doesn't give you a right to be approved, and the gap between those two things is where most of the disappointment lives.

This guide is the spouse row only. Sponsoring a child is [PR for a newborn or child](/guides/pr-for-child-newborn-singapore), and if you're on an EP or S Pass applying for yourself, that's [PR application strategy](/guides/pr-application-strategy).

## What ICA actually lists

| Who's being sponsored | Who logs in | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spouse of a citizen or PR | The local spouse, using Singpass | Not something that happens automatically on ROM day |
| Unmarried child under 21 | The parent sponsors | A separate form path |
| Aged parent of a citizen aged 21+ | The citizen child sponsors | **PR children cannot use the aged-parent row** |

Everyone added to the form as an applicant has to review it before you submit, using Singpass or ICA-issued credentials. That's why ICA tells you to start only once the eligible applicants already have Singpass working. Account approval can take 2–5 working days, and you don't want to find that out on the evening you'd set aside to file.

## Getting married does not get you PR

A solemnisation certificate, or an overseas marriage certificate, does not mint a blue IC. The forum posts describing an application filed the week after ROM are usually describing the rejection that followed, not a strategy anyone should copy. Plenty of citizen-foreigner couples stabilise the foreign spouse's stay on a Long-Term Visit Pass first, with a PLOC or LOC for work where that's available, and only file for PR once there's a household with some history to it. We've written both up: [spouse LTVP and PMLA](/guides/spouse-ltvp-pmla-singapore) and [PLOC and LOC](/guides/ploc-loc-ica-ltvp-singapore).

The stack couples actually run looks like this:

1. A legal marriage ICA can file on, meaning ROM or a properly registered overseas marriage, with certified translations where they're needed
2. A stable story on the sponsor's side, covering employment and housing
3. The foreign spouse's identity documents, their prior immigration history, and any children folded into the same household plan
4. Optional but common: months or years of LTVP residence, tax filings if they've been working, a family profile that hangs together
5. Then the spouse-sponsored PR application

ICA's published assessment themes still apply on top of all that: family ties, economic contributions, qualifications, age, family profile, length of residency, ability to integrate, and commitment to sinking roots. Notice what that list is and isn't. "We've been married X years" feeds one of those themes. It is not a published points table, and nobody is totting up a score you can reverse-engineer from a group chat.

## What it costs and how long it takes

| Stage | What ICA publishes |
| --- | --- |
| When you submit | S$100 per application, non-refundable |
| When you complete formalities | S$20 Entry Permit, S$50 for a 5-year Re-Entry Permit, and S$50 for the Singapore Identity Card when it's issued |
| Processing | About 6 months when the documents are in order, though some files take longer |
| How you hear | Email or MyICA, with older postal outcomes for submissions before 26 June 2024 |

"Non-refundable" is doing real work in that first row. The S$100 goes whether the answer is yes or no, which is the best argument against filing speculatively to see what happens. On payment, ICA lists Visa and MasterCard, AMEX, selected Singapore internet banking, and PayNow.

## Follow the checklist, not an agency's binder

Upload certified true copies, with official English translations wherever the original isn't in English. ICA accepts embassy translations, translations notarised in Singapore or overseas, and privately translated documents attested by an embassy or a notary. What it does not do is endorse commercial PR agencies, whatever the sales pitch implies.

The recurring argument online is about recommendation letters, MP letters and volunteering certificates that ICA never asked for. The safer rule is a dull one: submit what the live checklist and the form actually request. An unsolicited bundle buys you no published bonus, while a missing required document can stall the file or bounce it straight back.

If something material changes while you're pending, such as a new job, a birth, a property purchase or a salary jump, use the in-application upload path while it's open, and otherwise write to ICA quoting the application reference. There's more on that in [PR rejection and reapplying](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply). What you should not do is open a second concurrent application because the waiting got uncomfortable.

## The IPA is not the finish line

In-principle approval feels like the end of the process. It's the start of a second one. You'll need to book biometrics, complete the medical and MediShield steps the IPA names, print the Entry Permit and the Re-Entry Permit, collect the IC, and re-register Singpass against the new NRIC. The full day-one sequence sits on [after getting PR](/guides/after-getting-pr-checklist). **Do not travel on "almost PR" while formalities are still open** — that's how people end up in secondary inspection explaining a document set that doesn't match their status.

If the foreign spouse later sponsors children, fold [child PR](/guides/pr-for-child-newborn-singapore) and a working understanding of NS into the same household plan rather than leaving both as later problems. And if the sponsorship is failing because the marriage is, the pass anxiety belongs with [our divorce and custody guide](/guides/divorce-custody-foreigners-singapore) and with a lawyer. PR is not a substitute for counsel.

## If it comes back no

Spouse sponsorship refusals are common when the marriage is short, the household profile is thin, or nothing material has changed since the last file. ICA doesn't publish individual reasons, so you'll be reading tea leaves whatever you do. Use the official appeal e-Service where you genuinely have grounds. Otherwise wait until your circumstances actually change before paying another S$100, because reapplying into an unchanged set of facts tends to produce an unchanged answer. The [appeal and reapply playbook](/guides/pr-rejection-reapply) covers both routes.

## The short version

Sponsor spouse PR when the relationship and the roots story hang together and the documents match ICA's checklist. Not the week after ROM because someone said the timing was good. Use [spouse LTVP and PMLA](/guides/spouse-ltvp-pmla-singapore) and [PLOC and LOC](/guides/ploc-loc-ica-ltvp-singapore) to bridge stay and work where that's the published path, treat agents as form-fillers at best, and budget six months of patience after you hit submit.
