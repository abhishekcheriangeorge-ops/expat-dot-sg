The "my kid's foreign university wants a stamped citizenship certificate" threads usually end with someone paying for the wrong product twice. ICA's published answer is a digital Confirmation of Singapore Citizenship Status letter: **S$20**, about 3 working days, and no signature, no stamp, no QR. What the product is and who it's for sits on [confirmation of citizenship](/guides/confirmation-singapore-citizenship-status). This companion is about the part that goes wrong, which is the foreign authority at the other end and the FormSG route to get the letter in the first place.

## How to file (post–March 2024)

It's an online FormSG application now. Missions redirect you to [go.gov.sg/confirmationofsc](https://go.gov.sg/confirmationofsc), and the hardcopy route at ICA or at a mission is no longer the path, which is worth knowing before you book a consular appointment you don't need.

The fee is S$20 by card or PayNow, and it's non-refundable. Processing runs about 3 working days when your documents are complete. What comes back is a digital letter stating current status, SC or not SC, and ICA is upfront that it carries no stamp and no QR.

Eligibility is broad: a current or former NRIC or FIN holder, or someone holding a Singapore birth certificate. Be clear about what the letter does, though. It reports status as it stands today. It doesn't rewrite [ORAL](/guides/oral-dual-citizenship-age-21) and it doesn't reopen a [renunciation](/guides/renouncing-singapore-citizenship).

## When the foreign desk rejects the PDF

This is the expensive moment, so slow down before you buy anything else.

Start by asking the school or the mission, in writing, exactly what format they accept: wet ink, an apostille chain, embassy attestation. A vague verbal answer from a front desk is how people end up paying for three documents and using none of them.

Don't assume ICA will reprint a stamped version of this letter if you ask nicely. **The published product is digital only.** If the foreign desk won't accept a digital letter, the problem lives on their side of the table.

If what they actually want is attested copies of other documents, a birth certificate or an IC scan, that's the [apostille and notary](/guides/document-apostille-notary-singapore) stack, which is a separate process from this S$20 status letter.

And if the real question underneath is a child under 21 clearing the checkpoint on a foreign passport, that's [ROE](/guides/right-of-entry-roe-under-21-singapore), not this letter at all.

## Who can make the request

A child under 16 can't be the requestor. A parent does it, or a proxy the parent appoints. From 16 they can request as themselves. If you're going through an appointed proxy, that proxy needs an authorisation letter plus the requestor's Singapore IC.

None of this is difficult, but incomplete uploads burn the three-day clock and you only find out at the end of it. Work from the live FormSG document list rather than from a checklist someone posted a year ago.

## Decision rule

Confirm the foreign authority's required format first, pay S$20 once for ICA's digital letter, then escalate to apostille or notary only for the other documents they've specifically named. The parent overview stays on [confirmation of citizenship](/guides/confirmation-singapore-citizenship-status).
