The "I got retrenched on EP" threads on r/askSingapore always tangle three different questions together: what money am I owed, how long can I stay, and can I keep working while I hunt. The second and third are mostly immigration mechanics, and we've put those on [between jobs / STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore). This page is the money rail, and what "responsible retrenchment" actually obliges an employer to do.

## Retrenchment benefit is usually contractual, not a statutory cheque

If you've come from a country with statutory redundancy pay, this is the part that stings. MOM's [responsible retrenchment](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/retrenchment/responsible-retrenchment) page is blunt about it: what you get depends on what's written in your employment contract or your collective agreement. If there's no provision in either one, the quantum is negotiated, which is a polite way of saying it's whatever you can argue for.

There is a tripartite norm, and it's worth knowing even though it's advisory rather than a hard statutory entitlement. The published figure is about 2 weeks to 1 month of salary per year of service, moving with the company's finances and the industry. Employees with 2 or more years' service are treated as eligible for retrenchment benefit under the advisory. Under 2 years and you may get ex-gratia only, which means a goodwill payment rather than something you can insist on.

Two details quietly change the number. There's no CPF on retrenchment benefit for those in CPF. And if the company cut salaries shortly before the exercise, the computation should use the pre-cut salary — a point you may well have to raise yourself.

The Employment Act does not invent a universal retrenchment payout the way some home countries do. So read your contract the night you get the letter, not the week after.

## What you can still demand on the last day

Whatever happens with the retrenchment-benefit negotiation, the ordinary money sits separately. MOM expects employers to pay all salaries due on the last day, and that includes unused annual leave and notice pay, or salary in lieu, per your contract and whatever Employment Act coverage applies to you. Keep the two apart in your head while you negotiate. See [leave and notice](/guides/employment-act-leave-notice-ep) for the detail.

Where you take a complaint depends on what kind of complaint it is.

If the retrenchment benefit is contractual and the employer simply hasn't paid, that's TADM, and you have **6 months from your last day** to file. That window goes faster than you think while you're job hunting.

If there's no contractual provision and the employer is just coming in below the advisory norm, there's nothing to enforce, so you're negotiating. Non-unionised staff can seek TAFEP engagement, per MOM's FAQ.

Unpaid salary, leave or notice is TADM as well, and [salary disputes](/guides/salary-dispute-tadm-mom) walks that rail properly.

If you believe the selection itself was discriminatory, that's TAFEP or counsel, and it's a genuinely different fight from arguing about quantum. Don't blend the two into one email.

Already deep in a release-and-waiver argument with pass clocks running? That fork has its own page: [when retrenchment benefit goes unpaid, TADM vs TAFEP](/guides/retrenchment-benefit-tadm-tafep-ep-singapore).

## Your notice period is the hunting window

1. While you're still employed, and that includes garden leave and notice, the EP is still your work pass. That time is the most valuable asset you have right now. Interview on it.
2. On cancellation, ask HR **in writing** to request the longest STVP MOM allows. Peers consistently report having to ask specifically for up to 90 days; silence gets you the short default.
3. **You cannot work on an STVP.** IPA-before-start still rules, and [career pivots](/guides/career-pivots-singapore) has the sequencing.
4. Start the IR21 conversation early rather than late. [Tax clearance](/guides/tax-clearance-when-leaving) explains why it lands when it does.
5. Map the knock-ons the same week: lease, schools, helper, and any DP or LTVP hanging off your pass.

The deep stay mechanics live on [between jobs STVP](/guides/between-jobs-stvp-singapore). If the runway is genuinely dead, stop optimising the bridge and switch to the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook).

## Things to ask for that aren't entitlements

None of the following is something you can demand. All of it is askable, and the people who ask generally land better than the people who don't.

Ask for outplacement, extended notice, or paid garden leave even when the cash quantum is stuck. On an EP, time is frequently worth more than another week of salary, because time is what turns into an IPA.

Get the package in writing **before you sign a release that waives your claims**. After you sign, the conversation is finished whether or not the money has actually moved.

And keep the label straight. Retrenchment is not the same thing as misconduct dismissal, so don't let HR re-label it when the facts are plainly redundancy. Counsel is worth a call when the letter smells wrong.
