"Mum's LTVP expires next month" is a monthly fixture on r/askSingapore, and the threads reliably tangle three different things: what happens at first issue, what happens at renewal, and what happens if the sponsoring child leaves. Which product the family should be on in the first place, aged-parent PR against parent LTVP, we sort out on [aged parents PR vs LTVP](/guides/aged-parents-pr-ltvp-singapore). What follows is the renewal and sponsor-change layer for the parent LTVP.

One thing to clear out of the way first. Parents-in-law don't sit on the parent LTVP row. If someone has told you they do, they're repeating folklore, and filing on that basis won't make it true.

## The renewal clocks ICA actually publishes

File **at least 3 months** before the current LTVP expires. That lead time isn't decoration. The new validity runs from issuance, after you've paid the issuance fee, and whatever days were left on the old pass aren't carried forward onto the new one. The renewal doesn't stack on top of what you already have; it replaces it from the issuance date, which is also why nobody gets rewarded for renewing at the last minute.

Costs sit in the same family as first issue, and you should verify them live before you pay: S$45 to submit, S$60 on issuance, and S$30 more for a multiple-journey visa where one applies. Most applications come back within about 6 weeks when the documents are in order. Some take longer, and that tail is the real argument for the three-month lead rather than the rule itself.

Sponsor login is where people stall on the very first screen. If the SC or PR child is 21 or over, that child sponsors through their own Singpass. If the child is under 21, you need a local SC or PR sponsor aged 21 or above instead. It's the same shape as first issue, set out on ICA's parent LTVP page.

## If the sponsor wants out

ICA's published LTVP terms are considerably calmer than the forum panic, and they say four useful things.

The pass is issued on the basis of a local sponsor, so the sponsor is structural, not a piece of paperwork you filled in once. If that sponsor intends to withdraw sponsorship of renewals, **you have to notify ICA**. Another Singapore citizen or PR may take over, but only subject to ICA's approval, which means you're asking rather than swapping. And a sponsor generally can't unilaterally cancel the LTVP without the holder's consent, because ICA expects both consents before it will cancel; ICA itself can still cancel under the Immigration Regulations at its own discretion.

Put together, that means you shouldn't assume Mum's pass quietly survives after the sponsoring child leaves Singapore, renounces PR, or dies. Losing the sponsor is a same-week call to ICA and a same-week conversation about who else could sponsor. It's not a "let's finish the year and sort it out at renewal" problem.

## What renewing doesn't buy you

A renewed LTVP doesn't create subsidised citizen healthcare and it doesn't create HDB purchase rights. Before you treat another year on an LTVP as free, re-check the insurance position and be honest with yourself about caregiver capacity, because those are the costs that actually land ([health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-expat)). And if the household is heading for the exit anyway, the parent's stay needs its own line in the [leaving playbook](/guides/leaving-singapore-playbook) rather than being assumed to follow yours out the door.

Aged-parent PR, which only a citizen can sponsor, stays a separate discretionary file and is covered in the parent chooser guide. Rejections and appeals run on ICA's own appeal rails, whatever an agent tells you on WhatsApp.

## The short version

Diary the ICA renewal at least 3 months out. Don't count on leftover validity, because there's none. Tell ICA early if the sponsor can't continue, and line up another SC or PR only with ICA's approval rather than as a done deal. The choice between parent PR and parent LTVP stays on [aged parents PR vs LTVP](/guides/aged-parents-pr-ltvp-singapore).
